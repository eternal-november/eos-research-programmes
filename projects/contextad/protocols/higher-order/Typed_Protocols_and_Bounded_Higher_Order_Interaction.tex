\title{Typed Protocols and Bounded Higher Order Interaction\\[5pt]
\large Data evidence capabilities and result dependent execution}
\author{}
\date{1 November 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
This dossier gives a bounded typed language over the frozen O3 finite
protocol fragment. Finite public data and explicit evidence are separated
from a linear context of existing capability tokens. Result-dependent
occurrence binding carries each continuation into its declared data and
resource fibre. Data substitution and open procedure plugging are proved,
followed by soundness for causal raw-isomorphism and exact joint-law
equalities. Neither equality is silently promoted to equality of the
phase evaluator's chronological event word.

A finite higher-order extension admits function tables, ranked procedure
codes, fresh sampler codes, finite random selection of functions or kernels,
and bounded stateful services. A storing service implements a sampled
function for every bounded adaptive client at an explicit matched call
interface. Two-call tests distinguish that sharing from a once-selected
random kernel and from fresh execution. The construction gives a language
metatheory and narrow expressivity result in the existing retained,
operational and QBS models; it does not claim unrestricted higher-order
probability, completeness, full abstraction or O5 contextual refinement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and inherited commitments}\label{sec:scope}
The O4 contract~\cite{Contract} asks for a language, not a new notation for
a semantic hom-object. We retain CF-0.1 and its three interpretations from
O3~\cite{O3}: finite acyclic raw syntax, fixed token identities, explicit
disjoint occurrence splits, causal-tree polynomial content and the joint
probability--tropical-cost source. O3 is a frozen input; no operation,
observation or earlier conclusion is repaired by this dossier.

The language below is finite in the sense that every admitted term has a
finite derivation and all its data fibres, sample tables and code menus
are finite. Finite signatures may have arbitrary real probability weights;
the executable examples use rational weights. The finite syntax is not
claimed to enumerate every QBS morphism. There is no recursion, dynamic
fresh-token allocation, unrestricted dependent function space of
procedures, general feedback, posterior conditioning or physical
interleaving rule.

Ordinary data describe. Evidence supports a declared predicate on data.
A procedure code describes executable syntax with a specified input
interface. A capability token is the separate permission/resource required
by an operation. A transition's mathematical model does not manufacture
that token or guarantee an external device's physical execution. A latent
sample witness used to define a model or prove a theorem is not thereby an
observation accessible to the program. In this language, knowledge means
supplied public evidence and observations with typed uses, not a universal
epistemic logic or automatic discovery of the world's semantics.

\begin{theorem}[The bounded O4 language result]\label{thm:main}
For the finite typed language and declared extensions below:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item data/evidence substitution and capture-avoiding open-command plugging
preserve typing, reindex the dependent result and capability families,
and commute with denotation;
\item the operational evaluator has finite completed runs and preserves
the fixed-token ownership invariant; its causal/cost projection is the
retained O3 interpretation and its law is the O3 QBS execution law;
\item the stated raw-isomorphism and joint-law equality derivations are
sound and stable under the admitted language constructors and substitutions;
\item the O3 adaptive allocation protocol and an open two-call
resource-sensitive client translate without changing their declared
observations;
\item every admitted bounded higher-order sort has a positive model,
the shared-versus-fresh tests distinguish their protocols, and the two
sampled-function implementations agree at the bounded adaptive call view.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The individual proofs and their limitations are explicit below. The
source is intentionally uncompiled, as authorized. Source checks and
exact finite regression tests are not proof-assistant verification or a
rendered-layout check.
\section{Finite public data evidence and interfaces}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{The pure syntax}
Fix explicitly enumerated finite base sorts and declared finite function
tables. Types and pure terms are generated by
\[
\begin{split}
 A,B &::= D\mid 0\mid 1\mid A+B\mid A\times B
       \mid\Sigma(x:A).B(x)\mid\Pi(x:A).B(x)\mid\Ev_\varphi(v),\\
 v,w &::= x\mid d\mid\mathsf{table}_f(\vec v)\mid *
       \mid(v,w)\mid\pi_i v\mid\mathsf{in}_i(v)
       \mid\mathsf{case}(v;\vec w)\\
     &\hspace{1.5em}\mid\mathsf{dpair}(v,w)\mid\mathsf{dsplit}(v;x,y.w)
       \mid\lambda x.w\mid v\,w\mid\mathsf{evidence}_\varphi(v;\pi).
\end{split}
\]
Every dependent family here has an explicit finite table of its fibres.
A telescope $\Gamma=x_1:A_1,\ldots,x_m:A_m(x_{<m})$ therefore has a finite
valuation set $|\Gamma|$. The typing judgment is $\Gamma\vdash v:A$.
The usual finite sum/product rules are interpreted by their constructors,
projections and case distinction. The two dependent introduction rules are
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash v:A\quad\Gamma\vdash w:B[v/x]}
 {\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{dpair}(v,w):\Sigma(x:A).B(x)},
 \qquad
 \frac{\Gamma,x:A\vdash w:B(x)}
 {\Gamma\vdash\lambda x.w:\Pi(x:A).B(x)}.
\]
Dependent-pair elimination substitutes both components in the result
family; application substitutes its argument in $B$. Evaluation of a
lambda is its finite table, and evaluation of application is lookup.
Only pure terms occur in pure lambda bodies. These explicitly constructed
finite products do not assume arbitrary dependent products of effectful
procedures in a semantic category.

For a declared decidable predicate $\varphi$ on public data,
$\Ev_\varphi(v)$ has one inhabitant if $\varphi(v)$ is true and none
otherwise. The certificate $\pi$ verifies this condition at the admitted
public valuations; a complete finite verification table suffices.
Evidence elimination uses only the declared predicate. Evidence may be
copied, stored or discarded; there is no elimination from evidence to a
capability, from an almost-sure assertion to supplied evidence, or from a
latent operational witness to a public variable. A primitive may explicitly
return an observation as ordinary data, but that changes its declared
interface rather than revealing all its raw witnesses.

The finite-table constructor ensures that every old CF-0.1 pure map is
representable. Pure syntax is independently specified; we are not defining
terms to be all elements of a semantic hom-object. Pure equations are
$\alpha$-conversion, the pure $\beta/\eta$ equations, finite constructor/case
reductions and supplied pointwise table equations at their dependent types.

\subsection{Capability ownership and public configurations}
Retain the O3 finite pool of actual token identities. A capability context
$\Delta$ assigns kinds to a subset of that pool. A copied ordinary reference
to a token name is not a second ownership judgment. Identity names cannot
be actively renamed, generated or merged by a pure term.

An interface $E$ over $\Gamma$ is a finite family
\[
 E(\gamma)=\bigl(S_\gamma,(X_{\gamma,s})_{s\in S_\gamma},
                         (\Delta_{\gamma,s})_{s\in S_\gamma}\bigr),
 \qquad |E(\gamma)|=\coprod_sX_{\gamma,s}.
\]
A public configuration $a=(s,x)$ owns exactly
$\Delta_E(\gamma,a)=\Delta_{\gamma,s}$. A pure interface term
$f:E\to F$ is a pure finite table on $(\gamma,a)$ together with the
pointwise certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pure-tokens}
 \Delta_F(\gamma,f(\gamma,a))=\Delta_E(\gamma,a)
\end{equation}
on actual identities and kinds. Presentation permutations do not rename
identities. Ordinary data can be contracted or forgotten. Tokens can be
consumed, transferred, explicitly disposed of or retyped only by declared
primitive transitions. Every primitive's output token sets are disjoint
subsets of its input set, even on zero-weight branches.

Interfaces may depend on public data in $\Gamma$, so substitution must
reindex their capability families as well as their data. Numerical cost
is a distinct annotation, not an undeclared remaining-budget coordinate;
a cost bound does not grant ownership, and ownership does not prove a
numerical budget bound.

\section{Commands operations and typing}\label{sec:commands}

\subsection{Open commands and primitive declarations}
A declaration in the finite context $\Xi$ has the form
\[
 X:(\Gamma_X;E_X\Rightarrow F_X;\varepsilon_X).
\]
Its own telescope $\Gamma_X$ describes its public parameters. It denotes
open procedure code with an explicit resource interface, not a closure
owning hidden tokens. These self-contained declarations do not depend on
the caller's ambient $\Gamma$; caller substitution changes their actual
arguments, not their declaration. The command judgment is
\[
 \Xi;\Gamma\vdash t:E\Rightarrow F\;!\;\varepsilon,
\]
where $\varepsilon$ is a finite upper bound on primitive operation names.
It is not a cost bound or an execution permission. Nor does
$\varepsilon=\varnothing$ mean semantic purity: a structural fork can
produce a nontrivial causal tree and several outputs without calling a
primitive. Capability-free likewise does not mean pure. A larger effect upper
bound is allowed by weakening.

At an interface $E$, the current public input is an explicit pure binder
$a:|E(\gamma)|$. Primitive parameters, case selectors and invoked code
expressions are checked in $\Gamma,a:|E|$; pure interface maps already
mean tables in these variables. This distinguished current-input binder
is not automatically persistent after an effect. By contrast, the actual
parameter substitution in $X[\sigma]$ is restricted to persistent
$\Gamma$; its dynamic input is supplied separately through $E_X[\sigma]$.
Information from $a$ that is needed as retained lexical data must be
explicitly framed as below.

The primitive signature is exactly the CF-0.1 signature, with permitted
finite extensions specified separately. At every admitted input and pure
parameter it declares a finite response set, a finite ordered typed
post-list $[d_1,\ldots,d_n]$, and a token-transition certificate; a finite
raw sample set $W_e$ with $p_e(w)\ge0$ and $\sum_wp_e(w)=1$; a response
map $\rho_e$; an event label $\ell_e(w)$ of arity $n$; and a cost
$c_e(w)\in\mathbb N^R$. A continuation sees its own declared post-configuration,
not $w$ or an undeclared sibling. Public response information needed by it
must be in that configuration.

The command constructors are
\[
 t,u ::= \Ret(f)\mid\Op_e(v)\mid\Bind(t,x.u_x)
       \mid\mathsf{case}(v;\vec t)
       \mid\Batch_\chi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\mid X[\sigma].
\]
This is raw syntax: $\Bind$ is retained in the AST, not normalized to a
depth-first event tree. Every interface command is a finite input-indexed
family. Restricting a derivation to a singleton admitted input is an
admissible rule by structural induction: keep the same selected primitive
and pure tables, restrict the left input of bind and preserve all its
actual result continuations. This changes no token transition. We use
that restriction when an interface command is applied at an actual hole.
The main typing rules are
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash f:E\to F\text{ satisfying }\eqref{eq:pure-tokens}}
 {\Xi;\Gamma\vdash\Ret(f):E\Rightarrow F\;!\;\varnothing},
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,a:|E|\vdash v\text{ an admitted parameter for }e\text{ at }E
       \quad e:E\leadsto F_e\text{ has its declared transition}}
 {\Xi;\Gamma\vdash\Op_e(v):E\Rightarrow F_e\;!\;\{e\}},
\]
\[
 \frac{\Xi;\Gamma\vdash t:E\Rightarrow F\;!\;\varepsilon
 \quad(\Xi;\Gamma,b\in|F|\vdash
       u_b:\langle b\rangle_{\Delta_F(b)}\Rightarrow G\;!\;\varepsilon')}
 {\Xi;\Gamma\vdash\Bind(t,b.u_b):E\Rightarrow G
          \;!\;\varepsilon\cup\varepsilon'},
\]
\[
 \frac{X:(\Gamma_X;E_X\Rightarrow F_X;\varepsilon_X)\in\Xi
          \quad\Gamma\vdash\sigma:\Gamma_X}
 {\Xi;\Gamma\vdash X[\sigma]:E_X[\sigma]\Rightarrow F_X[\sigma]
          \;!\;\varepsilon_X}.
\]
Here $\langle b\rangle_{\Delta_F(b)}$ is the singleton public input
interface with exactly the actual post-capability context. Here $G$ is an
interface over $\Gamma$, pulled back to $\Gamma,b\in|F|$ in the
continuation premise. If the elaboration retains a local binder $b$ as an
extra environment tag, its token-preserving ordinary-data projection
removes that extra tag before assembly in $G$, unless $G$ explicitly
records it. The bind premise
is an explicit finite family of derivations, not an untyped command that
receives the whole output list. Every actual hole gets its corresponding
independent execution. Its number and capability kind may depend on the
response. If the desired final interface is itself $G_b$, form the explicitly
tagged sum $G=\coprod_bG_b$ and use its token-preserving injections in the
continuations. No fixed context is substituted for that dependence.

The case rule evaluates its pure selector in $\Gamma,a:|E|$, checks each branch in its
restricted/reindexed public context and requires a common declared target
interface (or an explicit tagged sum). Its effect bound is the union of
branch bounds. It adds no event for inspecting stored data. The batch rule
requires an explicit pure-data split and a pointwise token partition
$\chi:E\to(E_1,\ldots,E_n)$ with pairwise disjoint child contexts and union
the input context. It checks $t_i:E_i\Rightarrow F$, forms the ordered
$\mathsf{Fork}_n$ node and takes the union of child effect bounds. A reserve
must be an explicit child, for example a pure return; it never vanishes.
Distinct child targets may be embedded in a declared tagged target sum.
Nullary execution is legal only with the corresponding empty/disposed
context, not as an untyped deletion operation.

\subsection{Lexical data are carried through the actual interfaces}
The persistent public $\Gamma$ in these rules has an explicit elaboration.
At a fixed valuation, use ordinary-data framing to replace input $a$ by
$(\gamma,a)$ and every actual result hole $b$ by $(\gamma,b)$. Framing
copies only public data, leaving each token subset unchanged. Operation
and fork results carry that same $\gamma$ in each child. Thus a continuation
reads $\Gamma$ from its declared post-configuration, not from hidden source
history. If the source input $a$ is also needed later, it must likewise be
explicitly framed as ordinary data; it is not a silent lexical resource
reserve. Pure maps may drop unneeded public data. No frame carries $w$,
chronology, sibling results or the original input $\Delta$ as duplicable data.
This is the ordinary-data frame already admitted by O3 and O2, and its
leafwise relabeling commutes with grafting. More explicitly, if
$\iota_\gamma(b)=(\gamma,b)$, totalized output is
$T\iota_\gamma(\theta)$. A substitution $\sigma:\Gamma'\to\Gamma$
induces the endpoint map $(\gamma',b)\mapsto(\den\sigma(\gamma'),b)$;
the substitution comparison on totalized interfaces is this endpoint
square, while its fixed-fibre form is the isomorphism below.

\subsection{Finite code construction and invocation}
For first-class procedure values fix a finite library of templates
$s_i$ with rank $r_i$, a finite capture telescope $C_i$, and explicit
interfaces $E_i,F_i$ over that telescope. A value constructor
$\mathsf{code}_i(v)$ requires $\Gamma\vdash v:C_i$ and the checked endpoint
equalities $E_i[v]=E$, $F_i[v]=F$ at the advertised type $\Proc(E,F)$.
The code value is the finite tagged pair $(i,\den v)$; it captures no
capability. Write $\Proc_{\le s}(E,F)$ for the menu whose maximum template rank is
$s$, and fix a finite ceiling $R$ at top level. Within a rank-$r$ template,
the invocation rule is
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,a:|E|\vdash p:\Proc_{\le s}(E,F)
       \quad s<r\quad\varepsilon\text{ bounds all menu bodies}}
 {\Xi;\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{invoke}(p):E\Rightarrow F\;!\;\varepsilon}.
\]
At top level the side condition is $s\le R$ instead. When a rank is
omitted from $\Proc(E,F)$ it is the already fixed applicable finite menu,
not an unbounded code universe. Evaluation first evaluates the pure code
expression to $(i,\eta)$, then
executes $s_i[\eta]$ with the current explicit input ownership. A template
may invoke only smaller-ranked templates, and a code-valued capture must
range over an already constructed lower-rank finite code menu. A dynamically
invoked callback therefore also has rank below its enclosing template.
These level-indexed menus exclude recursive closure environments even when
a captured field is unused. This is finite case dispatch
and data substitution, not arbitrary syntax quotation or recursive
self-application. An open parameter call is similarly administrative; it
inserts no extra causal event. A wrapper that is meant to charge or record
a call must declare that primitive explicitly.

The finite $\Kernel(A,B)$ menu is also ordinary inspectable code data at
its native interface. Its invocation rule dispatches on the chosen code
and input to the declared unary sampler table, drawing a new raw witness
each time. The opaque bounded call adapter of Section~\ref{sec:call}
restricts its clients from inspecting that stored code; opacity is not
silently imposed on all old pure maps. Service calls are the explicitly
typed primitives of Section~\ref{sec:stores}.

\subsection{Complete freely available operations and exclusions}
Freely available syntax consists of the listed pure constructors and finite
tables; copying, forgetting and storing public data/evidence/code; pure
interface maps satisfying~\eqref{eq:pure-tokens}; case; the explicit
return/bind/batch constructors; and administrative calls with their exact
input interfaces. Effect operations are available only when declared by
the finite signature and when their data/evidence/capability premises hold.
Copying code does not copy a dynamic execution or a token. Ordinary aliases
of a name cannot be used to derive two disjoint owners.

A finite $\mathsf{ModelCode}$ sort and forecast table may be declared as
ordinary data. A separately declared capability-free simulation sampler
may use such a model. Neither produces an actuator permission: the physical
actuator operation still has its separate capability premise and declared
transition. No hidden rule converts model accuracy, an evidence term, a
probability table or a native-cell proof witness into that permission.

There are no additional implicit sampling, posterior-conditioning,
disintegration, process-rerun, raw-witness inspection, service-store
inspection, token-creation, token-disposal, effect-exchange or
resource-capturing closure operations. Sampling a command, inspecting its
stored return value and executing that command again are different
constructors or evaluation events. All command and library derivations
are finite; a family of arbitrarily deeper callbacks is not one admitted
finite derivation.

\section{Operational runs and denotations}\label{sec:semantics}
For a closed command at public valuation $\gamma$ and actual input $a$,
write
\[
 a\vdash_\gamma t\Downarrow^{h}_{p,c,w}\theta
\]
for its completed run, retaining raw derivation $h$, weight $p$, cumulative
cost $c$, chronological word $w$ and returned causal tree $\theta$.
Return has one run $(1,0,\epsilon,\eta f(a))$. A primitive has one run for
each $z\in W_e$, of weight $p_e(z)$, cost $c_e(z)$, word
$[\ell_e(z)]$ and tree $\ell_e(z)(\eta d_1,\ldots,\eta d_n)$.
The raw bind rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eval-bind}
 \frac{a\vdash_\gamma t\Downarrow^h_{p,c,w}\theta
 \quad\leaf(\theta)=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)
 \quad(b_i\vdash_\gamma u_{b_i}
       \Downarrow^{h_i}_{p_i,c_i,w_i}\theta_i)_i}
 {a\vdash_\gamma\Bind(t,b.u_b)
  \Downarrow^{(h,(h_i)_i)}_{p\prod_ip_i,\ c+\sum_ic_i,\ ww_1\cdots w_n}
       \theta[\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n]}.
\end{equation}
Batch multiplies child weights, sums costs, concatenates their words in
address order and returns $\mathsf{Fork}_n(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)$;
the structural fork is not inserted in the physical-event word. Case
selects its pure branch. A parameter call evaluates its supplied body,
and ranked invocation evaluates its selected instantiated template. These
administrative steps add neither event nor cost.

\begin{lemma}[Finite execution and ownership]\label{lem:execution}
Every admitted raw choice has a finite completed run. There are finitely
many runs, their weights sum to one, and every output token occurs at most
once and comes from the initial pool with its kind justified by a declared
transition. Public pure evaluation does not inspect raw witnesses or a
private service store.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct jointly on template rank and the finite command derivation. Pure
evaluation is total at each inhabited input. A primitive has a finite
normalized nonempty witness set. Bind chooses one left run and one run at
each actual hole, hence a finite dependent product; summing its product
weights gives one by finite distributivity. Batch is the corresponding
finite product. Case and invocation reduce to the selected smaller
derivation. The token invariant follows because disjoint subsets of
disjoint input subsets remain disjoint; pure maps preserve identities,
and only primitive certificates change kinds or consume tokens. The
noninspection assertion is a separate pure-term induction: every variable,
table, predicate and eliminator has only public arguments. The private
store does not belong to that grammar.
\end{proof}

For each $(\gamma,a)$ define the retained object by the actual finite
run choices
\[
 H(t)(\gamma,a)=
 \bigl(\Omega_t(\gamma,a),(p_t(h),c_t(h)),\theta_t(h)\bigr).
\]
Its coefficients lie in the same joint rig
$(\mathbb R_{\ge0})_{\mathrm{disc}}\times\Cost$ as O3, with
$(p,c)\otimes(q,d)=(pq,c+d)$ and
$(p,c)\oplus(q,d)=(p+q,\min(c,d))$. Its bodies lie in the unchanged
polynomial monad $T$ of planar causal event trees. For bind,
\[
 \Omega_{\Bind(t,u)}(a)=
 \coprod_{h\in\Omega_t(a)}\prod_{i\in\mathrm{holes}(\theta_t(h))}
                   \Omega_{u_{b_i}}(b_i).
\]
This is exactly O1 mixed occurrence composition, with every addressed
occurrence and zero-weight path retained. The formulas give a canonical
bijection with the operational runs by induction on their last rule.
Chronological words are additional operational diagnostics, not part of
this retained-body equality.

The full joint external law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 L(t)(\gamma,a)=\sum_{h\in\Omega_t(\gamma,a)}p_t(h)
                         \delta_{(c_t(h),\theta_t(h))}.
\end{equation}
It retains cost, causal events and all typed result configurations. On
store-bearing interfaces it includes the store in the semantic leaves
until a named closing projection erases it. The O3 law-composition rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law-compose}
 (\ell\star k)(a)=\sum_{(c,\theta)}k(a)(c,\theta)
 \bigl[(c_i,\theta_i)_i\mapsto
       (c+\textstyle\sum_ic_i,\theta[\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n])\bigr]_*
           \bigotimes_{i=1}^n\ell(b_i),
\end{equation}
where $\leaf(\theta)=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$. Finite regrouping proves
$L(\Bind(t,u))=L(u)\star L(t)$: each continuation depends only on its
actual configuration, so grouping histories by the recorded atom does
not alter its choice. Batch is analogous. Finite discrete QBS carriers
and these finite normalized laws supply the positive QBS interpretation;
the execution monad is the O3 $P(\mathbb N^R\times T(-))$, with its
specified independent-occurrence multiplication, not just $P$.

\section{Dependent substitution and open plugging}\label{sec:substitution}
A data substitution $\sigma:\Gamma'\to\Gamma$ is a list of pure terms,
whose $i$th entry has type $A_i[\sigma_{<i}]$. Define substitution on terms,
families, interfaces and certificates by capture-avoiding structural
recursion. In particular
\[
 E[\sigma](\gamma')=E(\den\sigma(\gamma')),
 \quad
 \Delta_{E[\sigma]}(\gamma',a)=
           \Delta_E(\den\sigma(\gamma'),a).
\]
The notation reindexes the actual resource interface; it does not rename
its token identities.

\begin{lemma}[Pure dependent substitution]\label{lem:pure-substitution}
If $\Gamma\vdash v:A$ and $\sigma:\Gamma'\to\Gamma$, then
$\Gamma'\vdash v[\sigma]:A[\sigma]$ and
\[
 \den{v[\sigma]}(\gamma')
      =\den v(\den\sigma(\gamma')).
\]
Identity and composition of substitutions hold up to bound-variable
renaming, including their action on the later telescope fibres.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the pure typing derivation. Variables use the corresponding
entry of $\sigma$; constants and tables evaluate at the same substituted
arguments. Products, sums and finite case preserve the selected constructor.
For a dependent pair, first substitute its first component and then its
second-component fibre. For a lambda extend the substitution by the new
bound variable and apply induction pointwise at each finite argument;
application follows by lookup. Evidence certificates are checked at the
same public valuation after substitution. The simultaneous telescope
induction proves the well-formedness of every later substituted fibre.
Structural recursion also proves the identity/composition equations.
\end{proof}

An implementation assignment $\Psi$ for $\Xi$ gives each declared $X$
a finite body
\[
 \Xi';\Gamma_X\vdash s_X:E_X\Rightarrow F_X\;!\;\varepsilon'_X,
 \qquad\varepsilon'_X\subseteq\varepsilon_X,
\]
with only its declared data captures and no captured ownership. Replace
$X[\tau]$ by $s_X[\tau]$ capture-avoidably. Finite assignments may use
remaining parameters in $\Xi'$, but a closing/ranked assignment cannot
create a recursion cycle.

\begin{theorem}[Typed substitution and plugging]\label{thm:substitution}
If $\Xi;\Gamma\vdash t:E\Rightarrow F\;!\;\varepsilon$, then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\Xi;\Gamma'\vdash t[\sigma]:E[\sigma]\Rightarrow F[\sigma]
!\varepsilon$ for every pure $\sigma:\Gamma'\to\Gamma$;
\item $\Xi';\Gamma\vdash t[\Psi]:E\Rightarrow F!\varepsilon$ for
an implementation assignment as above;
\item there are canonical cost-exact raw isomorphisms
\[
 H(t[\sigma])(\gamma',a)\cong H(t)(\den\sigma(\gamma'),a),
 \qquad
 H(t[\Psi])\cong H(t)[H(s_X)/X].
\]
They preserve all addressed primitive choices, weights, typed leaves and
causal trees. The corresponding operational statement holds after ignoring
administrative call markers, and the joint-law/QBS equations follow.
\end{enumerate}
These substitutions are unital and associative and commute with one
another under simultaneous substitution of actual data arguments.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), induct on the command typing derivation. For expressions at a current input, lift the substitution to
$(\gamma',a)\mapsto(\den\sigma(\gamma'),a)$; the distinguished
input itself is not replaced by a persistent lexical variable. Return uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:pure-substitution} and evaluates its token certificate at
the same valuation. A primitive selects exactly the same declared
operation instance and entire witness/post-context table, including zeros.
In a case the same public branch is selected. In bind, the substituted
post-family is exactly $F[\sigma]$; extend $\sigma$ by its actual result
variable and apply induction to each continuation with precisely
$\Delta_{F[\sigma]}$. The explicit $\Gamma$ frame supplies the same public
environment to every hole. In batch the split certificate is reindexed
pointwise, so its partition and disjointness remain true. At $X[\tau]$
compose $\tau$ with $\sigma$. At ranked invocation apply pure substitution
to the selected tagged capture and induction to its smaller-ranked body.
All cases preserve the declared effect upper bound.

For (ii), induct on $t$. At $X[\tau]$, part (i) applied to $s_X$ gives
exactly the call site's input $E_X[\tau]$ and output $F_X[\tau]$.
Every following result-dependent continuation is therefore well typed.
Other constructors use the induction hypotheses and unchanged interface
certificates. Each repeated occurrence of $X$ still needs its current
input ownership; a code substitution supplies no new split or restored token.

For (iii), use the same inductions on raw runs. A substituted primitive
has the identical selected witness table. A bind reindexes the same left
witness and the same dependent tuple of addressed child witnesses; batch
reindexes the same ordered product. Pure return adds no choice. At a call,
part (i) identifies its instantiated body; ranked invocation is finite
case dispatch followed by this substitution. Thus the maps and their
inverses preserve the specified observations and coefficients. Pushforward
along~\eqref{eq:law} proves the law/QBS assertions. The call case reduces
associativity and interchange of substitutions to pure substitution;
structural induction proves them for the remaining syntax.
\end{proof}
A typed open command context is precisely such a term with declared
parameters. The theorem supplies its actual plugging semantics. It does
not admit an arbitrary untyped client or authorize inspection of a private
witness simply because a metatheoretic implementation assignment mentions it.

\section{Two generated equalities and their soundness}\label{sec:equality}
We distinguish proof cells from law equality. Both judgments compare the
same typed interface, and neither is defined by declaring all semantic
equalities to be axioms. For an open judgment the claim is quantified over
a common well-typed implementation assignment for its procedure parameters.
Congruence is for the command constructors and pure equality of actual
finite code values. It does not identify distinct inspectable procedure
or kernel tags merely because their invoked bodies have equal laws.
A code constructor names a fixed library body; there is no body-law
congruence that changes its tag or capture value invisibly. Thus a client
that inspects native code data cannot exploit a hidden code quotient.

\subsection{Checked primitive cells}
A supplied primitive comparison consists, at every admitted input, of a
finite carrier map $r:W\to W'$ with exact certificates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native}
 p'_y=\sum_{r(w)=y}p_w,
 \qquad c'_y\le\min_{r(w)=y}c_w,
 \qquad\theta'(r(w))=\theta(w).
\end{equation}
The empty minimum is $\infty$. It is cost-exact if additionally
$c'_{r(w)}=c_w$ for every source witness. It is a native isomorphism when
$r$ is bijective and its inverse is also such a cell. These are the O3
cells in the joint probability/cost source, with equality of all typed
leaves, not only terminal ordinary data. For arbitrary real weights,
``checked'' means a supplied valid exact certificate; it does not assert
an algorithm deciding equality of arbitrary real presentations. Rational
finite tables permit the executable checks used in the examples.

\subsection{Causal raw isomorphism}
Generate $t\cong_R u$ by reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity; the checked
pure equations; bind left/right unit and associativity at matching
interfaces; constructor congruence; pure substitution and well-typed
plugging; and supplied cost-exact native \emph{isomorphisms} of primitives.
The unit equations use the identity interface map, and association reindexes
the result-dependent continuation families using their actual holes.
No general exchange law for effectful operations is included.

\subsection{Exact joint law equality}
Generate $t\simeq_L u$ from the raw-isomorphism generators and also every
supplied cost-exact native primitive map, with symmetry, transitivity and
the same well-typed congruence/substitution rules. This permits zigzags of
noninvertible maps. Reversing such an equality derivation does not assert
that the reversed primitive map exists in O1 or is an inverse source cell.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness at the two observation levels]\label{thm:soundness}
At every public input, and every actual private-store input for an
extended interface:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a derivation of $t\cong_R u$ induces a cost-exact invertible cell
$H(t)\cong H(u)$ preserving raw choices up to its stated witness
bijection, probability, causal tree and typed leaves; hence all-path
budget predicates and full joint laws agree;
\item a derivation of $t\simeq_L u$ implies $L(t)=L(u)$ for the full
O3 joint observation. It need not preserve raw witness structure or
all-path budget predicates.
\end{enumerate}
Neither conclusion asserts equality of the phase evaluator's chronological
word. No completeness or full-abstraction converse is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the equality derivation. A pure equation evaluates identically.
Bind units insert or remove the singleton pure witness. Bind associativity
reindexes a witness as one outer choice, its addressed child choices and
their addressed grandchild choices. The two bracketings have the same
probability product, sum of costs and grafted tree, with inverse reindexing;
the inherited O1 coherence diagrams commute because they flatten the same
addressed tuple. A supplied primitive isomorphism has the stated inverse.
Constructor congruence applies pointwise maps in case and addressed products
of maps in bind/batch, using their equal typed leaves. Substitution and
plugging use Theorem~\ref{thm:substitution}. Composition and inversion of
these actual cells prove (i); they preserve even zero-weight raw costs.
Coherence does not say that all unrelated derivations of one equation
produce the same source cell.

For the added primitive step of (ii), body and cost exactness give
\[
 \sum_wp_w\delta_{(c_w,\theta_w)}
 =\sum_y\left(\sum_{r(w)=y}p_w\right)
                        \delta_{(c'_y,\theta'_y)}.
\]
A target with no preimage has weight zero. Hence this is the target law.
Symmetry and transitivity now act on equal laws, not inverse source maps.
Bind and batch congruence follow from~\eqref{eq:law-compose} and their
independent products. Crucially a continuation depends only on the actual
recorded leaf; it cannot choose a different computation using an erased raw
witness. Private stores remain in those leaves across composition. This
proves the full joint-law claim by induction.
\end{proof}

The distinction is strict. A primitive with one weight-one, cost-zero
witness can map cost-exactly to one with that same witness plus an extra
weight-zero witness of cost $M>0$, with identical body. Its law is unchanged,
but its all-path budget $0$ fails. A surjectivity hypothesis would repair
raw coverage, as O3 proves; law equality alone does not supply it. Likewise
a native cost-lowering map from cost $2$ to cost $1$ is not a generator of
$\simeq_L$. Its separately named O3 monotone-coupling comparison is not
exact equality and is not the whole O5 contextual-refinement theorem.

The phase-Bind chronology distinction is equally concrete: two branch
stages can emit $q_1q_2r_1r_2$ under one association and
$q_1r_1q_2r_2$ under the other. Their grafted causal tree and joint law in
this theorem agree. No equality generator erases that operational fact.
\section{Bounded higher order values and executable code}\label{sec:higher}
The higher-order discriminator is a finite conservative extension, not an
unrestricted higher-order type theory. Fix finite types $A,B$, a finite
ranked library of procedure templates and finite data environments for their
captures. Code-valued captures must come from already constructed lower-ranked menus,
and every invoked callback has lower rank than its current template.
A template can invoke only lower-ranked templates. A call is
therefore evaluated by a finite unfolding; it is not a fixpoint rule.
The admitted sorts and their elimination rules are as follows.

\begin{description}
\item[Pure function tables.] $\Fun(A,B)=B^A$ is the finite set of tables.
A pure lambda is tabulated on the finite domain, and application is
lookup. A table and its ordinary data captures may be copied, forgotten
or inspected. They cannot contain a capability token. Applying a pure
table is not an effectful call and has no implicit sampling step.
\item[Procedures.] $\Proc(E,F)$ is a selected finite set of ranked command
templates with data-only captures and an explicit input/output resource
interface. Invocation supplies the actual input capability context and
runs the chosen template afresh. A procedure code may be copied; its
required capability argument is not copied by this operation.
\item[Sampler codes.] $\Kernel(A,B)$ is a finite inspectable menu
$\mathcal K_{A,B}$ of unary, capability-free sampler codes. For each
$k,a$ the signature supplies a finite raw sample table with output in $B$.
Every invocation draws a fresh raw witness. Write
$q_k(a)\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fin}}B$ for its extensional output law.
This code menu is not the set of all stochastic matrices and has no
conversion from an arbitrary semantic kernel. Its native code tag may be inspected as ordinary finite data; this does
not expose a future draw or the actual private witness of an invocation.
\item[Random functions.] $\Rand(\Fun(A,B))$ is notation for an admitted
sampling \emph{command} returning a function table, not a duplicable
computation value. After one execution, its returned table is reusable
ordinary data. Copying or inspecting that table does not repeat the
sampling command.
\item[Random kernels.] $\Rand(\Kernel(A,B))$ is admitted as an explicitly
declared finite command returning one code. Calling that chosen code
repeatedly draws independently conditional on the selected code.
Executing the code-selection command again is a different computation.
\item[Services.] $\Service^j_R(A,B)$ is an abstract method interface owned
by a preexisting linear token $r$, with finite hidden state $R$ and $j$
remaining calls. It is not a freely duplicable element of the public data
context. Initialization changes $\mathsf{Uninitialized}(r)$ to
$\mathsf{Ready}_N(r)$; a call changes $\mathsf{Ready}_j(r)$ to
$\mathsf{Ready}_{j-1}(r)$ and returns one $B$ value. There is no call rule
for $j=0$. A separate declared disposal operation is needed to remove the
token. Initialization never generates a new token identity.
\end{description}
No closure type captures a capability, and no implicit conversion identifies
these sorts. A service method may take a handle as an explicit linear
argument, returning its updated kind or consuming it by an explicit
primitive. An arbitrary resource-capturing closure would need a separate
linear closure rule; it is not disguised as a pure function here.

\subsection{Private stores and actual model interpretations}\label{sec:stores}
To interpret services, use an expanded finite semantic configuration space
\[
 \widehat E=\coprod_{a\in|E|}\Store(\Delta_a).
\]
The store has one private entry for each owned service token and records
its hidden state. The counter is part of the public protocol kind. A
client's $\Gamma$ does not contain the store, and its pure maps cannot
project, test or branch on it. A pure client map acts on public data and
carries every unchanged token's private entry identically. Declared service
methods alone read or update their entries. If a primitive separates the
capabilities among child occurrences, the store is restricted to the
same disjoint token subsets. A hidden state can therefore follow its one
owner, not two independent aliases.

For a service transition table
\[
 S:R\times A\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fin}}(R\times B),
\]
the declared raw implementation is a finite CF-0.1 table on the expanded
configurations. Its event node is unary, its output includes the updated
private store and its token transition is the declared counter decrement.
Thus composition retains state \emph{before} any observation projects it
away. This gives an actual O1 mixed interpretation over $\widehat E$, not
merely a public one-step kernel incorrectly reused as a state-free model.
On old interfaces the store is empty. Old pure maps and old operations
are unchanged; opacity on the new store does not restrict the old
CF-0.1 pure-map class.

\begin{proposition}[Finite higher order interpretation]\label{prop:higher-models}
Every admitted finite function table, ranked procedure, sampler code,
random-function command, random-kernel command and bounded service has
retained, operational and QBS interpretations. The inherited occurrence
composition comparison applies on the expanded store-bearing interfaces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function-table and code-menu carriers are finite. Table lookup is a
pure map. Each sampler or selector uses its declared finite normalized raw
table. Induction on template rank unfolds procedure invocation into a
finite typed command; induction on its typing derivation applies the
interpretation of Section~\ref{sec:semantics}. A finite service method is
likewise a primitive on $\widehat E$. Splitting a store by disjoint token
ownership gives exactly the separation premise of occurrence composition.
Consequently the frozen O3 finite interpretation applies to every unfolded
term. Finite discrete sets are QBS objects, all these finite tables are QBS
maps, and their finite laws are normalized QBS probabilities. No inversion
of a kernel quotient or disintegration is used.
\end{proof}
After forgetting execution cost and causal events and taking the returned
value marginal, the QBS meanings include maps
\[
 \Fun(A,B)\longrightarrow B^A,
 \qquad \mathcal K_{A,B}\longrightarrow(PB)^A,
\]
and the induced maps on random selections to $P(B^A)$ and $P((PB)^A)$.
These are explicitly forgetful output shadows, not identifications of a
code menu with all QBS morphisms. A procedure code instead maps to
$(K F)^E$ at its decorated execution interface, where
$KX=P(\Cost\times TX)$; a function-selection command has actual
execution type $K\Fun(A,B)$, whose returned-value marginal is in
$P(B^A)$. The procedure execution model remains
$P(\Cost\times T(-))$ with the O3 occurrence multiplication; the ambient
Markov Kleisli category $\operatorname{Kl}(P)$ does not by itself retain
these causal and cost observations. The existing QBS randomization theorem
is not a chosen executable inverse preserving repeated-call correlations
\cite{QBS2017}.

\section{Adaptive call observations and sharing tests}\label{sec:call}

\subsection{The bounded comparison interface}
A freely inspectable sampled function table and an opaque linear service
handle have different native interfaces. We compare their implementations
only after giving both the same adapter $\Call_N(A,B)$. Its client makes
at most $N$ sequential calls, may choose the next query or stop decision
from the previous answers and its own declared independent randomness,
and returns finite ordinary data. When it stops with $j$ calls remaining,
its final interface also returns the same owned token
$r:\mathsf{Ready}_j$; it cannot silently discard the handle. Matched
explicit disposal would be a separate declared operation. The client
cannot inspect the adapter's
stored table, selected code or private service state. It supplies the same
preexisting token to either adapter; both use the kinds
$\mathsf{Uninitialized},\mathsf{Ready}_N,\ldots,\mathsf{Ready}_0$.
For the following theorem the declared initialization cost is zero and
each call costs one. Each adapter call is an explicitly declared unary
method table on the owned $\mathsf{Ready}_j$ token: it computes $f(a)$
or samples $q_k(a)$, returns the same identity with kind
$\mathsf{Ready}_{j-1}$ and result $b$, and emits the public call node.
The selected kernel fixtures have zero-cost internal samplers. This is
not an implicit capability frame around a capability-free command, and
no generic such frame for multi-hole procedures is assumed.
These are matched interface data, not a claim that
all real implementations have identical costs.

A call with query $a$ and answer $b$ emits the same unary public label
$\mathsf{call}(a,b)$ in both adapters. Private initialization and management
labels in this comparison are unary. Define a tree map $q_T$ recursively:
it preserves public nodes and their ordered children, preserves holes,
and sends a private unary node $h(\theta)$ to $q_T(\theta)$. It follows by
induction on trees that
\[
 q_T(\theta[\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n])
 =q_T(\theta)[q_T(\theta_1),\ldots,q_T(\theta_n)].
\]
The assertion uses unary erasure; deleting an arbitrary branching event
would not in general be a hole-preserving tree-monad map.
We additionally erase the final private store only as a closing
observation, after the whole client has executed. This leaf projection
is generally not compatible with further service composition and does
not define a memoryless quotient functor. The resulting
\emph{call-view observation} records the public query/response transcript,
ordinary return value, remaining counter and cumulative matched cost.
The theorem below is not equality of the full unprojected O3 observations:
setup events and private stores can differ there. Nor is store erasure a
claim that sequential public kernels may be composed without their state.

\subsection{Sampled functions implemented by services}
Let $\mu$ be a finite law on $F=B^A$. The sampled-function adapter draws
$f\sim\mu$ once, retains it privately and answers $a$ with $f(a)$. The
service adapter initializes $R=F$ with the same law and uses
\[
 S(f,a)=\delta_{(f,f(a))}.
\]
Both adapters thread the same counter token and use the same public call
labels and costs.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded adaptive implementation equivalence]\label{thm:service}
For every finite $\Call_N(A,B)$ client, these two adapters have equal
joint call-view laws, including adaptively chosen queries and stopping,
final ordinary return value, remaining counter and cumulative matched cost.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Draw one common $f\sim\mu$ for the two initializations and couple the
client's declared independent random choices identically. Initially the
visible client states and counters agree. If they agree before a call or
stop decision, the client chooses the same query or decision in both runs.
At query $a$ both implementations return $f(a)$, decrement the same counter,
add cost one and emit the same public label. Their retained function is
still $f$. Induction on the at-most-$N$ calls gives equal complete
call-view observations in this coupling. Summing its masses proves equality
of the joint laws. Private raw initialization records need not be in
bijection; they are outside the observation claimed here.
\end{proof}
For a deterministic client making exactly $m\le N$ calls with adaptive
queries $a_i(b_{<i})$, the theorem is the explicit identity
\[
 \Pr(b_1,\ldots,b_m)
 =\sum_f\mu(f)\prod_{i=1}^m
     [f(a_i(b_{<i}))=b_i].
\]
Finite randomized clients are finite mixtures of adaptive decisions, and
the coupling already covers them. The result is a bounded interface
implementation theorem, not full abstraction or O5 replacement in every
possible future context.

For a genuinely adaptive instance, take $A=B=\{0,1\}$ and choose $f$
uniformly among the four tables. Query $0$ first. If the answer is $0$,
query $1$; if it is $1$, query $0$ again. The response-pair law is
\[
 (00,01,10,11)\longmapsto(1/4,1/4,0,1/2).
\]
The storing service has the same law. A fresh fair kernel on every call
instead gives $(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)$. In particular the client is not restricted
to predetermined query lists.

\subsection{Three protocols with identical one call marginals}\label{sec:three-tests}
Take $A=1$, $B=\{0,1\}$, two call credits and the same matched call
observation. Each protocol below has a fair one-call marginal.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}
\toprule
Protocol&$00$&$01$&$10$&$11$&Agreement\\
\midrule
One reusable sampled constant table&$1/2$&$0$&$0$&$1/2$&$1$\\
One selected kernel with fresh draws&$5/16$&$3/16$&$3/16$&$5/16$&$5/8$\\
Fresh fair kernel or reselected choice&$1/4$&$1/4$&$1/4$&$1/4$&$1/2$\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first row samples $Z\sim\mathsf{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ once and reuses the
table $f(*)=Z$. In the second, choose one sampler code with parameter

$\Pr(11)=\tfrac12(\tfrac14)^2+\tfrac12(\tfrac34)^2=5/16$.
The third uses a fixed fair sampler, or independently repeats the

More generally, choosing $k\sim\mu$ once gives the adaptive law
\[
 \sum_k\mu(k)\prod_iq_k(a_i(b_{<i}))(b_i),
\]
whereas flattening to the one-call kernel
$\bar q(a)=\sum_k\mu(k)q_k(a)$ gives
\[
 \prod_i\sum_k\mu(k)q_k(a_i(b_{<i}))(b_i).
\]
The formulas agree for one call and need not agree for two. The numerical
rows are a finite counterexample to treating that flattening as an
equivalence of open repeated-call services. Fresh draws from a fixed
selected kernel share the selection, even though they do not share each
call's sample.
\section{Conservative expressivity and open resource clients}\label{sec:expressivity}

\begin{proposition}[The old adaptive core is preserved]\label{prop:conservative}
Every closed CF-0.1 command translates to this language with empty public
and procedure contexts, preserving its raw histories, token transitions,
causal trees, costs and weights. Conversely, a closed finite first-order
term with implementations plugged in elaborates into CF-0.1 over its
explicitly totalized interfaces, without changing those observations.
Ranked code and service extensions elaborate in the same way after their
declared finite operations and store-bearing interfaces are added.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Translate old pure maps by their finite tables, retain every primitive and
raw $\Bind$ node, and retain each addressed split and fork. Induction gives
the identity choices at primitives and the same dependent products at bind.
For the converse, finite public valuations evaluate table/case selection
pointwise. Theorem~\ref{thm:substitution} expands open calls with their
actual interfaces; rank induction expands first-class code invocation.
Explicit $\Gamma$ framing gives the totalized CF interface. A service is
an admitted finite primitive on the expanded store-bearing configuration.
Every step preserves the actual run-choice tuple, rather than only a
terminal output simulation. No associativity normalization is needed, so
the raw phase-Bind policy is not silently changed.
\end{proof}

The genuine dependent benchmark is the unchanged O3 allocation/read term.
Its input owns $\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}$. An allocation selects a unary
$L_\alpha$ interface with probability $1/3$, retyping $\alpha$ to its
$L$-read kind and explicitly disposing of $\beta,\gamma$; or a binary
$[H_{1,\beta},H_{2,\gamma}]$ interface with probability $2/3$, retyping
$\beta,\gamma$ to their $H$-read kinds and disposing of $\alpha$. The
actual continuation family is
\[
 u_{L_\alpha}=\mathsf{read}_L,
 \quad u_{H_{1,\beta}}=\mathsf{read}_{H_1},
 \quad u_{H_{2,\gamma}}=\mathsf{read}_{H_2}.
\]
The reads consume exactly their owned token and return a Boolean with
empty capability context. Their success probabilities are $3/4$, $1/2$
and $2/3$, respectively. Allocation costs $(1,0)$; the $L$ read costs
$(1,0)$ and each $H$ read costs $(0,1)$. Bind therefore has six raw
histories, not a fixed resource fibre with a changed scalar parameter:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccc@{}}
\toprule
History&$L0$&$L1$&$H00$&$H01$&$H10$&$H11$\\
\midrule
Probability&$1/12$&$1/4$&$1/9$&$2/9$&$1/9$&$2/9$\\
Cost&$(2,0)$&$(2,0)$&$(1,2)$&$(1,2)$&$(1,2)$&$(1,2)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The external observation OR of the final Boolean list has probability
$29/36$. It remains an observation, not a new whole-list join command.
The changed number, types and ownership of child occurrences are exactly
the bind rule's actual post-interface. No construction duplicates $\alpha$
to synthesize $\beta$ and $\gamma$.

\subsection{Evidence used by an open resource client}
Take public $y:\mathsf{Bool}$ and $\varphi(y)\equiv(y=1)$. Declare an open
parameter $\mathsf{check}$ whose input owns $\alpha:\mathsf{Probe}$ and
whose output interface is the tagged sum
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \mathsf{Pass}(y',e),\ e:\Ev_\varphi(y')
       &\text{with }\alpha:\mathsf{Approved},\\
 \mathsf{Fail}(y')&\text{with }\alpha:\mathsf{Rejected}.
 \end{array}
\]
The implementation's primitive transition, not the evidence term, retypes
the same identity $\alpha$. Declare $\mathsf{commit}(y',e)$ to require
$\alpha:\mathsf{Approved}$ and consume it, and $\mathsf{release}(y')$ to
require $\alpha:\mathsf{Rejected}$ and consume it. Both return ordinary
acknowledgements with empty ownership. The open client is
\[
 \Bind(\mathsf{check},z.
 \mathsf{case}\ z\ \mathsf{of}\
 \{\mathsf{Pass}(y',e)\mapsto\mathsf{commit}(y',e),\
   \mathsf{Fail}(y')\mapsto\mathsf{release}(y')\}).
\]
It has a concrete implementation: a declared check table may choose
$\mathsf{Pass}(1,*)$ and $\mathsf{Fail}(0)$ with probability $1/2$ each,
emitting their unary public labels, costing one and applying the displayed
token transitions. Deterministic commit/release tables complete it.
Thus the open interface is inhabited, not just an assumed code type.

Every implementation with the same declared interface and effect bound
plugs into this client by Theorem~\ref{thm:substitution}. A tokenless Boolean
predictor is not a replacement for $\mathsf{check}$: it does not supply the
returned ownership and kind. Even a valid $\Ev_\varphi(1)$ with empty
$\Delta$ cannot type $\mathsf{commit}$, whose ownership premise is absent.
A batch using $\alpha$ twice fails disjointness; another check after
consumption/retyping fails its input-kind premise unless a separate
restoring transition has been declared. This is the explicit gap between
prediction, evidence and executable capability.

\subsection{The open two call client}
Declare a method parameter $m$ with a finite index $j\in\{1,2\}$ and
query $a:A$, input token $r:\mathsf{Ready}_j$, and unary output
$(b:B,r:\mathsf{Ready}_{j-1})$. An open client $\mathsf{twice}_m$ first
invokes $m$ at $j=2,a=a_1$, then invokes it at $j=1,a=a_2(b_1)$ and
returns $(b_1,b_2)$ with $r:\mathsf{Ready}_0$:
\[
 b_1\leftarrow m[2,a_1];\qquad
 b_2\leftarrow m[1,a_2(b_1)];\qquad
 \Ret(b_1,b_2).
\]
The displayed notation abbreviates unary $\Bind$ and explicit ordinary-data
framing of $b_1$ into the second call's post-interface. The same token is
threaded sequentially, not kept as a duplicable lexical variable. No third
call types because there is no $j=0$ call instance, and the two calls cannot
be batched on one $r$ because their proposed split is not disjoint.

The bounded call adapters of Section~\ref{sec:call} are valid implementations
for $m$. Plugging the two sampled-function implementations gives the equal
adaptive call-view law of Theorem~\ref{thm:service}; plugging fresh sampling
gives its distinguishing law. This expresses the resource-sensitive open
higher-order test in the same command language as the multiple-hole
allocation benchmark. The method code can be used repeatedly only because
it captures no owned token; each invocation still proves its separate
current resource premise.

\section{Rewrite counterexamples and unavailable principles}\label{sec:rewrites}
\begin{description}
\item[Copying a result is not rerunning its computation.]
With empty ownership, executing a fair flip once and returning $(b,b)$
has equal output bits with probability one. Executing a flip twice and
returning the two results has equality probability $1/2$. A pure
$\beta$ rule never substitutes an effectful command for a pure value.
The causal trees and costs can distinguish these programs even sooner.
\item[One call laws do not determine repeated calls.]
The table in Section~\ref{sec:three-tests} refutes both replacing a sampled
reusable function by a fresh fair kernel and replacing a once-selected
random kernel by its flattened one-call marginal. The comparisons hold
under matched permissions and costs, so they are correlation differences,
not artifacts of unequal resource availability.
\item[Effect exchange is not full causal equality.]
Let deterministic, zero-cost unary primitives $e$ and $f$ return the same
public unit and preserve the same ownership. The trees
$e(f(\eta *))$ and $f(e(\eta *))$ are different. Thus exchanging sequential
effects is not a $\simeq_L$ equation even if the terminal data laws agree.
A separately specified quotient could forget these events, but that is
not the full O3 observation. Exchanging independent batch addresses also
needs a stated endpoint/address comparison, not an implicit contraction.
\item[Raw isomorphism is not chronology equality.]
The phase-association example in Section~\ref{sec:equality} rejects any
claim that the raw causal associator preserves the chronological word.
\item[Joint laws do not certify every raw path.]
The added zero-probability expensive witness in Section~\ref{sec:equality}
refutes recovery of all-path budget validity from $\simeq_L$ alone.
\item[Evidence does not grant permissions.]
The open check/commit example has an explicit missing capability premise
for a predictor or an evidence-only substitute. There is no equality
rule that changes that premise. Numerical forecasts or a high probability
of success do not create a token either.
\item[Inspecting an observation is not conditioning.]
Pure case on a previously returned value selects a continuation and does
not renormalize a distribution. For a fair bit, keeping only the event
$b=1$ and renormalizing would change its law to $\delta_1$; it is not
inspection of the original command. The frozen signature contains no
posterior-conditioning operation, including on null events. Such an
extension would need its own type, hypotheses and soundness theorem.
\item[Implementation views must be named.]
The service theorem hides only the stipulated private unary management
events and private store, at matched costs. It is not an equation between
unrestricted function-table and handle values, and it does not erase
unequal setup costs or arbitrarily branching causal nodes by default.
\end{description}
\section{Existing theory and the precise increment}\label{sec:prior}
The general separation of linear and unrestricted assumptions has an
established categorical and typed-calculus baseline in Benton~\cite{Benton}.
Parameterized computation already treats type-changing state and separated
effects with typed calculi, as in Atkey~\cite{Atkey}. Dependent effect
elimination is not a free consequence of ordinary monadic bind: V\'{a}k\'{a}r
explicitly distinguishes the calculus without a general dependent Kleisli
extension from a stronger calculus with that additional principle
\cite{Vakar}. Dependent operations and fibred computational effects are
also established subjects~\cite{Ahman}. We do not claim novelty for these
general ideas, or derive their unrestricted type-theoretic principles from
our finite family construction.

QBS already provides a cartesian closed setting for higher-order
probability~\cite{QBS2017}. The 2026 quasi-Borel-family construction provides
a substantially stronger dependent-probability baseline than finite tagged
families alone~\cite{QBF2026}; O3 records its scope. The bounded code-menu
interpretation here does not replace that theory or assert that every
kernel has a chosen executable random-function representation. Our
finite discriminator directly shows why a one-call marginal does not
specify a reusable multi-call implementation.

The project-specific increment is the explicit conservative syntax,
resource/dependence substitution and checked observation comparisons in
the contracted O1/O3 mixed construction~\cite{O1,O3}. The result gives no
publication-novelty conclusion. Its service theorem is deliberately scoped
to the stated finite call interface; arbitrary callback contexts,
unbounded services and a contextual refinement theorem remain distinct
O5 obligations.

\section{Reproducibility and verification status}\label{sec:checks}
The companion source-and-checks archive contains this TeX source,
two standard-library exact-rational test programs, their recorded outputs, a
source-only checker, a reproducible archive builder, a README and checksums.
The finite tests enumerate the two-call laws and adaptive Boolean-query
law, test the token-counter discipline and check private-unary-tree erasure
on finite examples. They are regressions for the worked calculations and
scope restrictions. The additional finite AST fixtures test public table
substitution, dependent input-kind reindexing, open plugging and selected
rejected resource uses, including zero-weight branches. They cover neither
all pure lambda/evidence constructors nor all signature instances. These
regressions are not substitutes for the structural and rank-induction
proofs or exhaustive tests of arbitrary real-weight signatures.

The source checker verifies balanced groups and environments, resolved
internal references and bibliography keys, required principal statements
and absence of drafting markers. The archive checker verifies its explicit
file inventory and checksums. No TeX engine is invoked. Consequently no
compiled PDF, page count, visual-layout approval, mechanized proof or
physical execution result is claimed.
