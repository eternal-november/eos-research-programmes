Existing work and the scope of this package

Contextads
Capucci and Myers already cover dependent contexts, partial maps, writer/state/maybe effects and noninvertible action counits/coassociators. Their Section 7 replaces ordinary reindexing by coherent synthetic transport on a pseudocategory, but still requires invertible transport and unit/context-combination comparisons. Thus dependence, partiality or chosen transport alone is not a new generalization. Example 6.7 uses finite probability spaces and passage from a random variable to its law; Section 8 discusses explicit sample-space representations and prospective mixed contextad/contentad distributive laws. These are direct reasons not to claim the finite probability-family construction as a new research contribution. The construction and local reflection are proved explicitly in this package rather than inferred from that discussion.
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21889
Locations: Definitions 3.15, 3.26 and 3.34; Theorem 3.35; Example 6.7; Definition 7.9; Section 8.

The May 2025 Contextads abridged slides state a parametric-right-adjoint transposition theorem without supplying a proof. The v1 paper's Appendix A.4 is also explicitly unproved in its following remark. This package relies on neither general transposition claim.
Source: https://matteocapucci.eu/assets/2025/05/pssl-110.pdf
Locations: PDF pages 32 and 39–41, and paper pages 83–84.

Compensating transactions
Garcia-Molina and Salem's Sagas distinguishes compensation from restoring the original global database state, especially when other transactions observe or interleave with effects. Reverse-order compensators and recovery logs are established mechanisms, with failures and alternative recovery requiring attention. They do not establish physical rollback of unknown environmental dynamics.
Source: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs530/2005sp/papers/sagas.pdf
Locations: printed pages 250, 253–255.

Relation-relative recovery
Korth, Levy and Silberschatz formulate semantic compensation relative to a relation between histories/states, retain indirect effects in their reservation example, and discuss semantic information in recovery logs. Relation-relative compensation, residual effects and semantic logs are therefore prior work, not proposed novelty here. Their formal guarantees depend on specified semantics and additional commutation/algebraic conditions; they are not an automatic account of discovering or correcting action meanings.
Source: https://www.vldb.org/conf/1990/P095.PDF
Locations: printed pages 96, 102–103 and 105.

Partial inverses
Cockett and Lack's restriction categories provide structured domains for partial maps and a precise restricted-inverse notion. Exact partial inversion is distinct from selecting a compensator that meets a weaker residual or observational criterion. Adding partial domains alone does not solve recovery under unknown semantics.
Source: https://pages.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/~robin/FMCS/FMCS_06/RestrictionsI.pdf
Locations: Sections 2.1.1 and 2.3.1; Theorem 3.4.

Limits of this comparison
This is a targeted primary-source baseline, not an exhaustive novelty search. It does not exclude related results in probabilistic programming, control, categorical semantics, effect systems, abstract interpretation or compensation calculi. One application-specific proposal concerns which observation-based interfaces and recovery claims survive composition and justified simplification or semantic revision. That theorem is not completed here and does not define the wider programme of generalizing contextads.
