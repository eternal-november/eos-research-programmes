# Realizations, resource boundaries, and changing experiments

This continuation develops several specific regimes rather than a completed universal phenomenology theory.

- `realizations/monodromy`, `double-cover`, `exchangeable-width`, `positive-dynamics`, and `periodic-phase` examine realizations and their obstructions. `observation/finite-monodromy` separates finite evidence from all-length conclusions.
- `observation/world-factors` studies factors visible through observations.
- `formal/resource-observation` and `formal/refund-observation` contain narrow Lean 4.35.0 developments. Their READMEs state the exact assumptions, theorem boundaries, and replay commands; they use Lean's bundled libraries, not mathlib.
- `experiments/proof-discovery` records a finite list-rewriting experiment. `formal/certificate-replay` contains its certificate-to-Lean translation. Repeated certificate occurrences are not distinct discoveries. Later screening experiments are outside that historical Lean replay.
- `generation` contains generated-characterization, operational-invention and adaptive-experiment notes with small Lean fragments and finite checks. These fragments do not certify the whole programme.
- `changing-experiments/evolving_note_assembly_20261105/Changing_experiments_and_composing_characterizations.tex` is the integrated changing-experiments manuscript. Its neighbouring developments supply the detailed arguments and checks. The resource classification has independent hypotheses, not a general promise of all kinds of exactness.
- `residuals` contains the search for effects unexplained by the established ingredients, including controlled training-response results and the exploratory purification bridge that led toward LPDO research.

Python checks provide finite or floating-point evidence as documented by each script. They do not establish the general analytic or categorical proofs. Historical logs are not a fresh Lean replay. Run scripts in a disposable copy: several write diagnostic files. The full proof-search reproduction is materially more expensive than validating saved certificates.

The significance assessments and negative conclusions remain attached to their specific models. Neither the breadth of files nor a successful check establishes novelty or practical utility.
