# Preserving interactions in observable completions

4 November 2026

An observable description can be converted into a compact state space, but this does not automatically preserve the operations performed on the original states. The exact obstruction is failure of the observable algebra to remain closed under pullback by those operations. Repairing it can require a small boundary split, arbitrarily many predictive distinctions, or the full algebra of bounded functions.

The constructions below are applications of classical Gelfand duality, equivariant compactification, symbolic dynamics, and profinite completion. They are not novelty claims. Their purpose is to make the preservation requirement and its cost explicit.

## 1 The setting and the exact preservation criterion

Let X be a nonempty set and A a unital C*-subalgebra of ℓ∞(X), with the uniform norm and pointwise operations. Write K_A = Spec(A). Each original state defines an evaluation character

    j_A(x)(a) = a(x).

The image j_A(X) is dense in K_A. Every a ∈ A has a unique continuous extension â to K_A, and A is isometrically isomorphic to C(K_A).

There are two different questions about an operation F:X→X.

- **Descent to observational equivalence:** define x ∼_A y when a(x)=a(y) for every a∈A. A set map on X/∼_A exists exactly when x∼_A y implies F(x)∼_A F(y).
- **Continuous extension to the completed space:** a continuous map F̄:K_A→K_A satisfying F̄j_A=j_AF exists exactly when

    F*A ⊆ A, where F*a = a∘F.

The second condition is stronger. When it holds, the extension is unique and

    F̄(χ) = χ∘F*.

**Proof.** Pullback by F is a unital *-homomorphism on ℓ∞(X). If it preserves A, Gelfand duality supplies the displayed continuous map. Conversely, if F̄ exists, â∘F̄ is continuous on K_A, so its restriction a∘F belongs to A. Uniqueness follows from density.

No original topology on X is assumed here. The evaluation map is injective exactly when A separates points. If X already has a specified topology, a genuine topological compactification additionally requires j_A to be a topological embedding. An injective dense map alone need not preserve that topology.

## 2 The least enlargement and its universal property

For one operation, set

    A_F = C*({a∘F^k : a∈A, k≥0}).

Here C* means the uniformly closed unital self-adjoint algebra generated by the indicated functions. Since pullback preserves products, conjugation, and uniform limits, A_F is F*-invariant. It is the least such algebra containing A.

For a family of operations, replace F^k by all finite compositions, including the identity. This yields the least algebra invariant under the semigroup they generate.

**Universal property.** Suppose B is any unital C*-subalgebra of ℓ∞(X) containing A and invariant under all the chosen operations. Then A_F⊆B, so restriction of characters gives a unique continuous surjection

    π:K_B→K_{A_F},    πj_B=j_{A_F}.

It intertwines the induced operations. Equivalently, every compact realization with dense original states, continuous chosen operations, and continuous extensions of the initial observables maps onto this one. Thus this is the coarsest compatible compact realization, or a terminal object when arrows are factor maps from finer realizations to coarser ones.

This does not discover F from static observations: F is part of the supplied data. Nor does it certify preservation of every statement about the original system. It preserves the chosen operations and the continuous observables in the resulting algebra.

**Concrete realization.** For one operation, K_{A_F} can be identified with the closure of

    x ↦ (j_A(x), j_A(Fx), j_A(F²x), …)

in K_A^ℕ₀. The induced operation is the left shift. This makes the added points limiting observation histories, whether or not an original state realizes those histories.

**Size bound.** If A is separable and the chosen operation family is countable, the generated algebra remains separable. Its spectrum is therefore compact metrizable. A single operation does not automatically force a Stone–Čech-sized space.

The invariant-algebra approach and its topological-action qualifications are established equivariant compactification theory; see §3 of [Megrelishvili, Compactifications of Semigroups and Semigroup Actions](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0611786v2).

## 3 A minimal boundary repair

Take X=ℕ={1,2,…}, e(n)=1/n, and A=C*(1,e). Functional calculus identifies A with c, the algebra of convergent complex sequences. Its spectrum is the one-point compactification ℕ∪{∞}.

Define

    F(n)=1 if n is even, and F(n)=2 if n is odd.

The operation does not extend to this spectrum. Both the even and odd integers converge to ∞, but their images converge to different points, 1 and 2. Algebraically,

    e∘F = 1/2 + (1/2)1_even ∉ c.

The least repair is

    A_F = C*(c,1_even)
        = c₀ + span{1,1_even}.

It consists exactly of bounded sequences having one limit along the even integers and another along the odd integers. Invariance follows because every a∘F has just two values and because 1_even∘F=1−1_even.

The spectrum is

    K_{A_F}=ℕ∪{∞even,∞odd},

with even integers converging to ∞even and odd integers to ∞odd. The extended operation sends ∞even to 1 and ∞odd to 2. The factor map onto K_A collapses these two boundary points to ∞.

**What changed:** e already distinguishes every original integer. No additional distinction between original points was needed. The repair changes which limiting behaviors are identified, hence the compatible uniform structure and boundary. Both induced topologies on the original ℕ are still discrete.

## 4 Preserving every operation forces all bounded distinctions

Keep e(n)=1/n, but require compatibility with every set map F:ℕ→ℕ. For an arbitrary subset S⊆ℕ, the allowed family contains

    F_S(n)=1 on S, and F_S(n)=2 off S.

Therefore every compatible algebra B must contain

    1_S = 2(e∘F_S)−1.

Every bounded complex function is uniformly approximable by finite-valued functions. Since B contains every characteristic function and is uniformly closed,

    B=ℓ∞(ℕ),    K_B=βℕ.

More generally, for any set X, a unital algebra containing one nonconstant function and invariant under every self-map must equal ℓ∞(X): choose two points separated by that function and use the same two-valued-map argument.

This is an exact cost of **unrestricted** conversion compatibility. It does not establish that physically available, computable, or otherwise restricted operations have that cost. Indeed, all permutations of ℕ preserve c, so retaining every reversible set operation in this example does not force ℓ∞.

βℕ is nonmetrizable, whereas the countably generated examples in this note are metrizable. The spectrum-of-bounded-functions identification is the classical Stone–Čech construction, also situated within the function-algebra compactifications surveyed by [Uspenskij, Compactifications of topological groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204144).

## 5 Predictive refinement with an exact growing cost

Now take X=ℕ₀, with a different initial observable and operation:

    e(n)=n mod 2,    F(n)=⌊n/2⌋.

The time-k observable is the kth binary digit, read from the least significant end:

    e_k(n)=e(F^k(n))=⌊n/2^k⌋ mod 2.

For h≥1 define

    A_h=C*(1,e₀,…,e_{h−1}).

Two numbers agree on these h tests exactly when they are congruent modulo 2^h. All 2^h bit words occur. Products of the form

    ∏_{k<h} e_k^{b_k}(1−e_k)^{1−b_k},    b_k∈{0,1},

are the indicators of the individual residue classes. Consequently

    A_h ≅ C(ℤ/2^hℤ),    |Spec(A_h)|=2^h.

Any representation preserving these h outputs must distinguish at least 2^h profiles. That is h bits of worst-case state information, not an exponential number of storage bits.

**A finite horizon is not a stable quotient.** Pullback sends e_k to e_{k+1}, so F*A_h⊆A_{h+1} but F*A_h⊄A_h. More concretely, 0 and 2^h have the same residue modulo 2^h, but their images 0 and 2^{h−1} do not. Knowing h bits determines h−1 subsequent bits; it does not support an indefinitely repeatable closed update on the same h-bit quotient.

**No finite exact all-time model exists.** Suppose q:ℕ₀→Q, T:Q→Q, and r:Q→{0,1} satisfy qF=Tq and rq=e. If q(n)=q(m), then e_k(n)=e_k(m) for every k, hence n=m. Thus q must be injective, so Q cannot be finite.

## 6 The completed predictive space and its new behaviors

The least invariant algebra in the preceding example is

    A_F = closure(⋃_{h≥1} A_h).

It is the uniform closure of functions periodic modulo powers of 2. Its spectrum is

    K_{A_F} ≅ lim← ℤ/2^hℤ ≅ ℤ₂ ≅ {0,1}^ℕ₀.

The first identification follows either from the compatible finite residue quotients or from the orbit-history construction. The last identification is a homeomorphism via binary digits; it does not identify 2-adic addition with coordinatewise addition modulo 2. The standard inverse-limit description of the p-adic integers appears in Example 37.3 of [MIT Lie Groups and Lie Algebras lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec_full.pdf).

The natural numbers correspond exactly to eventually-zero binary sequences. They are dense because every finite initial bit word is realized by a natural number. Under the digit homeomorphism, the extended F is the one-sided full shift. In 2-adic notation it is

    D(x)=(x−ε(x))/2,    ε(x)=x mod 2 ∈ {0,1}.

The numerator belongs to 2ℤ₂ and has a unique quotient by 2 in ℤ₂. The two residue classes are clopen, so D is continuous. For general full-shift topology and cylinder sets, see [Petersen, Symbolic Dynamics notes](https://petersen.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/17054/2018/04/M261SymbolicDynamicsS982018_01_15.pdf).

**The added points have genuine additional dynamics.** Every original natural number reaches 0 after finitely many applications of F. The completion also contains the fixed point

    111… = −1 in ℤ₂,

as well as nonconstant periodic and nonperiodic binary histories. For example, 2^h−1 tends to −1 2-adically, while the number of shifts needed for 2^h−1 to reach 0 grows with h.

Thus extending the operation and every finite-time observation does not preserve the universal assertion “every state eventually reaches 0.” The completion admits all histories consistent with arbitrarily long finite observations, not only histories realized by original states. Calling these added states physically realizable would require a separate argument.

**Topology qualification.** ℕ₀→ℤ₂ is not an embedding of discrete ℕ₀. It is the completion for the observation-induced 2-adic topology. If preserving the original discrete topology is required, start instead with C*(c₀(ℕ₀),1,e) and take its invariant closure:

    B=C*(c₀(ℕ₀),A_F).

Since F is finite-to-one, F*c₀⊆c₀, so B is invariant. A concrete spectrum is the closure of

    n ↦ (1/(n+1), binary digits of n)

in [0,1]×{0,1}^ℕ₀. It consists of the discrete original points plus the remainder {0}×{0,1}^ℕ₀. Every remainder point is approached by arbitrarily large naturals with the required finite digits. The operation acts on that remainder by the same shift D. This gives a genuine compactification of discrete ℕ₀ with a Cantor remainder, rather than ℤ₂ as the whole space.

## 7 Continuous control parameters impose a further condition

Suppose a topological semigroup S acts by maps F_s and an invariant observable algebra B has been constructed. Individual operations extend to K_B. Their extensions form a **jointly continuous** action S×K_B→K_B exactly when, for every b∈B, the map

    s ↦ b∘F_s

is continuous in the uniform norm. Sufficiency follows by splitting variation into a uniform change in s and a continuous change in the state. Necessity uses joint continuity and compactness of K_B to obtain uniform control over all states near a fixed parameter.

Adding observables cannot repair failure of this condition for an observable already retained: its uniform norm on the original states is unchanged.

For example, let ℝ act on itself by translations and retain a(x)=exp(ix²). For every nonzero t,

    ||a(·+t)−a||∞ = 2,

because the phase difference 2tx+t² ranges over ℝ. No compact jointly continuous translation realization can extend this observable continuously, despite continuity of the original action and observable. Treating the same translations as a discrete operation family removes the parameter-continuity requirement. This is the additional uniform-continuity issue addressed by Definition 3.9 and Proposition 3.10 in [Megrelishvili](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0611786v2).

## 8 The stochastic extension requires a different closure

For a Markov expectation operator P, the corresponding preservation condition is P(A)⊆A. A positive unital operator on C(K_A) determines a weakly continuous family of Radon probability measures on K_A, hence a Feller transition kernel. The established duality is described by [Furber and Jacobs, Probabilistic Gelfand Duality](https://lmcs.episciences.org/1565).

Unlike deterministic pullback, P is generally not multiplicative. Consequently C*({P^k a}) need not be P-invariant. The least invariant C*-algebra is obtained by alternating the two closure steps:

    B₀=A,
    B_{m+1}=C*(B_m∪P(B_m)),
    B∞=closure(⋃_{m≥0}B_m).

The contraction property of P makes B∞ invariant. This is the discrete-time algebraic construction; continuous-time semigroups require an additional strong-continuity analysis. Pointwise transition preservation alone does not settle path regularity, conditioning, or every possible adaptive interaction.

## 9 What this establishes for the comparison

Three distinct effects should remain separate.

1. A description may already separate all original points and still fail to preserve an operation at its boundary. The reciprocal-observable example needs just two limiting points instead of one.
2. Predictive compatibility may force genuinely finer distinctions among original states. The binary example needs exactly 2^h profiles for h successive tests and no finite quotient for all times.
3. Completion can introduce ideal states with new infinite-time behavior. Neither finite observational adequacy nor continuous dynamics proves that these states belong to the original system.

Requiring every conceivable operation forces all bounded distinctions, while continuously adjustable operations may impose an obstruction that enlargement cannot fix. A useful representation therefore needs an independently justified choice of observables, operations, topology, and the kind of behavioral claims it is meant to preserve.

# Stochastic observation completion: exact extension, cost, and experimental meaning

## 1. Exact theorem (discrete time)

Let P be a countable discrete set, let p_a(n,·) be Markov probability kernels, and put T_a f(n)=Σ_m p_a(n,m)f(m). Let A⊂ℓ∞(P) be a unital C*-subalgebra, K=Spec(A), and q(n)=evaluation at n. Then q(P) is dense in K and pullback q*:C(K)→A is an isometric *-isomorphism. Injectivity of q is not assumed.

For each action a the following are equivalent:

1. T_a A⊂A.
2. There is a Feller kernel κ_a:K→Prob(K), continuous for the weak-* topology on Radon probabilities, such that κ_a(q(n))=q_*p_a(n,·) for every n.

The extension is unique. Proof: conjugate T_a|A by q* to a positive unital operator on C(K); for every x, evaluation at x composed with this operator is a state, hence a unique Radon probability by Riesz representation. Its integrals of all continuous functions vary continuously with x. Conversely, a Feller kernel pulls continuous functions back to continuous functions. Density gives uniqueness.

This extends stochastic dynamics as probability-valued transitions, not as deterministic maps. It is deterministic exactly when T_a|A is multiplicative. A character composed with a merely positive unital map is a state, not necessarily a character. Nondeterminism inside an observational equivalence class can become invisible without the physical process becoming deterministic.

For multiple actions, require invariance for each action. Algebraic relations among the T_a, including semigroup relations, then persist. In continuous time, individual Feller extensions do not by themselves establish strong continuity or a right-continuous strong Markov realization: additional hypotheses are needed. For arbitrary measurable P rather than countable discrete P, also check measurability of q and Radon representability; separability of A avoids a common Borel-measurability problem.

The construction is standard functional analysis. A directly relevant textbook treatment uses invariant C*-subalgebras to construct topological models of families of Markov operators: Eisner–Farkas–Haase–Nagel, *Operator Theoretic Aspects of Ergodic Theory*, §13.4, p.248. Its measure-preserving L∞ setting differs from the pointwise ℓ∞ formulation proved above. [Author PDF](https://www.math.uni-leipzig.de/~eisner/book-EFHN.pdf)

## 2. Minimal refinement, and what “minimal” means

Starting from A₀=C*(1,E), define

A_{j+1}=C*(A_j ∪ ⋃_a T_a A_j),   A_∞=closure(⋃_{j≥0} A_j).

Contractivity of T_a shows that A_∞ is invariant. It is the smallest closed invariant unital C*-algebra containing the original tests. Its spectrum maps continuously onto Spec(A₀) by restriction. Thus it changes which formerly convergent observational profiles must be distinguished; it need not identify any previously distinguished original states.

Unlike deterministic pullback, a stochastic T is not multiplicative. Therefore C*{T_w e : e∈E, w an action word} need not be invariant: one must also apply T to products generated at previous stages. Countably many original tests and actions produce separable A_∞ and a metrizable spectrum. That is not a bound on state-space cardinality, experiment complexity, numerical conditioning, or finite-dimensional representability.

Minimality is relative to the requirement “an invariant algebra yielding a Feller kernel on a space of point profiles.” It is not automatically minimal for the experiments an observer can actually perform.

## 3. Three different observation protocols

**Expected terminal scores.** The smallest closed unital real linear space V containing the selected tests and invariant under T_a is generated by action-word continuations T_w e. Equal states on V have equal expected terminal scores for this specified class. They need not have equal distributions of scores or equal joint histories.

V has a compact convex state space S(V), the weak-* closed convex hull of evaluation functionals. T_a induces a continuous affine map s↦s∘T_a on S(V). This is evolution of expected information. It does not determine a unique law of successor point profiles. For V=span{1,x} on [0,1], both kernels δ_x and (1−x)δ₀+xδ₁ are Feller and act identically on V. Equivalently, on P={0,1,2}, e=(0,1,1/2), δ₂ and (δ₀+δ₁)/2 agree on V but differ on e².

The function-system/state-space correspondence is classical Kadison representation theory; see Lupini, §2, for the positive-map/continuous-affine-map duality. [Primary paper](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/71728/55013/218398)

**Ordinary single-run trajectories.** If the current raw observation can be read and nondestructively recorded, let A₀ contain its continuous functions. For fixed finite action sequences, expected products of observed values are represented by

f₀ T_a1(f₁ T_a2(…T_ak f_k…)),   f_i∈A₀.

Their closed linear span W captures continuous cylinder-function expectations, hence the finite-dimensional observation laws. This is the smallest closed T_a-invariant A₀-module containing 1; it need not be an algebra. For finite observation alphabets this gives ordinary trace equivalence directly. Claims about adaptive policies or destructive measurements require specifying their protocol separately.

**Branching/reset experiments.** A_∞ additionally admits products of continuation functions such as (Tg)². If Tg is a success probability rather than a directly observed number, that product means running independent continuations from the *same current hidden state*. After an earlier random transition, a repeated run from the original initial condition does not generally reproduce that hidden state. A copying or checkpoint/reset ability at the intermediate state is needed to implement the branching test. Algebraic multiplication alone supplies no such physical capability.

In contrast, if an exact raw numerical value e is observed on a single trial, computing e² afterward is ordinary postprocessing and requires no copying. An oracle that returns only an estimated mean of e is a genuinely weaker observation model.

## 4. Identical trajectories, different branching tests

Take seven states s,t,m,m_b,m_c,b,c. The first five have visible label a; b and c have distinct visible labels and are absorbing. Transitions are

s→m;  m→½b+½c;  t→½m_b+½m_c;  m_b→b;  m_c→c.

Starting from either s or t, the complete visible trajectory is a,a,b,b,… with probability 1/2 and a,a,c,c,… with probability 1/2. No number of independently restarted single-run traces distinguishes the two initial states.

But for g=1_{b},

T[(Tg)²](s)=1/4,   T[(Tg)²](t)=1/2.

The test first moves once, copies the resulting hidden state, and requires two independent continuations both to reach b. Restarting at the initial state instead gives (T²g)²=1/4 on both sides and does not implement this test.

This is the established difference between trace and branching/bisimulation semantics, not a novelty claim. Bian–Abate, Figure 1 and Theorem 4.3, give the corresponding trace-versus-bisimulation separation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.04547v3)

The foundational source is Larsen–Skou, [Bisimulation through Probabilistic Testing (1991)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0890540191900306). Deng–Feng explicitly interpret multiplication in this testing tradition as copying capacity. [Paper, §2](https://scai.sufe.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/e9/ac/80b9a26a4999a4464ef7a1b16703/bca49180-98d8-41db-bd7f-72519098feb4.pdf)

## 5. A sharp stochastic completion obstruction

Let P={1,2,…}, e(n)=1/n. Then A₀=C*(1,e)=c, the convergent sequences, and K₀=P∪{∞}, with e(∞)=0. Define

p(n,·)=δ₂                    if n is even,
p(n,·)=¼δ₁+¾δ₃             if n is odd.

Then Te≡1/2, so V=span{1,e} is invariant. Yet

T(e²)(n)=1/4 on evens, and 1/3 on odds.

Since e²∈A₀ but T(e²)∉c, no Feller extension exists on K₀. Every original state was already distinguished by e. Failure arises at the limit: the two subsequences n→∞ demand incompatible successor laws.

Exactly one asymptotic split repairs this example:

A_∞=C*(c,1_even)={bounded sequences with separate even and odd limits}.

Indeed 1_even=4−12T(e²), and Tf lies in span{1,1_even} for every bounded f. The spectrum is P with two boundary points ∞even,∞odd, whose successor laws are δ₂ and ¼δ₁+¾δ₃ respectively. This is a cost in boundary resolution, not a loss of old observations.

If the original one-point boundary must be retained, there is an exact approximation price. For any Feller kernel L on K₀:

- sup_n |L(e²)(n)−T(e²)(n)| ≥ 1/24.
- sup_n TV(L(n,·),p(n,·)) ≥ 1/2, where TV(μ,ν)=sup_B |μ(B)−ν(B)|.
- With d(i,j)=|1/i−1/j| and d(n,∞)=1/n, sup_n W₁(L(n,·),p(n,·)) ≥ 1/8.

All three bounds are attained by the constant kernel with successor law

μ*=½δ₂+⅛δ₁+⅜δ₃.

Proof of the first bound: L(e²)(n) has one limit, which must approximate both 1/4 and 1/3. For TV, use the continuous indicator of the isolated clopen singleton {2}, whose true expectations alternate 1 and 0. For W₁, the two true laws are distance 1/4 apart; continuity forces the approximating even and odd subsequences to have the same weak limit, so the triangle inequality forces error at least 1/8. The mixture midpoint attains these bounds.

These are elementary, explicit calculations, not a claim of a new general theorem. The metric matters: means of e incur zero error even though full transition laws incur substantial error.

## 6. A modest general quantitative lemma

For the same one-point compactification K=P∪{∞}, let κ_n be arbitrary probability laws on a compact metric output space Y. Let d be any metric inducing weak convergence on Prob(Y), for example W₁ for a bounded compatible metric on Y. Then

inf_{F:K→Prob(Y) continuous} sup_n d(κ_n,F(n))
= min_{ν∈Prob(Y)} limsup_n d(κ_n,ν).

The right side is the smallest radius of a ball containing the asymptotic cluster set of transition laws. Proof: if F(n)→ν, the triangle inequality gives the lower bound. Conversely, set F(n)=κ_n on a finite initial segment and F(n)=ν thereafter; its worst error approaches the right side. Compactness and the Lipschitz dependence of the limsup on ν give a minimizing center.

This quantifies the obstruction when a single boundary profile is forced to absorb several incompatible limiting transition laws. It is an elementary approximation lemma; no novelty has been established. It does not automatically extend to arbitrary compactifications with interacting boundary points, where continuous selection and positive-operator constraints can matter.

## 7. Prior art and a defensible research direction

The closest prior programme is not merely adjacent topology. Mislove–Ouaknine–Pavlovic–Worrell construct a universal labelled Markov process as a C*-algebra spectrum of trace-tree tests and use Riesz representation for its transitions. [Duality for Labelled Markov Processes, §§4–7](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/lmpduality04.pdf). Mislove–Pavlovic–Worrell develop the related predicate-transformer duality further. [Labelled Markov Processes as Generalised Stochastic Relations](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/james.worrell/generalised.pdf). Any claim of a new spectral theory of interactive stochastic behaviour must be compared directly with these papers.

Quantitative error propagation is also established territory: Bian–Abate prove the sharp finite-horizon trace bound 1−(1−ε)^k≤kε from ε-bisimulation (Theorems 4.1–4.2). This alone is not a new result.

A meaningful next question is protocol-sensitive approximation: given only terminal-mean access, single-run traces, or explicitly allowed intermediate-state resets, and a horizon, precision, confidence, and sample budget, what is the least additional distinguishability needed to obtain a reliable dynamic model? One must define the protocol before proposing its algebra, topology, or minimax cost. The original two-mean example and the trace/branching separation show why these constraints are substantive. Finding a sharp tradeoff under a specified resource model could be new; that remains unverified and requires a dedicated literature comparison.
