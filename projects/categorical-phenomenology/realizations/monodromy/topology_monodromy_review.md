# Independent audit: cyclic-mixture stochastic realization obstruction

Date: 2026-11-03. Scope: finite, time-homogeneous, globally labelled stochastic realizations with fixed terminal vector of ones. This is an independent mathematical audit of the proposed example, not a novelty certification.

## Verdict

The exact 3-state signed versus 4-state stochastic separation is valid under the stated continuous/nonnegative model convention. The ordinary state-emitting HMM formulation also works after the explicit adjustment below. The finite-moment uniform robustness argument is valid and does not require an assumed continuity modulus or candidate rank condition.

No fatal mathematical blocker found. Essential qualifications:

1. The process is a nonergodic mixture of three IID laws. Its hidden transition matrices are not strictly positive or irreducible.
2. Every categorical emission probability, and every finite-word probability, is strictly positive. This does not mean that symbol matrices have all entries strictly positive.
3. The supplied stochastic lift is continuous, or C-infinity after flat-cutoff interpolation. It is not an analytic construction. The observable word laws and signed 3-state realization are analytic.
4. The spare state is deliberately unoccupied while its role changes. The proof does not establish an all-positive hidden initial law or a fixed initial law for the 4-state lift.
5. The obstruction concerns a fixed labelled state set across the parameter circle. A local or unlabelled three-state realization exists everywhere.
6. The topology, mixture identifiability, and ordinary-versus-positive-realization distinction are classical. Searches below did not locate this precise example, but that is not proof of novelty.

## 1. Exact family and rank

Use row-distribution conventions. Let c=(1,1,1)/3, a=(1,-1,0)/sqrt(2), b=(1,1,-2)/sqrt(6), and choose 0<delta<1/3. For t in R set

p_i(t)=c+delta[cos(2*pi*t/3+2*pi*(i-1)/3)a+sin(2*pi*t/3+2*pi*(i-1)/3)b], i=1,2,3.

Let V(t) have rows p_i(t), let pi=c, and let D_y(t)=diag(V(t)[:,y]). Let P have rows e_2,e_3,e_1. Then V(t+1)=P V(t), D_y(t+1)=P D_y(t)P^{-1}, and pi P=pi.

Every row sums to one. Since each coordinate of a*cos(phi)+b*sin(phi) has absolute value at most sqrt(2/3), every emission is at least 1/3-delta*sqrt(2/3)>0. The safe bound delta<1/3 is more restrictive than necessary.

The law is

p_t(y_1...y_k)=pi D_{y_1}(t)...D_{y_k}(t)1=(1/3)sum_i product_j p_i(t)(y_j).

It is stationary and exchangeable. It is generated by choosing one latent atom uniformly once, keeping it forever, and emitting conditionally IID. It is 1-periodic and analytic in t because a full loop merely permutes the summands. Thus it is a real-analytic family on S^1.

The single-letter prefix/suffix Hankel block is H=V(t)^T V(t)/3. Writing Pi_perp=I-J/3 gives

H=J/9+(delta^2/2)Pi_perp.

Its eigenvalues are 1/3, delta^2/2, delta^2/2. It is positive definite and constant. Consequently V(t) is invertible, the linear Hankel rank is exactly three, and the displayed 3-state representation is reachable and observable. Explicitly the one-letter reachability and observability blocks are R_0=V^T/3 and O_0=V.

## 2. Global signed realization with fixed initialization and terminal vector

Set S(t)=V(t)V(0)^{-1}, and A_y(t)=S(t)^{-1}D_y(t)S(t).

Because V(t) and V(0) are doubly stochastic, S(t)1=1 and pi S(t)=pi. Hence

pi A_{y_1}(t)...A_{y_k}(t)1=p_t(y_1...y_k).

Also sum_y A_y=I. The matrices are real and analytic. The relations S(t+1)=P S(t) and D_y(t+1)=P D_y(t)P^{-1} imply A_y(t+1)=A_y(t), including all derivatives. The initialization pi and terminal vector 1 are fixed globally. This explicit construction is stronger and safer than relying only on an abstract claim that an oriented real bundle over a circle is trivial.

These matrices cannot all be entrywise nonnegative around the circle: their sum is I, so nonnegativity would force every one to be diagonal, contradicting the obstruction below.

## 3. Every exact three-state stochastic realization is a relabelling

Let another three-state realization have initial row alpha in Delta^2, symbol matrices N_y>=0, terminal 1, and T=sum_y N_y with T1=1. No stationarity of alpha or reachability assumption is imposed on this candidate.

Define 3-by-3 matrices R and O by

R[y,:]=alpha N_y, and O[:,z]=N_z1.

For target two-letter moments and three-letter blocks define

H[y,z]=p_t(yz), H_a[y,z]=p_t(yaz).

Matching the target moments gives H=RO and H_a=R N_a O. Since H is invertible, both R and O are invertible. The target satisfies sum_a H_a=H because its canonical transition is I. Therefore

R T O=R O,

and invertibility forces T=I. Since each N_y is entrywise nonnegative, all its off-diagonal entries vanish. Thus every N_y is diagonal.

There is also a direct simultaneous-similarity proof. Put S=O_0 O^{-1}. Equality H=R_0O_0=RO gives R=R_0S; equality of the three-letter blocks gives S N_a=D_a S. Hence N_a=S^{-1}D_a S for every a.

Let q_j be the three emission tuples given by the diagonal entries of N_y. If S_ij is nonzero, the equation D_y S=S N_y for all y forces p_i(t)(y)=q_j(y) for all y. The p_i are pairwise distinct, and S is invertible; consequently the q_j are exactly the p_i in one common permutation. No extra diagonal similarity freedom remains in the actual stochastic parameters: the N_y are now fixed, and alpha is determined by the one-letter law.

Indeed p_t(y)=sum_z H[y,z] is known. Writing Q for the emission matrix with rows q_j gives alpha Q=pi V. Since Q is a row permutation of invertible V, this determines alpha=pi. There are exactly six parameter tuples, one per permutation. All degrees of freedom, including initialization, have been pinned down.

This derivation uses only probabilities of words of length at most three. Length-three probabilities alone suffice because shorter probabilities are marginals for any stochastic model.

## 4. Monodromy obstruction

Suppose N_y(t),alpha(t) were continuous and periodic. Each diagonal tuple q_j(t) must equal one of the three p_i(t). Because the atoms remain uniformly separated, on the lifted interval t in [0,1] the matching index cannot change continuously. Thus q_j(t)=p_i(t) for one fixed i. But p_i(1)=p_{i+1}(0), which differs from p_i(0). This contradicts periodicity of q_j.

Equivalently, the exact six-point parameter fiber has monodromy given by composition with a 3-cycle. Its six elements form two orbits of length three; there is no fixed point and therefore no continuous section. The three-atom cover itself is connected, but the six-labelling cover is not connected. Either formulation yields the obstruction; do not incorrectly call the six-sheet cover connected.

## 5. Four-state stochastic lift, with endpoint check

Take row-stochastic Q(t) of shape 3-by-4 and L(t) of shape 4-by-3 satisfying QL=I_3. Initially

Q_0 rows=(e_1,e_2,e_3), and L_0 rows=(e_A,e_B,e_C,pi).

Interpret Q as placing the three atom types in four fixed slots. The identity QL=I implies that the supports of different rows of Q are disjoint, and L's row at every occupied slot is the corresponding deterministic atom label. An empty slot's L row can be any probability row.

Use nine consecutive phases:

1. Recolor empty L_4 from pi to A.
2. Move Q_A from slot 1 to slot 4 by a convex split.
3. Recolor empty L_1 from A to C.
4. Move Q_C from slot 3 to slot 1.
5. Recolor empty L_3 from C to B.
6. Move Q_B from slot 2 to slot 3.
7. Recolor empty L_2 from B to A.
8. Move Q_A from slot 4 to slot 2.
9. Recolor empty L_4 from A to pi.

During every move both relevant L rows have the same atom label. During every recoloring the relevant column of Q is identically zero. Thus stochasticity, nonnegativity, and QL=I hold throughout. Linear interpolation yields continuity; a smooth cutoff flat at every phase endpoint yields C-infinity regularity.

At the endpoint the placements are A in 2, B in 3, C in 1, spare 4. Hence

Q_1=P Q_0, L_1=L_0 P^{-1}.

For a general edge-emitting stochastic realization use

X_y=L D_y Q, rho=pi Q, terminal=1_4.

Then sum_y X_y=LQ is row stochastic. Repeated cancellation of QL yields exact equality for every word, and endpoint equivariance gives periodic X_y and rho. Moreover rho(LQ)=rho, so initialization is stationary.

### Ordinary state-emitting HMM adjustment

The formula X_y=L D_y Q need not factor as diag(e_y)T at an empty slot whose L row is mixed. To avoid a model-class mismatch define instead

T=LQ, E=LV, rho=pi Q, and Z_y=diag(E[:,y])T.

This is an ordinary state-emitting HMM; E is strictly positive because each row is a convex combination of strictly positive emissions. At every occupied slot L is deterministic, so Z_y and X_y agree there. Every empty slot has a zero column in Q and hence in T, and zero initial weight. Such slots are unreachable at that fixed parameter value. Therefore Z_y and X_y give identical all-word laws. The endpoint identities make T,E,rho periodic, and rho is stationary.

The lower bound excludes three or fewer states, while this gives four. Thus the minimal globally continuous stochastic state count is exactly four.

## 6. Uniform finite-moment robustness

The robustness claim is correct in the full compact three-state stochastic parameter space, not just the diagonal-mixture subspace.

Let K consist of all tuples x=(alpha,N_1,N_2,N_3) with alpha in Delta^2, N_y>=0, and sum_y N_y1=1. K is compact: it is closed and every coordinate lies in [0,1]. Let F:K->R^27 list the length-three probabilities. Let f(t) be the target vector and E_t=F^{-1}(f(t)). The preceding proof shows E_t consists of exactly six tuples.

The unordered fibers E_t vary continuously, with smooth local labelled branches. Distinct branches have a positive uniform minimum distance eta in any fixed Euclidean norm. Fix r<eta/3. The distance d(x,E_t) is continuous on S^1 x K. The set

B={(t,x):d(x,E_t)>=r}

is compact and contains no zero of ||F(x)-f(t)||. Therefore

epsilon=min_B ||F(x)-f(t)||>0

(if B is empty the desired proximity conclusion is immediate). Every epsilon-close candidate is within distance r of E_t.

In this r-neighborhood there is exactly one nearest exact tuple: one branch is closer than r, while all others are farther than eta-r>r. For a continuous candidate x(t), this nearest tuple is a continuous selection, as is seen in any local chart of the finite cover. It would be a section of E->S^1, contradicting the monodromy.

Consequently there is a strictly positive lower bound on the worst-parameter length-three moment error for every continuous globally labelled three-state stochastic family. This argument assumes neither a prescribed continuity modulus nor candidate reachability, observability, or rank. It supplies existence of a positive margin, not a useful explicit numerical margin. The margin may tend to zero as delta tends to zero.

## 8. Wording to avoid

- Do not say "positive transitions" when only emissions/word probabilities are strictly positive.
- Do not say "the minimal linear state bundle is nontrivial": it is explicitly trivial here. The obstruction lies in continuously labelling the stochastic atom structure.
- Do not say an arbitrary HMM has unique parameters up to permutation. That conclusion is proved only for this exact law and dimension three.
- Do not infer mixture identification from one-step observations. The one-step law is constant here; three-step data carries the needed information.
- Do not call the six-labelling cover connected: its monodromy has two three-cycles.
- Do not promise an analytic four-state stochastic lift, an explicit robust error constant, or a universal n+1 theorem without additional proof.

## 9. Audit of the arbitrary-p circle generalization

The stated generalization is valid for a continuous unordered family of p distinct categorical emissions on a finite alphabet, with uniform weights and a frozen latent atom. Strict positivity ensures positive observed words but is not needed for the rank or topological arguments. The field must be R for the signed parity statement.

Let the labelled lift on [0,1] be q_1(t),...,q_p(t), with q(1)=P q(0), where P represents monodromy sigma. Let r=p-1. For each fixed t and atom i choose, for each j!=i, an affine linear function ell_ij with ell_ij(q_j)=0 and ell_ij(q_i)!=0. For example ell_ij(x)=(q_i-q_j) dot (x-q_j). The polynomial product over j!=i, suitably rescaled, is the Kronecker-delta function on the p atoms and has degree at most p-1. Using sum_y x_y=1, homogenize it to degree exactly r. Thus evaluations of degree-r monomials, equivalently probabilities of words of length r under the individual IID atoms, span R^p.

The atom-evaluation matrix O_r has row rank p. The uniform-mixture block H_{r,r}=O_r^T O_r/p therefore has rank p, proving pointwise signed and stochastic minimum p even when the one-step emission vectors are linearly dependent.

### Finite-horizon uniqueness and robustness

Use all prefixes and suffixes of length r. At each parameter choose p rows and p columns giving an invertible p-by-p minor of H_{r,r}. No globally fixed minor is required. Its symbol-inserted blocks use words of length 2r+1=2p-1. The same RTO=RO argument forces T=I in every p-state stochastic candidate matching these moments. Simultaneous similarity gives the same unordered emission tuples. The degree-r interpolation identifies the mixture weights, so the initialization is uniform. The exact parameter fiber is precisely p! points.

Consequently all moments through length 2p-1 suffice for the full lower-bound and compactness robustness proof. In fact length 2p-1 alone suffices after marginalization. The separation of exact parameter branches is uniformly positive by compactness and pointwise distinctness. A sufficiently close continuous p-state family would select a continuous exact branch. Monodromy acts freely on the p! labellings unless sigma=id, so nonidentity sigma forbids this. Three moments is a special improvement for the original linearly independent three-atom example, not the general arbitrary-p horizon.

### Signed dimension and the normalization constraint

The observable-state bundle is canonically the rank-p permutation bundle associated with the atom cover: local frames are the p independent atom-evaluation rows of O_r. It splits as a mass line plus the centered permutation bundle. Its determinant transition is det(P)=sign(sigma). A p-dimensional global real realization supplies a frame of this observable bundle; therefore it cannot exist when sigma is odd.

When sigma is even, choose a continuous path S from I to P inside the group fixing both 1_p and the uniform row pi_p. On the centered subspace this is GL^+_{p-1}, which is connected. The conjugated operators S(t)^{-1}D_y(t)S(t), with fixed initial pi_p and terminal 1_p, glue across the endpoint. They are normalized because their sum is I. A path constant near each endpoint yields smooth gluing when the emission lift is smooth and has the required endpoint derivative matching.

When sigma is odd, a positive p+1-state lift provides a signed p+1-state upper bound, proving the exact signed minimum p+1. If fixed signed initialization is additionally desired, it can be imposed without increasing the dimension. For any normalized row rho(t) and fixed uniform row pi_m, put

G(t)=I_m+1_m(rho(t)-pi_m).

Then G1=1, pi_m G=rho, and G^{-1}=I_m-1_m(rho-pi_m). If the original operators are B_y, the operators G B_y G^{-1}, fixed initial pi_m, and terminal 1_m generate exactly the same laws. This gauge need not preserve nonnegativity, so it only resolves the signed normalization issue.

Thus the exact counts are:

- sigma=id: signed p and stochastic p.
- sigma!=id even: signed p and stochastic p+1.
- sigma odd: signed p+1 and stochastic p+1.

These are real signed dimensions. Complex vector bundles over S^1 have no analogous orientation obstruction; do not transfer the parity claim to complex coefficients.

### Arbitrary permutation buffer and regularity

One spare slot suffices to implement any permutation, including transpositions. To exchange atoms A in slot i and B in slot j, move A from i to spare, B from j to i, and A from spare to j, recoloring only empty decoder rows between moves. Every permutation is a product of transpositions. Start with Q_0=[I_p|0] and L_0 consisting of I_p followed by the uniform row. Restore the spare decoder row to uniform at the end. The target endpoint is Q_1=P Q_0 and L_1=L_0 P^{-1}. The same QL=I cancellation and ordinary-HMM modification from Section 5 apply. Flat cutoff interpolation gives C-infinity operators when the unordered emission curve is C-infinity. No analyticity claim follows.

## 10. Additional close prior art for the buffer topology

The row-stochastic Q matrices with pairwise disjoint row supports are exactly the simplicial deleted-product/discretized configuration space D_p(Delta^{m-1}). In particular, D_p(Delta^{p-1}) is the discrete set of p! vertex assignments, whereas D_p(Delta^p) is a connected graph. This is an inference identifying the present matrix parameter space with the paper's definition, not a claim made there about HMMs.

Abrams, Gay, and Hower, *Discretized configurations and partial partitions*, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society 141(3), 2013, 1093-1104. Theorem 1.1 proves a general wedge-of-spheres description. Its proof on page 3 explicitly gives the discrete case and connected one-complex case. Verified primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.2935 ; record https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.2935 . The one-extra-slot connectivity ingredient is therefore classical topology, even if its HMM application is new.

Wolfer and Watanabe, *Geometric aspects of data-processing of Markov chains*, Transactions of Mathematics and Its Applications 8 (2024), tnae001, studies stochastic right inverses of lumpings/congruent embeddings. It is relevant surrounding literature for stochastic sections and lifted chains, but its results should not be represented as this monodromy theorem. Verified primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04575 ; publication DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/imatrm/tnae001 .
