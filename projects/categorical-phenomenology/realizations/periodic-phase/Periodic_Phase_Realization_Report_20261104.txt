Periodic phase realization result
4 November 2026

The exchangeable classification extends to a genuinely nonexchangeable temporal class, with the same exact continuous-width criterion. Stationarity is not required. The key is to reconstruct every observable phase, including phases with zero initial weight, rather than counting only the initial block-mixture components.

Observable assumptions

- Continuous normalized finite-alphabet word laws over a compact Hausdorff parameter space.
- A fixed positive period m. Within every complete collection of periods, probabilities are invariant under independently permuting the positions in each residue class modulo m.
- Constant finite ordinary Hankel rank p, using all word prefixes and suffixes, not merely aligned blocks.

Under these assumptions, the word laws determine a p-sheeted cover of distinct periodic emission profiles, a continuous phase-advance permutation with order dividing m, initial weights, and the predictive bundle. Initial weights may vanish. Averaging the first m observable time shifts produces strictly positive weights on the full phase cover.

Exact result

A continuous stochastic realization of width k exists exactly when the full phase-frame cover admits an equivariant map into Hom(K_p,K_k), the space of p probability rows with pairwise disjoint supports in k slots. This is also the criterion for a continuous stochastic encoding/decoding pair QL=I.

The lower bound applies to arbitrary native HMM competitors, including redundant, transient, unreachable, and nonstationary hidden models. Averaging their initial rows preserves their width. Two ordinary output blocks recover an approximate split, and an explicit continuous threshold construction makes it exact without adding states.

The upper bound is constructive: rho=wQ and N_y=L D_y Pi Q, where Pi advances the recovered phase. Thus phase advance itself adds no further compatibility obstruction under the arbitrary-native convention. Ordinary state-emitting HMMs have the same minimum width in either emission/transition convention, with the appropriate phase-shifted emissions.

Normalized signed width is the embedding dimension of the centered predictive bundle plus one. On a finite q-dimensional CW base, stochastic width is at most p+q. Width p is possible exactly when the full phase cover can be globally labeled.

Genuine temporal example

The existing three-sheeted cyclic cover over the two-dimensional Moore space supports a stationary three-phase family on four output symbols. Some two-output probabilities satisfy p(yz) != p(zy), so the example is nonexchangeable already at length two. Its exact widths remain:

- Pointwise stochastic and signed: 3.
- Continuous normalized signed: 4.
- Continuous stochastic: 5.

Its full rank-three predictive bundle is trivial, but its centered plane bundle is not. Every continuous four-state stochastic competitor has a positive uniform approximation error at an explicit finite horizon.

Additional observable reduction

For a one-symbol stationary process, exchangeability of aligned m-blocks already forces the block directing distributions to be products. No separate product-distribution hypothesis is needed in that stationary variant. The proof follows the empirical distribution of shifted blocks: repeated application of its explicit map removes within-block dependence.

Failure tests and limits

An alternating independent Bernoulli sequence can have initial aligned-block rank one but ordinary rank two. Counting initial atoms would therefore give the wrong width. Independent repeated-bit pairs are block exchangeable but not residue-wise exchangeable, showing why block symmetry alone is insufficient without stationarity.

This is not a theorem for arbitrary temporal HMM families. The probability-clock obstruction remains: finite rank, analyticity, and pointwise finite positive realizability do not imply a locally bounded state budget. Exact infinite symmetry, a known period, and constant rank are structural promises; finite samples alone do not certify them. One mixture trajectory does not generally identify all component weights. Uniform parameter-space certification needs a suitable sampling design and quantitative regularity.

Prior art and verification

Multiclass de Finetti representation, grouped-observation mixture identification, periodic HMM models, positive-cone realization, and Hom-complex topology are established ingredients. The manuscript compares Graham, Vandermeulen-Scott, Prosdocimi-Finesso, Touron, Monras-Winter, Mond-Smith-van Straten, and the relevant topology sources. No novelty or priority claim is certified.

The archive contains the self-contained TeX proof, independent mathematical and literature reviews, exact and numerical formula checks, their outputs, and background drafts. One exact checker verifies 255 words through length seven, both ordinary HMM conventions, observable phase averaging, the ordinary-versus-block rank distinction, the shifted-block factorization map, and a two-output order witness. An independent suite adds 1,023 exact word comparisons, a transient hidden model whose averaged outputs are stationary although its averaged hidden row is not, and deterministic classifier/robustness checks with no Monte Carlo sampling. These computations verify formulas and examples, not the global topological impossibility proofs.
