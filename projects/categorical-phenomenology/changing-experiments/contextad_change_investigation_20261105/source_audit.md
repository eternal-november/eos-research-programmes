# Source audit for the contextad connection

Checked 5 November 2026. This audit distinguishes restored project manuscripts from the published source and distinguishes versions of the published source. No full primary-source PDF is redistributed.

## Main finding

Contextads supply a real compositional change of operations, but not the observation family automatically. The concrete path is:

1. A correctly typed contextad morphism induces a strong double comparison.
2. An actual state interpretation and a backwards, exact, admitted-test translation give a Chu map.
3. The earlier common-generation theorem supplies the row, concept and partial-topology comparison, with different variances where appropriate.
4. Joint lifting, separation, and native witness reconstruction remain additional conditions.

This is a conditional derived synthesis of established constructions. It does not claim a new general contextad theorem.

Version 2: Matteo Capucci and David Jaz Myers, Contextful Arrows via Wreath Products, arXiv:2410.21889v2, 3 November 2026, 120 PDF pages.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21889v2

The unversioned PDF now resolves to v2. The v1 HTML endpoint returned 404; the versioned PDF was readable. The source was inspected with web PDF text extraction.

Relevant v2 locators (printed pages are one more than the tool's zero-based page index):

- Definition 5.25, pp. 63–64: contextad morphism consists of base map F, cartesian grade functor, lineator e' G → F e, invertible unit and multiplication comparisons, with coherence.
- Remark 5.26, p. 65: cartesian transport comparison. The source's rendered formula has awkward indices; our note writes its typed version for u:A→A', namely G_A(u*P') ≅ (Fu)*(G_A'P').
- Remark 5.28, p. 65: a strong contextad morphism can have noninvertible lineator. Strength refers to the grade comparison data.
- Example 5.33, pp. 67–68: comonad morphism lineator D'F→FD and corresponding strict contextad morphism. This directly supports the reader refinement example.
- Example 5.35, pp. 68–69: display-map functor preserving required pullbacks induces a contextad morphism. This directly supports the display-span comparison; it is not new.
- Definition 5.36, p. 69: Ctx trifunctor.
- Equations (5.6.20)–(5.6.21), p. 70: strong double functor sends (P,f) to (G P,F(f)ℓ), including endpoint squares.
- Remark 5.40, p. 71: explains strong double functor versus merely laxly linear contextad map.
- Section 5.4, pp. 55–56: the explicit Kleisli tricocompletion presentation is still qualified by a conjectural identification. Footnote 19 distinguishes triessential surjectivity from the adjoint triequivalence issue.
- Theorem 5.19 and Remark 5.20, p. 60: wreath product statement and explicit qualification about taking its higher formula for granted.

Our note uses the concrete map calculation and separately states the source's foundational qualifications. It does not claim the v2 update resolved all caveats in v1.

## Original checks in this investigation

- Reader refinement: exhaustive comparison of 256 pairs of coarse finite reader functions; composition preserved. The 16 coarse functions embed faithfully into 256 rich functions; image equals the fibre-constant functions and is not full.
- Orbit span: C2 acting diagonally on C2×C2 has two orbits, while the product of separate orbit sets has one element. Relative equality distinguishes the two retained orbits. The canonical comparison is oplax, not a strong span compositor.
- Operation enlargement: a 3-state example in which two old rows agree but one added operation distinguishes them. The new operation fails to descend to the old quotient.
- Probe descent: a nonconstant bit observation does not descend to a one-point environment.

The scripts establish those finite calculations only. The source contains the general proofs. No Lean certification or TeX compilation is claimed.
