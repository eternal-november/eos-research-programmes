# Independent evaluation of the revised research contract

## Recommendation

**The revision is a substantially better provisional research charter. It can sensibly authorize a bounded Gate A investigation. It is not yet a proof-ready theorem contract, a demonstrated new theory, or a novelty-cleared programme.**

This distinction matters. A provisional charter can legitimately ask the researcher to identify the first theorem. Here Gate A has useful outputs and stopping rules, rather than merely requesting more exposition. But the central theorem is still to be chosen. Accepting the direction should not be described as accepting a settled mathematical deliverable or evidence that the entire programme is tractable.

The most important correction succeeds: discovering an unsupplied decomposition is now a demanding flagship, not the only admissible form of emergence. Derived relations, operations, invariants, sufficient dynamic states, and explained selection among representations can qualify. The revision does not solve that flagship or quietly replace it with an easier result.

## What is materially better

1. **The central success criterion no longer mistakes one output form for all emergence.** Gate B names an output and operational use, requires a nontrivial positive family, and distinguishes novelty relative to an instance from novelty of the theorem. This avoids both the old decomposition-only gate and the opposite error of treating a familiar quotient as a new result.
2. **Comparison has substantive obligations.** The common fragment, directions, semantics, permitted translations and claimed losses must be stated. Failure of a particular functor or inverse is no longer allowed to imply universal non-comparability. Stacey is correctly treated as mathematics to build upon, including construction and conditional comparison, rather than a metaphor or a guarantee of universal adjunctions.
3. **The empirical distinctions are sharper.** Available records, the repertoire of executable experiments, a full exact law on that repertoire, and an extension to hypothetical experiments are separate. More evidence is not automatically more access. Ordinary confidence sets are not automatically nested. A full law on available experiments need not determine inaccessible intervention laws. These distinctions are important independently of which categorical language is ultimately selected.
4. **Warrant is not collapsed into canonicality.** Doctrine-relative exact construction, statistical identification, and objective- or prior-driven selection remain different achievements. Their outputs can be useful without identifying a unique physical ontology or an executable latent system.
5. **The earlier hard work remains a baseline.** Generated images and their doctrine changes, prediction/filtering, native positive realizations, and cylinder comparisons are not reissued as novelty. The joint report's fixed-witness initiality and finite row-polytope constructions are not mistaken for arbitrary multistage assembly through stochastic intermediate maps. The retained native targets therefore still ask for genuinely additional obligations relative to those reports.
6. **Gates can credit intermediate results without awarding unsupported conclusions.** A pure exact theorem need not wait for a finite-sample learner. A generative result need not solve native decomposition. Negative results, restricted repairs and open gaps have separate statuses. These are substantive improvements in research governance.

## What is not yet demonstrated

### A. The central theorem is still unresolved, not merely unwritten

“Substantial class,” “nontrivial family,” “new positive bridge,” “sharp boundary,” and “tractable presentation” identify desirable qualities, but do not yet specify a theorem that an independent reader could prove or falsify. Gate A correctly requires the missing specification. It does not make that work disappear.

Before promoting the charter to a theorem-level commitment, a short theorem statement should fix:

- the class of inputs and their effective or exact presentation;
- the output object or invariant and its operational interpretation;
- the comparison class, variance, common fragment and preservation property;
- the independent assumptions, distinguished from the sought conclusion;
- the resource or error measure, where one is claimed;
- the meaning of “sharp”: an if-and-only-if criterion, a counterexample removing a hypothesis, or matching quantitative bounds;
- the particular two mechanisms counted as unlike, and why the comparison is not just the existing generated-image theorem under another name;
- the strongest predecessor and the exact additional conclusion;
- positive and negative families that discriminate success from a restatement.

The final Gate A now explicitly requests these distinctions. This is a meaningful improvement in the charter, not evidence that the missing theorem has already been selected. Its execution should narrow one branch to a result that can actually be attempted, rather than add more programme sections.

### B. Novelty and tractability remain open

The text is appropriately candid that no new central bridge has been established. Its conjunction of ambitions is not evidence that any individual contribution is new: a new list of requirements can be assembled from old theories. Nor does the absence of one paper covering the entire list establish a research gap.

The learning route is especially exposed to established nonlinear realization, observability and observable-algebra methods, as well as finite-dimensional Koopman-style representations. Declaring a closure under update pullbacks or flow derivatives is a promising organizing move, but not a novelty result. The first precise claim needs a focused reduction audit against those methods.

Relative to the existing local reports, the stochastic flagship clearly demands more: genuine stochastic intermediate links, sequential reuse, independently constructed legal assembly routes, and explicit native resources. That shows an extension of this project's proved scope. It does not establish that the extension is new to the literature, achievable in the desired generality, or best tackled first.

### C. “Unsupplied” needs its instance-level audit

The revised criterion is sensible if it means the designated answer, factor maps, explanatory coordinates or output relations were not included as privileged input. A hypothesis class, broad search language, experimental syntax, or learning rule is allowed. Requiring the output to have no information-theoretic dependence on supplied structure would make derivation impossible.

The remaining danger is solution encoding: choosing a presentation or promise that already selects exactly the desired answer. Gate A should inspect this for the chosen family. Uniformity over a declared family, relabelling invariance, and independent verification against response/context equations help. They do not automatically guarantee empirical identification or novelty. Listing all consistent models, enumerating every decomposition, or merely listing equalities in the response table still needs an additional structural consequence.

## Learning dynamics: useful candidate, distinct questions

The scalar control is mathematically useful. For the supplied Euclidean gradient dynamics with predictor w = uv, current prediction does not determine future training responses; adding s = u² + v² closes the displayed dynamics. The derivative identities and the bound s ≥ 2|w| check. The revised regularity assumption and common interval of existence remove a well-posedness caveat raised in review. No global minimality or empirical discovery result follows from this example, and the source does not claim one.

There are nevertheless two different questions:

1. Reconstruct a sufficient state for a supplied learning system from its allowed responses.
2. Explain the formation or selection of a representation during training.

The first is a legitimate reconstruction problem but does not by itself solve the second. The optional prospective learning benchmark gives the second a more meaningful test by withholding final-checkpoint information and evaluation interventions. That benchmark is still a proposal, not evidence of general discovery or a required experiment for every pure theorem.

Learning is a strong candidate because it separates present predictive equivalence from future training-response equivalence. The final revision appropriately makes this a prominent candidate rather than a mandatory test and explicitly separates sufficient-state reconstruction from representation formation. These changes resolve a scope concern raised during review. Choosing it first remains a research decision; the factorization-only gate has not been replaced by a learning-only gate.

## Provisional adoption and decision points

**Gate A is suitable as a first provisional scope.** Its deliverables are an input/output ledger, actual theorem candidate, semantics and assumptions, strongest-predecessor comparison, and falsifying controls. Its stop conditions reject renamed known work, hypotheses containing the answer, and inaccessible required probes. Independent review can determine whether those artifacts actually contain a research residual. A finding that no proposed residual survives is an informative outcome of this investigation, not permission to claim the positive programme completed.

The final source now makes the following programme choices explicit. They remain decisions for any later adoption, rather than silently accepted requirements:

1. **Comparative and generative achievements.** Keeping both as ambitions is coherent and respects the central observation-to-structure question. Requiring both as acceptance conditions for every first result is unnecessarily restrictive. One theorem may supply both conclusions; a useful result in one branch can be credited while the other remains open. The final source places that paired condition at programme level and allows the first concrete theorem to serve either goal or both. This is a coherent staging choice; adopting the paired programme is still optional.
2. **Learning as the first generative test.** It has a clear mathematical motivation, but its novelty audit is not complete. Select it after comparing a concrete learning theorem candidate with concrete alternatives, rather than making it mandatory because it is a vivid example.
3. **Two-sided presentation.** Retain it as a preferred candidate to test, not an axiom of success. The prior note and Stacey justify taking it seriously. They do not show that every empirical or dynamical problem benefits from the same presentation or that a one-sided formulation cannot prove the desired result.

The hard native flagship should remain available, with its current positive obligations and explicit open status. It should not be allowed to consume the first scope unless that is the selected theorem family. The final source also supplies the requested applicability ledger: narrow results face the controls relevant to their claims; whole-programme generality eventually faces the whole suite. This resolves the earlier risk that every small theorem would be required to settle all 20 disparate tests.

## Overall assessment

The revision is more honest and more flexible without making success vacuous. It preserves ambitious mathematics while separating exact construction, empirical recovery, native realization and explanatory interpretation. Its best feature is the ability to say exactly which branch has succeeded and which has not.

Its principal unresolved risk is still locating a theorem-level residual that is both nontrivial and workable. The correct next commitment, if the direction is adopted, is to resolve that question through Gate A. The present document supports a serious research programme; it does not yet demonstrate a new central theorem, a universal theory of phenomenology, or the feasibility of the full programme.
