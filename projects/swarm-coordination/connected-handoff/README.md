# Connected support handoff

Run `python3 -B pilot.py` under Python 3.12, without optimization. Replay all saved traces using `python3 -B pilot.py --json | cmp results.json`.

A fixed line of crews supports loads in transit. Load j can be held only by its original crew j or adjacent successor j+1. Each crew can bear one load. An authorized transfer can start only if the receiving support is unoccupied. It completes one tick later. Until actual acceptance, the original support remains committed. No load disappears, no crew bears two loads and no receiver may promise a simultaneously occupied support.

Both compared histories place four loads on their native supports at tick −2. At tick −1 final rack receipts discharge jobs 1 and 2 in the first history, and only job 2 in the second. Crew 3 is occupied by unrelated load 3 in both. At tick 0 crew 0 requests the same relief, due by tick 4. It still physically holds the same undischarged load and has not observed the distant rack receipts. The prefix is executed in the finite model, not evidence of a physical apparatus.

A request moves one hop at a time under the installed policy. A recipient that still holds its own job forwards authorization for its own transfer. An already free recipient does not forward. At each tick, accepted transfers update the physical supports first, then authorization deliveries are processed, then newly authorized free receivers may start a transfer. The fixed policy has no global chain-length or scenario bit. The actual relief at tick 2 uses crews 0 and 1 in the first history. In the second it takes until tick 4 and also needs crew 2 to support crew 1's live load first. No authorization or physical effect crosses from the free receiving crew into the unrelated live suffix.

For k contiguous live loads from the root, exactly k transfers advance the repair; even with all authorizations present they take at least k ticks. The installed authorization law prohibits initiating an incident transfer before receiving authorization from the preceding crew. Under this law, the earliest relief time is the sum of the first k positive hop delays plus k. This information delay is a property of the authorized interface, not a physical lower bound. Preauthorized assistance, centralized change authority, bypass support or a simultaneous atomic transfer primitive would change the comparison. No such option is silently assumed impossible outside this model.

An independent endpoint matching enumerator finds a unique supported assignment with root empty. A separate breadth-first placement search verifies exact feasible timing at given authorization times, including preauthorization and an unavailable necessary receiver. The simulator is checked across 84 configurations (one to seven incident positions, three delay patterns, both exact and adjacent deadlines). Missing a deadline leaves the old root support intact; safety does not imply that relief was delivered. The physical support radius and the authorization/delivery horizon are separately tracked.

Factored matching and timed contract reasoning already derive the transfer necessity, the local stopping seam and the timing limit. This is a finite prototype, not a proof-reuse advantage, an independent held-out transfer, or a claim about general swarms.
