This protocol is the review procedure for C11-R, C11-P and C11-T. They ask different questions: whether a cascade occurs, whether a lawful policy can repair it, and which parts of the proof can be carried across the change. A failure trace, a working policy and a transferable proof are distinct deliverables.

\section{Before the first run}

Select a joint task with a real service requirement: for example, a simulated relay handoff during ongoing survey work and a separate commitment to a shared access or charging resource. Keep the task choice open. The initial model must let coordination fail without making the safety or service law tautological, and it must admit at least one productive history for each workload used to claim success. Fix the held-out selection procedure now; the reviewer picks the case after the implementation and proof interface have been frozen.

Publish the primitive event laws, resource model and owner of each permission; the initial states and nominal installed controllers; the observations, local clocks and permissible actions; and the admitted environmental schedules. Set the safety invariant, service deadline or minimum throughput, and any preauthorized refusal budget. Identify the affected party who is owed that service. Do not amend the promises after observing the failures. Describe what the simulator abstracts away and which claims depend on that abstraction. No hardware tests are part of acceptance.

Use the \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/e1348964f73704212d2d8b48d5982a5ed600a2ff/projects/byzantine-control/FAULT-COVERAGE.md}{fault inventory} to form a coverage register. For each modeled mechanism give its onset, duration, who notices what and when, what prior work survives, and the other faults or repairs with which it can interact on the same history. For omitted mechanisms, say what premise excludes them and which claims would need revisiting if that premise were relaxed. Cover accidental or environmental failure; include a common-cause or recovery-induced chain in the case register. A benign narrowing of the model is allowed if declared before evaluation; do not discard failed histories afterward.

\section{C11-R: Establish the cascade}

Provide a replay from an allowed initial state to a common cut with a concrete unsettled commitment. Name its authority, bearer, resource, deadline and remaining effect. Starting there, run four continuations with the same incumbent controllers: nominal, first change only, second change only, and both. Replay the same underlying external choices wherever they still apply; derive all endogenous observations, grants, actions and physical outcomes separately in each arm. If one change makes an external input ill-typed, declare and justify the coupling of the continuations before comparing them. Specify the event order and also test the reverse order when it is admitted. One failing order is sufficient if scoped to that order.

Under the named matched conditions, the first three arms must meet the \emph{same} end-to-end guarantee. The combined arm must violate it and change the premises of downstream obligations across at least two distinct interfaces. Identify the initiating events, retained effects, decisions and resulting resource or authority constraints at those interfaces. Distinguish that chain from a predeclared graph path: either intervene on an intermediate mechanism and show what failure disappears, or give a matched case that changes the proposed intermediate condition and separates the downstream outcomes. Merely invalidating a sufficient certificate is not a violation. Report any claim of universal single-change tolerance separately, with its quantifier and evidence.

Provide a second reachable cut on the same topology and with the same edits, but with materially different live commitments or evidence and a different required response. Vary at least one parameter, give a range in which the witness persists or an exact analysis of the event-order boundary, and record a nearby nonfailure. A small exact enumerator or model checker is a reference at its enumerated scope. The timed simulator must also reproduce the intended mechanism and external guarantee, rather than consuming the enumerator's answer as its control input.

\section{C11-P: Establish repair and a lower bound}

Install the repaired policy family before the incidents. In each admissible execution its decisions use only the observations, memory, clocks, resources and permission available at the decision. It must serve across the admitted stagger in detection and carry already-issued commitments to settlement, valid transfer or authorized cancellation. A repair may reserve existing capacity or leave some actors on the verified incumbent policy; record the cost and the effect on other live service. Neither perfect diagnosis nor a direct broadcast of the global arm is a valid input unless already furnished by the model and charged in both arms. Keep the safety invariant and service promise from C11-R. A stop or refusal is creditable only within a previously specified allowance; it cannot account for all productive work.

Show a feasible repair on the declared family, with a nonempty unchanged part that is coupled to the edited part through a live resource, observation or duty. Give a proof of feasibility under the envelope plus checked simulations of the positive and adverse cases. An exhaustive proof for a finite model is admissible if the model covers the stated envelope and the executable controller follows its local observation constraints.

Name a narrower repair class, including which controllers, resources or authority may change. Prove no member of that class meets the promise. For example, two histories may be indistinguishable to all authorized actors before the decision yet require incompatible actions, or a capacity argument may cover every eligible action in the class. Proving that one narrower policy fails is insufficient. If the feasible supports have incomparable minima, report that fact; distinguish a controller that must change in every repair from one that belongs to a particular inclusion-minimal repair. Separately report which deployed policies change, which unchanged policies receive different inputs, and which proof obligations require revision. These sets need not agree.

\section{C11-T: Establish proof reuse and check the boundary}

Select a named, revision-pinned Contextual Composition theorem. Supply the actual maps from the simulation law and execution histories, the retained shared boundary, and the hypotheses needed to compose. If using whole-scope assembly, construct the coupling from the primitive resource law and pending work; a supplied law already containing the verified global answer does not qualify. If using residual profiles, prove the relevant tests descend and reflect on the actual admissible histories. Check any newly reachable histories and physical effects separately; the quotient test does not establish reachability. Name a reused guarantee \emph{outside} the immediate edit and exhibit its derivation from the named theorem and the newly checked premises. Expose the premises that cannot be recovered from the component marginals or from an unchanged terminal state.

Compare the proof with a direct proof that is already modular. Give both routes the same plant, certified component facts, admissible histories, observations, fault and service requirements, and the same opportunity to cache or factor prior work. For the primary and transfer cases list the statements and dependencies discharged by each route. Distinguish reused verbatim, transported through a proved interface, rechecked without new reasoning, newly proved, and invalidated. Include setup, adaptation, cross-component checks and replay cost in the relevant column. Lines changed and modules touched may be diagnostic, but cannot stand in for the proof comparison. Any measured effort claim needs a measurement method; a structural reuse claim can instead be proved directly. A tie passes the comparison if both routes are shown to discharge the same obligations.

Freeze the interfaces, implementations, baseline and acceptance checks. Have a non-author choose a materially different transfer from the declared class without tailoring it to the existing proof. The transfer must vary the relevant mechanism, history or composition, not just its seed or names, and impose a real new compatibility check. Require one positive transfer using the frozen theorem and one deliberately rejected case that witnesses an exact missing premise. If a discovered obstruction requires an interface revision, retain the failed attempt, record the changed interface and costs, and run another independently selected transfer on the refrozen version. Adjusting the accepted evidence after seeing a held-out result does not count as reuse.

\section{Evidence and review}

Submit the model and parameter files, controller, exact reference, simulator, replay commands, case register and traces. Include local histories at the decisive events, along with the global trace used by the reviewer. Provide the lower-bound argument, pinned theorem and instantiation, matched direct comparison, transfer selection record, and a statement of what remains open. Reserve enough information to check an unexpected competing explanation of the cascade. The proofs should point directly to the code that evaluates their hypotheses; logs establish what ran, not what the theorem means.

Two existing results calibrate rejection. \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/340}{Byzantine Control PR 340} compares two proofs on a bounded timing model; both reuse the receiver proof and must recheck the same timing premise. It establishes a tie, not an advantage. \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/341}{PR 341} runs four arms on B's timed receiver from a supplied cut. The combined edit suppresses a diagnostic read, but the experiment does not reach that cut from an installed B system or exclude coverage by a sibling. Use it as a negative control for C11-R, not as a positive cascade witness.

A non-author replays the matched arms, repair and held-out case, checks the reachability and necessity claims, and audits the actual dependencies in both proofs. Before dispatch, ask that reviewer for the cheapest technically passing solution and the strongest direct baseline known. Reject a supplied failure state, an invented global observer, independent histories glued after the fact, an enlarged resource budget hidden in the repair, or proof savings shifted into an adapter. Keep five outcomes separate: completed investigation, verified cascade, verified repair, verified transfer, and advantage or novelty. A scientifically useful negative result can settle its own question without passing a positive obligation.
