# Fork relief: when does a local receipt change what can be done?

Run `python3 -B pilot.py` in this directory under Python 3.12 without optimization. Check exact recorded behavior with `python3 -B pilot.py --json | cmp results.json`.

A root relief carriage can serve one of two branches. The finite prefix places both loads on their original supports. At tick 1 one distal rack actually receives a load, so only the other branch still needs relief. The distal witness samples this live lane and issues one authenticated receipt. The root sees its own installed keyed crossover, which either preserves or reverses which distal lane each root route reaches. It does not see the distal rack. Two physical binary choices therefore determine the required root route: the remaining distal lane and the keyed routing of left/right sockets. The deterministic transition model realizes the orientation by reversing its two-entry live-load vector, not by prescribing a desired controller response.

At tick 2 the root may commit left, right, or wait. A committed carriage is no longer available for another move, and physical acceptance takes until tick 3. Before acceptance the old carrier remains committed. Waiting or making an empty trip preserves existing support but misses required service. One carriage excludes dispatching both routes or keeping a second carriage in reserve. These are finite plant assumptions, not physical test data. The allowed controller does not get more carriages, a remote latch sensor, a faster move, or a special branch bit in its observation.

## Local information, not central feasibility

With the same observed crossover and no usable receipt by tick 2, the two reachable distal rack completions give identical root views and require opposite actions. Each physical history admits a timely schedule if its live branch is known. No single policy on the root's view serves both. A probabilistic choice cannot meet a universal guarantee either.

The fixture issues its measured live-lane receipt at tick 1. Two positive-duration hop events take it through a relay and into the root's received register. For the successful case the receipt arrives and is consumed before decision in the same tick (2). An extra quarter-tick makes both contrary cuts indistinguishable at that decision. The trial with delivery one full tick late, at 3, also fails: the result cannot retrospectively change the committed carriage movement. Only the delivery changes; the observations' source, deadline, routes and capacity stay fixed.

## What the paired relation preserves

Two hypothetical exact support laws admit different crossovers together with the distal live lane: equal bits in one law, opposite bits in the other. Both have the same marginals (each binary value has mass one-half) and both are individually feasible without a receipt. The first requires a policy that always goes left and the second always right. The local marginals therefore do not determine the policy, and multiplying them would invent extra histories. The checker uses uniform rational weights only for exact bookkeeping, not probabilities estimated from plant behavior. It retains the full admitted pairing; off-support raw entries have zero mass.

The direct comparator groups per-world contract factors by the root’s actual local observation and intersects their allowed actions. Exhaustive enumeration of fixed policy tables separately checks the complete relation and its feasibility. The contextual calculation reassembles the admitted carrier with exact conditional rows, projects the accepted actions and intersects them within each local observation. It agrees with the direct comparator. Rebalancing the two hop delays without changing receipt time changes one internal relay event but leaves the exact relation from root views to allowed actions identical. The comparison identifies no changed rows for the relay edit and two changed rows for a diagonal-to-anti-diagonal coupling edit, one for each crossover observation without a receipt. The unchanged exact relation suffices to retain the root policy check; certifying equality is not free. The main model has only one decision-maker. It shows neither proof-work reduction over the direct comparator nor a complete distributed update or synthesis method.

A separate schematic check eliminates one shared policy choice `z` from two constraints `a0 XOR z = s0` and `a1 XOR z = s1`. Equal signs constrain the two retained policy entries to agree; changing one sign constrains them to differ. Each entry alone can still take either value. This illustrates why a separator can need joint policy entries. It is not a second plant model or evidence that the fork has a distributed synthesis advantage.
