\title{How many old-occupation histories can rule out a new launch?}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Scope.}
A dispatcher must select one integer start from $0,\ldots,N-1$ for an inspection of duration $d>0$ on an exclusive passage. Each candidate world has the same admissible local record at every choice and contains one already-authorized use with actual interval $[s,s+h)$, $h\ge h_0>0$. The controller has no authority to cancel that use. The worlds are alternatives, not simultaneously occurring occupations. A new launch is excluded by that world exactly at starts in $[s-d+1,s+h-1]$, of width at least $w=h_0+d-1$ before clipping. Adjacent actual intervals may share a boundary. We allow an old use to occur across either applicable horizon endpoint. No physically calibrated distribution, extra observation or signed release premise is assumed.

A safe common launch exists if and only if the union of the forbidden intervals misses a slot. It can be unsound to keep just the earliest and latest legal times, since the forbidden interval can split each world's legal starts in two. This is the unary disjunctive fragment of \href{https://cse.unl.edu/~choueiry/Documents/DechterMeiri-AIJ.pdf}{Dechter, Meiri and Pearl}'s temporal constraint model. The exact decision and ordinary farthest-end greedy cover are established tools, not novelty claims.

\paragraph{Sharp source-witness bound.}
Write $N-1=q(w+1)+r$ with $0\le r\le w$. The largest possible number of required histories in a smallest infeasibility certificate is $2q+1+\mathbf{1}_{r>0}$. The upper bound applies to arbitrary widths at least $w$; equality can be attained using width exactly $w$. This is a lower bound for a certificate by source worlds, not a bit lower bound for all representations. For fixed $w$ it grows with the horizon, unlike the convex one-start case.

In an inclusion-minimal cover of size $k$, order the forbidden intervals by left endpoint $u_1<\dots<u_k$. The right endpoints are also strictly increasing, since a contained member cannot have a private slot. A private integer slot for an interior interval lies past the preceding right endpoint and before the succeeding left endpoint, so $u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}\ge w+1$. For $k\ge2$, a private slot for the first needs $u_2\ge1$, and a private slot for the last implies the penultimate right endpoint is at most $N-2$, hence $u_{k-1}\le N-w-1$. For odd $k=2m+1\ge3$ step from $u_2\ge1$ through $m-1$ pairs to get $N\ge m(w+1)+1$. For even $k=2m\ge4$ step from $u_3\ge2$ through $m-2$ pairs to get $N\ge(m-1)(w+1)+2$. For $k=2$ the bound is $N\ge2$, and $k=1$ requires $N\ge1$.

For attainment with $k\ge2$, place width-$w$ intervals with gaps between successive starts alternating $w,1,w,1,\ldots$. Begin the horizon at the last slot of the first interval and finish one slot after the penultimate right endpoint. This gives equality in the applicable minimum horizon and every interval has a private slot. Then relax left and right truncation one slot at a time to take the full union. Finally increase the start gaps one at a time up to $w$, extending the right endpoint. The cover stays gapless and each private region survives since the sums of adjacent gaps cannot decrease. This realizes every horizon between the minimum and $kw$. A disjoint partition by $w$-slot intervals clipped at the last slot proves that the maximum feasible $k$ is at least $\lceil N/w\rceil$; the upper bound therefore has $kw\ge N$. The one-slot case uses a single interval. Each constructed forbidden interval is realized by a possible old start and physical duration $h_0$.

\paragraph{Revalidation of the actual consumer.}
The receiver holds a frozen set of source IDs with exclusion claims, not a global picture of the running world. On a known replacement family, its negative certificate survives exactly when the claims cover the slot set in every admitted vector of replacements. A gap invalidates this certificate; it is not automatically a safe launch if other old effects were omitted. If the replacement choices are freely combinable across IDs, this is equivalent to the union of the individual all-variant intersections covering the slots. The converse picks a variant at every ID omitting the same slot. This equality fails under coupling. On slots $0,1,2$, two replacement modes can swap which owner occupies $[0,1]$ and which occupies $[1,2]$ without admitting modes where both occupy the same interval. Every actual vector covers; their Cartesian closure falsely weakens the proof and leaves an endpoint. The proof must retain the source relation and not just state that two independent permissions were recognized. The example supplies the relation directly and does not yet implement its distributed enforcement.

\paragraph{Contextual Composition import.}
Apply the \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/main/projects/contextual-composition/feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Residual_Profiles_and_Admission_Reflection.tex}{residual-profile criterion} to the named obligation-exclusion interface. With the candidate horizon and dwell fixed, the primitive tests are whether a particular supplied old effect excludes each candidate start. These unary sections factor exactly through the clipped exclusion set. The source model has a timed mechanically retained work right; the target model uses a powered gate physically latched until its reopening event. On actual modeled carriers, map a local old hold $(s,h)$ to the gate's endpoint pair $(s,s+h)$. The half-open calculation gives reflection (T) of every test; inverse subtraction supplies a source representative for every attained target profile (C), including when additional gate metadata is present. These prove the hypotheses for a shared presentation and let the unchanged outcome consumer combine the passed exclusion sets. Old role identity is not consumed by this narrow decision; this is not a claim that other consumers can discard it. A gate remaining latched for one additional tick fails reflection whenever this changes a tested slot, even if the receipt remains unchanged. Changing the new inspection's duration changes the tests themselves. A matched factored direct interval check provides the same result and can also reuse the intervals. Nothing here proves native concurrent recovery or independent test transfer.

\paragraph{Verification.}
The simple sorted-interval greedy cover minimizes witness count: among intervals covering the first uncovered slot choose one ending furthest to the right, replacing any such interval in an optimum without decreasing coverage of the remaining slots. The replay matches this with explicit minimality and the native occupancies, and checks 249,839 finite equal-width families, 3,750 sharp constructions, and 4,000 variable-width cases. Further exact checks test both source-to-interface maps and 5,000 Cartesian replacement comparisons. Lean checks the overlap and spacing kernels; the full theorem follows by the interval argument above, not solely from the Lean result. No priority, operational advantage over a matched published method, or full contract obligation is claimed.
