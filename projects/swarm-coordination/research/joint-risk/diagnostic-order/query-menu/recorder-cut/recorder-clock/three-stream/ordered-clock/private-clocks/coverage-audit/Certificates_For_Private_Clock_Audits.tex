\title{Upper certificates and feasible counterexamples for private-clock audits}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

An exact worst-case empirical cap is hard with independently unknown
clock offsets. A conservative refusal need not follow from that
hardness. The same audit can produce a verified upper bound and a
lower bound backed by one feasible set of joins. If those bounds
straddle the cap, it must say so.

\paragraph{Model.}
Remove episodes already known to fail at the anchor and add their
count back at the end. Let \(P\) be the remaining anchor positions.
Within a group of sources, the ordered acquisition law has a single
shared offset in a specified interval. Across groups, offset and
residual choices are independent in the sense of feasible histories:
an admissible choice in each group forms an admissible joint history.
Each group has at least one admissible complete join. Let
\(\mathcal S_i\) be the possible subsets of \(P\) that fail in group
\(i\) under one such join. The objective is the largest size of
\(\bigcup_i S_i\) over \(S_i\in\mathcal S_i\). These are possible
empirical episode flags, not independent probabilities of failures.

\paragraph{A checked upper certificate.}
Use rational weights \(0\le\beta_p\le1\) and define
\[
 W_i(\beta)=\max_{S\in\mathcal S_i}\sum_{p\in S}\beta_p,\qquad
 U(\beta)=\sum_{p\in P}(1-\beta_p)+\sum_i W_i(\beta).
\]
Each covered position is charged at least one unit by its constant
\(1-\beta_p\) plus its weight in one or more groups. Uncovered positions
also have a nonnegative charge. Summing proves the upper bound without
having to identify any episode pairs.

The ordered-clock construction gives an exact weighted oracle.
At each common-offset cell of a group, form its virtual failure
slots, including the constraints from successes. Check each stream
for feasible full linkage. Across all streams in that group, match
weighted anchor positions to the slots. A feasible covered position
can be represented by one matched slot. Conversely, extend a partial
slot match in each stream, retaining its occupied positions, then
decode that stream's sorted real records. Because weights are
nonnegative, any added failures do not hurt the bound.
The replay sorts positions by weight and augments their matching.
On each prefix in descending weight order, the matching cardinality
is optimal: adding a new vertex can only increase the maximum by one,
and any new augmenting path can be taken from that added vertex.
Previously accepted positions stay matched. Summing the prefix
cardinalities against successive weight differences yields the exact
weighted objective. Maximizing over the cells gives \(W_i\).

\paragraph{Matching lower witnesses and the remaining gap.}
The strongest bound of this form is the dual of the fractional coverage
linear program: put a probability vector \(x_{i,S}\) on the sets in each
group and maximize \(\sum_p z_p\) subject to
\(0\le z_p\le1\) and
\(z_p\le \sum_i\sum_{S\ni p}x_{i,S}\).
The dual has \(\beta_p\) for the second constraint and a surcharge for
each position whose weight is below one. Weights above one can be
clipped without worsening the objective. For the theoretical
polynomial separation algorithm, use the group oracle to find violated
inequalities of the form \(w_i\ge \sum_{p\in S}\beta_p\). The replay
optimizes an explicit finite LP in small cases and verifies the
reconstructed rational primal and dual certificates. It does not
benchmark a general implicit solver.

A feasible LP distribution also supplies a feasible joint witness.
Draw the set in each group independently for the purpose of finding
a witness, not to model the actual clocks or failure rates. For a
position whose marginal coverage summed across groups is \(q_p\),
the probability it is covered is at least \(1-e^{-q_p}\), hence at
least \((1-e^{-1})\min(1,q_p)\). The expected count is at least
\(1-e^{-1}\) times the LP objective. Once each group's distribution
has finite support, fixing choices in succession by conditional
expectation yields a deterministic compatible count at least as large
as that expectation. Explicit feasible choices, not the expectation
alone, give the lower certificate.

For an integer cap \(K\), an upper bound with \(\lfloor U\rfloor\le K\)
certifies the cap; a feasible witness above \(K\) disproves it.
Anything between is inconclusive. If cross-group coupling exists,
a conservative superset can still yield a valid upper bound, but
a rounded choice need not be realizable and cannot be used as
a lower witness without a separate common-history check.

\paragraph{Executed comparisons.}
With one private clock facing offset demands of opposite polarity,
the unlinked cap calculation allows two newly failed positions.
A certificate with both weights one gives the sharp upper bound one.
A compatible witness attains one. With the four clauses using both
polarities of two variables, the LP says four while the largest
realizable count is three. The rounded witness reaches three but
the fractional upper cannot certify that cap. These are reductions
through the same stated timestamp law, not a case of a numerical
solver returning an incorrect result.

The replay independently checks 420 weighted group inputs against
whole-offset permutation enumeration and verifies rational
certificates and rounded choices on three named controls. It does
not establish a calibrated source law or full R4 acceptance.
