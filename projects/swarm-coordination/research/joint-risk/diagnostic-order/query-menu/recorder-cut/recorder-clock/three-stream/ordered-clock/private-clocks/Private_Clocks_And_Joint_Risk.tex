\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}
\title{Independent unknown clock offsets make exact joint failure caps hard}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

Ordered timestamps and a single common unresolved offset permit
a polynomial worst-case empirical count. With independent unknown
offsets, that result does not extend even to separated two-episode
blocks with one fixed residual bound and two admissible permutations
per block. The decisive constraint is the same offset throughout each
stream, not a lack of time order within the individual blocks.

\paragraph{Declared source law.}
One complete anchor stream has a record for each episode at a known
acquisition time \(s_i\) and a binary failure flag. Each of \(K\)
complete other streams has local acquisitions \(t_j\) and flags,
one unknown but constant offset \(d\in[0,1]\) private to that stream,
and per-acquisition error at most \(9/4\). A record can match anchor
episode \(i\) if \(|t_j-s_i-d|\le9/4\). A whole-stream bijection
under a consistent offset is required. No other constraints tie the
residuals or offsets together. A realized episode fails if at least
one matched record has a failure flag. This is an empirical count
over all compatible full joins; it is neither a fault frequency
estimator nor a claim about a calibrated device.

\begin{theorem}
With the number of private clocks variable, deciding if there is a
compatible join above a given cap is NP-complete. Deciding if every
join meets the cap is coNP-complete, even at a fixed rational cap
fraction \(0<\alpha<1\). The construction has a feasible complete
join for every admissible tuple of offsets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A certificate gives a bijection per stream. All residuals in a
stream give closed rational intervals for its offset; intersect
them with \([0,1]\) to check the certificate in polynomial time.
For hardness, express a Boolean formula as non-tautological CNF
clauses. Let each clause be one block and each variable be one
non-anchor stream. A clause block based at \(h=10j\) has anchor
times \(h,h+2\) with flags \(0,1\). The first position is the
additional failure to be justified. In the stream for variable \(x\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
  \text{occurrence in the clause} & \text{local stamps} & \text{flags}\\
  \hline
  x & h+1,\ h+3 & 0,1\\
  \neg x & h,\ h+2 & 0,1\\
  \text{absent} & h,\ h+2 & 0,0
\end{array}
\]
An anchor stamp is at most two units from its block base, and
other records are at most three units from theirs. Even after
an offset in \([0,1]\), different blocks have absolute residual
at least seven and cannot join. The sorted pairing within each
block is valid at every offset. If \(c\) is the local shift
(\(1\) for a positive occurrence, \(0\) for a negative one),
the swapped pairing requires both
\(|c+2-d|\le9/4\) and \(|c-2-d|\le9/4\). The additional
failure is therefore possible exactly for
\(d\in[c-1/4,c+1/4]\cap[0,1]\). These positive-width intervals
for the two polarities are disjoint. Choices of permutation
in the blocks are independent once the offset is fixed.

Any offsets and linkages induce a Boolean assignment by thresholding
each offset at \(1/2\). Every additional failed position then
belongs to a clause satisfied by that assignment. Conversely, for
a Boolean assignment choose private offset zero or one and make
a swapped pair in every block where the variable's literal is
satisfied. The maximum union count is exactly the number \(m\)
of anchor-prefailed positions plus the maximum number of satisfiable
clauses. Hence a count of \(2m\) is possible iff the formula is
satisfiable. At a fixed fraction \(\alpha\), add isolated forced-safe
one-record blocks far from all previous blocks to reach
\(\lceil(2m-1)/\alpha\rceil\) episodes; they admit the same trivial
pairing at all offsets and do not change the count.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Controls.}
With clauses \(x\) and \(\neg x\), the worst count is three of four.
Pooling pair eligibility over offsets gives four by using incompatible
values for the same stream. With clauses \(x\) and \(\neg y\),
private offsets give four, but constraining the two streams to
one common unknown offset gives three. The exact replay checks
300 finite formulas against direct truth assignment search, independently
computing the admissible timestamp permutations by interval arithmetic.

There is also a one-stream obstruction to reusing the common-offset
slot representation after dropping the offset. With anchor stamps
\(0,5,6,9\), local stamps \(1,5,16,17\), failure flags on the
first and third local records, error bound \(7\) and offset in
\([3,7]\), the possible failure sets in zero-based indices are
\(\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{1,2\}\).
For bases \(\{0,3\}\) and \(\{1,2\}\), removing \(0\) from
the first basis leaves no permitted replacement from the second.
Thus the pooled feasible sets are not the bases of one matroid;
one cannot represent them by one ordinary saturating-slot matching.

\paragraph{Where the previous algorithm still applies.}
For any fixed offset vector, the ordered-slot solver remains
exact. If only a fixed number \(g\) of groups have independent
unknown offsets, with all clocks in each group sharing their one
offset, enumerate the Cartesian product of the groups' interval
cells and run the one-matching maximum in each cell. This is
polynomial for fixed \(g\), not a uniform polynomial bound as
\(g\) grows. Aligning or certifying offsets has value here because
it constrains a reused decision, not because the actual episode
identities must all be revealed. No deployment, calibrated
sampling law or full R4 acceptance follows.
