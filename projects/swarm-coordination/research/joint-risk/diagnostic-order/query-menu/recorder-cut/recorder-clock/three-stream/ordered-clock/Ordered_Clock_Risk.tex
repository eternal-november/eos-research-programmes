\title{Ordered acquisition restores a tractable worst-case failure count}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

The three-stream hardness result uses arbitrary permitted-link graphs.
With bounded timestamp error and one offset, time order does more than
make those graphs sparse. It compiles the permitted failure placements
into ordered intervals. The worst empirical count is then an ordinary
matching problem even if there are many streams, without ignoring the
constraints on successful records.

\paragraph{Source law.}
An anchor has one record per episode, ordered by acquisition stamp
\(s_1\le\cdots\le s_n\). Every other stream is complete, with one
record per episode, stamped \(t\) in its local clock. For a fixed
offset \(d\) and nonnegative residual bound \(\epsilon\), a record
may be joined to exactly the positions with \(|t-s_i-d|\le\epsilon\).
The same matching must cover all records within its stream; after
conditioning on the offset, any collection of stream matchings
is jointly realizable. Residuals may vary independently within
their bounds. This law excludes dropped records, additional
ordering or drift constraints, and dependence beyond the shared
offset. A message stamp format or recorder alone does not imply it.

\paragraph{Compile the forced and available prefix counts.}
Each record has an interval of positions \([l,u]\); an empty
interval means inconsistency. Sorted record stamps give both
endpoints in nondecreasing order. If two same-flag records are
assigned in reverse order, swapping their positions keeps each
in its interval. Thus a set of failure positions is feasible iff
its sorted elements match the sorted failure intervals and its
complement matches the sorted success intervals. The matches
can be built independently because the two position sets are
disjoint. Ties in stamps have the same interval.

For each prefix of length \(r\), let \(F^-(r)\) count failures
whose right endpoint is at most \(r\), and \(F^+(r)\) count failures
whose left endpoint is at most \(r\). Define \(S^-,S^+\) in the same
way for successes. A prefix with \(f(r)\) failure positions must satisfy
\[
 L(r)=\max(F^-(r),r-S^+(r))\le f(r)\le
 U(r)=\min(F^+(r),r-S^-(r)).
\]
Conversely these inequalities for all prefixes force each ordered
failure and success into its assigned interval: at one below the
left endpoint too many occurrences of the flag would violate the
upper count, and at the right endpoint too few would violate the
lower count. This proves that the prefix bounds fully describe
the originally permitted flag placements, including the effect
of the complementary successful records.

For the \(k\)th failure position \(p_k\), compile the constraints into
\[
  1+\max\{r:U(r)<k\}\ \le\ p_k\ \le\
      \min\{r:L(r)\ge k\}.
\]
The two sequences of endpoints are nondecreasing in \(k\), so an
matching that covers these virtual failure records can be
sorted without losing its occupied positions. If any prefix bound
is inconsistent or the virtual failure records cannot all be
matched, the source law is inconsistent. The encoding preserves
the feasible sets of failure positions, not the identities of the
actual sensor records; a witness can be expanded by the separate
sorted assignments above.

\paragraph{Optimize on that exact condensed input.}
For the maximum, build one bipartite graph of all virtual failure
records and the anchor positions not already failed. Its maximum
matching plus the anchor failure count equals the worst possible
union count. Any realization yields a representative failure for
each newly failed position, giving one direction. In the other,
extend the chosen edges within each stream to a full match of its
virtual records. Augmenting paths preserve every previously
occupied position, though the matched record may change.
The sorted expansion recovers real successes as well as failures.
This works with arbitrarily many streams at a fixed offset.

Both extremes can also be computed by a prefix dynamic program
for a fixed number \(K\) of other streams. The state is the
vector of \(K\) failure counts so far. At each position choose
one flag per stream, advance those counts, reject states outside
their prefix bounds, and add one to the score iff some flag
(including the anchor's) is failure. There are at most
\((n+1)^K\) states per position and \(2^K\) candidate flag vectors.
The replay uses this as a direct exact comparison to the single
matching for the maximum.

If one unknown scalar offset is shared by the non-anchor streams,
partition its allowed interval at all pair eligibility endpoints,
checking boundary singletons separately. The same graphs hold
throughout each open cell. Optimize per cell, then take the
extrema over feasible cells. There are \(O(Kn^2)\) cells, so the
maximum remains polynomial in both \(K\) and \(n\). Distinct
unknown offsets per stream need a joint cell treatment; this
argument supplies no polynomial bound in \(K\) for that model.
Nor does it give such a bound for the minimum with variable \(K\).

\paragraph{Executed separation.}
Anchor stamps \(0,1,3\), zero offset, and error bound one:
the first stream's successful stamps \(0,1/2\) must fill the
first two positions. Its failed stamp \(2\) can therefore
only occupy the last position. A second stream's failed stamp
\(3\) also occupies that position. The exact worst count is one.
If the successful records are simply discarded, the first
failure is also considered possible at anchor stamp one, and
the apparent worst count becomes two. Compiling their constraints
instead of dropping them gives the exact saving in the representation.

The replay checks 440 fixed-offset and 90 shared-offset inputs,
including inconsistent inputs, against sorting-free permutation
enumeration and whole-assignment clock interval intersections.
Only synthetic acquisition laws are exercised. No claim is made
about clock calibration, IID sampling, actual room data, or
full R4 acceptance.
