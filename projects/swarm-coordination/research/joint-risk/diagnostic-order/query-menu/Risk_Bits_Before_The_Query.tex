\title{The cost of sending mission risk before knowing the next query}

\maketitle

\paragraph{Result.}
For \(d\) distinct equal-sized candidate bundles, all larger than
the diagnostic order, there are fixed mission laws with identical
low-order reports and all \(2^d\) possible admission menus at one
common risk cap. Thus exactly \(d\) bits may be necessary and sufficient
for a deterministic fixed-length handoff of exact admission answers
before the receiver chooses its query. The result can include
a prior exposure in every bundle. It does not bound a dialogue
in which the next query is identified to the reporter in advance,
or a conservative controller allowed to reject some safe requests.

\paragraph{The queried decision.}
Let \(X\in\{0,1\}^n\) be a hidden vector of potential failures
drawn once from a fixed law. If the receiver selects \(S\), the
mission failure event is \(\{\exists i\in S:X_i=1\}\).
Every \(S\) in the menu contains the same earlier commitments
\(H\), whose physical exposure and original risk are retained.
The reporting actor knows this law in the specified finite family;
the receiver knows the family and all named marginal reports
of order at most \(k\), but not the law.
Neither observes a realized failure before choosing admission.
The sender cannot condition a message on the later selected \(S\).
There is no cap reset, per-site adversary, or unknown law change.
This is an information lower bound, not a technique for obtaining
the law or a safety certification of a deployed cap.

\paragraph{Independent choices at a single cap.}
For a nonempty set \(T\), write
\(\chi_T(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in T} x_i}\).
Use a density relative to the uniform bit vector,
\[
         1+\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{T\in\mathcal A}\sigma_T\chi_T(x),
         \qquad \sigma_T\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal A\) is the collection of \(d\) candidate sets
of size \(m>k\). Each density is at least \(1/2\),
and integrates to one. Summing out all but any set of
at most \(k\) bits kills every perturbation: at least one
factor in every \(\chi_T\) is an unconditioned uniform sign.
This proves equality of the entire named marginal assignments,
not just of the all-failure moment in one pool.

On the all-clear event for \(S\), the uniform expected
character is zero unless \(T\subseteq S\), in which case it
is one. Since all candidates have size \(m\), only \(T=S\)
contributes to the no-failure probability for \(S\).
If the exposure window is active with a known probability
\(\rho>0\), and all indicators are zero outside that window,
the original mission risk of \(S\) is
\[
  r_S=\rho(1-2^{-m})-\frac{\rho}{2d\,2^m}\sigma_S .
\]
The active status is not observed before the decision. At the
single predeclared cap \(c=\rho(1-2^{-m})\), the sign independently
determines each admission bit. No two sign vectors can be given
one message: a query at a differing coordinate would otherwise
give the wrong answer for one fixed law. Sending the answer bits
once in a known order attains \(d\) bits. The same result holds
for any antichain with suitable query-specific baseline caps;
one common cap here uses equal sizes.

\paragraph{More than two calibrated levels.}
For any collection of distinct high-order query sets
\(\mathcal A\), possibly nested, use density
\(1+\sum_{T\in\mathcal A}\alpha_T\chi_T(x)\).
Restrict each \(\alpha_T\) to \(M\) equally spaced levels
between zero and \(1/(2d)\). Then all densities are valid,
and the lower-order reports still agree. At a query set \(S\),
the no-failure probability under the active law changes by
\(2^{-|S|}\sum_{T\in\mathcal A:T\subseteq S}\alpha_T\).
In increasing inclusion order, after scaling each output by
\(2^{|S|}\), this is triangular with diagonal one. Thus different
coefficient vectors have different risk query vectors.
For each differing coordinate there is a rational cap between
the two risks. Allowing the declared rational cap menu makes
every pair distinguishable, without changing the law or choosing
a cap after seeing a realized outcome. The sharp worst-case
fixed-length message is \(\lceil d\log_2 M\rceil\) bits.
A compact enumerated coefficient index achieves it; successive
subtraction recovers the coefficients from the risk values.

The same triangular rank shows why, if unrestricted real-valued
laws and linear population summaries are the interface, \(d\)
additional independent linear statistics are needed to answer
all those risk queries exactly. Fewer has a null direction within
this full-dimensional coefficient family; small perturbations at
an interior point keep the laws valid. Sending the \(d\) exact
query risks is sufficient. This is a dimension claim about linear
statistics, not a claim that arbitrary real encoding uses \(d\)
machine words or has finite precision.

\paragraph{Stress checks and alternatives.}
The exact replay checks all 1,024 menus for six sites with
all labeled pair reports, one earlier committed site across
all candidates, and ten three-site choices. It uses
\(\rho=1/10\), cap \(7/80\), and distance to the cap
\(1/1600\) for each candidate in the construction. It also
checks all 243 vectors in a five-query nested example with
three levels and 29,403 distinguishing pairs. The checker
sums over labeled states rather than assuming the triangular
answers.

The perturbation uses the standard Boolean Fourier basis
(\href{https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/analysis-survey.pdf}{O'Donnell}),
and the response matrix is an ordinary subset-sum transform.
The result isolates the cost of one exact pre-query interface
at fixed evidence. An exact common-information planner ties
once given the encoded vector. With no further facts, a
planner guaranteeing safety across the entire ambiguous family
can reject every disputed candidate, but cannot claim to know
the individual law's safe menu. If the query can reach the
reporter and only admission is required, returning a single
bit for that query suffices given the same information at
the sender. The margin in this construction shrinks as the
menu grows, so a weaker precision requirement may allow
compression. No measured communication, field probability,
or full R4 acceptance follows.
