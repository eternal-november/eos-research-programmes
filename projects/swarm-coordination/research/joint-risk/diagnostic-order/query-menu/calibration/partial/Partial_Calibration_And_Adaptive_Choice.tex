\paragraph{Result.}
In the fixed-cap admission family, reading at most one candidate
bundle per calibration episode costs order
\(d^3\log(d)/\rho\) episodes to recover all \(d\) admission bits
with fixed whole-menu error at most \(1/4\). The lower bound holds
even for adaptive choice of measurement and with the active-window
flag supplied. This is a factor \(d\) above
\href{../Calibrating_An_Admission_Menu.tex}{joint full-vector calibration}
in the same family. Reading strictly fewer coordinates than a
bundle cannot identify the menu at all. The statements count
episodes, not per-coordinate sensing cost, and do not establish
whether real vehicles or experiments supply these measurements.

\paragraph{Same law and risk cap.}
Let the \(d\ge2\) distinct candidate sets \(S_j\) have size
\(m\ge2\) and contain every old commitment. Conditional on the
window being active, the density relative to uniform on the
potential-failure vector is
\(1+\epsilon\sum_j\sigma_j\chi_{S_j}\), with
\(\epsilon=1/(2d)\), signs \(\sigma_j\in\{-1,1\}\), and the same
single unknown sign vector throughout. An inactive episode makes
all coordinates zero, with known or unknown prevalence parameter
\(0<\rho\le1\) for active episodes. At the predeclared original
cap \(\rho(1-2^{-m})\), the admission answers are the signs.
If \(\rho\) is unknown only the recoverability of these signs is
asserted, not knowledge of that numerical cap or its enforcement.
All calibrated vectors are independent draws from this fixed law.
An experimenter chooses \(B\), \(|B|\le m\), before the current
draw, possibly using all previous data and independent randomness.
The laboratory thought experiment supplies \((X_i:i\in B)\);
activation-induced changes of outcomes are out of scope.

\paragraph{Projection splits the calibration.}
Marginalizing onto \(B\) kills every unobserved character.
At size at most \(m\), the only informative measurement is
\(B=S_j\), and it depends on the single sign \(\sigma_j\).
Even a visible window flag has a law independent of all signs.
If no such bundle is measurable, induction on the adaptive
transcript shows that every law produces the same transcript
distribution. Under the uniform prior the success probability
of any exact menu decoder is then at most \(2^{-d}\).
This holds even if enough separate episodes cover every
coordinate. The dependence needed by a query is within one draw.

\paragraph{Why adaptivity does not remove the factor.}
For the lower bound give away the window flag. A queried active
marginal distinguishing plus from minus has per-observation
KL divergence in either direction
\[
 D_{\mathrm{act}}=\epsilon \log\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}.
\]
The factor follows by summing over the two parity classes.
With the flag and inactive component, divergence per purchased
episode is \(D_\rho=\rho D_{\mathrm{act}}\). Non-bundle readings
have zero divergence. Take independent uniform prior signs.
Even under adaptive selection the posterior factorizes after
each transcript: conditioned on the independent random seed,
selection supplies the same factor for that transcript under
every law, and each outcome factor depends on one sign at most.

Let \(L_j\) be the posterior log odds for sign plus, \(u_j=|L_j|\),
and \(e_j=1/(1+e^{u_j})\) the conditional sign error. The best
posterior whole-menu success is \(P_*=\prod_j(1-e_j)\).
A procedure with worst error \(\delta\le 1/4\) must have
\(\mathbb E(1-P_*)\le\delta\). With probability at least
\(1-2\delta\), \(P_*\ge1/2\); on that event
\(\sum_j e_j\le\log 2\). Since
\(e_j\ge e^{-u_j}/2\), Jensen gives
\(\sum_j u_j\ge d\log(d/(2\log 2))\).

The true signed odds have expected sum at most \(N D_\rho\)
after \(N\) episodes. This follows by conditioning each next
log-likelihood increment on the true signs and the selected
measurement; the expected increment is one directional KL or
zero. Given the transcript the expected contribution of the true
sign at \(j\) is \(u_j\tanh(u_j/2)\), which differs from \(u_j\)
by at most \(2/e\). Consequently a fixed budget must satisfy
\[
 N\ge \frac{d}{D_\rho}
 \left[(1-2\delta)\log\frac{d}{2\log2}-\frac{2}{e}\right]
\]
when the bracket is positive. At fixed error and large \(d\),
\(D_\rho\) has order \(\rho/d^2\), so adaptive calibration still
needs order \(d^3\log(d)/\rho\).
For small \(d\) the explicit bound can be vacuous; the two-law
one-flip test yields a positive order-\(1/\rho\) bound there.
This is a hard episode budget; an expected duration is distinct.

\paragraph{Sufficiency without flags or exact prevalence.}
On a query for \(S_j\), pick any \(i\in S_j\) and let
\(W=(\chi_{S_j}(X)-\chi_{\{i\}}(X))/2\).
The singleton and bundle parities are distinct. Under the active
marginal the singleton is uniform and independent of the tilted
bundle parity. Inactive observations contribute zero. Thus
\[
 P(W=+1)=\rho(1+\epsilon\sigma_j)/4,\quad
 P(W=-1)=\rho(1-\epsilon\sigma_j)/4,\quad P(W=0)=1-\rho/2 .
\]
The sign of the sum estimates the admission bit without a flag
or knowledge of \(\rho\). At either sign, the wrong-sign
probability after \(N_j\) repetitions is at most
\[
 (1-\rho/2+(\rho/2)\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2})^{N_j}
 \le \exp[-N_j\rho/(16d^2)] .
\]
This follows by Chernoff bounding the signed sum at half the
log likelihood ratio of one nonzero outcome. Equal allocation
with \(N_j\ge(16d^2/\rho_{\min})\log(d/\delta)\) suffices
if a positive lower bound \(\rho_{\min}\) is supplied for
budgeting. With full observations all contrasts can be computed on
every episode, reaching the full-vector rate without knowing exact
prevalence either. Alternatively count nonzero outcomes rather than
raw episodes. Stopping each arm after
\(8d^2\log(d/\delta)\) nonzeros suffices by the same bound
conditioned on nonzero, with expected but not hard total
episode cost \(2/\rho\) times the total number of nonzeros
requested. Round to integers in both rules. There is no
uniform fixed budget as \(\rho\) tends to zero, and at \(m=1\)
the unflagged singleton subtraction does not apply.

\paragraph{Comparator and verification.}
This is a special thresholding-bandit interface; the
\href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/locatelli16.pdf}{thresholding
bandit work of Locatelli, Gutzeit and Carpentier}
already treats loss defined by any wrong threshold answer.
The added issue here is that one experiment can reveal several
joint exposures or merely one eligible marginal, and that a
centered contrast works despite unobserved dormant episodes.
No new generic bandit rate is claimed.
The replay checks all 420 projected single-flip comparisons,
30,720 exact ternary distributions, and full-posterior
factorization at every leaf of a history-dependent three-arm
policy. An exact flagged Bayes oracle also compares the optimal
adaptive and fixed allocations at equal horizon in that small
fixture; adaptivity improves its success probability, so the
lower bound is not a statement of identical finite-horizon
performance. Neither the lower bound nor the sufficient
sampler assumes observations of the full vector when the
budget restricts the experiment.
