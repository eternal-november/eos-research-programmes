\paragraph{Scope and obligation.}
An authority can tighten a risk cap on all causally relevant mission exposures, including irreversible old effects, once a qualified report has actually arrived. A proposed new operation must pass that cap and any independent whole-mission cap. A favorable report does not literally undo an old exposure. A probe may also accelerate physical isolation of an earlier effect; that changes which future exposure occurs and must be evaluated on the same history as the report. Distinguish setting the cap, receiving the usable report, physical isolation, actual contact, and settlement of any next operation. The examples are civil discrete inspection and sampling models; they are not empirical concentration estimates, calibration specifications, or permits to operate field equipment.

Let a latent label $i$ be drawn under one unknown prior $j$, fixed for the mission. Prior $j$ has mass $a=(1-w)/n>0$ on each label plus mass $w$ on label $j$, with $0\le w<1$. All source randomness conditional on the label is part of the physical time trace. The conditional source kernel may depend on the lawful policy (including which instrument layout was acquired) but not further on the prior index. This is a restricted model of ambiguity, not a general assumption of separability. For one actual report $h$ and a specified locally admissible policy, let
\[
L_i=\Pr(h\mid i),\qquad C_i=\Pr(h\ \text{and actual mission violation}\mid i),
\quad 0\le C_i\le L_i .
\]
Here the event includes applicable retained exposures and their true overlap with prospective exposures. Denote their sums by $B=\sum_i L_i>0$ and $A=\sum_i C_i$.

\paragraph{A computed effect-side test.}
The exact robust conditional violation probability is
\[
\max_j \frac{aA+w C_j}{aB+w L_j}.
\]
At a given authority cap $q$ this is equivalent to the signed test
\[
 a(A-qB)+w\max_j(C_j-q L_j)\le 0 .
\]
Each denominator is positive and uses the same law as its numerator. The index maximizing the latter expression need not be the one with the largest raw failure mass or most likely report. There is no separate worst posterior law selected for each half of a trace. If $L$ is held fixed, all these risk tests are monotone coordinatewise in the joint masses $C$. A cap on just the new work does not certify the same protected event as this union test.

\paragraph{An action can preserve the sensor likelihood and change the judgment.}
There are eight equally likely source phases within each label. An earlier contact at tick 0 has already occurred in phase 0 of label 0; neither acquisition action changes it. A pending older effect may cause a later contact at tick 2 in phases 1 to 6 if the line has not actually closed. The two modeled acquisition layouts use the same instrument report, green in phases 0 to 3 and amber otherwise. The first layout closes early in green phases and the second closes early in amber phases. The real mechanism is a two-outcome close time (tick 1 or 3), not the dispatch message. With no prospective transfer, each layout has the same marginal old exposure under every label and the same report likelihood; which pending contact is still incurred depends on the same phase as the message.

For $n=4$, $w=1/4$, and a possible new contact on phase 2, the conditional union risks on green are respectively $23/64$ and $55/64$. The half-risk cap approves one and rejects the other. Marginal old exposure multiplied by report likelihood would equate the two. Worse, in the late-close green layout the authority can refuse all new work and still fail because of the actual earlier effect. A model in which the field control is instead a prospective waiver is a different law, not a proved reduction in exposure.

Even separately knowing old and new conditional risks does not supply the union. In a third layout the old unresolved contact occurs in phase 1. Test a new contact concentrated in phase 1 or phase 2. Under green, both choices have identical marginal reports and old and new risks under every fixed prior, but old/new overlap differs. The two union risks are $23/64$ and $39/64$, again on different sides of the same cap. The model's fixed action-response map uses only the report in this comparison, never an unobservable phase or prior index.

An exact policy replay adds a whole-mission cap $3/5$, with conditional caps $1/2$ on green and $4/5$ on amber. It scans locally realizable deterministic policies: choose one layout before the cut, then a permitted dispatch or no dispatch after each authenticated report. Useful service means a new completed transfer without a violation, not just issuing the command. A positive robust useful completion probability as high as $57/128$ is attained in the example; no continuation can satisfy those particular caps under the late-close green layout. There is no adversarial reselection of the source law between reports. In a separate two-label example the priors weight labels 0 and 1 at $4/5$ and $1/5$ or vice versa, reports are equally likely and independent of the latent label, and the failure event in each report occurs only at its designated opposite label. One fixed law evaluates both leaves to a whole-mission risk $1/2$; separately the robust risk on either leaf is $4/5$. A per-report cap of $1/2$ therefore changes the obligation. Taking branchwise maxima and treating them as one sampled risk law would also overstate the ex ante bound.

\paragraph{What can be decided with incomplete evidence.}
If only $L_i$ and the unconditional old-event mass $R_i$ given $i$ are known, the true $C_i$ is in
\[
[\max(0,L_i+R_i-1),\min(L_i,R_i)].
\]
If the two known masses are instead $P_i=\Pr(h,\mathrm{old}\mid i)$ and $Q_i=\Pr(h,\mathrm{new}\mid i)$, the union's mass in that report is in $[\max(P_i,Q_i),\min(L_i,P_i+Q_i)]$. These are exact Fréchet bounds: allocate the two events in the common mass so that their intersection is at either feasible extreme. If the source law admits all such couplings simultaneously across labels, the two envelope endpoints give a trichotomy. If the upper risk passes, every coupling passes. If the lower robust risk fails, every coupling has some offending fixed law. Otherwise neither acceptance nor failure is determined by the supplied margins. The missing physical linkage is the relevant new evidence. No claim of independent behavior follows from the intervals; narrower coupled physical possibilities can improve them.

For a separate calibration class suppose each pair $(L_i,C_i)$ can vary with $L_i\in [l_i,u_i]$, $C_i\in[v_i,z_i]$ and the incidence condition $C_i\le L_i$. Admit the stated feasible sets jointly across labels for this report. A worst sharp corner for all $q\in[0,1]$ at once is
\[
 C_i^*=\min(u_i,z_i),\qquad L_i^*=\max(l_i,C_i^*).
\]
When $l_i\le C_i^*$, any $C_i-qL_i$ is at most $(1-q)C_i^*$; otherwise use $C_i\le C_i^*$ and $L_i\ge l_i$. The displayed pair attains these inequalities together. Since the law weights are nonnegative, this proves the original signed cap at the paired worst corner, not one fictitious world with impossible $C_i>L_i$. If the corner is the all-zero report, either the report is unreachable or its event numerator is zero and imposes no positive risk. If the physical calibration couples different labels or different report channels, the corner is only an outer bound unless attainability is established.

\paragraph{Sensor replacement and retained history.}
When the event is an old set of exposed labels $S$, rather than a random physical contact conditional on a label, $C_i=L_i{\bf1}_{i\in S}$. Its exact robust risk simplifies to $(a\sum_{i\in S}L_i+w M)/(aB+w M)$ where $M$ is the largest $L_i$ in $S$ (zero for an empty event). Enhancing a label outside the event cannot make the base conditional ratio worse; within the event the ratio increases with the report likelihood. Exposed sets of a common size can be indistinguishable at the prior cap, yet distinguishable after a legally relevant report. For example, use weakenable source tests with positive likelihood $\lambda/(\lambda+1)$ at one named label $q$ and $1/(\lambda+1)$ at every other label, $\lambda>1$. Both reports have positive probability at every label. Under the same late cap between the two posterior values, the test recovers whether $q$ lies in the irreversible old set. An ex ante fixed-width summary that supports every $q$ and every $k$-label set still needs $\lceil\log_2{n\choose k}\rceil$ bits; the set rank attains it. This is an application of exact membership, not a new communication complexity result.

For this fixed-event family, equality of normalized likelihood vectors $L/B$ preserves every conditional robust event test. Conversely, comparing all singleton events forces that equality since their robust risks are the injective function $x\mapsto(a+w)x/(a+wx)$ of normalized likelihood. This is exact relative to the full declared test family, not necessary for an arbitrary narrow receiver. If only a relative error guarantee is available and the propensity of the same fixed event is unchanged, keeping zero support and varying factors independently across labels in $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$ gives a worst new robust probability
\[
  \frac{(1+\epsilon)r}{(1+\epsilon)r+(1-\epsilon)(1-r)}
\]
from the old bound $r$, $0\le\epsilon<1$. The lower endpoint swaps the factors. The corner factors raise every in-event likelihood and lower every complement likelihood, extremizing the ratio for all priors in the same admissible family. Use it as an exact reachable margin only if the physical instrument admits the stated corners; if the action also alters the actual event, the earlier joint-mass test is needed.

These are source-side hypotheses for the \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/main/projects/contextual-composition/feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Residual_Profiles_and_Admission_Reflection.tex}{existing residual-profile criterion}: observe the numeric normalized weights, not just a repeated color label, and check that all consuming cap tests are reflected at a fixed duration, effect meaning and support. A changed accepted effect can reopen the profile even if all normalized report likelihoods agree. No full native transport result follows from an equal boolean decision at one cut.

\paragraph{Evidence and prior.}
\href{https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/740}{Robust POMDP game semantics} already separates static versus reassigned ambiguity and observation assumptions; \href{https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~dbackus/Exotic/1Ambiguity/EpsteinSchneider%20priors%20JET%2003.pdf}{prior-by-prior conditioning and rectangularity} supply the conceptual distinction. \href{https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mers/papers/Ono-Williams_AAMAS10.pdf}{Distributed risk allocation} is a useful conservative comparator for budgeted marginal constraints, not the strongest direct exact comparator for the known joint effects here. Direct finite same-law physical row enumeration agrees with the compiled checks. Normalization, margin bounds and exact scalar tests in this specified family do not supersede those approaches or constitute an empirical sensor claim.

The replays cover 3,600 posterior controls, 960 multiplicative replacement families, 2,200 row-level joint guards, 340 support boxes, 1,424 incomplete-claim given-margin cases, and 792 complete deterministic policy comparisons. Seven Lean kernels pass without sorryAx; the causal input timings and which side has an effective grant are supplied by the simulation, not by Lean. No held-out selection, R4 acceptance, published-planner superiority or real risk calibration is claimed.
