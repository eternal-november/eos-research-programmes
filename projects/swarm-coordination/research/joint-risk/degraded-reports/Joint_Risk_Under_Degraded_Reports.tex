\paragraph{Risk and interface.}
Two support crews provide two dispatches at ticks 3 and 4. A dispatch loses support exactly when both crews are unavailable, represented by state $(1,1)$. The physical loss is at least one such dispatch failure by tick 4, not an absent report and not a failure rate measured only among survivors. Dispatch times, actions, and service obligations are fixed. Ticks 1 and 2 are monitoring decisions, not dispatches. Their observed history contains the chosen communication mode and the actual received or missing event; it does not contain either hidden health bit. A fixed public policy first uses a shared channel and retries that channel after a miss, otherwise switches to a fair, uninformative channel. The channel action changes the observation likelihood only. In particular, withholding dispatch after a bad certificate is not a repair that meets this service contract. Claims are both at the decision time after the two reports and ex ante at tick 0, with the quantifier over a declared two-law family.

\section{When do local posterior certificates compose?}
Fix a reachable public history, its chosen action, and a predicted product law $P_{ij}=p_iq_j$ on the active rectangle $I\times J$ of positive row and column masses. Let $L_{ij}\ge0$ be the likelihood of a specified observed event under that action, with positive predictive event probability $Z$. Then
\[
P^{\rm new}=Z^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(p)L\operatorname{diag}(q).
\]
On the active rectangle, the two diagonal scalings are invertible, so the posterior is a product if and only if $L$ there has rank one, equivalently all $2\times2$ minors vanish. A nonzero nonnegative rank-one matrix factors as $uv^T$ with nonnegative factors: choose any positive entry as pivot and use its row and column to factor the matrix. This also proves that a nonzero sparse event must have rectangular nonzero support to preserve a product, and rectangular support alone is insufficient when positive entries have nonzero minors. A zero-probability event yields no posterior. A one-row or one-column active support makes the test automatic. Failure of a minor is an obstruction to maintaining the \emph{whole} posterior by separate marginals, not proof that every scalar risk needs the missing information.

With independent component transitions conditional on the actual public history and action, a product predicts a product. Induction therefore gives an exact repeated filter for any public-action policy for which the event test holds at every reached history, including censoring, survival and a decision-selection event if the estimate conditions on them. Conversely, starting from a product prior in this class, the first positive-probability failure of the test implies that the whole posterior at that prefix is not a product. Later resets can erase dependence; the test is not a characterization of models in which only one terminal posterior happens to be a product. Matching observations across agents with private information requires a separate accessibility argument; the calculation assumes genuinely shared evidence and known action, not private views treated as if public.

For binary crews, let $u=\Pr(X=1)$, $v=\Pr(Y=1)$ and $d=P_{11}-uv$. Then $d=\det P$ and $(u,v,d)$ reconstructs the entire posterior. For instance $P_{00}=(1-u)(1-v)+d$ and $P_{10}=u(1-v)-d$. Independent row-stochastic transition matrices $K_X,K_Y$ give prediction $K_X^T P K_Y$, so $d$ is multiplied by $\det K_X\det K_Y$. This is an exact interaction coordinate, not a memory saving against the three independent parameters of the unrestricted binary joint law. When a local transition has determinant zero it can erase interaction at prediction; this does not excuse losing interaction before an earlier consumed risk query.

\section{A temporal separation with equal local certificates}
Initially $X,Y$ are independent fair bits, with independent flips of probability $1/8$ at each step, in both laws. For the shared mode, the $+$ law reports missing with probability $3/4$ in equal health states and $1/4$ in unequal states. The $-$ law swaps these probabilities. Conditional on the current state and mode, the reporting randomness is fresh. One law is chosen before the run; no switching across histories or rectangular adversary is admitted. Flipping the second hidden bit on the whole trajectory transports the state and observation law of $+$ to $-$, but does not preserve the joint-loss cell. Global simultaneous bit complementation leaves each observation law invariant, so every positive report word in either law leaves both local unavailability marginals equal to $1/2$. The laws give identical probabilities to every report word of the fixed policy.

After two misses, the evidence mass is $73/256$ under either law. The interaction is $+25/146$ or $-25/146$. With these fixed marginals,
\[
\Pr(\text{loss at tick 3}\mid h)=\tfrac14+\tfrac9{16}d,\qquad
\Pr(\text{any loss at ticks 3 or 4}\mid h)=\tfrac{79}{256}+\tfrac{459}{1024}d .
\]
To obtain the second coefficient, predict to tick 3, remove its $(1,1)$ mass, then sum the surviving states' tick-4 mass sent into $(1,1)$ and add the tick-3 loss. Equivalently use the substochastic recursion below. Independent path enumeration checks both formulas for the report histories. After two misses, the next dispatch hazards are $809/2336$ and $359/2336$. The two-dispatch risks are $57611/149504$ and $34661/149504$. Their maximum is sharp for the declared two-law family. Multiplying the local marginals at the decision instead gives $79/256$ for the two-dispatch risk, below the illustrative threshold $1/3$ even though the $+$ law exceeds it. Retaining $d$ is sufficient and, on this pair at these exact marginals, losing its value loses the risk distinction. The ex-ante physical mission risk is $79/256$ in both laws: the action changes which evidence is obtained but not what the plant experiences at its two mandatory dispatches.

\paragraph{Survival is a different observation.}
A dispatch is not survived at $(1,1)$, so its survival likelihood is $1$ at the other three states and $0$ there. Its minor is nonzero. A uniform predicted law becomes uniform on three states after survival, with $d=-1/9$. Compute physical mission risk from the unnormalized survivor vector: predict and zero the failed state on each dispatch, then subtract total survivor mass from one. After the first dispatch starting from a uniform predicted law the survivor mass is $3/4$; among survivors the next risk is $5/64$, and the physical risk is $1/4+(3/4)(5/64)=79/256$. A conditional risk for a fixed pre-dispatch report word can instead be computed by carrying its unnormalized joint probability through the filter and dividing its killed mass by the word's probability. Never replace missing/dead paths by a survivor-only average.

\paragraph{What the evidence repair does.}
Two independently corrupted per-crew reports have factored likelihood and preserve the product filter from the start. Alternatively, in the two-miss branch revealing one actual bit at the second report makes that posterior a product even though the first update coupled the bits. Weighted over the extra reading the old risk is unchanged. This repairs representation/computability given the known law, not the plant, and it does not make later survival a separable update. The checker treats the extra reading as a stipulated truthful observation with no claim that the plant provides it for free. Without a new physical action or service resource, a failed uniform conditional bound remains a failure.

\section{Comparison and reproducibility}
\href{https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Boyen%2BKoller%3AUAI98.pdf}{Boyen and Koller} already identify loss of independence under repeated evidence and analyze approximate factored filtering. Here the rank test and binary interaction are exact elementary Bayes, and a joint forward model or appropriately factored Bayes checker gives the same result. \href{https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/provably_safe_multi_robot_coordination_with_unreliable_communication.pdf}{Mannucci, Pallottino and Pecora} derive a safety guarantee for delay and channel-dependent bounds under packet loss for a specified fleet coordination method. This pilot instead asks whether the local posterior certificates themselves suffice after selecting on shared reports. \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-025-10231-6}{Silva, Yu and Hsieh} plan temporal tasks with communication through rendezvous and a risk constraint; this pilot provides no motion plan, stronger guarantee for their models, or replacement for rendezvous planning.

The \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/e1348964f73704212d2d8b48d5982a5ed600a2ff/projects/contextual-composition/foundations/shared-world-native-assembly/Whole_Scope_Block_Decomposition.tex\#L30-L47}{whole-scope block decomposition} preserves a supplied joint law when grouping participant atoms. Applied only at this raw-law level, the two blocks are the health trajectories and the matching label is the actual public history; reassembly must retain their coupled posterior, including zero atoms. The decomposition does not force independence or derive either law. The risk proof here uses the Markov and likelihood assumptions separately. No proof-reuse or computational advantage over direct analysis is shown. This is a conditional C6 result for a finite observation and dispatch model, not a validated risk model, a safe new operating policy for all laws, or a closure of v0.5.

Run \texttt{python3 -B pilot.py --json | cmp results.json} with Python 3.12. Rational forward updates, direct world-path summation, compact-coordinate reconstruction, and the separate survival cases check the claims without simulation noise.
