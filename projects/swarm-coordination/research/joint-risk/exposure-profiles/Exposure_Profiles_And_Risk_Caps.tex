\title{How much exposure history must survive a change of risk cap?}
\date{24 November 2026}

\maketitle
\paragraph{Source and law.}
A civil inspection has \(n\) persistent defect modes, one for each site.
One hidden label \(X\) is sampled once for the mission and not resampled
at a handoff. Under law \(j\), each label has base mass \(a=(1-w)/n>0\)
and label \(j\) has additional mass \(w\), with \(0\leq w<1\).
The law is unknown but fixed; the guarantee must hold for all laws.
Visiting site \(i\) incurs a data-validity exposure that makes the
mission fail if \(X=i\). The outcome is not seen before the decision
studied here. Returning to the same site leaves the failure event
unchanged. The initial itinerary visits \(k\) distinct labels,
\(1\leq k<n\), held in one still-open inspection record.
All \(k\)-subsets are admissible itineraries, chosen without observing
\(X\). These are finite model stipulations, not empirical probabilities.

One report about the old exposure goes to a dispatcher, then a future
query \(q\) for one additional site is revealed. The sender does not
learn \(q\) before this report; the receiver has no other history-dependent
information. The interface requires exact admission for every permitted
query, not mere soundness with blanket refusal. The cap counts any mission
failure, including old exposure and the new admission together.

\paragraph{Exact rule and interface size.}
For a past site set \(S\) the failure event after accepting \(q\) is
\(X\in S\cup\{q\}\). For every nonempty \(U\),
\[
  \sup_j\Pr_j[X\in U]=a|U|+w.
\]
The bound holds under every \(j\) and is attained by any \(j\in U\).
No independent worst law is selected for past and future exposure.
At cap \(ak+w\), exactly the queries \(q\in S\) are admissible.
Distinct \(k\)-sets have different answers at some query in their
symmetric difference. Therefore they must have different states.
Encoding the rank of \(S\) among \(k\)-subsets attains the lower bound:
\(\lceil\log_2\binom nk\rceil\) fixed-width bits (97 at \(n=100,k=50\)).
This is classical exact one-way \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06257}{indexing}
on a fixed-weight promise, realized in a mission-wide risk interface.
If the source itineraries are restricted to contiguous \(k\)-site blocks,
there are \(n-k+1\) distinct states and the block start is a tight code
(6 bits in the same example). The gain comes from the restricted
itinerary family, not the number of open records.
If the query reaches the sender first, the sender can instead answer
that particular query with one bit, not counting the reverse query.

\paragraph{Revision with old exposure.}
At cap \(a(k+1)+w\) every singleton probe passes, so this verdict
requires no history-specific message. A later authorized cap \(ak+w\)
that covers old exposures restores the full membership requirement.
Notice before admission, plus the retained report, suffices to enforce
the new rule. Notice after committing a new \(q\notin S\) cannot
undo its physical exposure. A prospective exemption can preserve an old
permission but does not prove the tighter cap for total mission failure.

In the terminology of Contextual Composition's
\emph{Residual profiles and admission reflection} (the primitive-test
criterion), the declared factor is the admission test on local boundary
arguments \(S\) and \(q\). Under the old cap it has identical sections in
\(S\); under the tight cap those sections are the membership signatures.
With the identity on raw itineraries, this change fails test reflection.
No common adequate profile compatible with the previously retained constant
report recovers the new verdict. Declaring both caps before taking the
quotient keeps \(S\), so the same retained profile evaluates either
test. This is an instance of the criterion, not general native gluing
or efficiency beyond the direct set-tracking implementation.

\paragraph{Checks and bounds.}
\texttt{ExposureProfiles.lean} checks the mass formula, admission,
binomial cardinality, the state lower bound, and the cap-change test.
The replay enumerates the actual model laws on 18,324 finite queries.
Direct same-law tracking gives the same result. An additive per-visit
budget can overcharge a repeated site and is not the strongest baseline.
See \href{https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mers/papers/Ono-Williams_AAMAS10.pdf}{Ono and Williams}
for distributed chance-constraint budgets. This is a reference result,
not novelty or full R4. Observational updates about the hidden mode,
reversible exposures, and several concurrently proposed admissions
remain outside this result.
