\paragraph{Decision and evidence.}
The \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/417}{pinned guarded MRWS replay} supplies outcomes, not fault probabilities. Take its first notice delay $D=0$ or $1$ tick, visible before the recovery choice. A second critical stop $F=1$ can happen at tick 23, and a channel condition $E=1$ erases the second report if it is sent; otherwise it arrives immediately. When there is no fault there is no required report. $E$ is a defined potential channel condition, not something inferred merely from silence. Within the admitted family the source replays checked before recovery choice match in their actual history across the two arms. All choices preserve the native movement guard. The original operation must complete by tick 100.

Guarded discard completes at 93 or 94 in all four $(F,E)$ combinations at its delay context. Guarded retention completes faster with fewer new picks whenever it finishes, but misses the deadline precisely on $(1,1)$. The omitted tick-20 physical stop is a separate tested failure, not covered by either policy. This study's declared secondary objective is to maximize the expected fraction of retained-load choices, as a measure of use of the faster recovery. It does not turn fewer pickups in a failed run into a service benefit or measure field production utility. The chosen policy draws one coin with probability $\lambda_d\in[0,1]$ of retention after seeing $D=d$ and before learning $(F,E)$; no later switch is admitted. The cap is 0.1 ex ante for the same named deadline miss event, not a per-context or physical safety cap.

The historical protocol runs guarded discard. It preserves the known $D$ distribution and context-specific marginal frequencies of $F$ and $E$, from separate recordings. A second permitted recording can retain the pair $(F,E)$ but only pooled across $D$. The pooled recording mode is chosen before the new run learns its context; it retains no timestamps, trial keys, or companion output permitting a join back to context. Without that restriction, for example with linked 93/94 completion times from the same records, the conclusion would not apply. The archive used for the replay keeps full traces; its selective recording and probability laws are authored, not historical claims about MRWS.

\paragraph{Sharp bounds under one fixed law.}
Let $p_d$ be the context mass, $a_d=\Pr(F=1\mid d)$, $b_d=\Pr(E=1\mid d)$, and $x_d=\Pr(F=1,E=1\mid d)$. The four cells are $(1-a_d-b_d+x_d,b_d-x_d,a_d-x_d,x_d)$, so exactly
\[
 L_d=\max(0,a_d+b_d-1)\le x_d\le U_d=\min(a_d,b_d).
\]
Write $y_d=p_dx_d$, $l_d=p_dL_d$, $u_d=p_dU_d$. Pooling fixes $\sum y_d=z$, a single coupled constraint across contexts. The risk is $\sum_d\lambda_d y_d$. Its sharp upper bound is obtained by starting at $l$, distributing the remaining $z-\sum l_d$ in descending $\lambda_d$ order, with capacity $u_d-l_d$ at each context. Moving mass from a lower to an unfilled higher coefficient cannot lower the objective; the derived extremum is attained by the given four-cell law at every context. Reverse the order for the lower bound. Without the pooled constraint the upper bound is $\sum_d\lambda_d u_d$. This is one admissible law for the whole mission, not a separate worst draw at each context.

The pooled field is sufficient for a specific consumer when its coefficients $\lambda_d$ agree across all unpinned contexts, apart from constant contributions. Conversely if $\sum l_d<z<\sum u_d$, a feasible point can be chosen by giving every positive-width box the same fractional fill. At that point moving a little mass between two such contexts with unequal coefficients leaves the logged summaries unchanged and changes risk. Endpoint pool sums pin every coordinate. This instantiates the \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/f318c0ad68b17eef1ddeabaa46f4637efc927cba/projects/contextual-composition/feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Residual_Profiles_and_Admission_Reflection.tex}{residual-profile test}: the pooled quotient preserves the fixed common-action consumer, but it need not preserve an admission consumer that varies its recovery by delay.

\paragraph{An exact logging intervention.}
In the example $p_0=p_1=a_d=b_d=1/2$. In law A, $(x_0,x_1)=(0,1/2)$; in B it is reversed. Both yield the same context-specific marginals and the same full pooled four-cell table, with each cell $1/4$. Even adaptive choices of the allowed historical recording modes cannot separate the pair: conditional on the past and the next chosen mode, the fresh observation has the same distribution, so induction gives identical transcript laws. There is no claim about policies that observe cross-mode run identities or deliberately select a context before pooled recording.

With marginals only, the worst risk is $(\lambda_0+\lambda_1)/4$ and optimal retention share is $1/5$. With the pooled pair, $z=1/4$ and the sharp bound is $\max(\lambda_0,\lambda_1)/4$; the optimum is $2/5$ with both rates $2/5$. Conditional link evidence allowing A or B to be distinguished gives optimum $7/10$: always retain in the safe context, and use it with probability $2/5$ in the other. These optima follow from the bounds and proposed feasible policies, not from a grid optimizer alone.

Preallocate one optional audit slot for a synchronized diagnostic that keeps $D,F,E$ on the same trial. It records the actual stop and the channel condition from a pinned diagnostic operation, not merely an absent fault report. In the stated A/B family the conditional supports of $(F,E)$ are disjoint for either $D$, so one perfect linked reading distinguishes the two; no amount of the old logging can. This added channel and its opportunity cost are assumptions of the experiment. For arbitrary mixtures, a single read does not identify a population rate; knowing one exact conditional paired frequency would suffice with the pooled total and two contexts. Noise, induced traffic, missing source keys, or an audit that changes the future failure law require recalibration.

\paragraph{Comparison.}
The broad identification comparison is \href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v286/marmarelis25a.html}{Marmarelis et al.'s off-policy sensitivity model}; our finite box and pooled mass are different restrictions, not a reproduction of their dynamics. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0858}{Nitinawarat et al.} separate controlled collection from hypothesis testing, and \href{https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r199-jasa.pdf}{Balke and Pearl} give a primary precedent for sharp probability bounds by linear optimization. Here a directly enumerated fixed-law vertex solver agrees with the greedy risk check, and the direct likelihood decision agrees on the two exact alternatives. Four Lean statements verify the two-context sharp bound and retention caps. The incremental result beyond \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/398}{the earlier missing-pair study} is that even fully paired measurements are insufficient when pooled over a context the controller uses, with a sharp loss measured against pinned physical counterfactuals. This is scoped R3b/R4a evidence, not field risk or full contract acceptance.
