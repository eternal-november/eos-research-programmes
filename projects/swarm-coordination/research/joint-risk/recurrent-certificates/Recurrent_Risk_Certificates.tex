\title{Recurrent risk certificates across changing report access}

\maketitle

Two loading supports can have the same individual failure histories under two admitted laws and still need different use of a reserve. Their local certificates combine if indexed by a shared source of dependence and the dispatcher carries forward the surviving weights of those modes. A joint censor can break the rule even when all of those conditional marginals remain unchanged.

\section{Plant, evidence and guarantee}
A civil loading fixture owes three timed transfer attempts. In each slot the ordinary mechanism fails the service if both guides are unavailable. This is a missed dispatch in a fail-closed plant, not a prediction of injury, collision, or field reliability. A separately installed isolated support can protect the middle slot exactly; this consumes one optional support booking, reserved before the mission, and does not change the other slot laws or waive an ordinary commitment. No controller may decline a required attempt merely to improve its reported risk. Calculation of the event of any failure by slot 3 can stop at first failure without interpreting that truncation as a control action.

The calibration family fixes two installation orientations $Z=(Z_A,Z_B)$ through the mission. Under the aligned law $Z_A=Z_B$; under the opposite law $Z_A=1-Z_B$. In both laws $Z_A$ is fair. Choose one law before the run; it may not change after observing an event. Fresh guide failures $X_t,Y_t$ at different transfers and across the two guides are independent conditional on $Z$. For each guide of type $z\in\{0,1\}$ its unavailability probability is $q_z=(1+2z)/4$. Thus the two complete marginal guide-failure trajectories, not just their one-slot marginals, have the same laws across the aligned and opposite installations. The joint trajectories differ.

At the report cut before each attempt a public schedule selects $A$, $B$, both, or neither. Reporting guides send their own unavailability reading, flipped with probability $1/4$ independently given the actual bits. The selected reports are actually available to the dispatcher by that cut; the dispatcher does not infer them from a private reading. The main trace uses $A$, neither, then $B$. A received report is followed by loss of access and then access at a different guide. The scheduled mute is public and independent of current failure, not state-dependent missingness. A truthful initial comparator can measure whether the installation orientations agree and send the bit before the middle-slot reserve is committed. Neither private guide failure nor either separate mode bit is given to the reserve policy. The measurement and timely dispatch reports are additional modeled interfaces, not free consequences of calibration.

The reserve policy is judged by the probability of any physical service failure by slot 3, ex ante and uniformly over the two fixed laws, with cap $1/2$. The audit also computes the exact risk of future failure at each reached shared report history given no previous failure, under each law. Those suffix probabilities answer a different query; the policy does not promise that every suffix stays below the ex-ante cap.

\section{Composition at report and service cuts}
More generally, let $z$ range over finitely many persistent joint types, with separate initial weights under each law, local current-state/report kernels conditional on $z$, and fresh innovations conditional on $z$ and the actual common history. Conditional kernels may change with the announced regime and prior shared decisions. At a fixed $z$ the two state/report pairs must be independent; service survival need not be a product event. A shared gate retains masses indexed by the joint types, not two unconditioned posterior marginals.

Local certificate $i$ for an actually received observation $e_i$ supplies, for each $z$,
\[
 u_i(z)=\Pr(e_i\mid z,H),\qquad
 f_i(z)=\Pr(e_i,X_i=1\mid z,H).
\]
Here $H$ is the prior common history including survival. A scheduled mute gives $u_i=1$ and $f_i=\Pr(X_i=1\mid z,H)$. The gate carries $w_z$, the unnormalized mass for the current live history and that type. Any initial parity measurement updates by its actual per-type likelihood, under each law separately.

\begin{proposition}
The report branch has pre-dispatch mass $v_z=w_z u_A(z)u_B(z)$ at type $z$. Its current first-failure mass is $g_z=w_z f_A(z)f_B(z)$ if unprotected and zero if protected. The successor ledger is $w'_z=v_z-g_z$. Repeating gives the exact joint mission loss and exact conditional future loss on each reached live report history.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional independence factors the report probability and the joint failure probability together with the report. A reserve action taken on an actually available report is fixed on that branch; the protected mechanism has no failure there. Joint survival need not preserve independence of the current failure bits, but those bits are not hidden inputs to later guides after conditioning on the type and the relevant history. Survival reweights the type, and freshness supplies the next local states. Induction gives the exact masses of reached pre- and post-dispatch branches. First failure events are disjoint, yielding the ex-ante total. At a positive-mass report cut sum descendant first failures and divide by its reaching mass. A replacement report regime changes the next local factor, not the mass accrued through earlier history.
\end{proof}

With no reports, aligned hazards are $1/16$ and $9/16$. After one ordinary success the next hazard is $39/176$, not the initial $5/16$. Redrawing a mode independently at each cut gives three-slot risk $2765/4096$, not $2237/4096$. Multiplying unconditional local marginals instead gives $37/64$. The error is not always conservative: with the middle reserve installed, the marginal multiplication gives $7/16$, which passes a separate audit threshold $9/20$ while the true aligned risk $119/256$ fails it. That threshold is a negative control, not the main policy's cap.

The mode ledger need not be irreducible. If future probes are only no-report unprotected suffixes with constant hazards $h_z$, the exact statistic is total mass at each distinct $h_z$. For $k$ hazard values the tests of lengths $0,\ldots,k-1$ give $\sum_h w(h)(1-h)^t$, a nonsingular Vandermonde system. A normalized cut already knows the length-zero value. The statement assumes those probe lengths are available; a single fixed deadline may require a coarser statistic. If later reports or actions distinguish modes with the same hazard, the argument does not justify merging them.

\section{A received event that creates new dependence}
On an active product rectangle within a retained mode, a common event multiplies the predicted table by its likelihood $K(x,y)$. For positive event mass, posterior product holds exactly when $K$ has rank one on that rectangle, by invertible diagonal scalings by positive prior masses. This is the criterion of \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/350}{PR 350}, applied within a mode. Failure of the whole-posterior criterion need not invalidate every scalar bound.

In a negative control, replace public mute by an observed censor event $E=1$. At a retained mode with marginals $p,q$, compare
\[
 K_0(x,y)=1/2,\qquad K_1(x,y)=1/2+(x-p)(y-q)/2.
\]
Both are valid likelihoods for the event, with their complements for $E=0$. The event has probability $1/2$ in each mode and the same selected marginals $p,q$ under both kernels. Under $K_1$, the post-event joint failure probability is $pq+p(1-p)q(1-q)$, not $pq$. The difference is $9/256$ in every modeled mode. Neither the retained weights nor the locally selected marginals tell the kernels apart. For the contract's separate three-bit regression, independent censor $L$ and $L=1$ iff $X=Y$ give the same three pairwise laws, but joint failure conditioned on $L=1$ is $1/4$ versus $1/2$. The replay checks both regressions. Neither is an unannounced enlargement of the primary risk envelope.

One repair adds a certified joint event/failure likelihood for that slot, then uses the corrected survival weights in later slots. Another refines the event with a timely actual first-guide bit and calibrated second-guide law conditional on the refined event. The refined product then has one row. Merely revealing the bit does not certify an unknown censor kernel. These repairs restore the calculation; changing a certificate need not change physical risk.

\section{A service-resource intervention}
For the stated mission, one minus the mixture of surviving masses gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\text{no reserve}&\text{protect middle}&\text{protect middle iff orientations agree}\\\hline
\text{aligned}&2237/4096&119/256&119/256\\
\text{opposite}&1899/4096&87/256&1899/4096
\end{array}
\]
The no-reserve choice fails the uniform cap without initial joint information. Among unconditional deterministic choices the feasible rule always reserves. Even independent randomization before the mission requires probability $r\ge21/37$ of using the reserve: solve
$2237/4096-r(2237/4096-119/256)\le1/2$.
A timely parity measurement permits the stated contingent policy for both fixed laws and avoids the optional-booking cost in the opposite law. This is a per-law benefit, not a strict worst-case cost improvement over the best randomized policy. A parity report after commitment cannot implement it. The intervention changes actual resource use and loss, not just the estimated threshold. The aligned parity policy has a maximum reached suffix risk of $309/512$ in the main report calendar, reinforcing why a uniform conditional cap is not the claim.

\section{Comparators and scope}
The matched direct comparator uses both fixed laws, the same timed reports and reserve, and joint first-loss trajectory mass. It gets the same guarantees without a conditional factor representation. Exact independent path enumeration over both fresh bits checks all $64$ three-slot report calendars, both laws and three policies, including 7,182 reached report cuts, arrival and survival masses and descendant losses. The replay also checks the no-report statistic and both censor regressions. The generic rank criterion and one-off parity intervention are already in \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/351}{PR 351} and its predecessor; the increment here is the explicit recurrent latent mixture, survival accounting, and within-mode failure. No priority or proof-work saving is inferred.

\href{https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/provably_safe_multi_robot_coordination_with_unreliable_communication.pdf}{Mannucci, Pallottino and Pecora} derive a channel-dependent bound for precedence violations under packet loss and safety under delay alone on their model. This fixture instead has fixed service deadlines and a report event that can invalidate the evidence factorization rather than a precedence message. \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-025-10231-6}{Silva, Yu and Hsieh} use rendezvous in temporal task planning to maintain intermittent connectivity under a risk constraint. Here the report calendars are fixed and the charged intervention is a protected service slot. There is no rendezvous planner, field calibration, general independence under censored reporting, or stronger guarantee for those models.
