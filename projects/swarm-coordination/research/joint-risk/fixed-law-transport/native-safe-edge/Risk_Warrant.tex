\section*{A safe support edge that invalidates an old risk warrant}
\paragraph{Scope.} This is an R4 experiment on a serial harness running pinned
SubT broker dispatch and actual SQLite commits. Timing, packet format, reliable
path, common receiver and available sender roles are fixture assumptions.
The lower and upper receive caps also apply to individual transmitters, which
each send at most once and never bind in the focal case. No frequency is
estimated. The ambiguity set is all fixed laws on the generated support with
specified outcome marginals; it is not a claim about how the same upstream
law behaves when the cap changes. A deployed single-writer journal, a new
risk mechanism relative to an equally informed scheduler, and mission
acceptance are outside scope.

\paragraph{Source construction.} Time is in sixteenths of a second.
A one-byte fast report $Y$ may be sent at 0. A two-byte job $X$ may already
send at 1. Each may also be absent, independently in the enumeration of
\emph{possibilities}, without assigning probabilities to the four inputs.
The callback for $Y$ commits a named old job. The evaluator reads the durable
row, reopens the database, and pairs two executions from these same inputs:
one admits the optional two-byte packet $O$ at 17, one does not.
The policy may read whether $X$ arrived, not whether $Y$ committed, when
choosing $O$. A separate sender always dispatches the two-byte full old
report at 18; it is not available in the earlier slot. If it arrives,
the dock settles the named job unless the fast report already did.
A test message for an unrelated receiver still arrives at 17.
The serializer and possibility of one opportunity for $O$ are part of the
experiment, not features established for every broker client.

The pinned receiver counts the age-16 debit inclusive. Thus at cap 2,
if $Y$ arrived, $X$ cannot arrive; all three remaining output pairs
$(X\text{ arrived},Y\text{ settled})$ have concrete witnesses.
At cap 3 the history where both attempt gives the new pair $(1,1)$.
At 17, $Y$'s debit is gone but $X$'s is still live, so $O$ arrives precisely
when $X$ did not, if admitted. At 18 both earlier debits are gone.
Only $(0,0)$ incurs new old-duty loss on admitting $O$: the first report
did not settle the job, and the fresh optional packet blocks its full
retry. This loss mark is unchanged on every old edge, and the new edge is
safe. The old duty succeeds everywhere if $O$ is withheld. Moving its
dispatch one tick earlier can block it even when $X$ did not arrive;
moving it one tick later can also make the $(1,0)$ pair harmful.

\paragraph{Fixed-law consequence.}
Let $p,q$ be the two marginal probabilities of one and set
$t=\Pr[X=1,Y=1]$. The four masses in lexicographic order are
\[
 (1-p-q+t,\ q-t,\ p-t,\ t).
\]
For independent admission with probability $a_0$ on visible row 0,
the worst old-duty loss is $a_0$ times the maximum first mass. With cap 2,
$t=0$ and the asserted marginals must satisfy $p+q\le1$.
With cap 3, $t\le\min(p,q)$ and the upper endpoint is feasible.
At unchanged marginals feasible in both configurations, worst loss
therefore increases by exactly $a_0\min(p,q)$. For $p=q=1/2$ the old
off-diagonal law is forced and has zero loss; the enlarged graph admits
a diagonal law with mass $1/2$ on the \emph{old} harmful edge. Its
witness uses one newly admitted, harmless pair to balance the old one.
No independence assumption was added. Multiplying the half marginals
would already assign positive mass to an impossible old pair.

For an always-admit proposal, the old transport certificate
$u=(1,0),v=(0,-1)$ proves zero risk. Its constraint on the new safe pair
would read $-1\ge0$; any certificate with the new diagonal inequalities
has objective at least $1/2$. This invalidates the old warrant with the
source-supported edge itself, without assigning a new local damage mark.
The worst-risk budget $\varepsilon$ gives $a_0\le\min(1,2\varepsilon)$
after enlargement at the half marginals. Since optional completion is
$a_0/2$, the zero-tolerance guarantee drops from $1/2$ completions to zero
over these fixed slots; nominally admitting on row 1 cannot restore
completions. This is a service comparison against reusing a stale warrant,
not superiority over a new exact transport calculation. The latter ties,
and changing decision times, evidence, or packet formats is a different
interface. In particular a capacity increase does not force a field
risk increase: if the same post-broker law stayed supported on the old
edges and risk marks stayed fixed, the old certificate would still apply.

\paragraph{Verification.} The independent integer ledger matches 3,456
native replay rows and checks completeness of input enumeration.
The exact marginal calculation is separately checked by coupling
enumeration on 289 rational marginal pairs per focal configuration.
Lean checks the bound, feasibility at the maximizing endpoint, support
gap, and diagonal dual obstruction in integer probability units.
The replay exercises the broker's sampled accounting and the fixture's
journal, not an end-to-end radio, live robot, or independent mission.
