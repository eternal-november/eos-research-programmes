\paragraph{Result and stakeholder law.}
An exact old mission-risk ledger can forget a dependence that a lawful
prospective amendment needs. In this model the old any-failure risk is
the same for every local policy. So are the separate old and new failure
marginals under an unconditional dispatch. Nevertheless the proper
amendment requires a different service decision in two histories with
the same timely receipt contents. The old mission owner keeps its
original bound. The new beneficiary sets a separate cap on delivery
failure that still counts episodes where old work fails elsewhere. The new
requirement is thus not another estimate of the old mission event.
The result identifies exactly what must be retained for the declared
test family; it is not a field estimate for any robot or warehouse.

\paragraph{Timing and finite fixed-law model.}
Before optional admission, old work is irreversibly committed with
terminal loss event \(O\), of probability \(q\in(0,1)\).
There is one optional service opportunity named \(i\), chosen
before the hidden outcome is drawn. The dispatcher
either commits before the action cut or forgoes this service.
A designated authority either issues the new requirement before that
cut or does not issue it. This is a scripted experiment alternative
fixed before draws, not an actor free to choose the requirement after
seeing failures. In the amendment cases the receipt is either early
(\(T=0\)) or late (\(T=1\)); late is after admission but before
old or new physical settlement. Write \(p=\Pr(T=0)\in(0,1)\).
The controller's view at admission is an early authentic notice or
silence. Silence includes an issued order not yet read and the unchanged
authority case. The latent transport cohort \(T\) need not be
observable in the unchanged case. The new obligation, if undertaken,
cannot be withdrawn after admission; old exposure cannot be withdrawn
at any of these cuts.

Take \(T\) independent of \(O\), and allocate a hidden subset \(H_t\)
inside each cell \(\{O,T=t\}\), each of probability
\(s\), where \(0<s\le q\min(p,1-p)\).
A candidate fixed physical law is specified by a word
\(b\in\{0,1\}^m\). If option \(i\) is activated its new loss occurs
exactly on \(H_{b_i}\); without activation there is no new loss.
This is a model of potential outcomes, in which failure of two job
classes can share a cause and notice timing can covary with the
new hazard without changing either marginal. It is not an assertion
that reading the order itself causes failure or that a different
transport regime has the same joint law. The unchanged authority
case has the same physical potential outcomes, but all views at
admission are silence. Each physical law is fixed for the whole
experiment; it is not chosen anew in each cohort. In this design
at most one option is offered per scenario; simultaneous options
are outside the declared family.

A predeclared local randomized policy activates with probability
\(x_0\) on early notice and \(x_1\) on silence. Its seed is drawn
independently of the latent law's outcomes. The audit is ex ante
over the scenario under that policy, separately for each admissible
fixed law and authority alternative. It is not the conditional
cap after seeing the random draw, occurrence of \(O\), or a receipt
cell. Equivalently integrate over the hidden seed; no hidden
resampling of the physical law is permitted.
Under every lawful policy \(N\subseteq O\), so the original
union audit \(\Pr(O\cup N)\le q\) holds with equality; this
does not make the new delivery infallible.

\paragraph{Three different amendments.}
In an authorized prospective \emph{issue}-effective amendment,
the additional cap \(\gamma\) covers failures of the new commitments
entered after issue, including silent commitments entered before
actual receipt. The additional audit therefore measures
\(\Pr(N)=s x_{b_i}\). For a \emph{receipt}-effective amendment
that expressly exempts new commitments already entered before
receipt, the additional audit measures \(s x_0\) if \(b_i=0\)
and zero otherwise. Exempting the late cohort from the additional
audit does not delete its actual loss or the original audit.
These are separate lawful variants selected before results.
If instead the revised cap would cover all unresolved exposure
through settlement rather than prospectively scoped new work,
a tighter cap \(\gamma<q\) is impossible, with or without
timely notice or new activation. A new signed rule cannot remove
the inherited unavoidable event. The unchanged branch by design
requires only the original cap; its dispatcher still uses the same
\(x_1\) as when an issued order has not arrived.

\paragraph{Exact policy and service loss.}
Set \(\lambda=\min(1,\gamma/s)\), with \(\gamma\ge0\).
A counted cohort at an admitted law imposes exactly \(x_{b_i}\le
\lambda\); no other coordinate is constrained except to be in
\([0,1]\). Actual successful optional deliveries per offered
episode have expectation
\[
 U_b(x)=p x_0+(1-p)x_1-sx_{b_i},
\]
dispatched and not lost to the \emph{new} event. The separately
reported old mission measure remains \(q\); a success of this
optional operation does not erase a failure elsewhere. In the
unchanged authority branch successful optional service is
\((1-s)x_1\). Every coefficient of \(U_b\) is nonnegative, since
\(s\le q\min(p,1-p)\). Thus the componentwise greatest allowed
vector is optimal for every admitted law at once, even for
maximizing the worst case. This is a full comparison among
all locally realizable randomized policies having the stated view
and one choice, not a comparison to a selected preconfigured policy.

For issue-effectiveness, known \(b_i\) requires throttling only
its counted cohort, while uncertainty permitting both values
forces \((x_0,x_1)=(\lambda,\lambda)\). Under receipt-effectiveness
only \(x_0\) need be throttled when \(b_i=0\) is possible; all
other coordinates may be one. In the worked case
\(p=1/2,\ q=1/4,\ s=1/8,\ \gamma=1/16\), maximum worst-case
successful optional service under issue-effectiveness is
\(7/16\) without the bit and \(11/16\) with it.
With either law still possible, the receipt-effective alternative
achieves \(5/8\), and keeps successful optional service \(7/8\)
on unchanged silent episodes instead of \(7/16\).
This is a difference of authorized event scopes, not dominance
of one policy under the same operative law. Under issue-effectiveness
a known early-hazard bit lets the unchanged branch attain \(7/8\),
whereas a known late-hazard bit still caps it at \(7/16\) because
of the indistinguishable live order. A qualified law certificate
arriving only after admission cannot affect this policy using the
same local information. The certificate is not inferred from an
early receipt or purchased for free in a real mission.

\paragraph{Exact retained distinction.}
Even the full function sending a realizable policy to original
union risk retains no bit of \(b\). Also all \((O,N_i)\) joint
outcome tables under unconditional activation are the same,
since \(N_i\subseteq O\) and \(\Pr(N_i)=s\) in each law.
What goes missing is the \emph{joint mass of newly chargeable
loss with the receipt cohort}, here one location bit per option.
Against the prospective query for \(i\) with
\(x=(1,0)\) and \(\gamma=s/2\), admission is permitted if and
only if \(b_i=1\), under either prospective binding rule.
Thus all \(2^m\) words are contextually distinguishable and
any exact deterministic fixed-width summary, without a hidden
lookup of the distribution, needs at least \(m\) payload bits
for this family. The word itself suffices when the common masses,
authorized scope and notice mechanism are already pinned.
This does not prove a lower bound for a single permanently chosen
option, a different permissible query set, or a physically richer
family. Separate risk of newly covered harm inside old-loss
episodes cannot be reconstructed by subtracting risk already
charged to the original mission union.

\paragraph{Comparators and verification.}
\href{https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mers/publication_uploads/Publications/2013/ono_williams_blackmore/ppcdsubr.pdf}{Ono,
Williams and Blackmore} give risk allocation for joint chance
constraints. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04570}{Wang and Gros}
address recursive feasibility for a mission-wide probabilistic
safety objective. Neither is being asserted to use the lossy ledger
tested here. The negative control is defined by handing forward
only the old exact objective and the unconditional marginals.
The strong comparator is direct enumeration on each fixed
latent law with the same information, same received scopes and
same policy randomization. It agrees with the exact resumed
policy calculation. No generic advantage over a proper
continuation planner is claimed. Unlike the
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/357}{previous
reserve-lock study}, the new event is masked under even a
fully retained old risk function, and here the audit is over
the pre-choice policy, not a per-realized-action cut.

The exact rational replay covers 3,888 history-grid evaluations,
1,440 direct optimization comparisons on grids that attain the
stated cuts, and 3,586 later query decisions through \(m=8\).
The continuous bound and counting proof are not inferred from
finitely sampled rates. Lean checks monotonicity, enforcement of
a risk row and the distinguishing test. The mathematical
source is authored; no calibrated occurrence rates, hardware,
real authorizing procedure, independent field case, or complete
R4b disposition.
