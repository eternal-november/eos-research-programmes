\title{One fixed risk law across tree-separated evidence cuts}

\paragraph{Lift.}
An episode has one finite vector of binary potential physical
failures and diagnostic events \(X\), drawn from one unknown but
fixed joint distribution. The probability inputs are the exact
one-site and specified two-site tables on a known tree. Do not
impose conditional independence beyond the published constraints;
cyclic edges, calibration uncertainty, or changes of latent source
law are not silently discarded. At one authorized evidence cut
the bit \(O\) is the OR of specified diagnostic sites. The selector
uses that one observed bit to choose an allowed old/new work set
\(S_0\) or \(S_1\), with all old still-binding exposures in both.
A model-potential failure outside the selected set does not itself
violate the work law, but may still constrain the joint law.
This is a finite episode, no fresh risk budget or resampling of
the law. Availability, action authority and the observation map
are assumed and not inferred from statistical fits.

\paragraph{The finite extended formulation.}
For fixed declared policy and diagnostic set, assign to each node
a bit mask of the Boolean ORs that its failure bit contributes to:
the report flag, failure under each permitted continuation, and
any separately audited prospective-only event. Let \(S_i\) be
\((X_i,Z_i)\), where \(Z_i\) is the mask of OR outcomes in the
subtree. For each node and its children put a nonnegative mass
on local assignments \((S_i,(S_j)_{j\text{ child}})\), zero unless
the parent's OR recurrence holds. Normalize the root table.
For every child, match its whole boundary \(S_j\) marginal on
both sides. Require the specified one- and two-site tables
(the binary marginals and joint ones are sufficient). If separately
calibrated, constrain an observable root-flag marginal on this
same joint distribution.

The formulation is exact. Any lawful original distribution
induces these local marginals by deterministically extending each
atom. Conversely sample the root factor, then recursively sample
each child factor conditioned on the boundary already selected.
At positive boundary mass, consistency gives a normalized
conditional law for that factor, and induction recovers its
entire declared marginal. At zero mass the conditional is never
used. Every resulting atom obeys the deterministic OR relations.
Project out the flags to recover one admissible law on all original
sites, not a separate law for each observed report. The gluing is
a witness construction, not a physical tree-Markov assumption:
the augmented state holds correlated evidence. With a fixed number
of ORs and bounded branching, the extended formulation has linear
size in the number of sites. A reusable subtree can present the
polytope of feasible joint boundary distributions. Equality of
that polytope preserves its valid neighbors' feasibility queries;
it does not imply a bounded-bit representation or a way to acquire
the values in a blackout.

\paragraph{Three different admission questions.}
Write \(U_b=\bigvee_{i\in S_b} X_i\) and \(F=U_O\).
Under the original ex ante cap, maximize \(\mathbb E[F]\).
For a cap of \(\epsilon_b\) in every reachable observation cohort,
the exact condition is
\[
 \max_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_Q[
      \mathbf1_{\{O=b\}}(U_b-\epsilon_b)]\le0 .
\]
For \(Q(O=b)>0\) this is just the conditional bound after
multiplication by its positive denominator, and zero-support
cohorts do not contribute. A legitimately prospective amendment
can designate a different future event without removing any old
physical event or making the original bound disappear. That
requires an independent authority premise. Each score is a
linear functional of the same root flags and ambiguity set.
Adding separate worst conditional or joint terms can silently
allocate the same old exposure to two different cohorts.

\paragraph{Discriminating fixed-law witnesses.}
Let the tree be a star with fair center bit. Four leaves are
old failure \(X\), optional failure \(Y\), and two diagnostic
bits \(D,E\), all marginal \(1/4\), each with joint-one mass
\(1/8\) with the center. Only state \(O=D\vee E=1\) admits
the optional job; suppose the report probability is separately
calibrated at \(1/2\). The actual audited event is
\(F=X\vee (O\wedge Y)\). The exact worst original risk is
\(1/2\), by the union bound on \(X,Y\) and attainable when
their disjoint quarters occupy the two alarm quarters.
The best possible risk is \(1/4\), attained by placing
\(Y\) outside the alarm. The maximum quiet-cohort joint
failure is \(1/4\), achieved when the old quarter is quiet;
the maximum alarm-cohort joint failure is \(1/2\),
achieved when both work quarters are in the alarm.
Their sum \(3/4\) cannot occur under one law, even with the
same known report frequency. These examples retain every
center/leaf pair table by coupling the fair center independently
to these explicit atoms. The uniformly safe ex ante bound
\(1/2\) does not imply a conditional bound \(1/2\):
the worst alarm-conditional risk is 1. A cap \(0.4\) that
binds live old and new exposures rejects admission, while an
authorized future-only cap \(0.3\) on \(O\wedge Y\) passes:
its worst risk is \(1/4\). The original audit stays unchanged.
If notice is late after irreversible optional entry, the
full-law bound does not improve by observing the amendment
later. This is a timing interpretation of the scopes, not
a model of the administrative amendment protocol.

Dropping inactive *exposures* from a score is not deleting
their source nodes from the tree. In the three-node path
where both edges enforce equality of all bits and each
marginal is \(1/4\), the two outside counted failures have
worst union \(1/4\) even when the center is not itself
counted. Removing the center constraints admits disjoint
outside quarters and spuriously gives \(1/2\).
Even keeping separate probability intervals for failure
and for the boundary state, instead of their joint state,
cannot in general support a later conditional query.

\paragraph{Checks, comparisons and limits.}
The executable LP matches whole-law enumeration over
small source state spaces on 320 extrema and reconstructs
a single law from the factored solutions, verifying its
marginals, objective and constraints numerically. Larger
binary trees up to 127 sites check formula construction.
The small stated rational witnesses and pointwise bounds
are separate proofs; solver decimals alone are not
deployment certificates. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06321}{Padmanabhan--Natarajan}
already give compact tree-marginal LPs for thresholds on
a fixed sum. The present use is a few overlapping ORs,
evidence-indexed activation, and changing risk scope;
standard exact junction-tree lifts and a same-information
allocator tie. \href{https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mers/papers/Ono-Williams_AAMAS10.pdf}{Distributed
risk allocation} is an alternative where its chance-
constraint and budgeting interface applies; preserving a
whole-law coupling is the consumed premise here. No
field calibration, independent transfer or full R4
closure follows.
