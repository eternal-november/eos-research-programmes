\paragraph{Result.}
In a finite indoor-cart plant, two complete anonymous bearing frames determine the collision outcome of a timed crossing, despite a camera that merges simultaneous projections and never reports peer identity. A one-frame controller cannot match that service guarantee. Clipping or masks can make even the entire available image history insufficient. This is a statement about an explicit kinematic class, not performance of a physical detector or a general guarantee for flocking.

\section{Plant, obligations, and observation budget}

Two fixed, mutually separated parallel guideways cross an orthogonal conveyor's swept footprint. There are at most two other carts, at most one on each guideway. Their longitudinal coordinates are $x_j(t)=a_j+v_jt$, with $a_j\in\{-5,\ldots,5\}$ and $v_j\in\{-1,+1\}$. A loaded ego trolley approaches outside both guideways in the observation phase, can commit only at tick 0, and occupies both crossing points throughout $[0,2)$ when it commits. It clears and completes service at tick 2. If it waits, the docket expires; waiting is safe but is not service. A hit occurs exactly when any other cart crosses $x=0$ during the occupied interval. This abstraction models a trolley whose swept footprint reserves both crossing points for its whole maneuver. It uses point cart centers and an exclusive crossing line rather than finite physical dimensions, braking uncertainty, motion plans of peer groups, or an arbitrary entrance schedule. A schedule conditioned on an unknowable latent state is not installed.

The only peer sensor is one onboard synthetic binary silhouette row of 19 calibrated bearing cells. The projection maps both guideways to the same anonymous cell for a given longitudinal $x$; it can be viewed as a one-dimensional orthographic camera with no depth resolution. An unchanged ego approach moves the known viewing window center to $t$ at exposure tick $t$. The half-width is 2 or 12 cells. The optional known opaque screen masks cells $-2,-1$; the remaining visible silhouettes are OR-composited, so coincident projections do not reveal multiplicity. There are attempted exposures on the common period-1, -2, or -4 clock, within ticks $-3$ through 0. Delay is 0, 1, or 2 ticks. Only timestamped exposures which arrive before the tick-0 commit phase are readable, including arrivals earlier in the same tick. The only proprioceptive information used is the common optics calibration and known approach schedule. There are no symbols, markers, exact identities, active light patterns, radio channels, grants, or peer broadcasts. Peer motion is observed passively.

Both controllers get the same exposures, timestamps and view mask. The direct controller uses the latest angular occupancy stencil, taking every admitted motion consistent with that snapshot as possible. The tracker detects contiguous occupied spans and retains feasible anonymous trajectory associations across all arrived frames. Its finite consistency filter is compiled to an observation-key table before operation; the reference records the latent hypotheses, not an additional online observation. Neither controller scans the latent world during the run or chooses a different policy for each true scene. Both are implemented within at most 76 input pixel tests, four frame words, 276 precompiled rows, and 10 sorted-key comparisons including equality per decision. The track key has at most 16 integer span endpoints (as well as tuple boundary/length information); available resource limits are matched maxima, not measured wall time, equal actual resource consumption, or inference costs of a neural network. The single-frame policy does not need all these resources. A direct temporal occupancy history with the same admissible trajectories is a further comparison: span detection is invertible on this ideal binary raster, so that direct history gives the same partition and the same guarantee as the tracker. This checks that keeping temporal evidence, rather than a particular detector architecture, causes any advantage reported here.

\section{Exact observation boundary}

For any reached observation key $z$, let $\mathcal W(z)$ be the admissible trajectories inducing it. A deterministic local policy can commit at $z$ while guaranteeing noncollision iff no $w\in\mathcal W(z)$ touches the crossing during $[0,2)$. Sufficiency follows from the exclusive crossing law. If one compatible trajectory collides, the identical local decision in that trajectory makes any commitment unsafe. With a zero-collision guarantee, randomizing cannot enable a positive commit probability on such a key. The policy that commits on every all-safe cell maximizes service pointwise within the class of safe policies, not just for a selected distribution. An absent frame and a delivered blank frame are distinct at the interface, even though either can leave the status unresolved in a particular configuration.

There is an explicit two-complete-frame rule. Let $A,B$ be the sets of lit cells at distinct exposures separated by an integer $h>0$ with no clipping or masking of admitted positions at either exposure. Match $a\in A$ to $b\in B$ only if $|b-a|=h$. Any admissible world covers both sets with at most two such edges. If either set is a singleton, the relevant edge support is fixed by the other set; duplicates of one edge correspond to identical motion and cannot change occupancy. If both have size two, competing matchings would require all four edges. For distinct $a,a'$, the two possible neighbors $a\pm h$ and $a'\pm h$ have intersection of size at most one, excluding this. Empty images at both complete exposures imply no peer. Thus the support of possible velocity trajectories is uniquely determined (without identities or duplicate multiplicities). Swept contact is an existential property of that support. This proves oracle-equivalent admission for the no-mask wide aperture with at least two complete arrived frames. Two-frame sufficiency does not assert that every clipped or occluded pair requires refusal; the observation-cell criterion is exact in those cases.

One complete frame is not generally enough. At tick 0 a single cart at cell $-1$ can be heading away or toward the crossing, yielding the same latest image; only the latter hits at tick 1. Their wide four-frame histories differ, so the tracker safely serves the former while the one-frame controller must wait in both. Narrow viewing can erase this gain: a departing cart at $(a,v)=(1,+1)$ by itself gives the same four silhouettes as that same cart plus one with $(1,-1)$. The second cart is invisible at earlier times and merges into the same bearing cell at the decision, but it collides with the crossing trolley at tick 1. Neither anonymous tracking nor knowing that motion remains smooth can disambiguate these worlds. Counts of lit cells alone are not counts of neighboring vehicles.

The viewing relation really changes during motion. With aperture 2, two carts $(-5,-1),(-2,-1)$ are seen by the ego as the first only at $-3$, both at $-2$, then the second only at $-1$ and 0. These source labels are for the audit and never appear in the frames. In a separate two-cart witness the projections of $(-1,-1),(-1,+1)$ remain distinct through tick $-1$ and superpose at tick 0; the controller must not give a permanent identity to the merged component. An opaque strip can further erase useful observations, while longer delay alone need not matter if two complete earlier frames have arrived. Retaining observed trajectories is not a guarantee that a peer honors an undisclosed future commitment: allow an unannounced velocity reversal just after the decision and the same complete predecision history of the cart at $-1$ moving outward can now lead to crossing at tick 1. Both branches are indistinguishable at the decision; serving the formerly safe branch would violate universal safety in the enlarged envelope. This is a boundary in the plant promise, not an accusation of Byzantine behavior.

\section{Exact replay and comparison}

There are 276 distinct multisets of admissible trajectories, including the empty scene; 190 have no prospective crossing contact. These are counts in a finite model, not empirical frequencies. For each of 36 aperture, occlusion, sampling, and delay configurations the simulator exposes and delivers images, makes a decision from the installed policy, and checks continuously swept contact over each half-open tick interval. Separately, a rational-time calculation solves each peer's crossing equation and computes the observation partition. The two methods agree on 19,872 decisions. All admitted policies have zero collisions by construction, and the replay records misses separately. The two-complete-frame association check covers all six stamp pairs and all 276 scenes.

A no-arrived-frame control makes service incompatible with universal safety across this scene family. The near-side remedy is to recover a second complete frame before committing; if the mask or visibility still aliases a dangerous motion, only a different observation, a revised traffic guarantee, or a refusal is justified. More frames are not a cure for permanently indistinguishable histories. The results do not distinguish two exact same-motion peers from one, need not recover source identities, and cannot prove a new interaction law after trajectories change.

\paragraph{Established comparison and limits.}
The detector--tracker--controller separation follows the approach of \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01245v2}{Schilling, Schiano, and Floreano}, who used a real detector, a multi-agent state tracker, multiple cameras and a flocking controller. The component-span detector and exact bounded-kinematics filter here are synthetic comparators, not a reproduction of those experiments. \href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0711437105}{Ballerini et al.} infer topological rather than metric interaction from observed starling flocks; their data motivate local interaction, not exclusive reliance on sight or an enforceable crossing commitment. Keeping a coherent group and guaranteeing service under persistent obligations are different tasks. This paper proves the latter for one declared cart setting. It does not compare deployable perception systems or estimate a real field safety rate.
