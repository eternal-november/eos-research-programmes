\title{Native Warehouse Observations: Pose Aliasing and a Movement-Acquired Request Cue}
\date{2026-11-27}
\begin{document}\maketitle

\paragraph{Audit.}
Use the pinned RWARE warehouse source named in \texttt{SOURCE.tsv}.
Two possible initial positions of a civilian inspector share a
known layout and goal, but no trusted initial pose outside the
actual observation is provided. Reach the known bay in the
fewest guaranteed actions. The evaluator sees physical simulator
position; the decision procedure sees only its per-agent native
observation, retained actions, and the declared task/cue law.
There is no independent trial, hardware observation, or claim that
this initial ambiguity persists when odometry or initialization
provides a reliable pose.

\paragraph{Selected-source boundary.}
The harness executes the original selected
\texttt{\_get\_default\_obs}, \texttt{\_make\_obs}, float vector writer,
and original one-mover update bodies. The existing graph shim restricts
one \texttt{FORWARD} per tick; simultaneous conflicting motion is
not adjudicated here. The Gym shim supplies only the fixed flattened
vector length, and no model of sensor noise is invented.
The native dictionary path divides positions by axis extent when
\texttt{normalised\_coordinates} is enabled, then
casts the two coordinates to \texttt{np.int32}.
Two different interior locations can therefore both report \((0,0)\).
The flat path with the same option writes the fractions into a
float vector. Without normalization, both paths retain the tested
absolute locations. The source defaults to flat without normalization.
The claim is about the tested configuration, not every RWARE
observation interface, and flat/dictionary resource encodings
are not assumed to cost the same on a wire.

\paragraph{Opposite first moves.}
At positions \((2,3)\) and \((4,3)\) facing up with the goal
\((3,3)\) and an otherwise indistinguishable local crop,
the normalized dictionary exports exactly the same whole
observation. In two allowed actions, only right--forward
works in the first world and only left--forward in the second.
Even a future distinguishing observation after the first
action cannot repair a wrong first turn by that deadline.
The actual normalized float emitter distinguishes the worlds.
All 36 variants (three grid sizes, three sensor ranges, two
axes and two headings per axis) reproduce the same conditional
boundary: 144 paired observation comparisons and 1800
native action trials. Observationally selected flat controls
use 144 native steps and reach their goals.

\paragraph{Movement changes what is available to read.}
In the \(7\times7\) two-pose case, initially neither local
view includes the relevant shelf at the north end of its
lane. Turning in place does not uncover it. A northward move
lets the inspector read the current \texttt{shelf\_requested}
value from its local crop. With paired shelves on opposite
lanes and exactly one marked, the post-move crops differ
only by this flag: \emph{the physical placements are the same
under both queue qualifications}. The known binding from
producer to shelf lets the flag locate the inspector. With
both visible shelves neutral, or either marked shelf admissible
without a known binding, the flag does not have that implication.

The source event was exercised directly. The old carrier already holds
its requested shelf and moves into the actual native goal. That
updates the request queue before the observer samples it.
The fixture uses two reproducible RNG choices, one selecting
each next shelf, not a probabilistic reliability estimate.
At that instant the inspector's output is unchanged because
the new mark is still out of range. After its own northward
probe, the positive output in one pose/queue pair is identical
to the positive output in the opposite pose/opposite-queue pair.
So is the negative output in the complementary pair.
Without the old delivery the mark remains absent.
Under a known assignment calibration, the inspector actually
executes the observation-selected route and reaches the same
bay in five actions from the old-delivery cut, six clock ticks
including that preceding native delivery. The known calibration
is extra information about the cue's reference, not inferred
from it or available in the unknown-assignment arm. No shelf
delivery by the inspector is asserted.

\paragraph{Exact finite reference.}
For the sensing comparison the inspector is authorized only
to move, turn or wait; it cannot pick up a shelf to change the
test. The planner partitions possible native successor states
by the full available observation. A belief set can stop when
all its states have reached the bay; otherwise at budget \(j>0\)
one common action must send every resulting observation cell
to a belief that can finish in \(j-1\) actions. This is an exact
induction on the action budget, and it permits contingent
future motion rather than requiring a fixed pair of paths.
Native observations and transitions are same-cut in the finite
reference, with no relevant reward/info leak in the sensing motion.
There is no assumed early knowledge of either the true pose or
the chosen mark, except in expressly calibrated arms.

The minima are two with the flat location, five with the
collapsed pose plus a known request binding, and eight with
the collapsed pose and no usable binding; the latter value
holds even when the post-delivery queue always marks one of the
two shelves. Neutral paired shelves are a matched geometry control.
These are exact results for the selected support and policy class,
not on all warehouse reset histories. A trustworthy absolute pose
or a known request-source mapping are separate remedies, and the
same-information finite direct reference already exploits either.
No contract or deployment acceptance follows.
