# Blind selection against the frozen interface

Selection used only the frozen interface and baseline data. The state machines and source laws are synthetic. Expected results below are deductions from the published inequalities, not checker observations. Slots are numbered 1 through 6 from the start of the work window.

**Positive: early quota depletion, late batch gate.** The admitted setup records are (express dispatch, clean fixture), (buffered dispatch, clean fixture), and (buffered dispatch, obstructed fixture), each paired with either report-channel outcome. The setup population models a dispatch regime that admits only a clean fixture to its express configuration *before* the handoff; buffered configuration admits both fixture conditions. The condition of the modeled fixture determines seated versus partial; D never reads it. D's first symbol is its local count of already-queued tickets: express has three, buffered has two. The queue is populated from the dispatch setup, not from a latch probe. A local clock then tells D to settle the early quota, wait twice, arm its late batch, and open its gate. At the settle tick, D requests the remainder stored from its previous transition, so that identical symbol yields one or two depending on the prior queue count. One late pulse accounts for both late units.

The profiles are (3,1,0,0,0,2) in express and (2,2,0,0,0,2) in buffered; each has early/late work (4,2). The joint sums are (4,2,0,0,0,2) in express/clean, (3,3,0,0,0,2) in buffered/clean, and (4,4,4,4,0,2) in buffered/obstructed. Report receipt does not alter these sums. All six worlds should pass; taking a product of marginal profiles would invent express/partial and incorrectly fail at slot 1 (5). This positive witness relies on the expressly declared source support: admitting express/obstructed destroys compatibility, and the checker cannot validate the exclusion outside this synthetic generator. For this model, the two buffered cases share the identical local word across distinct H latch outcomes.

**Negative: an auxiliary retry armed by an inbox observation.** Independently enumerate an auxiliary D-ticket flag, a clean/obstructed fixture, and a received/missing report channel. Include all eight combinations, not only those with fitting requests. D's local timer starts and closes early work, emits a retry tick in slot 4, and starts and closes late work. D reads its own inbox in slot 3: a ticket arms its pending state; no ticket leaves D available. In slot 4 the very same local timer symbol emits a one-unit auxiliary request only if a ticket previously armed it, and clears the pending state. There is no H input, no inference from a grant failure, and no current or future H evidence. The auxiliary attempt is outside both protected D windows and receives no work credit.

The profiles are (2,2,0,0,1,1) without a ticket and (2,2,0,1,1,1) with it. Both fulfill the stipulated early and late duties within capacity when run alone. With H, only the two ticket/obstructed worlds should fail, both at slot 4 with aggregate demand 5. Their protected D work-window requests still fit pointwise; failure is simultaneous occupation pressure outside those windows, not inadequate D work or a change in H's policy. Removing the auxiliary pulse would repair this instance, but that is not the selected machine.

Both machines change control structure: quota memory plus batch gating in the positive case; pending-ticket memory and a timer-triggered retry in the negative. The selections establish neither the physical reachability of these source records nor correctness of any implementation or checker; no checker was read or run. The report-channel flip has no effect on this model's capacities but retains both of the specified report outcomes for each latch outcome.
