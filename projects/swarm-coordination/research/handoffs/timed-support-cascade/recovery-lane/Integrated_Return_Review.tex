\paragraph{Disposition.} The two warehouse workloads meet the written
cascade requirements at the declared simulated scope. No proof-work
saving, whole-plant equivalence, or readiness for physical operation
follows. This disposition does not amend either v0.5 document.

\paragraph{Reachability.} The \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/356}{first workload} combines a
permit delay with longer terminal dwell. In the second workload a
locally observed inspection jam triggers a required retry, which now
competes with the downstream handoff for the same lane. The affected
interfaces are the next crew's permission to start, the lane and named
acceptance that free the middle crew, and that crew's availability to
accept root relief. The no-conflict second prefix is actually reached
by previous settlement. A diagnostic intervention retains both
initiating edits and the retry but assigns it an extra independent lane;
this removes the delay in root relief and is not credited as a repair
at the original budget. Joint arms are deterministic, matched replay
schedules, not a statistical independence assumption. The fault
register covers the five operation groups, ordinary failure and the
recovery-induced chain, and states the conditions to recheck when
excluded mechanisms are admitted.

\paragraph{Repair.} At the declared deadline, the incumbent completes
14 of 16 cases in each workload and the preauthorised repair completes
16 of 16 in each. There are no refusals in either comparator; deadline
misses are recorded as misses, not safe service. The earlier issue
requires a signed order at tick $-1$ and spends an earlier opportunity
on the same channel, not an additional packet or support. It cannot
authorize an early response to a surprise order. Receivers use local
receipt, adjacent reservations and acceptance; the inspection crew
observes its failed start and its fixed permitted retry slot. The
resource rule checks the inspection use before terminal starts in the
same phase. Delayed authority and actual settlement give the
narrower-class earliest-finish obstruction. Outside the stated service
envelope, the interlock can still preserve support under its own ideal
assumptions, but the timed guarantee is not asserted.

\paragraph{Reuse and independent selection.} The first workload's
already frozen receiver proof is carried through the independently
selected bridge without changing its tests. The positive bridge and
ordinary target each serve 11 of 12 scenarios, preserving the adverse
cases as well as successes; the later gate serves 9 of 12 and first
fails primitive authorization reflection at tick 1, even though the
map image covers target residual classes. The bridge adds an explicit
one-place transport buffer to the selected source, not reserve to
either installed repair. Both proof routes assess precisely those
source and target plants. The second workload is an authored
materially different workload, not a second held-out selection; its
one-tick transfer analysis uses an explicitly narrowed subcarrier.
The two-workload branch of the integrated requirement does not
require the independent bridge itself to change the physical workload.

\paragraph{Independent replay and comparison.} A non-author reproduced
the original plant, receiver proof, transfer, and second workload, and
checked frozen selection evidence, locally executable policies and
scope. Each workload's event simulator agreed with its independent
reference on 96 cases. The least changed passing implementation sends
the same permit one tick earlier and leaves the receivers fixed. The
strongest direct proof combines the cached start rule with the
replayed authorization and resource reads and the same pending-grant
and next-tick acceptance invariant. It needs neither a full-product
reflection audit nor residual coverage to prove the pointwise timed
relief guarantee. That comparison does less proof work here, which is
not a failure of the transport result.
