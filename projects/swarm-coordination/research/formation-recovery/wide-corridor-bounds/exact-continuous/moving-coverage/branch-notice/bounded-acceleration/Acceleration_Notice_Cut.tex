\paragraph{Audit.}
Use the same two worlds and physical speed/separation/coverage
constraints as the preceding known-time notice study. The mobile
robot now starts at rest and also obeys \(|\dot v|\le\alpha=10\)
with \(|v|\le1\). Acceleration may switch without a jerk bound;
position and velocity are continuous. The analysis is for the
specific peak \(h=637/100\), the initial positions \(0,4\), and
the same horizon \([0,2]\), with physical coverage and radio
preserved throughout. Rest by time 2 is not required. The
only distinguishing input is still a truthful notice at the
declared time. This is a model contract on motion, not evidence
that a physical motor or message carrier meets these constraints.

\paragraph{A useful endpoint upper bound.}
For fixed start velocity \(v\in[-1,1]\), the maximum positive
displacement over horizon \(T\) is
\[
  P(T,v)=T-\frac{(1-v)^2-(1-v-\alpha T)_+^2}{2\alpha}.
\]
It is the integral of the pointwise velocity ceiling
\(\min(1,v+\alpha t)\); its maximizing trajectory uses that ceiling.
At reveal \(r<c=2/3\), the late branch must be back at or below 4
by \(c\), and the far branch must reach \(h-1\) by 2. For the same
position and velocity at \(r\), their available displacements
combine to the necessary bound
\(h-5\le P(F,v)+P(L,-v)\), for \(F=2-r\), \(L=c-r\).
Maximizing over the shared velocity gives \(F-L+\alpha L^2\)
when \(\alpha L\le1\) and \(F+L-1/\alpha\) otherwise.
Indeed the sum is concave with derivative
\(\min(F,(1-v)/\alpha)-\min(L,(1+v)/\alpha)\);
the maximizing \(v\) is \(1-\alpha L\) or \(0\) respectively
since \(F\ge L\). This is only an endpoint upper bound:
it drops the far branch's still-live overlap condition.
At \(r=16/25\) it already gives \(1508/1125<137/100=h-5\), although
the preceding speed-only controller can guarantee that cut.

\paragraph{The missing seam gives the sharp bound.}
Use
\[
 \sigma=4/h,\quad \eta=c-\sigma,\quad
 \delta=7-h-\sigma,\quad k=1-\sqrt{2\alpha\delta}.
\]
These are fixed by the model. Here all are positive, \(k<1\),
the post-far-seam horizon \(2-\sigma\) is at least \(2/\alpha\),
and \(1-k<\alpha\eta\). Suppose \(r\le\sigma\) and write
\(d=\sigma-r\), \(L=c-r=d+\eta\). At the far seam \(\sigma\) the
actual far-branch state \((X,V)\) must have \(X\le4\).
The terminal condition and the saturated-speed displacement bound
require
\[
 X\ \ge\ 4-\delta+(1-V)^2/(2\alpha),\qquad V\ge k.
\]
For the common state \((x,v)\) at \(r\), the far branch also obeys
\(X\le x+vd+\alpha d^2/2\) and \(v\ge V-\alpha d\).
The late branch's actual position at \(c\) is at least
\(x+vL-\alpha L^2/2\), so this latter expression cannot exceed 4.
Eliminating the common state gives
\[
 X+V\eta\le4+\alpha(d^2+2d\eta+\eta^2/2).
\]
The term \((1-V)^2/(2\alpha)+V\eta\) is nondecreasing for \(V\ge k\)
by \(1-k\le\alpha\eta\). The smallest compatible left side is
therefore at least \(4+k\eta\). Lean checks this algebraic
combination. The resulting necessary condition is
\[
 L^2\ge k\eta/\alpha+\eta^2/2,\qquad
 r\le r_a:=c-\sqrt{k\eta/\alpha+\eta^2/2}.
\]
Numerically \(0.604725<r_a<0.604726<\sigma\).
A controller feasible at any later cut beyond \(\sigma\) could
also run with an earlier notice just above \(r_a\) by ignoring it
until the intended time. Since that earlier instance has the
same two histories, this would contradict the obstruction.
The bound does not merely rule out learned policies that wait
at rest until receiving the notice.

\paragraph{Exact construction at equality.}
Hold at the initial position until
\(\sigma-5k/(2\alpha)\). Accelerate backward at \(-\alpha\)
for \(k/(2\alpha)\), coast at \(-k/2\) for another \(k/(2\alpha)\),
then accelerate at \(+\alpha\) for \(3k/(2\alpha)\).
The planned state at \(\sigma\) is exactly \(X=4,V=k\).
The minimum preparatory position is \(4-k^2/(2\alpha)\),
and the entire excursion before the far seam stays at or below 4.
At the cut \(r_a\) the robot is still on the third stroke;
the common motion is safe for both worlds up to the notice.
For far, keep that stroke then accelerate to unit speed and
coast. Its peak becomes exactly \(h-1\) because
\((1-k)^2/(2\alpha)=\delta\). After \(\sigma\) the far
left endpoint advances at slope greater than one, so the
near edge of the robot's footprint cannot overtake it.
The upper speed bound and equality at terminal time ensure
coverage of the far edge all the way.

For late, change to \(-\alpha\) at \(r_a\) and keep it through
\(c\). By equality in the eliminated condition the position
is exactly 4 at \(c\), with velocity \(k+\alpha\eta
-2\alpha(c-r_a)\in(-0.07,-0.04)\). Apply \(+\alpha\) to arrest the
small backward speed, then hold. Thus the robot stays at or
below 4 as the mandatory overlap begins. It never approaches
the lower gap, and before \(c\) its temporary forward excursion
is allowed because the stationary robot still covers the
whole required interval. The known constants put the start,
notice and arrest in their stated order; all clearance and
unit-speed inequalities allow the paths.
An earlier notice can be deferred to this cut. Both worlds
are therefore individually feasible even with bounded
acceleration, and this is a strict causal cutoff distinct
from the model's physical impossibility at larger far peaks.

\paragraph{Replay and residuals.}
The replay tests the general endpoint bound against exact
piecewise-derivative maximization on 1950 duration/acceleration
triples. It checks 53 rational reveal cuts in the sharp
example, with 16 constructive rational branch pairs and 37
analytic rejections. Feasible branch witnesses use a rational
upper enclosure of \(k\) and check the position inequalities
at exact quadratic extrema. The isolating interval brackets an
irrational formula, not a measured time error. The optimization
and direct matched reference gain no extra information from the
plant. This does not supply a usable observation channel, preserve
the same cutoff under bounded jerk, or solve arbitrary partially
observed acceleration-limited formations. No independent
mission or contract acceptance is asserted.
