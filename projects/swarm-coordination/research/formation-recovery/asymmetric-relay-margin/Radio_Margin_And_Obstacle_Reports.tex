\title{When an extra radio margin can replace an obstacle report}

A moving relay must stay linked to both fixed anchors while crossing
a blocked region. The preceding relay-lens study found the deadline
for learning which side is blocked when no route can cross blind.
Here, increasing the range of just one link makes a blind crossing
possible. It does not make that crossing as fast as knowing the
obstacle location. The two options give the exact arrival frontier
for the same service obligations.

\section{Model and result}
Use the speed-only model of the
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/396}{preceding study}.
The anchors \(P_\pm=(\pm a,0)\) have possibly unequal reach
\(R_\pm\), so admissible positions lie in the intersection of their
closed disks. The relay moves from \((0,-b)\) to \((0,b)\) at speed
at most \(v>0\). Exactly one open forbidden disk of radius \(\rho\)
is centered at \(C_+=(c,0)\) or \(C_-=(-c,0)\). Assume
\(0<c<\rho\le b\), \(c\le a\), and
\[
  R_\pm\ \ge\ \max\{\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\,a-c+\rho\}.
\]
Thus the endpoints and the shorter route in each known world fit.
The obstacle sides have identical observation histories until a
truthful report at the given time \(\tau\); a move does not itself
reveal the side. A policy may finish before \(\tau\). There is no
acceleration or packet-service model; link reach is a geometric
constraint. Touching a boundary is allowed.

Put \(h_*=\sqrt{\rho^2-c^2}\) and write \(L(h)\) for the shortest
known-world route from \((0,-h)\) to \((0,b)\). For \(h\ge h_*\),
define \(d_s=\sqrt{h^2+c^2}\) and \(d_g=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\); then
\[
 L(h)=\sum_{(d,z)\in\{(d_s,h),(d_g,b)\}_{\rm multiset}}
 \left[\sqrt{d^2-\rho^2}+
       \rho\bigl(\arctan(z/c)-\arccos(\rho/d)\bigr)\right].
\]
Define the two candidate arrivals
\[
 A(\tau)=\tau+\frac{L(\max(h_*,b-v\tau))}{v},\qquad
 B=\frac{L(b)+4\rho\arctan(c/b)}{v}.
\]
A common trajectory without a report exists if and only if
\[
                  \max(R_+,R_-)\ \ge\ a+c+\rho.
\]
In that case its earliest arrival is \(B\). With a report, the
exact minimum worst-world arrival is \(A(\tau)\) below that
range threshold and \(\min\{A(\tau),B\}\) at or above it.
In particular the second expression optimizes over \emph{all}
causal policies, not just two proposed controllers.

\section{Blind crossing}
At \(y=0\) the two excluded intervals overlap because \(c<\rho\).
A common crossing on the right requires \(x\ge c+\rho\), hence
\(R_-\ge a+c+\rho\); the left case requires the same of \(R_+\).
For sufficiency suppose the right crossing is available. Use
the two endpoint tangents and the right arc of the disk centered
at \(C_+\). The arc lies in \(x\ge c\): its two tangency angles
from the positive horizontal ray are \(\pm\beta\), where
\[
 \beta=\pi-\arctan(b/c)-\arccos(\rho/\sqrt{b^2+c^2})
 \quad\text{and}\quad 0<\beta\le\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
The upper bound follows from \(\rho\le b\).
On this arc the distance to \(P_-\) is at most \(a+c+\rho\);
the distance to \(P_+\) is at most \(a-c+\rho\). Convexity
then puts both straight segments inside the link region.
All three pieces lie in \(x\ge0\), where \(C_-\) is no closer
than \(C_+\). Thus they avoid both disks.

Any path in the right-disk world that crosses to the right
has total angular variation about \(C_+\) at least
\(2(\pi-\arctan(b/c))\): split at the right crossing, and
bound the variation of each piece by the smaller angular
separation of its endpoints. This argument also covers
paths that wind or cross repeatedly. With endpoint radius
\(d=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\), minimum encountered radius \(m\ge\rho\),
and this angular lower bound \(\Delta\), the radial inequality
in the preceding study bounds the length by
\[
  2\bigl(\sqrt{d^2-m^2}-m\arccos(m/d)\bigr)+m\Delta.
\]
Its derivative is \(\Delta-2\arccos(m/d)>0\). Take
\(m=\rho\); the tangent--arc construction attains the bound.
Reflection gives the same bound for a left crossing in
the left-disk world. The difference from the immediate
known-world distance is \(4\rho\arctan(c/b)\).

\section{Why a hybrid policy cannot beat both candidates}
The central policy advances vertically to depth
\(\max(h_*,b-v\tau)\) and then takes the short route indicated
by the report. It gives \(A(\tau)\) whether or not blind
crossing is feasible. The blind construction, when available,
gives \(B\).

For the lower bound, if the common prefix has already crossed
\(y=0\) at report time, choose the world with the disk on the
side of that crossing. The long-side length bound applies to
the whole trajectory in that world, giving at least \(B\).
Otherwise the position at report time lies strictly below
the axis. If its horizontal coordinate is nonnegative,
choose the right-disk world. If its subsequent path ever
visits the right part of \(y=0\), the same long-side bound
applies. If it first crosses the axis on the left, it must
have reached \(x=0\) below the axis after the report.
If the report position already has \(x=0\), use that position.
At the time \(t'\ge\tau\) of such a centerline visit, the
depth is at least both \(h_*\) by clearance and \(b-vt'\)
by the speed limit. The remaining route then takes at
least \(L(\max(h_*,b-vt'))/v\). Here \(L(h)\) is increasing,
and \(A(t)\) is nondecreasing by the triangle inequality
along the common vertical approach. The arrival is thus
at least \(A(\tau)\). The other sign is symmetric. If the
lens offers no blind crossing, only this final case can
occur since neither long-side tip fits. All bounds are
pathwise; a randomized policy cannot improve a requirement
that holds for every outcome of its random seed.

\section{Deadline and example}
For a deadline strictly earlier than \(B\) the report is
indispensable even if both links have arbitrarily large
range. For a deadline at least \(B\) the report can be
omitted once the reach threshold is met. Below the reach
threshold a deadline is attainable exactly when the report
arrives early enough that \(A(\tau)\) meets it.

For \(a=3,b=3.5,c=0.5,\rho=1.75,v=1\), radii \(R_+=R_-=5\)
admit each known-world short route but no blind crossing.
Increasing either radius alone to \(5.25\) allows a blind
arrival at \(8.453063\), compared with \(7.459783\) if the
report arrives at time zero. For a deadline of \(8.25\)
even this wider lens still needs notice by time \(2.278280\)
(see replay for numerical values). These are model values,
not radio, energy, field, or hardware measurements.
