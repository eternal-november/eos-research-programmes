\section*{R6: jointly bounded braking and terminal heading after a second fault}
\paragraph{Material requirements.}
A civil differential-drive rover is already stopping. A further
fault weakens one wheel and changes the owed terminal heading.
The controller must bring both wheels to rest by step five and
stay within a cumulative travel budget. The positive alternative
allows steering toward the replacement heading while braking.
This is a path-length limit on the rover center, not proof of
clearance in an obstacle mesh, link maintenance, or safe handling
of a payload. Wheel radius is one, spacing is two, initial forward
wheel speeds are four, and commands are applied at one-second
boundaries. No reverse, reacceleration, slip, or automatic
plant-dependent clipping is admitted. Both wheel speeds are
linearly interpolated between boundaries, so their integrated
distance and bearing change are exact in this plant. Before a
truthful diagnosis the same issued and realized wheel profile must
be lawful in both worlds. An actuator that instead clips requests
would change what is controllable and observable.

\paragraph{Source command cut.}
The \href{https://github.com/gazebosim/gz-sim/blob/9b1151a9c1f5fe6d1af4c6e0d33d1f0992e8a811/src/systems/diff_drive/DiffDrive.cc}{pinned Gazebo DiffDrive}
limits body forward and angular command scalars separately.
The paired target then equals \((v-\omega,v+\omega)\) at the chosen
normalization. With independent per-axis step bounds of one, a
command from \((v,\omega)=(4,0)\) to \((3,1)\) passes those two
limits, but the actual paired target is \((2,4)\). That requires
braking the left wheel by two, beyond a weak-wheel bound of one.
A coupled admissible target \((3,4)\) is available at
\((v,\omega)=(3.5,0.5)\). The fixture uses the actual source target
and joint writes through the staged updates; it separately verifies
the old write before the new target is consumed. The numerical
physical bounds and plant following are assumptions, not properties
of the native joint setter. The observation deadline below is at a
usable input cut, with subsequent scheduled write, not at message
receipt if computation or a physics cycle is still owed.

\paragraph{Exact split between physics and information.}
The two physical states have brake bounds
\((1,2)\) (owed straight) and \((2,1)\) (owed a one-radian turn).
Let \(X_L,X_R\) be twice each wheel's path length. Arc length is
\((X_L+X_R)/4\); the doubled difference \(X_R-X_L\) is zero or four
respectively. With state known, the exact minimum arc lengths are
eight and seven. The weak wheel must travel at least eight
distance units, and the heading then forces the other wheel's
minimum distance in each state. Samples attaining the bounds are
replayed. A common profile before a notice at steps \(0,1,2,3,4,5\)
has minimum worst-case arc length
\[
  8,\quad 8,\quad 8,\quad 9,\quad\text{infeasible},\quad
  \text{infeasible}.
\]
In particular the arc-eight case needs the notice by step two;
increasing its distance budget to nine permits step three.
Below eight, the straight state fails even with oracle information.
At step four both end speeds must go to zero in the one remaining
linear interval, producing the same heading change in both worlds.
A later notice cannot repair this.

\paragraph{Sharp real-valued lower bound at step three.}
Both prefixes are common and each wheel can have braked by at most
one per step. If \(p,q\) denote the contributions to \(X_L,X_R\)
excluding only the step-four speeds, then \(p\ge16\). With
\((x_i,y_i)\) the chosen left/right speeds at step four in world
\(i\), the final heading equations are
\[
 q+2y_0=p+2x_0,\qquad q+2y_1=p+2x_1+4 .
\]
The weak-wheel final tick implies \(x_0\le1,\ y_1\le1\); both
other speeds are nonnegative. Subtracting the equations forces
\((x_0,y_0)=(1,0)\), \((x_1,y_1)=(0,1)\) and \(q-p=2\).
Consequently both worlds require \(p+q+2\ge36\) in the scaled
sum. The two checked profiles attain 36 with a shared prefix.
This bound holds even at real-valued sample velocities in the
declared interpolation model; the finite grid is not the source
of the gap.

\paragraph{Comparison and residual.}
An exact state-based backward check and independently enumerated
individual wheel profiles agree across 5,280 integer cases.
Eight boundary profiles produce 40 checked native paired joint
target writes; Lean checks the scaled arithmetic. An ordinary
joint actuator allocator using the same bounds, information and
time sees the same frontier. No general advantage over that
allocator follows. Independent scalar authority, actual actuator
feasibility and late diagnosis remain different contents of a
replacement certificate. Physical tracking, sensor production,
an independent mission, and full R6/I1 are not established.
