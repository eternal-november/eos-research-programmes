\paragraph{Pin and comparison.}
R6 asks for non-waivable formation invariants jointly with bounded
restored service, not merely a path or a graph at sampling instants.
First run the published
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29939v1\#S3.SS2.SSS3}
{managed make-before-break controller} on its unchanged two
point-mass examples and inspect all four departure cuts. The original
controller concerns an intentional departure. Neither it nor
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19912}{Geurts et al.'s connected
one-hop contract MPC, Definition 1 and Theorem 1}, promises to
initialize on the disconnected abrupt-loss request poses of the
prior pinned study. That theorem needs initial compatible trajectories
and preserves a connected contract graph at its ordered discrete
replanning instants. It supplies a maintenance reference when those
premises hold; it does not certify a continuous evidence exchange.
\href{https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016003223003046}
{Maeda, Endo and Matsuno} address recovery around a stopped robot.
The \href{https://en.endo-lab.jp/research/detail/499}{authors' description}
confirms local neighbor motion, pre-failure direction, and wait/avoid
behavior. Their exact control equations and faithful crash replay
are not supplied by this experiment.
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01354}{Breeden and Panagou's
input-constrained barrier composition} is a distinct viability
comparison: an admissible instantaneous acceleration alone cannot
prove the required residence and subsequent service.

\paragraph{Measured in the published simulator, not over RF.}
For each departure use the exact set of edges about to be removed
and the exact set just added by bridge activation. The source
sets a nominal one-unit switch dwell. Between recorded activation
and recorded departure both edge families remain established for
one time unit in each of the four runs. For the native
constant-acceleration segment with relative acceleration $a$ and
duration $\Delta$, relative position differs from its chord by
at most $\|a\|\Delta^2/8$. Thus the minimum chord distance minus
that margin bounds separation, and the maximum endpoint norm
plus the margin bounds edge distance. In 5,600 pair-segments
the least separation lower bound is about $0.76610$ at source
clearance $0.45$. Upper bounds for the named links are below the
respective nominal ranges $1.6$ and $1.65$ in all four intervals.
These are floating-point checks with strict slack (not interval
certification of decimal input). Geometric coexistence and
established-set membership are checked separately.

An authored two-message service contract opens a request only
while both link families are authorized, and requires a matching
callback before their joint availability ends. This spends a
known residence duration $h$ (the example selects values, not
measured packet latency). If the final callback and departure
have the same time, ack-first permits the effect and release-first
does not. A lost reply or wrong identity cannot close the
handoff even with identical geometry. At an abrupt initial loss,
the old link premise is already false, which is not a violation
caused by any controller. The test does not identify this service
contract with an implementation in the published KTH source.

\paragraph{An exact bounded-acceleration obstruction.}
Consider a relay on $(x,0)$, $0\leq x\leq1/4$, with
monotone speed $0\leq v\leq1$, acceleration bound $|\dot v|\leq1$,
actual initial speed $u$ at $x=0$, range $5/4$, and clearance $1/3$.
Fixed old and new endpoints are $(-7/8,3/4)$ and $(1,3/4)$.
A third vertex $(1/16,3/2)$ connects those endpoints; the failed
body $(1/16,-1/2)$ is physically present but not a graph vertex.
The old and new ranges overlap exactly on $[0,1/8]$.
All admitted trajectories on the track are collision-free,
and the surviving geometric graph stays connected. The task,
however, needs both links continuously for $h=1/4$ from entry,
a matching timely acknowledgement, and advance to $1/4$ by time
$1/2$. The range definition is closed and the equality witness
consumes the acknowledgement while still at the old geometric
endpoint under an ack-first convention.

For acceleration bound $A>0$, every trajectory has
$v(t)\geq\max(0,u-At)$. Braking at $-A$ immediately, then holding
at rest if needed, attains that lower envelope and the least
advance $D(u,h)=u z-Az^2/2$ for $z=\min(h,u/A)$. It follows that
geometric residence is possible exactly when $D(u,h)$ does not
exceed the overlap width (provided the track has no further
constraints). This is both a full-information obstruction and a
constructive existence argument; observing more cannot rescue
an already impossible entry. At $A=1$, $h=1/4$ and width $1/8$,
the critical entry speed is $5/8$. At unit speed even immediate
maximal braking advances $7/32$ by the required callback. At
$5/8$ the policy decelerates for $1/4$, hits the old endpoint
at $3/8$ speed, then accelerates for another $1/4$ and reaches
the target. A $1/2$ entry speed and constant motion give a second
attainment. The minimum one-dimensional advance for braking
before entry from speed $1$ to $5/8$ is $39/128$; first acquiring
a useful report exactly at the entry line cannot supply that
distance.

No spatial grid or coarse sample is used for this claim. The
independent direct comparator integrates a general piecewise
affine minimum-speed envelope and agrees on all 2,288 rational
checks. Lean checks the exact entry threshold and braking
arithmetic, not the full ODE or original simulator. Only the
stated speed profiles and geometry are guaranteed; the
residence threshold does not by itself guarantee the separate
advance deadline for every lower initial speed. The scheduled
protocol and scalar model are author-selected, not an independent
mission. The local crash comparator and general bounded control
from the four published disconnected poses remain open.
