\paragraph{Audit.}
A route index known only to the mover can select a later
site reply. The extra information comes from an issued
movement that the site operator can observe before replying,
not from decoder confidence. Under complete independent
support, an early byte and a late byte can answer a requested
bit of a 24-bit site word with two lawful observable endpoints,
but at most 16 independent bits with one endpoint. The second
endpoint can be illegal with a tight carried physical duty.
Also, if a site readout is unacknowledged, a reply encoder
may need to distinguish histories across different site readouts.
Two small, separately sufficient codebooks need not combine
when the mover does not know which one the site used.
This is scoped R1/L2 development rather than a field or
independently selected non-radio transfer.

\paragraph{Deterministic complete support.}
The site initially knows $x\in\{0,1\}^K$. It sends $p$ bits.
The mover privately learns $j$ later, can physically produce
one of at most $2^q$ reliable, timely feedback symbols, and
then receives $r$ bits from the site. It must output $x_j$.
The early packet must reach both protocol participants'
respective state (sent/received as appropriate), and each
participant knows the agreed codebook. No timing, silence,
actor identity, variable length, or unpriced extra signaling
conveys additional information. All $x,j$ combinations are
admitted here, and each selected feedback symbol must be
legal wherever the mover selects it.

The exact capacity in the fully feasible, reliably observed
product regime is
\[
                  K\leq p+2^q r .
\]
For a fixed early symbol let $F$ be its source fiber. Within $F$
the feedback partitions the indices into at most $2^q$ groups.
In one group, two source words giving the same reply must agree
on every index of the group. Thus the group's projection of
$F$ has at most $2^r$ members. The product of the projections
injectively represents each source word in $F$; hence
$|F|\leq2^{2^q r}$. At most $2^p$ fibers cover all $2^K$
words. To attain the bound, send up to $p$ coordinates early,
divide the rest into groups of at most $r$ coordinates,
use a feedback symbol to select a group, and send its bits.
An early-known index need not consume a new decoding decision;
the fixed length reply may still be sent. This is a variant
of the familiar communication problem INDEX, not a claim
to the one-way communication bound or graph coloring as
new techniques (cf. \href{https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/on-randomized-one-round-communication-complexity/}{Kremer, Nisan and Ron}).

\paragraph{Finite guarded support, and who observes the readout.}
On an arbitrary support of $(x,j)$, an exact synthesis
procedure partitions the site words into at most $2^p$
early classes. For a fixed class $F$ and index $j$, choose
a feedback action that is legal in \emph{every} admitted
world $(x,j)$ with $x\in F$. The action cannot depend
on the hidden $x$ beyond the early message. For each
feedback symbol, make a characteristic graph on site
words: an edge joins two words when some index assigned
to that feedback is admissible for both and needs different
answers. Exactly a coloring with at most $2^r$ colors is
an implementable last reply. Necessity is equal-view decoding;
sufficiency is taking the unique needed bit in each nonempty
class of receiver view and color. This specializes
\href{https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1055607/}{Witsenhausen's
zero-error graph criterion} to the task-relative output and
includes an earlier action-authorization search, not just
late code design. Knowing which words occur, or which
endpoints occur marginally, does not determine the joint support.

For nondeterministic sensing at the site after a fixed feedback
policy, vertices instead include $(x,y)$, where $y$ is the
actual site-side output (including an admitted erasure).
Edge formation uses exactly what the mover sees. If it knows
$j$ but not $y$, vertices with distinct $y$ can conflict.
Only a timely qualified acknowledgment can put $y$ in its
decoding view and remove such conflicts. In the two-bit
control a site-only bit splits four words into two locally
one-bit-codeable classes; unacknowledged, the combined
response graph nevertheless needs four colors. Based on
the same support, a direct encoder/decoder enumeration agrees.
No feedback or acknowledgment is silently added to the
same-information comparison.

\paragraph{Pinned optical and radio controls.}
The \href{https://github.com/AprilRobotics/apriltag/tree/94be783968e5091bcc9972c72c84fd63efce2935}{actual
AprilTag detector} sees the same printed tag at one of two
authored aperture positions. A current authorized frame
can deliver the group. A blank frame, different tag, certified
old epoch, or arrival after the reply cut cannot. The same
pixels in the latter two controls still decode successfully;
epoch and time are separately provided provenance.
This does not say that a tag authenticates an operator or
verifies a mechanical release, that a real lens sees that
scene, or that passive anonymous detections would support
a named duty. The aperture positions and metadata source
are authored, not output of a physically validated camera.

Native SubT receives the actual early and late one-byte
application payloads with independent endpoint callbacks.
A missing eligible group suppresses the late packet in
the implemented protocol, and an erased late packet spends
transmitter bytes without a receive debit. An output already
supplied in the early byte does not need to be reconstructed
again. Timing and absence supply no extra answer value.
These are byte-level simulator checks, not claims about
physical framing overhead, cryptographic authentication
or reliability in a cave. The site reply is a function
of its word and its own readout only, not of the hidden
route index. The mover decodes with its own index and
received bytes. The late policy is evaluated only on declared
timely qualified observations for the constructive rate claim.

The tested old collision resource reuses PR 596's pinned
warehouse replay, not a completion inferred from tag decode.
In the authored coupling, issuing port 1 imposes the old
one-quantum hold: physical clearance changes 18 to 19 or
19 to 20, under a deadline of 19. It is the issued move,
not the success of seeing it, that costs existing service.
If it is forbidden for the entire tight-old product family,
an envelope with two bytes but only one legal feedback
symbol admits at most 16 independent queries. No assertion
is made that every real route source is independent, or
that outcome bits are available in those supports. The
two simulator traces are joined under a declared scope law.

\paragraph{Verification and next loss boundary.}
There are 174 guarded synthesis comparisons, 29,808 exact
cross-readout encoder comparisons, ten native optical frame
controls, and 8,640 native packet cases including negative
controls. Eight additional staged calls hold the actual radio
process open after the early byte, decode the later generated
frame at the site, and then submit the qualified reply. The direct
counterparts agree. The sampled native
runs do not exhaust all 24-bit inputs; the proof covers the
declared product. Decoder equivalence and side-information
refinement were checked in Lean. The remaining R1 question
is a separately frozen, independently selected modality
substitution with source-backed authority, clock and physical
budgets. Presence of two test media does not discharge it.
