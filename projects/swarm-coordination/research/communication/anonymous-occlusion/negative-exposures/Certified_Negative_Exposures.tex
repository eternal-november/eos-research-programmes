\paragraph{Audit.}
Follow-up to the anonymous two-disc motion model. The left and right
positions are observed anonymously at the two boundary cuts. Between
them, an orthographic camera supplies binary occupancy of a calibrated
closed cross-section $[-w,w]$ along the passage coordinate. On a qualified
exposure, every permitted robot center in the cross-section is guaranteed
to generate a detection, anywhere across the transverse passage.
The proof also requires the correct exposure time, not arrival time,
and actual availability at the decision cut. A known absent frame,
a late frame, and an unqualified decoded empty frame are not negatives
used by this certificate. The scene is a synthetic civil raster with
idealized observability; there is no claim of this calibration or
qualification guarantee for real cameras.

\paragraph{Sharp gap test.}
If a continuous path from the left to the right occurs between negative
exposures, consider its last entrance to the closed slab before its
next exit to the right. This passage lies strictly inside one gap
between consecutive validated negative exposures, since a robot
cannot occupy the closed band on such an exposure. Its duration is
at least $2w/V$ by the coordinate speed limit $V$. Therefore if the
maximum certified exposure gap is at most $2w/V$, neither robot could
cross, and the absolute names carry through the anonymous anchors.
These conclusions do not require detecting appearance, linking track
velocities, or estimating the global elapsed outage. Equality is safe
for a closed detectable slab; if detection is guaranteed only in the
interior, it is possible to be exactly on the opposite boundaries
on the two samples and equality no longer suffices.
If an allowed gap is strictly longer, the sharp counterfamily, under
adequate approach/settle time, consists of two robots crossing side by
side on separate lateral paths. Start just outside either side, move
with speed $V$ through the band, and use the opposite lateral lanes
with enough fixed clearance to avoid each other. The other validated
exposures can occur outside the band. Return to the swapped visible
positions outside the slab. This is not a claim that every layout
admits such a crossing. When a lane is physically incapable of passing,
that is a separate no-exchange certificate.

\paragraph{Resource budget and clocks.}
For a mission gap of length $T>0$ with two known-outside anchors and
a failure-free, arbitrarily schedulable exposure mechanism, at least
$\lceil TV/(2w)\rceil-1$ internal qualified negatives are required to
obtain this certificate, by summing the gap bound. Equal spacing
attains it, conditional on the frames actually being negative.
For a periodic camera of period $h$, at most $r$ consecutive discarded
or unqualified scheduled exposures, and independent timestamp errors
in $[-j,j]$, the maximum possible gap is $(r+1)h+2j$.
A common additive offset cancels, leaving $(r+1)h$.
The given local bound assumes preserved order and anchored endpoints.
Rate drift, different cameras used at the endpoints, and aliasing
between adjacent episodes are not common offsets. Two negative
half-view images cannot be treated as one full negative without a
shared exposure or a valid motion-and-coverage proof. The general
comparison is an exact speed-and-observation reachability argument,
not an unconditional cadence heuristic.

\paragraph{Matched raster control.}
Render two identical passive silhouettes. Horizon 2, half-width of
detectable band $w=3/5$, speed cap 1, observations at 0, 1, 2.
With an intact negative middle exposure the maximum gap is 1, so
the naming is sound. There is a continuous swapped trajectory with
a prep and recovery outside the band and a center separation at
least $3/10$ throughout. Its middle full exposure detects the
bodies. Drop that sample and the same remaining frames and anonymous
anchors are compatible with the stationary and swapped trajectories.
An opaque mask on that exposure gives a fully decoded zero raster
that exactly matches a healthy empty raster, but the independent
coverage condition does not hold. The binary detector alone cannot
tell them apart. This comparison is modeled source ancestry, not
field identification of an actual optical failure.
The rasterized guaranteed band follows from the projected center
and a nearest lit pixel for this ideal diffuser, not from assuming
perfect recognition beyond the mask.

The clean replay checks two constructed plant histories, 161 exact
rational path samples, 168 timestamp comparisons against a direct
extreme-pair check and 150 placement cases. Four Lean statements
check the interval obstruction and clock arithmetic.
\href{https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109815003490}{Yin and Lafortune}
give a general dynamic-observation control problem, not the stated
speed-and-projection model. An equally informed direct observer that
uses the same reachability and timing facts obtains the same guarantee.
A recovered name still does not authorize a pending physical effect.
This is held-in R1/R6 calibration, not the independent non-radio
substitution or a camera performance claim.
