\title{Partial salvage that preserves a live order}
\maketitle
\paragraph{Lift.}
The inherited obligation is one completion of order \((8,7,6,5,4,3)\),
by tick 12 at the existing station, while a separate local inventory
unload goes ahead. The model uses one ingest port and initially only
append actions at the station. Operator control of stock motion, a
station withdrawal interface, guaranteed field latency and
disposal of inaccessible inventory are not in the main envelope.

\paragraph{Native counterexample.}
An initial fault has stranded classes 7 and 6 on A. Their replacements
join preexisting class 8 at ticks 5 and 6. At tick 7 the native LIFO
partial transfer of one item from B's inventory \((5,4)\) takes 4,
not 5. Delivering it to the station fulfills a narrow truthful local
claim: class 4 was taken into station custody under the grant then in
effect. At tick 8 B's critical fault strands class 5; the authorized
replacement can pick and carry it, fulfilling the acquisition handler's
separate physical claim. Jointly the handlers fail the order: 5 cannot
follow 4. The native station first empties the donor, then throws on
the monotone-rank check, so the failed entry is not an identity
transition. The obstruction is the missing continuation condition for
the first append, not false detection or failure to pick class 5.

\paragraph{Continuation law.}
At a station that takes a decreasing sequence until exact multiset
completion, a decreasing prefix is structurally extendible iff its
multiset is a submultiset of the required order and no missing rank
exceeds the final accepted rank. For an empty prefix use an infinite
final rank. Necessity follows from monotonicity and exact completion.
For sufficiency, append the multiset difference in decreasing rank;
all its elements fit. This proves a language test, not inventory
availability or arrival by a deadline. Tied item ranks do not
identify item names. The replay compares this rule with all decreasing
permutations in 6,897 small cases with multiplicities and ties.

\paragraph{Declared positive family.}
The same partial operation can put 4 in a named separately authorized
buffer at tick 7, without appending it. Declare both an available and
an unavailable transfer at that tick and both a truthful tick-9
local read and a missing read, followed by the same B fault at 8.
The successful buffer effect remains in custody through the tick-11
use. With four pickups available and a due tick of 12, all four
declared maximal cases settle the order and finish the side unload.
When actual named custody is verified on time, only three pickups
are used. Otherwise pick the fourth item. A cap of three restricts
the positive scope to the timely positive observation: absent a read,
the same local view can hide the history with no buffered 4; that
history necessarily needs 7, 6, 5 and 4 newly picked. For the model
with no other eligible suppliers this is a lower bound on every
safe hard-service action, not only on the tested selector. The
time bound uses stipulated append release at 12 for C's class 3,
not the speed of the simulator. The qualification of the
custody read, available station and buffer port times, and
dedicated side operation are modeled, and do not follow from a
battery notice or an item message. The model does not claim that
a failed transfer always costs no time; the null arm has no
completed salvage, and the declared window has no competing
task that uses that slot.

\paragraph{Comparable alternatives and costs.}
Buffer custody costs one set-down and, if used, one retrieval in
addition to the local read. The no-read policy may leave the
buffered 4 behind as a residual instead of claiming it was consumed.
After a premature deposit, an authorized compensating station pop
of 4 before trying 5 also works with three pickups. The tested
compensation includes the original deposit, withdrawal and retrieval;
it employs the inherited native pop but expressly adds a grant
not supplied by the tested normal station workflow. Thus the
append-only impossibility is not an impossibility in all control
interfaces. The frozen admission kernel uses the exact pickup
debits, qualified custody fact, both deadlines and cut-local view.
The direct rule built from the same multisets, observations and
costs gives the same result. This is a held-in constructive
composition check with a matched negative, not an independent
mission or an advancement over the equally informed scheduler.
