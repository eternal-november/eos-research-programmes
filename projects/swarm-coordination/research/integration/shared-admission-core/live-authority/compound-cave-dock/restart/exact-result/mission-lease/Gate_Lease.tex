\paragraph{Held-in mission test.}
This extension of the
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/803}{cave result replay}
turns an exact old value into a verified egress action. It is a
known-map civil simulation fixture, not an independent I2
mission, Gazebo vehicle dynamics, a field experiment, or evidence
of an advantage over the strongest established planner.
It retains the actual network and named SQLite credit from the
parent. The selected source assets are 249,901 triangles from
14 placed Cave Circuit 01 meshes, their supplied pose data,
and four staged fences. The motion and observation models below
are explicit, not a calibration of real sensors or actuators.

\paragraph{Producing the report.}
An observation station M at \((100,60,8)\) uses a modeled
5 m ray along the positive \(y\) axis. The selected cave mesh
and stationary bodies clear the entire tested ray tube.
Exactly one of two modeled gate panels is active.
Under gate A the first panel intersection is at range 2.45 m.
Under gate B the same ray does not hit that panel and reaches
its 5 m limit. Thus one native packet M-to-R before the
historical operation transports the actual derived distinction.
The stored code is 256 when the first ray hits and 128 otherwise;
the two codes carry no extra physical precision. R's journal
uses the actual received value rather than a globally supplied
route at Q. The classifier depends on the declared one-closed-gate
law; the absence of the near hit alone does not identify
arbitrary other layouts or simultaneous gate closures. The
pinned radio model is used as in the parent: the added panel
is a collision and observation barrier and is not given an
unmeasured new RF attenuation law.

In this positive family the qualified old scan report already
settled at historical R. The first attempted forwarding link
then fails and the already-modeled second fault moves R out of
its dock after the courier's last grant. The actual R-to-S
handoff migrates the result and authority; S uses the SQLite
key without repeating the old credit. A separate inspection
packet sends before settlement. Q receives the eventual typed
reply by an actual callback on the same clock. A case with
no reply holds the courier at the checked starting anchor;
it is not a completed egress. The earlier relocations and
other vehicles' later egress are outside this fixture.

\paragraph{Route construction and comparisons.}
Q uses the actual returned code to select a verified route,
without reading the evaluator's true gate. The routes reuse
the diagonals/reversal challenge from
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/790}{the stronger cave comparison},
but include the stationary S and D bodies, the observation
station, and retired R. In particular the old approach and
departure endpoints at the body centers of S and D are not
reused unchanged. The local approach splices avoid those
bodies. A chord optimizer considers the listed waypoint
library, not unrestricted paths. All swept boxes of the
output are checked against the actual mesh, stationary bodies
and the active panel. Each route's centerline has a strictly
interior collision with the other modeled panel: interchanging
the hidden modes late is not harmless.

After the reply we construct a stop-to-stop trajectory along
the chosen legs with acceleration magnitude at most one and
speed at most three. Under the compact protocol the response
arrives at \(15/16\) s. The modeled finishes are
121.31371974 s for closed A and 136.70375282 s for closed B.
The matched full-record wait control delays the retry until
the two-byte migration has expired at S, then receives the
full result one second later. Both paths also finish, one
second later, and both variants fit 200 s. A full-record eager
retry is charged and fails at the tight cap; this is a
diagnostic arm, not the strongest planner. The wait control
uses the same response-gated start, not independently optimized
motion that can overlap waiting. A factored direct controller
using the same compact protocol and route library ties.
No new deadline has been chosen to separate the methods.

\paragraph{Which lifetime the route really needs.}
An exact old result alone does not guarantee current egress
safety. The closed mode must remain valid until Q clears the
\emph{other} panel's swept contact interval. We model changing
which panel is instantaneously active, not the moving swept
volume of a real door. Each panel check uses the expanded
box and an exact rational slab interval in the leg parameter.
We invert the prescribed accelerate/coast/brake trajectory
to calculate the actual overlap times. In the compact case
the last possible contact is 53.55032115 s for closure A,
and 26.58799294 s for closure B. For the stated family,
protection through that cut suffices; switching the active
panel while Q is strictly inside the crossing interval
collides. The independent implemented check evaluates the
mid-contact body's pose, rather than only detecting overlap
of bounding intervals. Waiting for the full record shifts the
required expiry one second under the compared scheduling.
An assurance requiring the closure mode to remain fixed
through all egress would hold it about 68 or 110 seconds
longer in these particular tests. An interlock preventing
any switch while occupied has a different admitted family.

The Lean checker formalizes the attained last-contact and
time-shift rule and the first eligible discrete send slot.
It is not a proof of the source radio implementation.
The certificates at this nominal box size have a small
reported geometric separation bound. No tolerance beyond
the specified boxes is inferred. The actions, source hash
checks, input paths, and exact replay are given alongside
this note.
