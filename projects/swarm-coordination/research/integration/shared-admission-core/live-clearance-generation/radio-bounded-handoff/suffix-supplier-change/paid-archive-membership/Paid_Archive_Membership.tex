\section*{A paid membership cue at a receipt-recovery cut}
One eligible reader can verify any of the three named historic receipts
$A,B,C$; the other can verify exactly its one surviving named receipt.
Subjects are chosen before role resolution; assignment to readers may
wait for it. Every chosen set must be feasible in every possible role.
Service is the minimum inclusion probability over all subjects and roles.
Compare designs while preserving the mandatory old delivery and unrelated
motion, not policies allowed to discard either to pay for a cue.

\paragraph{Information bound.}
With no cue, any pair omits one role's only sparse receipt, so cannot be
guaranteed. Summed inclusion probabilities are at most one; an equal
lottery over singletons attains $1/3$. On a positive answer to whether the
sparse archive contains the exact receipt for $A$, mix $AB$ and $C$
equally. On a negative answer, mix $BC$ and $A$ equally. Both policies are
feasible throughout their cells. In the positive cell $BC$ is forbidden;
in the negative cell $AB$ is forbidden. Each incompatible pair has summed
inclusion probability at most one, proving the exact $1/2$ service bound.
If qualifying the cue consumes one verification slot without counting as
either audit, even truthful information leaves at most one check and the
bound returns to $1/3$. This is an analytic control, not a source claim.

\paragraph{Radio and evidence costs.}
The source checks a named SQLite receipt against its logged simulator
effect. The publisher is assumed to have the immutable archive and
manifest for the current episode. The short message contains neither
receipt nor cryptographic binding: a positive bit incorrectly carried
from role $A$ to role $C$ can wrongly admit $AB$. The endpoint, codebook,
timing and episode linkage are commissioned fixture facts. Where that
qualification cannot be established, keep all compatible roles: silence
is not a negative answer. A late decode cannot revise the choice at the
earlier cut.

A two-byte preparation command must reach the selected supplier.
Supplier and membership fit jointly in one octet; the separate literal
framing uses two. Thus the two framings need three and four bytes
respectively in the pinned byte window. This is not a lower bound
against an equally informed codec: all six joint supplier--role values
also fit in an octet. The packed frame couples erasures: its loss can
hide the supplier as well as the membership, while an erased separate
audit cue leaves the supplier observation intact. The replay imposes
these particular erasures; it does not estimate their probabilities.

The native broker controls admission, debit, and delivery; the warehouse
executes cached restart and unrelated motion. Schedules, prior debit,
the source--mode association, reader capacities, and the cross-product
of archive and mission are authored. Actual audit outcomes and incentive
enforcement are not executed. An equally informed matcher with the same
framing ties. No independent mission or stack acceptance follows.
