\paragraph{What is transported.}
The earlier bay-and-radio fixture made the source promise that no
old return had been accepted in a sealed issuer epoch. This development
transfer replaces the \emph{receiver-acceptance} part of that promise
with the selected ROS-Industrial VDA adapter. It retains a modeled
closed issuer stream, unique issue identity, trustworthy observation
incarnation, a disjoint interpretation of the replacement route,
and the separate physical-clearance condition. It does not equate
a received version, a finish notification or a publish request with
movement or clearance.

\paragraph{Ordinary update race.}
An incumbent route has released anchor A and an unreleased continuation.
The earlier update releases A--B. A higher update proposes A--X.
The harness runs the pinned validator, subscription logic, state
construction, publication request, and publication to an inert protocol
sink. If A--X wins, the later-arriving lower release is rejected. If
A--B wins, the higher proposal anchored at A fails to stitch. A separate
subsequent update anchored at B can be accepted without retiring the old
A--B effect. The report edge array is empty in all these cuts, so it is
not the evidence being transported.

For the closed three-update stream, accepted A--B is an invariant
against the competing A-anchored replacement. Thus a fresh report
matching the exact acknowledged A--X payload excludes A--B acceptance
in this stream. The conclusion is negative and named; it does not
cover another issuer, an alternate edge or a new receiver incarnation.
The report fields are bound to their retained payload and incarnation
by the replay's authored interface. In an actual deployment these are
separate missing provenance obligations. A request to publish does not
itself advance the last observed report in the fixture. A lost report,
a report merely requested, a prior-incarnation report, and lack of
a closed origin each leave the early decision unresolved. Lean checks
the invariant for arbitrary words in the closed three-input model.

\paragraph{Common downstream decision.}
The measured selected receiver outcome drives the original
accepted-return motion and sealed-journal fixture as a named source
interpretation. No no-right announcement is created from a silent or
unmatched observation. A qualified certificate uses the original
early transmission and fallback after the second proof-route loss.
The downstream test for entering on the no-right report and the
subsequent release/clearance path are unchanged; the replay shows the
exact transformation of the upstream source seam. If the old right was
accepted, an actual selected RWARE move and departure still precede
the qualified late release. The VDA route outcome by itself cannot
certify that step. This is a joined development fixture, not a
receiver-to-drive integration or an independently selected mission.

\paragraph{Outcomes and residual.}
The three orders of update/anchor and four unusable evidence controls
are exercised under both route availability and fallback conditions.
The qualified policy preserves the mandatory return when it exists and
admits the visitor by tick 9 in its finite envelope. Among its 160
cases it admits 12 early; the conservative wait admits none early.
Across all 480 histories, an equally informed direct calculation gives
the same results. Actual native side work survives at tick 5 in the
fixture. An unmatched early certificate, physical re-entry, or a
report from an issuer path outside the declared stream needs additional
protection. No stock applicability to cancel semantics, field service,
architecture disposition or I1 acceptance is shown.
