\paragraph{Audit.}
An extension of the held-in warehouse restart fixture. The new duty may
undo the physical state left by the old one: recording the old result is not
freezing the shelf forever. Delivery of a delayed command and changes of
eligible publisher are authored alternatives. Attempted physical steps are
executed on pinned RWARE. No field, independent selection, or generic
graph-cut novelty is claimed.

\paragraph{Same pose, different duty.}
An authorized first operation A puts the named shelf down at the refuge.
A separately authorized operation B must pick up the very same shelf and
carry it away. B restores the identical carrier pose, heading, shelf position
and custody of the pre-A cut. The serialized guard for holding the same
shelf at the same refuge becomes true again. An old delayed A accepted
after B's pickup and before departure physically sets down the shelf a
second time. The break is in B's custody, not in the audit of whether A
originally paid. Greater measurement precision cannot distinguish equal
physical states associated with different duties.

\paragraph{Selective protection and timing.}
A consumes at tick 0, when an independent native delivery also pays.
B uses the shared publisher at 2 or the backup publisher at 4 and must
depart loaded at 6. A's only delayed arrival can occur at 1, 3, 5, 7,
or never. At 7 a successful vehicle has physically moved out of the
guard's site. Keying the live guard by immutable duty or a verified
nonreused epoch rejects delayed A and still lets B depart at 6. A restart
reusing the old epoch passes A and fails the same test. A whole-publisher
fence prevents the old toggle but also prevents B when it shares that
publisher. With separate publishers the broad fence can retain service.
All attempted writes have to pass the asserted boundary; this run does
not establish this property for deployed publishers.

\paragraph{Preserve joint histories.}
Under a latch-only guard the hazardous interval is after B's pickup and
before departure. The two timing/publisher pairs (old at 1, primary
pickup at 2) and (old at 3, backup pickup at 4) are safe without a fence.
Their marginal product adds (old at 3, primary at 2), which really
fails when independently admitted, but is not a history of that specific
coupled source. Admitting that pair changes the proof without changing
either marginal or post-A view. The by-duty fence works on both supports.
The correlation is stipulated, not learned from a successful replay.

\paragraph{Comparator.}
For a frozen complete continuation graph in which a fence only removes
edge labels, safety is ordinary reachability after deletion. Finding
a cheapest such cut is classical
\href{https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jyc/papers/label-cut.pdf}{minimum label cut}
(Zhang et al.). A cut is not a service proof. The common interpreter
separately requires A, the independent payment and a physical loaded
departure, and rejects an unintended later A effect. The graph check
establishes safety, not an arbitrary decentralized strategy.
All 100 native cases agree with a separate factored direct calculation;
30 frozen graph controls and four Lean kernels pass. Identically typed
direct fencing ties. No full obligation or architecture disposition is claimed.
