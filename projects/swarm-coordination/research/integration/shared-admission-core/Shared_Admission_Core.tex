\title{One admission interpreter for physical clearance and report custody}
\author{}
\date{26 November 2026}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Scope and result.}
Two pinned simulation producers feed a single typed interpreter for an
outstanding inspection workflow. A warehouse robot and its load must
actually clear a bay, a report must arrive exactly once by its deadline,
and a separate radio delivery must still complete. The same local
decision cannot assume away a busy bay, already spent transmit capacity,
a previously delivered report, or authority that ends before use.
The one-cut interpreter and a factored direct check agree in 21,600
row--option comparisons; no planner-performance advantage is claimed.
This is an I1 development fixture, not architecture coverage, I2 or an
independent held-out case. It couples two checked code paths by a
declared one-way model, not a physical integration of the simulators.

\paragraph{Interfaces and declared kernel.}
The two source adapters provide ordered physical effects and
actor-indexed evidence, not feasibility verdicts. The spatial producer's
warehouse command trace witnesses actual relocation at tick 6, 18 or
19. The radio producer executes version-pinned broker and RF model
code. Times are aligned by fixture declaration: one second of broker
simulation is 24 warehouse test ticks. The physical availability of the
local report port requires a cleared bay. An alternative receiver can
be used without that bay, subject to the authority and path in the
fixture. The cross-plant clock, berth-to-radio implication, delivery
of truthful local evidence, and static path and permission facts
are authored, not learned. In one separately added control a permission
withdraws at tick 14. There is no inference that healthy or quiet
hardware without a report has a clear bay or a durable permission.

The decision at tick 9 is irrevocable for this catalogue. Its choices
are a tick-15 send via the berth, a disjoint remote send at tick 15, a
remote sender using the original receiver at tick 15, a delayed local
send at tick 30, or no new send. Allowing a later choice at another cut
is a different control class. The simulator receipts at the cut are
explicit test inputs; their propagation latency and failure model are
not implemented by the plants. Warehouse paths are not optimized
or re-synthesized by this catalogue, and none of those paths responds
to the simulated radio decision.

\paragraph{Interpreter.}
A trace atom has a time and tie order, required positive facts, required
absences, fact effects, resource checks/debits, and identifier(s) it
actually settles. A resource check reads the recorded usage at
$t-W\leq u\leq t$, in the atom's order, adds the requested usage, and
compares with the limit for that attempt. A failed physical outcome may
retain a transmit debit without a receive debit. No alternative is
credited unless the trace records the corresponding effect. Sequential
interpretation follows the explicit time/order relation; it does not
silently identify a terminal callback, a resource release and a physical
clearance. Input adapters must keep logical fact meanings compatible
and assign a unique sequential order to effects; this kernel is not a
general conflict-resolution law for simultaneous assignments.

Let $H$ be the finite set of authored possible histories. A partial
observable option is approved for view $o$ exactly when
\[
  \forall h\in H:\quad \operatorname{view}_{9,a}(h)=o
    \Longrightarrow \operatorname{check}(h,\operatorname{option},D).
\]
The scope is the implemented finite, single-cut action catalogue.
Soundness follows by substituting the actual row in the view class.
If a tested choice is rejected, some row in the class violates a
resource/authority/physical guard or a due effect. This does not show
that no other information-acquisition strategy could succeed. The core
only proves what its declared world set covers. It does not certify
that world set against the plant.

\paragraph{What joins, and why it matters.}
The join uses a declared common binding, separate from anything
available to the controller. It rejects an unmatched declared binding
rather than dropping a possible history. Within a binding the product
must either be justified or treated as a conservative overapproximation;
the algorithm does not establish completeness of the inputs.
Across bindings it is not a Cartesian product unless
independence is justified. The two extremal mistakes appeared in
controls. First, taking a clear bay from one trace and an available
path from another can incorrectly admit a report whose local option is
unsafe in both traces. Second, in a deliberately authored common-port
case a shared condition puts a two-unit setup debit on just one side.
The two valid rows leave enough for a two-unit report under a
five-unit cap; the Cartesian product invents a four-unit prior debit
and refuses the report. An exclusive coupling must be established, not
selected because it yields an attractive result. The original native
simulators establish neither the shared control-port meter nor the
exclusive coupling of this separate control. This also shows why an
unknown coupling must not be treated as a known compatible binding.

\paragraph{Matched comparison and evidence accounting.}
Each deadline has 2,160 complete rows and 133 distinct local views.
The direct check keeps the same site and receipt distinctions, computes
transmit and receive inequalities separately and checks deadlines and
a prior successful delivery directly. It matches all approved option
sets. The short deadline admits a guaranteed option in 84 classes; the
longer deadline does so in 99. These are counts of tested cases, not
probabilities or relative operational improvement. Ninety-eight radio
controls include independent transmit/receive aliasing, an erased
optional send, pre-existing successful delivery and unavailability;
ten warehouse controls replay the pinned source. Five subsequent
commands are selected by the common controller and checked against
native radio outcomes: early local, early remote, retain after prior
delivery, refuse, and defer. The guard for a remote send to the
original receiver distinguishes a failed optional from a successful
one even though both debited the same sender.

The saved replay reports exact query, visited-atom, meter-check and
prior-debit-read counts for the exhaustive comparison. It does not show
that enumerating all rows or distributing receipts is cheap. All tested resource charges and motion events are
shared by the exact comparison. The next material test adds a source
that can change its effects \emph{in reaction to} an authorized probe
or repair, with independently justified bindings and measurement and
intervention costs. A real accepted case would also need the relevant
published planning comparator and the frozen, independently chosen
mission under the contract.
