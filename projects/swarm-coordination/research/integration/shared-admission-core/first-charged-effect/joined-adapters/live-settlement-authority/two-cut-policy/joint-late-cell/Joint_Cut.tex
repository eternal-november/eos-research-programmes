\section*{A late observation can resolve a joint obstruction and still consume its service budget}
\paragraph{Fixed interface.}
Continue \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/823}{the two-cut test}.
The sender has one early view and chooses a dock. The chosen dock
uses one received occupancy bit (or no bit) and may request one
substitution. Three native warehouse moves are eligible to settle
the same duty in the declared fixture. A separate native movement
blocks exactly one of their destinations before actuation; the
other two remain available. The actuator checks its chosen
destination before executing and cannot choose another. The
positive service predicate requires the received request, the
actual move, and its exact result received by the sender. An
unrelated warehouse move runs in all trials. The optional prior
radio side delivery is separate and retained wherever present.

\paragraph{Exact summary and negative witnesses.}
For fixed dock, observation, and positive action menu $A$, let
$G_h\subseteq A$ be the actions that meet the service predicate
in history $h$. A reached cell $C$ admits a guaranteed action iff
$S_C=\bigcap_{h\in C}G_h$ is nonempty. Merging cells intersects
their summaries. For consumers that can restrict the allowed
actions to any $B\subseteq A$ but cannot change history support
or act semantics, this action set is exact and distinction-minimal:
any differing action is separated by a singleton restriction.
It does not summarize facts needed to split a cell later or change
the menu or source law. A negative certificate needs at most
$|A|$ histories: pick one history ruling out each action.
Blocking one distinct action per history attains this bound.
Here every pair of unseen obstructions admits a common move,
yet neither dock has a common move for all three. Lean checks
the merge, reflection, and certificate laws and the sharp
three-action example. An exhaustive same-view policy search
matches the intersection check.

\paragraph{Paid evidence at the actual cut.}
The probe does not report the whole three-valued fault identity.
It reads actual native occupancy of destination 0 at its own cut.
If destination 0 is occupied, choose 1; otherwise choose 0.
A fresh bit suffices under the exact-one-block premise. A truthful
read before the blocking move reports clear in all three worlds
and does not suffice. The pinned SubT communication code carries
the one-byte observation, four-byte request, and four-byte reply
with two fixed addresses available for the owed request.
With a four-byte bound for the request's sender configuration,
the broker actually admits the fresh diagnostic but cannot admit
the request while its debit is live. The corresponding five-byte
bound suffices when that dock has no live prior delivery. With
the four-byte prior delivery at dock 0 and a five-byte bound,
putting the only probe there yields no guaranteed route;
putting the probe at dock 1 too admits dock 1 alone. Nine
bytes suffice at either dock when both receive the fresh probe
on selection. The capacity at a given reception check is
taken from the transmitting radio configuration by the pinned
code; the test does not assume an independent fixed receiver
configuration. The policy is rerun on the observed packets,
not fed the blocking-move identity. The 165 executions cover
all actions, both destinations, all three histories, seven
declared regimes, policy/refusal witnesses and explicit successful
pair-restricted controls for the no-probe case.

\paragraph{Kernel.}
The relation between dock authority and warehouse actuation, the
placement of the occupancy observer, and the clock ordering are
fixture declarations. Configured observation routes and stable
test links do not establish signal availability in a robot or
a real network. This is an executable refinement and obstruction
witness for fixed action menus and these selected simulator
sources, not I1/I2 acceptance or a gain over a direct solver.
