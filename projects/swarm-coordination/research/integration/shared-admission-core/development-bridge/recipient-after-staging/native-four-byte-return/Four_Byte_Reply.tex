\section*{A four-byte exact reply after live dock selection}
\paragraph{Scope.} Extend the pinned two-dock warehouse/broker fixture of
PR 835. The collector receives a one-byte authenticated-by-fixture dock hint,
then issues the same four-byte request to the chosen dock. The dock observes
the same qualified occupancy bit, retains the same authorized four-byte
historical result, and performs the same new lane operation. The acceptance
output at the collector is the exact pair of the acted-on lane and the
historical result of the named old job, not a success bit or a fresh reading
in place of the old result. The collector is not given the donor journal,
a table of candidate rows, or a separate channel.
\paragraph{Law and kernel.} Let the four result octets be $(t,a,x,y)$, with
$t<128$, and let $m\in\{0,1\}$ be the lane. Encode
$(m,(t,a,x,y))$ as $(t+128m,a,x,y)$. The high bit recovers $m$ and
reduction modulo 128 recovers $t$. The wire representation therefore loses
no distinction on the declared domain; in particular it does not infer the
old key-to-row association from an equal motion log. Reject outside the
domain before applying the new operation in this replay. This is a bound on
the historical result field of this protocol, not a bound on elapsed time,
motion ages, or all future station results. Protocol agreement and source,
job, and dock qualification are separate obligations. A sender displaying the
correct bit is not, by this codec, evidence of authority.
\paragraph{Cut and obstruction.} In the pinned live window an in-band
hint leaves four bytes for the reply at cap five. The five-byte raw reply
can be lost after the move is already committed; the four-byte reply arrives
and decodes without moving the hint or delaying either duty. Below cap five
at the common setting, the dock's four-byte request and one-byte occupancy
cue cannot coexist in its receive window. At cap five a qualified in-band
hint and both staged copies are still needed; neither a missing hint nor a
stale lane sample is repaired by encoding. For a fixed receiver view with
both lanes possible independently of an arbitrary four-octet result, there are
$2\cdot256^4$ distinct exact queries to answer, exceeding the $256^4$
fixed four-byte words. This counting claim is about fixed four-byte words
without side timing, length, identity or other signaling; it is not an
impossibility for all multi-message or variable-length protocols.
\paragraph{Timing comparator.} Keep the operation at time 2 and buffer the
uncompressed reply. At time 2.125 the old hint is still charged, at the
one-second endpoint; at time 2.1875 it is no longer charged. The delayed
five-byte reply then gets the same successful plans as the immediate
compact reply. It changes the completion time, not the action or report
order, and requires that the tested authorization and link remain usable
until transmission. An immediate reply deadline excludes this alternative;
a deadline allowing the later cut does not. The immediate result has
no advantage over the same ordinary qualified bit packing.
\paragraph{Evidence and residual.} Run the same native RWARE/SQLite and
SubT callback fixture under both encodings, exercising its 45 configurations
and comparing results of 3,288 replays per mode (raw immediate, raw at expiry, raw just after expiry, and compact immediate). The one newly winning
configuration is cap five with the in-band hint and qualified lane sample;
the unchanged shared selection core and equally informed direct analyses
agree. The codec exhausts both lanes and all values of the changed octet;
the round trip and necessary range follow by direct arithmetic and the fixed-width obstruction by counting. The
source-backed replay is serial, with authored staging, authority and
forecast envelope. It is not a deployed interlock, new physical fault
envelope, named-competitor advantage or I1 acceptance.
