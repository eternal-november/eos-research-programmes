\section*{R3b: a protected report can conceal the advantage of early preparation}
\paragraph{Estimand and admitted evidence.}
A civil inspection retransmits one exact named four-byte result.
Before the route fault is known, choose staging arrangement \(a\in\{0,1\}\);
the fault later names the required dock \(U\). The schedule's latent
orientation is \(V\), so dock \(V\mathbin{\oplus}a\) gets the early
durable copy. Both docks really obtain the same result. The estimand
is service by the original cut under each staging action, not whether
a protected calibration run eventually returns the result. The study
compares known finite source laws, not estimated field rates. Episodes
are independent under one fixed law; the two marginals may be learned
from separate calibration episodes. No historical port in the baseline
permits a same-episode join of the original-cut timing and route records.
This is an evidence-interface premise, not a claim that an unconstrained
operator cannot join archived logs.

\paragraph{Source mechanism and lawful scope.}
The early copy arrives at \(0.875\), the other at \(1.0\) or \(1.125\).
The native broker callback stores payload and receipt time atomically
in SQLite, and the two databases are reopened before service.
At the target request cut \(2.0\), a cap-four dock only admits the
four-byte request if its retained receive charge is strictly older
than one second. Therefore target service is exactly
\(U=V\mathbin{\oplus}a\). The result value can vary; a successful
retry returns the exact record, not a success flag. The protected
calibration is permitted to move the request to \(2.25\) and then both
staging ages allow it. A present-fit audit at that later cut is
also always positive. Thus protection changes the outcome that is
reported, even without changing either original receipt time.
Geometry is represented only by the required dock; no robot motion,
field clock validation or approved real audit port is shown.

\paragraph{Non-identification and policy consequence.}
Two fixed laws each have fair marginals: one always has \(U=V\),
and the other always has \(U\ne V\). A protected outcome and disclosed
route reveal only \(U\); an isolated timestamp calibration reveals only
\(V\); a protected present-fit read adds no distinction. Conditional on
any admitted previous transcript, a next instrument sequentially
chosen from those arms still has the same response distribution under
both laws. Induction gives equality for every adaptive historical
policy in this class, including random selection and stopping.
Yet the best preparation switches, and service is one under the right
preparation versus zero under the other. Without further evidence
the robust value is \(1/2\) with a shared random choice of
preparation; a fixed deterministic choice has worst case zero.
Allowing ordinary failure at the original cut in the training data,
or recovering the original paired records, invalidates the premise.

\paragraph{Paired remedy and counter-controls.}
In the protected arm a qualified local reader \(H\) evaluates whether
the selected dock's actual receipt predates \(1.0\).
The archived timestamp and known scope of the original request
identify \(V\) when combined with \(U\) and \(a\).
With a one-byte audit on the same collector, either the audit or
the full exact four-byte result cannot share that live cap-four
window; putting the audit first prevents the exact result.
Reserve a separate authorized audit endpoint and bind the audit
to the same episode/dock/key. A one-byte tag there and the exact
protected result at the ordinary collector both arrive. The
co-sited reader and episode qualification are expressly supplied;
a wrong-dock historical timestamp would reverse the forecast.
The direct receiver-qualification and rolling-capacity calculations
give the same answers.

A strong non-tag control retries at the real original cut, then uses
the protected cut only on failure. The ordinary sender cannot make
that retry: the failed first attempt spent all four live bytes.
A separately authorized fallback sender can, preserving the report
and learning the counterfactual from whether the early result arrived.
With common staging cost omitted, native totals are eight charged
bytes on an early success or twelve on a fallback, versus nine on
the protected historical-tag arm. At a fair untested choice of
\(a\) under the two extreme laws, the early-success probability is
\(1/2\), giving ten versus nine. Above an early-success probability
of \(3/4\), the fallback uses fewer expected bytes; it also sometimes
returns earlier. The two variants assume different permissions
(read and route vs retry and receive) and the side receiver does not
magically grant a new sender. No general architecture superiority
follows.

\paragraph{Sharp selected-data extension.}
Let \(p=\Pr(U=1)\), \(q=\Pr(V=1)\) in the target population.
Let \(\ell_{ij}\) be the unconditional probability that an episode has
a successful qualified pair audit revealing \((i,j)\). These masses
need a target-cohort denominator and stable original timestamps;
the conditional composition of an unknown selected sample is not
enough. For arbitrary selection the sharp range of \(x=\Pr(U=V=1)\)
is
\[
  \max(\ell_{11},\,\ell_{00}+p+q-1)
  \ \le x\le\
  \min(p-\ell_{10},\,q-\ell_{01}).
\]
An empty interval rejects the stated provenance or marginals.
The four underlying masses are
\((1-p-q+x,q-x,p-x,x)\), each at least its labelled mass.
Every feasible table yields a selection rule that labels the
fraction \(\ell_{ij}\) divided by the corresponding cell mass
(with zero if the cell is empty), proving sharpness.
Service under preparation zero is then
\(\theta=1-p-q+2x\). For feasible endpoints \(\theta_L,\theta_H\),
the best worst-case service allowing ex ante randomization is
\(\max(\theta_L,1-\theta_H,1/2)\).
A mixture of the two actions has affine payoff in \(\theta\);
the worse endpoint changes only at equal mixing, proving the formula.
Complete unbiased same-episode evidence point-identifies the joint
law in this model, not in a population with different staging
distributions or a clock intervention that changes the historical
variable itself.

\paragraph{Checks and relationship to the comparator.}
The pinned deterministic SubT broker and real SQLite run 288 native
cases, including an exact one-second endpoint, both result
payloads, and native charged-byte controls. An independent table
calculation checks 17,874 selected-count classes, 53,622 robust
candidate values, and 56 counter-law observational comparisons.
The bounds are an elementary four-cell instance of partial
identification and transport, not a general causal algorithm or
a claim of advantage over
\href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v286/marmarelis25a.html}{causal sensitivity MPC}
or the matching exact contingency-table computation.
The source-side distinction narrows the scope of a protected
experiment. It is partial R3b, not field identification, held-out
mission success, or I1 acceptance.
