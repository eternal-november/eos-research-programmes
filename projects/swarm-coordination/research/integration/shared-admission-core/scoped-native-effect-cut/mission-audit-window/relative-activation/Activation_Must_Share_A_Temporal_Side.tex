\section*{Final calibration agreement is not a service-window agreement}
At two eligible audit cuts \(q_0<q_1\), every registered member
needs a current pose. Each observer has an old calibration plan
that makes it miss only \(q_0\), and a replacement calibration
plan that makes it miss only \(q_1\). The replacement takes
effect exactly when that observer consumes its actual native
revision delivery. Both plans separately produce useful service
if held for the whole window. Assume no other blocker in
these two cuts; all work and deadlines are civil simulation.

Let \(t_i\) be the native activation cut of observer \(i\); at any
coincident physical time use the event order of admission before
the readiness read. The first audit opportunity works exactly
when all observers already use their replacement plans, i.e.\
\(\max_i t_i\le q_0\). The second works exactly when all are
still using their old plans, i.e.\ \(\min_i t_i>q_1\).
The exact condition for the old audit is the disjunction.
Even simultaneous activation strictly between the two cuts
fails: the old plan has already blocked the first and the new
plan blocks the second. Mixed early/late activation also fails.
No check solely on final agreement suffices; the original
blocked cut remains part of the same history.

For arbitrary qualified joint timing support, this condition
must hold on every allowed arrival tuple. Two correlated
regimes, entirely early or entirely late, can be safe even
when an observer viewed by itself is uncertain. Replacing that
joint support by independent marginals can generate failing
mixed histories. When each observer's supported set is
independently selectable, there are at least two observers
with nonempty sets, and simultaneous early and late choices
are not otherwise coordinated, universal service holds exactly
when all supported times are early or all are late.
Indeed a middle choice already fails; otherwise any two
distinct actors supporting opposite sides give a counterexample.
If only one actor supports both sides, its choice opposed to the
fixed side of a different actor suffices. This is a law of
allowed arrival tuples, not a distributional independence claim.

The actual test runs all nine pairs of before, between and
after arrival slots on the pinned native dispatcher. A named
observer changes plan only in the callback of its own actual
control frame. The original audit is observed in exactly two
pairs, while both replacements eventually take effect in all.
With the explicit power to defer the control frames until
after the audit window, the conservative direct schedule
preserves every old audit and both actual plan receipts.
This power is not presumed for compulsory earlier reactivation.
The serial fixture does not give stock software a distributed
atomic commit primitive or invent guaranteed field receipt
times. The result delineates the scope of the earlier atomic
calendar repair, which happened before the service window.
