\section*{A common target is scoped by the actual clock cohort}
Suppose the old plan blocks the first protected audit cut and
the new plan blocks the last. Replacing both plans is required,
but no replacement may act before \(L\) or after \(D\). Each
observer receives an activation program before or at its local
arm time \(A_i\) and compares the common numeric target \(h\)
to \(t+\delta_i\) on its own clock. The effective trigger is
\(T_i=\max(A_i,h-\delta_i)\) when it has actually armed. All
receiver grants and service slots in this analysis are modeled
civil obligations; there is no default permission to postpone
one past its deadline.

The admissible targets of a fixed joint source history are
exactly the targets satisfying all of the following:
\[
\begin{split}
 & A_i\le D,\quad h\le D+\delta_i,\quad
          A_i\ge L\ \text{or}\ h\ge L+\delta_i\qquad(\text{each }i),\\
 &\bigl(\text{all }A_i\le q_0\text{ and all }h\le q_0+\delta_i\bigr)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \bigl(\text{each }i\text{ has }A_i>q_1\text{ or }h>q_1+\delta_i\bigr).
\end{split}
\]
The first line is \(T_i\in[L,D]\). The two branches of the second
line give, respectively, every replacement acting by the first cut,
or no replacement yet acting at the last. At equal sample times
the modeled order is activation before readiness read. A robust
prearmed common target is in the intersection across actual
allowed tuples of arming times and clock offsets. This is not an
intersection of separate local interval claims unless their
actual source law allows taking their product. Lean checks the
projection and the concrete exact threshold.

For \(q_0=4,q_1=6,L=3,D=9\), both armed at 1 and qualified common
offset respectively 1 or \(-2\), the only common target is 5.
Actual activations then occur jointly at 4 or jointly at 7.
The product of the same marginal offset sets admits no common
target. Native controlled fixtures implement both actual timer
readers without granting them either physical offset. Every
qualified common case preserves the old audit and both new duties.
Split offsets defeat the old audit despite the same target.
Even a single native group send does not establish the arming
premise. The dispatcher attempts each member, and with a real
six-byte receiver debt one member actually misses the arm.
It then strands the old audit under a completely shared clock.
The test adapter preserves actual native host routing to distinct
client callback objects; it does not treat one callback as a
receipt for the whole fanout.

\paragraph{Sharp chance envelope from margins alone.}
In the regime where each activation is either early or late
(no middle cases), the old audit occurs exactly on the two
constant side vectors. For \(n\ge2\), if the early probabilities
are \(p_i\) and their dependence is otherwise unknown, let
\(p=\sum_i p_i\). The sharp interval is
\[
 [\,\max(0,1-p)+\max(0,p-(n-1)),\
                  1-(\max_i p_i-\min_i p_i)\,].
\]
The two lower bounds on the all-late and all-early events follow
by applying the union bound to their complements. They are
attained together by laying successive intervals of lengths
\(p_i\) on \([0,p)\), drawing a uniform \(u\in[0,1)\), and
selecting a component if one of \(u,u+1,\ldots\) lies in its
interval. Each marginal has the correct length, and the number
selected is \(\lfloor p\rfloor\) or \(\lceil p\rceil\), so the
extreme counts occur only at the required rates.
For the upper bound, two extreme marginals entail a mismatch
of at least their difference; the common uniform nested
construction attains it. These constructions and all 21,731
small equal-weight coupling laws checked independently verify
the finite consequence. No stochastic independence or
field frequencies are inferred. In particular, the native
two-observer support can give service 0 or 1 with the same
half-early margins.

This is a scoped exact boundary and matched direct result for
an already armed modeled cohort. Unknown source drift, unconfirmed
arming, objectives permitting different controls, and an
independently selected mission are outside its conclusions.
