\title{A port handoff without an empty port}

\maketitle

In a native warehouse simulation, the next carrier can deliver in the same tick as the previous one leaves. The port is occupied at both cuts, and the old carrier still owes its shelf return. A suppressed command on the far side of the rotation cancels both moves. The same cancellation occurs with every command present if an intervening shelf that should travel with its carrier is instead standing on the floor. An unrelated move still executes in either case. These are finite simulator effects, not claims about continuous clearance or a distributed motion protocol.

\paragraph{Admitted transition and source.}
The replay executes the unmodified selected step, agent, shelf, grid and reward code of \href{https://github.com/semitable/robotic-warehouse/blob/96fbc64e3eae5fee915e0d390f864fa06ddccd47/rware/warehouse.py}{RWARE 96fbc64}. It extends the earlier one-mover harness with functional directed graphs. Initially each robot is on a distinct cell. The admitted proposed movements are cardinal nonstationary forward moves with pairwise distinct targets; other robots wait. A subset of those proposals may be invalidated by a missing grant or blocked shelf. A loaded robot may follow into a cell with a shelf carried by its departing predecessor; if that shelf is standing and would stay, the native simulator cancels the loaded motion. There is no unsupported assertion about branch arbitration when originally distinct moves share a target. Native two-robot swaps are also forbidden. The replay does not import Gym or the full NetworkX package, and the graph harness rejects cases outside its checked shape where an acyclic component has competing paths.

\paragraph{What must happen together.}
Draw an edge from each proposed mover to the current occupant of its target, if any. A move needs that occupant to move in this same tick. Thus a moving group is closed under following these edges, and every robot in it must have an actual valid grant and an admissible target. Waiting occupants, moves barred by standing shelves, and the members of a two-robot swap are unavailable. An endpoint safety check alone is insufficient for the two-robot case. Under the distinct-target assumption these are the only combinatorial constraints for endpoint-safe native motion. In particular, no other agent can independently claim the same destination. For a requested mover $i$, every reachable dependency, including $i$, must be eligible. Conversely, if all are eligible, the reachable set itself is closed and safe to admit. The set is smallest in this exact sense: suppressing any member alone defeats previously feasible motion by $i$. Distinct-target chains into free cells commit as a whole; omissions can leave a downstream suffix that still commits. A longer rotation commits if all members remain eligible and otherwise commits no part of the rotation.

To see that these rules agree with the selected native step, after eligibility filtering every disabled or stationary occupant has a self-loop. A component either has a path ending at an unoccupied cell, a self-loop, or a cycle. Distinct targets for moving edges imply there is no branch except immediately at a final self-loop, with its stationary edge and incoming path. The unique longest path in the acyclic case is the moving chain; only the stationary occupant commits in a self-loop component, and native code commits precisely the cycle in the remaining case unless it is a forbidden two-cycle. This is not an analysis of arbitrary competing route choices. The Lean lemmas check the closure construction and same-support replacement over an abstract relation; the source interpretation in this paragraph is verified by the stated case split and executable checks, not by a theorem about the whole simulator.

\paragraph{What can be reused.}
A receiver consuming an actual mover transition needs the grants and physical compatibility along that motion's dependency closure. With the dependency graph fixed, a change outside that closure cannot invalidate the transition; within it a newly missing grant or newly standing shelf can. If targets or occupancy change, the dependency graph itself can change and the old closure cannot be trusted without checking the visited nodes. A controller can recompute whether all components are admitted in linear time on this restricted one-cut graph, but materializing a distinct explicit support list for each member of a long chain can cost quadratic size. Receipt generation, discovery, other duties, and wall time are not part of this bound. A corrected occupancy-free checker without a no-swap premise would also disagree with native execution. The comparison is against an exact target-and-vacancy checker, not an intentionally more conservative rule that demands every cell be empty before a move.

\paragraph{Executed controls.}
A four-carrier rectangle includes an old shelf at the delivery port, the next requested shelf immediately upstream, and a third shelf on another carrier. A fifth carrier takes an independent step. If all four cycle moves are issued and possible, the port's next occupant delivers and earns native half credit at tick 1. The old shelf remains on its carrier, whose return flag remains active. No preexisting empty-port read is needed. Withholding one remote move leaves the whole ring still while the fifth carrier advances. A clean retry delivers at tick 2. In the separate cargo control, the same robots and shelves are in the same cells and all requests are issued, but the third shelf is not on its carrier. The old carrier cannot move a loaded shelf into that station; only the fifth moves. A native pickup in a separate tick and then a simultaneous retry restores delivery at tick 3. Neither repair claims completion of the old shelf return. All frequencies and faults here are deliberately authored.

The actual native step agrees with exhaustive simultaneous safe-subset enumeration on 30,975 nominal-or-omitted commands on the $3\times 2$ grid, then 3,761 additional controls with loaded and standing cargo. There are 107,008 per-mover dependency predicates in the no-cargo enumeration. The native two-swap is separately confirmed. The accepted square is a direct counterexample to carrying the one-mover premise of \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/427}{the earlier release check} over to simultaneous moves. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08291}{Stern et al.} distinguish following, rotating cycles and swaps in MAPF; \href{https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/SOCS/article/view/18415}{Atzmon et al.} address robust execution under delays. Here mapped clean execution and the matched joint-transition reference tie. No advance over delay-robust MAPF, independent source transfer, or I1 and R2 discharge is claimed. The scope of the next question is whether a receiver can consume an actual joint transfer after a supplier or physical movement mechanism changes, without importing all the old actuator assumptions.
