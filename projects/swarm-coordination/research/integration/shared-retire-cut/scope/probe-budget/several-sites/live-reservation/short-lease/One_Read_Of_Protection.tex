Keep the three baseline states $A=(W_L,R_L),B=(W_L,R_R),C=(W_R,R_R)$. The environment starts anywhere and may make at most $s$ changes, seeing the query names, with more than one change permitted in a gap. The controller can read either side of either resource against current baseline availability. It needs a positive verification for each chosen site. A warehouse positive can optionally replace the single warehouse reservation with one valid immediately and through exactly the next read and its ensuing commitment cut. Renewing costs another positive warehouse read; a negative does not renew it. Radio readings are bare. A valid warehouse reservation guarantees its use independently of subsequent baseline changes without preventing the radio choice, as in the preceding model. A historical positive at either unreserved site still needs a current proof of availability to be used. No stock interface or wall-clock lease duration is asserted here.

\begin{theorem}
For one-read warehouse protection, the exact sure optimal budget is $s+3$ on the source path $A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C$. On the graph also admitting a direct $A\leftrightarrow C$ change at unit cost, it is $3$ when $s=0$ and $s+4$ when $s\ge1$. These are exact values for all nonnegative $s$, established by the finite certificate below, not an extrapolation from a cutoff simulation. Longer protection on the path cannot improve the result, since even permanent protection needs $s+3$ there.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{State and recurrence.}
At a read boundary retain (i) for each possible world its largest remaining switch budget, or absence; (ii) the warehouse reservation and its one-read lifetime; (iii) the four Boolean flags for sites ever positively verified. A smaller remaining budget at the same world contributes no further continuations. Before a new read, propagate each budget along shortest directed paths, subtracting the distance. Then filter by the answer. After a positive warehouse read both allowed choices are considered: keep the old remaining reservation or acquire the new one. Old reservations age whether or not the read is positive. Commitment is possible exactly when each resource has a site either still reserved or already verified and available in every current possible world.

The optimal value $V$ is zero in a committing state. Otherwise its recurrence is one plus the minimum over the four query names of the maximum over possible truthful answers of the minimum continuation value over allowed reservation responses. Every adversary outcome represented by that recurrence occurs on a continuation from a possible initial history; future switching depends only on the current world and its remaining budget, so no earlier information was lost. This compares with a direct causal controller at exactly the same action and observation interface.

\paragraph{Why a finite certificate proves all budgets.}
After a targeted read the supported set is a singleton or one of the adjacent pairs $AB,BC$. Within an adjacent pair the largest remaining budgets differ by at most one, since propagation first allows crossing that adjacent edge. Thus with $M$ the largest budget, there are finitely many support shapes obtained by subtracting $M$ from present values. The initial three-state shape has all values $M$. Reservation states and positive flags also have finite shape. For each shape the replay generates exact finite-horizon values up to $M=24$, including extra legal-form states not necessarily reachable from the initial one. It builds candidate affine tails, separately for the four residues of $M$ modulo four, beginning at $M=13$. The period and threshold are choices of certificate representation, not assumed properties of the game.

The program then verifies the recurrence, including the terminal-zero condition, independently of whether those candidates repeat in the finite table. For each shape and residue use a representative $M\ge 15$ and write its larger budgets as $M+4q$. Every world is reachable before the next read and the relative successor shapes and switch decrements are now independent of $q$, because every distance is at most two and subtraction never encounters the zero limit. Each child has largest budget at least 13. Therefore each candidate child value and parent value is affine in integer $q\ge0$. The recurrence takes minima and maxima of these lines. Every intersection of unequal-slope lines lies at or below the maximum absolute intercept difference when $q\ge0$, since the slopes are integers. Checking every integer through that maximum plus three, including the parent line, checks every order change and two points in the final fixed-order region. The finite table checks all smaller boundary transitions explicitly. Nonterminal candidates are positive and all slopes nonnegative.

An integer-valued function passing this recurrence is the exact sure budget: at a nonterminal state choose its minimizing query and then its minimizing allowed reservation response after the answer; the value drops by at least one. Conversely against any query the adversary can choose an answer for which every allowed continuation has value at least the old value minus one. Hence a smaller read budget cannot reach a zero-value terminal. The replay verifies the displayed initial-state formulas both in the finite region and in each affine tail.

\paragraph{What the distinction buys.}
On the path a simultaneous change of both groups requires two moves. Counting the same transition as one can raise the verified deadline by one read even though the admissible instantaneous states and the lease interface are identical. With permanent protection either source graph needs $s+3$. A generator for failures must therefore say which changes can happen together and at what cost, not just list which states are possible. The certificate gives no evidence that a warehouse or radio implementation supplies bounded protective authority. Actual grant lifetime, a protective effect rather than an acknowledgment, and precedence relative to the last read require independent evidence.
