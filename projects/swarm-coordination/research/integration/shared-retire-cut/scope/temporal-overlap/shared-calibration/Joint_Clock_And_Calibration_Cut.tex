\paragraph{Admission question, pins, and kernel.}
At an integer cut $t$, how much of a shared site's capacity can old
admitted work still occupy? Hold $i$ has nonnegative demand $w_i$,
a positive half-open interval $[e_i,x_i)$ in a single feasible
integer simple temporal network, and a retained coordinate $u_i$ in
a fixed calibrated gauge. At this particular cut its demand falls on
the site precisely when $e_i\leq t<x_i$ and $u_i+z\in Q_i$.
Here $z$ is \emph{one common} unknown planar translation,
$z\in K$ for a nonempty compact rational polytope $K$, and $Q_i$
is the closed rational polygon of reference positions at which hold
$i$ contributes load (it may include a specified footprint margin).
The network and $K$ can be jointly instantiated independently.
This is an occupancy abstraction for holds, not a claim that an issued
target proves a robot has reached it. Position trajectories, rotation,
actuator changes, hidden timing--geometry dependence, and probabilistic
risk are not encoded by this product model. Each changes the checks
required before using it for physical admission.

The preceding adapter replay, \texttt{released-base-cut/calibration-capture}
(PR 531), justifies distinguishing the \emph{captured} local target from a
new target recomputed from the order. It supplies neither the shared
translation law nor the occupancy abstraction.

\paragraph{Exact assembly.}
Use the earlier temporal closure to compute eligibility at $t$ and the
required-before relation between eligible holds. For a fixed $z$, restrict
that graph to the holds whose translated polygons admit $z$; weighted
antichain gives exactly the achievable load at this $z$. There is no reason
to use either the temporal peak or the geometric peak as the joint value.
To compute the joint maximum without sampling, enumerate all intersections
of nonparallel boundary lines of $K$ and the eligible
polygons $Q_i-u_i$, keeping points in $K$. Evaluate each resulting mask
of jointly compatible holds with the temporal procedure.

For completeness, take a maximizing admissible schedule and translation,
and let $S$ denote the holds that contribute to its load. The common
feasible set for those holds is
\[
   C_S=K\cap\bigcap_{i\in S}(Q_i-u_i).
\]
It is nonempty and compact. A vertex of $C_S$ lies on two independent
constraint lines: otherwise a sufficiently small movement in either
direction tangent to all active lines would stay feasible, contradicting
extremality. This also covers a segment endpoint or a singleton
enforced by closed inequalities. That vertex is among the enumerated
points, and the original temporal witness is still admissible at it
by the product hypothesis. Extra contributing holds cannot lower
load. Conversely an enumerated point paired with the antichain witness
schedule is a legal joint history. The implementation checks attained
loads, not just the maximum formula, and independently compares against
enumerating job subsets with exact Fourier--Motzkin elimination and a
difference-constraint schedule witness. It does not time these methods
against an optimized comparator.

\paragraph{Two pairs of separations.}
For unit loads, take four old holds $A,B,C,D$. Let $A$ be required to
end before $B$ starts or at the same instant; likewise for $C,D$.
Make $A,B$ share one retained reference position, $C,D$ another, and
choose a site narrow enough that translations that place the first
position in it cannot also place the second in it. Every job is
separately eligible geometrically and temporally. The timing peak is
two via cross-pair overlap and the geometric peak is two via within-pair
overlap. The joint peak is one. A capacity-two rule with one proposed
additional unit can therefore admit at the cut under the joint check
but not under either separate peak. This does not certify occupancy at
later cuts or authorize the new operation independently of its other duties.

Even when the timing relation is empty, testing all geometric pairs
loses information. For the site $x,y\geq0$, $x+y\leq3$, retain the
three targets $(0,0),(-2,0),(0,-2)$ and allow translations in
$[-4,4]^2$. Every pair can share the site, but all three would demand
$z_x,z_y\geq2$ and $z_x+z_y\leq3$. Changing the two $-2$ coordinates
to $-1$ preserves all pair answers and makes the triple possible.
Conversely, checking geometry for all subsets of at most three jobs,
always intersecting with $K$, is sufficient in this planar convex model.
For proof, let a minimal infeasible family of the convex sets
$K\cap(Q_i-u_i)$ have at least four members. For each of four members
pick a point belonging to all sets except possibly that member.
The four lifted vectors $(x,y,1)$ are linearly dependent. Separating
positive and negative coefficients and normalizing to sum one gives
the same point as convex combinations of disjoint groups. Each set
contains every point of at least one group, so their common combination
belongs to every set, contradiction. Thus the geometric conflict rank
is sharply three, even though the temporal conflicts have rank two.

\paragraph{Reuse and the boundary of the claim.}
For a change limited to $K$, keep the intersections of the old footprint
lines and their membership masks. Filter them against the new $K$ and
add intersections involving at least one new support line, then evaluate
current temporal eligibility and the weighted antichains. No old
verdict or bound is inherited without the new cut check. A moved old
target changes its polygon, so this reuse premise is then false.
A direct planner retaining the same half-planes and clock graph can use
the identical work; the comparison establishes a reusable interface and
a gain over separated summaries, not a gain over equally informed
optimized planners.

If a common cause jointly selects a calibration support and a clock
network from a finite set of labelled product branches, compute the
maximum inside each branch and then take the maximum over branches.
Dropping the labels can reintroduce impossible joint states: one replay
has the old hold active only in the branch where it is geometrically
out of the site, and the projected product reports load one instead of
zero. Treating that product as an overapproximation is safe only when
it really contains every physical history. In particular a nominal
timing network with unjustified fixed transit times need not contain
the histories allowed by the calibration fault. All tested polygons
are closed; a corner that survives only at equality can contribute the
maximum. Neither that boundary convention nor the common-error model is
validated by robot or field observations.
