\title{Selective unreliability across distinct evidence domains}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Model.}
Consider a civil warehouse transfer with three authorized alternative sites.
The relevant physical history $h$ determines a set $A(h)$ of sites
where the already promised transfer can finish safely on time. It also
determines a truthful binary string $s_h$ at the cutoff, one bit per
provenance domain. An abstract fault allows at most $t$ such domains in
an episode to supply an arbitrary bit instead of their truthful bit.
A domain can answer truthfully on some histories and not others.
This is a logical fault law, not a network, attack, or hardware model.
Nothing here establishes that sources are in distinct domains, timely,
or correctly associated with the relevant effect.

For tape $y$ the same acting crew must choose a site in the intersection
of $A(h)$ over histories with $d_H(y,s_h)\le t$. This condition is exact
for one deterministic decision with no other observation.
An empty set of possible histories is a contradicted input model, not
a successful admission. Pairwise intersection of physical site sets
does not imply a common choice across a report's full cohort.

\begin{theorem}
Three binary strings have a common tape at radius $t$ precisely when
their pairwise distances are all at most $2t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In each nonconstant coordinate of three binary strings, a single string
disagrees with the majority. Let $a_i$ count dissent for string $i$.
The distances from the majority are $a_i$ and the pairwise distances
are $a_i+a_j$. Triangle inequality forces the bound. If an $a_i>t$,
no other exceeds $t$. Change the majority at $a_i-t$ positions where
that string dissents. Its new distance is $t$, and each other distance
is $a_j+a_i-t\le t$. If none exceeded $t$ the majority already works.
\end{proof}
An explanation involving three physical histories can be checked by
distances alone in this binary law. The extension to four strings is
false: $000,011,101,110$ have pairwise distances at most two but no
common radius-one tape. The binary alphabet is also material:
$00,11,22$ in two ternary domains have pairwise distances two and no
common radius-one tape.

\paragraph{Extension check.}
Take truthful strings $000,110,101$, with physical choices respectively
$\{L,C\},\{C,R\},\{L,R\}$. Every pair shares a lawful site, but
tape $100$ admits all three histories at $t=1$ and no site is common.
A fourth binary domain distinguishes the first history from the other
two. At the same one-domain budget every possible tape consistent
with the model now permits a safe site: the new distance between the
first history and either other history is three. Counting a second
indication from the first old domain as a new domain is invalid.
One fault of that shared domain still allows the original obstruction.
The comparison is exact direct enumeration at matched information.
No superiority over a direct robust planner or satisfaction for a
whole mission is claimed.
