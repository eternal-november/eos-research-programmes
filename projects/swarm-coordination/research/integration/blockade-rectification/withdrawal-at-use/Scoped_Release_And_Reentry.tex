\section*{Reservation withdrawal, recreated identifiers, and physical clearance}
\paragraph{Question and scope.}
Can a receiver treat a settled RELEASE or a reconstructed route range
as a continuing exclusion of the old mover? This civil crossing is an
in-memory, source-pinned Open-RMF blockade test with a separate stipulated
motion check. It uses the complete native moderator and participant rather
than a target-mode stub. A local wrapper controls serialized delivery and
models one already held readiness notification, not network traffic,
ROS, hardware, or a deployed fleet. Physical motion starts only in
the separate modeled attempt; no claim follows about actual door leaves or brakes.

\paragraph{Two independent cuts.}
A reaches checkpoint 1, at $(-2,0)$, with extension through the intersection
not activated. B then receives a crossing assignment. Execute a genuine
new readiness intent, hold it and withdraw it before it is first delivered,
or hold a genuine readiness intent from before the handoff and deliver it
after withdrawal. In the same reservation, the old callback can undo the
withdrawal at the moderator. Rebooking without replacing the participant
advances its reservation from 1 to 2, so the old 1 callback is ignored.
Destroying the participant and creating a fresh moderator and participant
under the same participant identity can reuse reservation 1, even after
the old participant's cancellation was processed. The retained callback
and new legitimate callback then have the same native key and checkpoint.
The tested moderator prefix and callback alone cannot distinguish them.
This assumes a held callback survives; a proven transport drain or
nonreused ordered identity is outside this indistinguishability result.

Independently, when B has reached the intersection and is ready to leave,
the native output may offer A the segment too. It can produce the same
range after B actually reaches the next checkpoint. In the stipulated
perpendicular geometry, A's attempted traversal intersects B in the
former continuation and stays clear in the latter. A grant and B's clearance
are therefore different facts. This defense checks both the current intent
and clearance before starting the attempted movement, not merely after a
range is published.

\paragraph{Available ports, not their atomicity.}
The serialized positive arm reads the active participant's actual
reservation and \texttt{last\_ready} immediately at forwarding. It checks
use-time permission for the idempotent readiness effect, not historical
identity of an individual notification. The adapter is able to read the
current participant with no intervening withdrawal between qualification
and forwarding in this fixture. That authority and serialization are not
guaranteed by the stock receiver interface. An earlier read alone misses
all nine late withdrawal cases in the matrix, even with distinct
reservation IDs: the new withdrawal and the held current-ID callback are
within the same reservation. If the legal intent can also change after
forwarding and before movement, the movement requires its own qualified
cut or a justified span. Nothing in the allocator cancels prior physical
motion retroactively.

\paragraph{Comparison.}
The 108 native runs cover three recreation choices, three physical cut
positions, three delivery modes, and four chronology histories
(new, withdrawn before first delivery, withdrawn after a prior accepted
copy, or withdrawn after the sample). The accepted-copy control really
reduces the original assigned end from 2 to 1 at RELEASE; the later
copy can restore the range in the observed receive order.
The separate motion scorer checks 36 raw native outputs: grants alone
give 17 wrong starts, intent qualification alone three, and clearance
alone seven. The conjunction makes the three correct starts and never
confuses the two queries. A distinct remote producer check before
forwarding fails without a span that prevents withdrawal; conventional
receiver-side fencing with the same read/serialization and a clearance
gate has the same effect. The two predicates can be collapsed to their
conjunction only for this one fixed admission question, not an interface
that must explain or repair either premise separately.
This is a source-backed obstruction and scoped positive alternative,
not a deployed interlock, positive advantage over the matched method,
independent mission, or I1 closure.
