\title{A fresh blockade moderator does not inherit an occupied crossing}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

A replacement moderator that knows no old reservations can grant a
crossing that the old moderator would hold. Re-registering the missing
route is not sufficient to reconstruct the old active range. Even
reconstruction of the full native prefix does not make a new assignment
evidence that another robot has physically cleared the crossing.

\paragraph{Native test.}
Compile the pinned blockade moderator, participant, rectifier,
conflict, constraint, and geometry implementations without replacing
their logic or data structures. A synchronous writer delivers calls
to either the original moderator or a fresh moderator, as selected
by the fixture. There is no ROS, MQTT, fleet driver, or real robot.
Geometric routes and where the robots are taken to be at each
checkpoint are explicit test inputs.

The original moderator grants the eastbound participant its approach
to the crossing at \((0,0)\) and records it reached there. Its model
position is now stopped at the crossing. A second route approaches
the crossing from the south. The original moderator holds it at its
starting checkpoint while granting advance to an unrelated route at
\(x=12\). In the matched reset, neither the parked robot nor its old
clearance changes. If the south route registers and requests advance
before the parked participant rectifies, the fresh moderator grants
it. The test does not execute that movement.

Next the original participant responds to the fresh moderator's
missing record. The first rectification writes only the reservation,
which opens an assignment of \([0,0]\) and does not withdraw the
south route's grant. The next rectification retransmits readiness
and progress. The old progress at checkpoint one exceeds the fresh
assignment; the moderator reports a critical error and retains
checkpoint zero. Here the ranges are produced by the native geometry
and assignment logic, not supplied as fixture values.

\paragraph{Retaining an old grant, not only position.}
In another matched pair the old vehicle is modeled at \((-2,0)\)
in both runs. One run has an old assignment ending there; the other
already has an assignment that lets it move to \((2,0)\). The old
moderator grants the southern approach only in the first run.
An empty moderator makes the same grant in both runs. Thus remembering
the vehicle's position would distinguish neither the applicable old
authority nor the portion of track it may still occupy. Any method
that has only that position and the empty state must either withhold
the grant in the first case or risk issuing overlapping authority
in the second. This is a claim about the matched fixture: the earlier
permission remains exercisable, the driver has not accepted a fence,
and no new authority can be obtained from the retired moderator.

\paragraph{Strong stock comparator and remaining check.}
Rebuilding the old moderator through its native accepted calls,
before serving the new request, matches the original assignments.
It does not hold the unrelated route. Then the old participant
signals readiness to depart. The native assignment admits the new
southern approach before the old participant reports arriving at its
next checkpoint. The contract-style guard in the fixture lets the
new vehicle proceed only after a separate modeled clearance witness.
That witness is the old participant at \((4,0)\), with a closed
remaining envelope that cannot return it to the crossing. The
fixture binds this witness to the reached event by definition;
there is no claim that arbitrary native reached events prove tail
clearance, localization quality, or compliance with a fence.

The strongest relevant stock reconstruction already preserves
disjoint work in this example. This is a source-backed distinction
between assignment recovery and the task's physical admission law,
not a performance win over an equally informed direct guard. A
deployment-level upgrade would have to justify the durable effects
through an actual reset, enforce the gate on the vehicle, and check
its service bounds under a second fault. The replay does not prove
a deployed failure or discharge I1.
