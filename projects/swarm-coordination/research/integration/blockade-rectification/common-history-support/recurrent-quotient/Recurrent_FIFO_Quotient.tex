\section*{Exact recurrent reservation summaries under two withdrawal laws}
\paragraph{Interface and kernel.}
There are \(n\) known, distinct names waiting in FIFO order for a single
mutually exclusive span. Before each grant a qualified context may
withdraw specified waiting names; the preceding holder must actually
clear before the next grant is consumed. A query asks for at most
\(m\ge1\) successive grant identities (including exhaustion).
The initial summary is fixed before the context picks any withdrawals.
An adaptive context can depend on earlier grants, with the same
actions on identical visible histories. No new entries, hidden
priority changes, proof timestamps, or physical motion are outputs
of the queue-summary query. Reservations and clearance do not
substitute for each other.

\paragraph{Two exact quotients.}
For a cumulative deletion budget \(k\ge0\), the ordered prefix of
length \(d=\min(n,k+m)\) is necessary and sufficient, in the sense
that there are exactly \(n!/(n-d)!\) equivalence classes of initial
orders. For a fresh budget of \(k\) deletions before each successive
grant, \(d=\min(n,m(k+1))\) instead, with the same class formula.
This is a changed budget law, not an implementation choice. For
example, \(n=6,k=2\) under the cumulative law gives 120 states for one
grant, fitting one octet, and 360 for two, requiring at least nine
fixed-width bits for the initial summary alone. This counts neither
shared codebook distribution nor radio/authorization overhead.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For sufficiency, before grant \(i\), the number of names consumed by
previous grants is at most \(i-1\), and the total number withdrawn
is at most \(k\) or \(ik\), respectively. If the prefix is not the
whole known set, it follows that some name in the prefix survives
to each promised grant. The first surviving name is the full-queue
answer. Exhaustion is exact when the whole initial queue is retained.
This inductive simulation serves adaptive queries without predicting
their later actions. Withdrawals of omitted names can be charged
without learning their order; the common name universe was supplied.

For necessity, take two different prefixes whose first disagreement
is after \(j\) common names. Set \(u=\max(0,j-m+1)\).
In the cumulative case \(u\le k\); withdraw \(u\) common leading
names before the first release. The disagreeing answers appear by
grant \(m\). In the fresh-budget case \(u\le mk\); remove up to \(k\)
of the remaining common leading names per phase, advancing one grant
when that phase's withdrawals are done. The total \(u\) fits within
\(m\) phases. While a deletion remains to be made, the names used
for both the deletions and intervening grants are still common:
if \(u>0\), their initial common count is \(j=u+m-1\).
The same fixed actions therefore force distinct answers by grant \(m\).
The distinguishing tests are legal sequences of named withdrawals,
not access to the unobserved queue during execution. The argument
uses no random guesses or future outcomes. With \(m=1\) this is the
original first-grant result.

\paragraph{Native checks and material boundary.}
In the pinned Open-RMF civil fixture, \texttt{reached} at a qualified
exit triggers ordinary native allocation of the next holder. The
eligibility of each such exit is stipulated, not derived from a
reservation alone. The replay tests all orders and all upfront
withdrawal subsets for \(n=2,\ldots,6\), then separate adaptive
deletion policies during the held interval, under both budget laws.
The fresh-budget control starts with two queues sharing their first
three names. With one allowed withdrawal per grant, withdraw name
2 and grant 3, then withdraw name 4. The second grant is 5 or 6.
A single cumulative withdrawal does not distinguish them within two
grants. There are 50,360 upfront and 5,232 adaptive native replays. Separate finite
calculations check 106,444 abstract transitions and 68,132 paired lower
witnesses. No claim extends the native geometry beyond the
tested family, and no physical delivery, contact, or timing
guarantee is inferred. Full FIFO rechecking gives the same outputs;
reduced representation size is not a demonstrated wire/service
advantage. Holdout and I1 remain open.
