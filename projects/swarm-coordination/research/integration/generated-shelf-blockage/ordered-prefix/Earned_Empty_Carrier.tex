\paragraph{The refusal to revisit.}
The wider matched warehouse replay found three cases where the all-home route guard, designed to protect shelves, caused a load-retaining replan to refuse: neither surviving carrier was empty. One held item 9, the next item the station was entitled to accept. The other held items 3, 2 and 1. No required shelf was occupied by either stopped robot in either arm of these three paired cases. Being idle at admission and being able to free a carrier through earlier delivery are different conditions.

\paragraph{Exact small planning interface.}
Fix an ordered task and partition its pending sequence into held, directly admissible segments and missing segments of at most one carrier's capacity. A held segment has a unique available owner and may be accepted when its turn arrives. Transferring or discarding cargo, or picking into a loaded carrier, is excluded. Every fresh segment requires an empty carrier at its turn; each accepted segment returns its carrier to the pool. Physical access, station authority, local observations, further faults and sufficient time are separate premises. Under this serial resource model, a nonempty plan can proceed precisely when there is an initially empty carrier or the first segment is held. The first segment cannot borrow capacity freed later. For sufficiency, use the initial source for the first segment, then require actual acceptance before reusing its capacity. One carrier remains available after each completion. Lean checks the abstract ordered-step implication; an independent explicit state graph confirms 1,533 small cases. Neither certifies the native routes or deadlines.

\paragraph{Native action rule.}
The pinned load-retaining planner uses existing cargo and the actual accepted prefix, not future fault opportunities. The amendment allows the first retained segment ahead of missing work to supply a future empty carrier when the current pool is empty. It does not claim capacity has already been freed. The native queue orders settlement then new pickups. The audit checks the actual inventory clear and station acceptance before each observed use of this capacity. When the initial pool is nonempty the planner is unchanged. With an unfilled first segment and no empty carrier or permitted preceding release, the exact model still refuses.

\paragraph{Development replay.}
The preregistered follow-up crosses the same 264 exogenous opportunity settings with three route scopes. In each, the old and modified planner use the same native movement, authority and station guard. Across 792 paired comparisons, six earlier no-empty refusals now finish; no previously completing arm becomes incomplete. Six receipt-to-reuse chains were checked from actual trace events. In the three previously lost wide-guard cases, the holder of item 9 actually delivers it and becomes empty before returning to collect the missing items. The other carrier retains its later segment. The broader route guard can stay. The three finish at ticks 92, 92 and 93 rather than refusing; the earlier unguarded control finished them at 77. Under the 200-tick horizon, the broad route guard plus amendment has 34 more completing settings than the earlier unguarded planner and loses none. This is not a gain at every deadline, or a statement about field rates or arbitrary warehouse schedules.

\paragraph{What this leaves to verify.}
An earlier repair can accelerate a pickup and thereby create a shortage of idle vehicles even without blocking a physical source. The unchanged cargo and its authority are exactly what can enable the later pickup. A Boolean indicating that no carrier is empty erases the difference between a held first segment and a leading gap. This is a real service change over the previous retain implementation, not an advantage over an exact serial planner using the same mechanism. Independent validation, general composition with repeated faults, and contract acceptance remain open.
