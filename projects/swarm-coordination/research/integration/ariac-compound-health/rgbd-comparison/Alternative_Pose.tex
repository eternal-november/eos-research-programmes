\title{A different camera is a necessary comparison for a logical-camera-only outage}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Native distinction.}
NIST's \href{https://pages.nist.gov/ARIAC_docs/en/2024.1.0/competition/sensors.html}{2024 sensor documentation}
lists static RGB-D and basic logical cameras with the same
listed cost of 500 dollars. The advanced logical camera is
not the comparator. At pinned ARIAC 2024.3.0 the selected
depth callback publishes either the qualified finite measurement
or a blank, and the health callback for that decision uses
\texttt{camera}, not \texttt{logical\_camera}.
The blank can retain a new header stamp. For a finite reading
the depth callback uses the parent's reported measurement time,
rather than the wrapper's own current clock.
The exact selected methods run over substituted message and
frame types. No full ROS/Web bridge, actual clock calibration,
or physics is measured. A stamped blank is not a valid pose.

\paragraph{Matched and unmatched conditions.}
Take a known designated object on a calibrated principal ray
with known orientation. This admits a bounded depth-to-pose
interpretation under the stated visibility and geometry, not
arbitrary six-degree-of-freedom pose estimation. Both comparators
have one camera and, where the experiment permits it, the same
right to retain a scoped earlier sample; the RGB-D policy may
then use that sample too. In the logical-only outage with
common successful pre-exposure and enabled qualified
post-announcement depth, the RGB-D alternative never loses
service the staged logical camera can give, and in 27 of the 48
matched geometry cases captures an extra service opportunity.
When prior capture is disallowed, this can change feasibility.
The actually native observed fact is the payload/status/time seam;
a fresh, timely, service-relevant RGB-D pose remains a premise.

Use separate controls for later authority loss, absent depth,
a blocked or out-of-range ray, and loss of the camera class.
In the broader mixed matrix, outcomes are incomparable because
the modalities have different observed pose and timing supports.
Do not interpret those counts as a fair dominance comparison,
costs of processing and placement, or coverage rates. The
one-use ARIAC service is a separate source, not an RGB-D
implementation or a permission to read private scoring data.

\paragraph{Overlapping scope.}
The combined camera fault for this comparison is interpreted
using the admitted per-type active intervals. The earlier
\#476 diagnostic already established that a source expiry
assignment can restore flags despite another challenge; the
new comparison gives no credit for data from that anomaly.
There is no execution claim beyond the selected callbacks.
The scoped prior-frame mechanism remains useful where the
alternative sensor does not furnish admitted timely evidence;
it is not a new capability for every logical-only outage.
No HeRo comparison or independent mission has been run.
