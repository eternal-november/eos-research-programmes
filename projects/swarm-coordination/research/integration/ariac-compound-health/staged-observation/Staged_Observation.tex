\title{The last usable observation can precede the act that creates its duty}
\maketitle

\paragraph{Environment and interface.}
The source schedule is evaluated in a bounded serial civil
simulation fixture. An already announced and ready order \(A\)
can be submitted after an update at tick \(s\). The selected
native ARIAC acceptance path and next world update announce its
descendant \(B\) at \(s+1\). If configured, that same update
signals a logical-camera blackout and/or a floor-robot
malfunction. The selected Gazebo Classic producer and ARIAC ROS
publication path consume the camera health outcome along this
clock. The native scorer, transport, scene and physical
controllers remain controlled stubs. The potential capture is
offered on one unknown tick, an experimental source envelope.
The controller observes the actual public event only when it
occurs. The authoritative parked-episode and capture-clock
metadata are experimental additions that travel with a published
sample, not stock messages or privileged cache access.

\paragraph{Choice and service.}
The caller must preserve old floor work through its declared
completion cut if the submission would disable that resource.
The new order needs one lawful fresh pose at or after its
announcement and before its deadline. In one arm, the owner
permits stable, prospectively scoped samples to be retained before
announcement. That permission gives no power to act on an
unannounced order. The pose must still be qualified for the same
parked episode at use, within four ticks of exposure. The
alternative exposure law disallows pre-announcement captures.
If the submission causes the camera outage, this latter law
cannot be met by the selected producer: frames before \(s+1\)
are too early and the public branch is suppressed thereafter.
Simulator ground truth or scoring-cache reads are not remedies.

With prospective permission and the same outage, wait for the
first observed still-usable pose and the old completion cut before
submitting. The actual submission is staged after those events,
and the new order spends its named pose read after
announcement. If there is no camera outage, submit as early as the
old effect permits and wait for the authorized pose. No choice of
a later submission could open an earlier deadline or make a late
pose younger on the fixed scope. For one realized public opportunity,
these cases characterize feasibility against enumeration over
all possible submission cuts. When a further modeled disruption
moves the tray into another parking episode before pose use,
the earlier pose is not usable and the journal refuses it.
A whole-family service guarantee is not claimed across that
disruption; the tested independent duty was prequalified and
retains its separate result.

\paragraph{Checked comparison.}
Native submission codes produce 400 selected state cuts. The
native camera and public-wrapper replay covers 24 input types
used by those state cuts, and 1,152 joined fixtures use the
recorded native effects and actual SQLite admission.
271 complete the named service; 85 of those use the blackout
arm. 127 feasible fixtures would lose service under immediate
submission. These are enumerated model counts, not field rates.
An exact matched scheduler agrees with the staged policy, and
the policy does not claim to rescue an arbitrary hidden second
fault. A legal changed authority alternative or producer, when
one exists, must preserve the changed parked-episode cut. This
is a concrete conditional connection between source-triggered
fault timing and active evidence accessibility; it is not a
frozen independent case or a comparative claim about HeRo.
