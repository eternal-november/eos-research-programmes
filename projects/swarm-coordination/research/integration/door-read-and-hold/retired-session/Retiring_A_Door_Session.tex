\title{What an empty door hold log forgets about a delayed request}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

Open-RMF's selected door supervisor remembers the latest OPEN
timestamp while a requester has an active session. It drops that
record on a qualifying CLOSE. A delayed OPEN can then recreate
the retired session. A later holder may be unable to close even
though all current tasks have finished with the door.

\paragraph{Executed behavior.}
Compile the pinned OPEN, CLOSE, message publication and heartbeat
methods with inert clocks and publishers. OPEN inserts the requester
or increases the active timestamp. CLOSE for an active requester
compares its timestamp to the active one; it removes the requester
unless the close is strictly older. After removal a CLOSE command is
published only if no other hold remains. The heartbeat includes only
the active requesters and their request times.

In one matched pair the same requester opens at time 10 and then
closes at 12 or 15 respectively. Both heartbeat states have an
empty active list. An OPEN received afterward with timestamp 13
is accepted in both. Under an order law where CLOSE retires its
own and earlier attempts, it is a valid newer open in the first
history but a delayed old attempt in the second.
The tests inject these delivery orders at the method seam; they
do not assert that a healthy single writer and transport reorder
messages.

A separate positive case retains an unrelated valid session, so
closing the first requester emits no CLOSE. Then a delayed
duplicate OPEN for the retired requester recreates its hold.
The unrelated requester later closes, but the stale hold now
prevents the expected CLOSE command. The replay checks the
supervisor publication, not physical actuation. Another control
keeps a genuinely newer active hold when a stale CLOSE arrives.

\paragraph{Exact repair for a specified order law.}
Fix an authenticated requester, door and epoch with one trusted
ordered version domain. Keep the greatest terminal version after
the active entry is removed. Ignore an OPEN whose version is at
or below that terminal version; record every authorized CLOSE
in the watermark even if it does not remove a newer live hold.
An equal-version close wins by the declared law. The guard in the
replay applies this repair before invoking the native methods.
It rejects the delayed attempts, admits the newer attempt and
lets the unrelated holder's close publish when no real hold
remains.

For each requester this retains the exact needed threshold for
future admissible OPEN tests. If two terminal histories differ
at versions \(c_1<c_2\), an incoming OPEN at \(c_2\) separates their
answers. An empty active map cannot serve that consumer, and with
multiple independent requesters the same ambiguity occurs for
each. A safely drained delivery frontier can authorize dropping
a watermark; an absent active entry alone cannot. Clock reset,
reuse of identities and untrusted version fields require a separate
epoch or authority fence. This is a source-backed comparison
with an ordinary version-watermark guard, not an advancement
claim over a direct versioned session protocol.

Even the repaired ledger does not establish that a robot
physically cleared the doorway or that the door did close.
Those claims still require a valid measurement and a transition
history. No deployment is modified or tested.
