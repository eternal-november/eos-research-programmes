\section*{Spend a read after a confirmed erasure, not at a fixed next phase}
\paragraph{Named observation law.}
Use the four actual modeled position/mode trajectories of PR 867.
The numerical channel has one unknown static offset and bounded
error $e=7/4$ deficit units per read. A distinct authorized slot
can be used once, and every attempted read costs one, including
the erased attempt. At most one read is lost. Its loss is learned
before choosing the next slot. A numeric return does not reveal
what other possibly erased value it substitutes for. The plant
effect of sampling is assumed passive and the selected local
proof arrives by cut 8 or 10 as specified. No delivery-time or
sensor power is inferred from source status.
The one-action label distinguishes whether the late right pulse
is safe. The full-profile label distinguishes the two old profile
types and answers a wider prospective class of queries.

\paragraph{Exact feedback and fixed comparison.}
At any successful time set, two conflicting histories remain
possible iff their sampled difference span is at most $4e$,
using PR 867's common-offset calculation. Therefore the
erasure-only scheduler can use state (next possible phase,
attempts left, losses left, successful phases) and branch on
attempt success or loss. It declares success only once every
conflicting pair is eliminated for every admitted numerical
transcript. The recurrence tests every possible next phase and
requires both branches when a loss remains. A fixed schedule
passes exactly when every single-deletion subset separates.
This is standard finite experiment scheduling, not a new
algorithm relative to ordinary adaptive search.

For the one-action query by cut 8, three attempted reads are
enough with erasure feedback. Try after update 1. If it succeeds,
try 4 and use 5 only if 4 is lost. If 1 is lost, try 3 and 8.
Every terminal branch identifies the actual legal right-pulse
decision. No two-attempt numerical policy can suffice after
one loss and one arbitrary offset; every fixed three-attempt
schedule fails, with an explicit equal-transcript witness for
each of the 56 schedules. Four fixed attempts suffice.
For the larger profile query, the erasure-only controller needs
four versus five fixed attempts by cut 8; by cut 10 it uses
three versus four. The replay checks the returned decision
trees and an actual normalized same-read witness for each
failed tested fixed schedule at the distinguishing budgets.

\paragraph{Observation power and an honest stronger reference.}
In the mode-only arm, source publications at cuts 4 and 5 both
decide the one-action query. Either one may be erased and two
fixed attempts still suffice, less than the numeric-only rule.
By contrast, with one erasure even observing mode at every
phase through 10 cannot recover the full profile: there is a
conflicting pair with at most one differing mode sample.
The gain for one-action numerical resource use is therefore
conditional on unavailability of the stronger mode interface.
No superiority over a same-interface adaptive direct scheduler,
sensor availability in hardware, or independent mission follows.
