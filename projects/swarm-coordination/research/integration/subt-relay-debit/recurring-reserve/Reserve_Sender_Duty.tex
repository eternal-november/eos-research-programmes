\paragraph{Result.}
A reserve robot can obtain the right report and deliver it sooner while
losing its own separate required transmission. Sending to different receivers
does not prevent this: the same sender's allowance is used twice. In the
40-frame calibration the early reassignment delivers all 40 reports and
none of the separate replies. Waiting for the primary sender delivers both
sets. A second route condition flips the decision: the primary cannot
deliver before deadline, but the reserve's later reply is far enough
away that early reassignment now delivers both.

\paragraph{Source and operating envelope.}
This offline replay reuses the selected osrf/subt radio, broker and delivery
bodies at \texttt{083c6a8acb0c25aa1a35e34517e628cd3b52ae77}, as pinned
in \texttt{SOURCE.tsv}. Deterministic path and endpoint fixtures replace
the world, not the byte-accounting branches. A transmitter has a rolling
one-second guard. An accepted transmission debits it before propagation and
before a possible receiving-side refusal. A debit stays live at age exactly
one. The monotonically stamped quarter-second fixture carries native
state across repeated rounds. Its 3.75-second spacing lets each charge
from a preceding frame expire before the next use of that ledger. An
unrelated still-live charge on the opposite direction does not block
the next copy. A frame boundary does not reset any allowance.

In a frame, primary $P$ sends the report copy to reserve $Q$ at $-1.5$
and $Q$'s local application sees the data before the decision. Separately,
an old failed transmission at $-0.25$ has charged $P$. The controller can
use $Q$ immediately to send the report to Base or wait for $P$ to send at
$1.0$. No earlier Base transmission is authorized in this fixture.
The $Q$ reply to $P$ is mandatory at an independently specified slot,
and cannot be moved or serviced by another actor. Application callbacks
count deliveries at the named destination, not sender status. Frame
identity is local in this exact fixture; equal-size packets do not
establish identity after arbitrary rerouting or replay.

\paragraph{Exact boundary.}
Let $a$ be report size, $b$ reply size, and $C$ the byte capacity:
each packet alone fits. If the reply arrives $\delta$ seconds after
the early reserve send, the reply remains feasible exactly when
$\delta>1$ or $a+b\le C$, in the absence of other charged traffic.
For the late-primary choice, $P$'s old debit is strictly older than one
when it sends; the report then needs a good primary path at $1.0$.
$Q$ retains its allowance for the reply. There is no Base-side
interference in these fixtures because the reply is to $P$. In other
topologies both send and receive credit must be checked.

A concrete pair has $C=5$, $a=3$, $b=4$, old debit $4$. In one history
the reply is due at $0.5$ and the late primary path works. The
early choice irreversibly spends the reply's capacity. In the other
the reply is due at $1.25$ and the late primary path fails, so only
early reassignment meets both obligations. Assume that before deciding
the reserve has the same copy and local past in both histories and has
not learned which future branch holds. Its admissible initial choice
then cannot guarantee both obligations. Trying both senders does not
repair the first history once the early transmission has spent the
allowance. A truthful, timely distinction would suffice in this
restricted two-action envelope; no claim is made that such a signal
is available from stock radio state, a deadline report, or a mesh heartbeat.

\paragraph{Comparison.}
An explicit uncapped event list with the same inclusive window,
both endpoints' accounting, and the same paths matches the selected
native bodies. The suite checks 110,784 grid frames plus the named
40-frame calibration for each policy and history. No field rate or
advantage over an equally informed scheduler is implied. The positive
lesson is to charge the reassignment to the sender's other pending
duties before changing the role. This is not independently selected
I1 or I2 evidence.
