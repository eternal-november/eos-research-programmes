\title{Finite boundary summaries for a paced directed handoff pipeline}

\paragraph{What is and is not solved.}
This is a decidable recurrent subclass, not a result about
general action-dependent evidence or endogenous changes of
route. Fix a civil corridor of \(n\) consecutive handoffs between
stations \(0,\ldots,n\). Both endpoints of a handoff use
one exclusive service port for its operation. Each link has a
nonempty finite menu of verified configurations, specifying
which port at each end and which transfer/latch discipline the
hardware can use. An endpoint can support two incident handoffs
at once only on different independent ports. The configured
ports are chosen once at commissioning; changing them consumes a
maintenance operation prohibited while this continuous workload
is offered. This is a plant restriction, not a synthesis search
bound. The replay uses two ports and four configurations;
the proof works for arbitrary fixed finite configuration alphabet.

\paragraph{Supply, effect and clocks.}
Offered jobs enter in order with lower spacing \(P\).
The service contract on each station is to complete and export
a qualified handoff within \(g>0\) of the preceding qualified
arrival, including the front offer. Scope is one downstream
direction, with no overtaking or alternate route. A qualified
local start must be realizable when both declared ports are
available; its actual physical transfer and recognized evidence
can take any admitted duration in \((0,g]\), with an all-\(g\)
environment supported. This is a bounded service assumption
on approved macro-operations, not proof that the interlock,
motion, notice or recovery is physically realizable in \(g\)
for a new configuration. A schedule cannot fabricate the latch.
At a successful physical transfer the receiver becomes the sole
next certifier for that load; acknowledgment of a proposal
does not transfer custody. The effect/latch primitive provides
scoped non-replay: no copy of an earlier use can qualify a later
use of the port, and the old producer relinquishes practical
control before the result enables the next use. This can be by
an actual physical one-slot interlock; importing stale network
receipts instead requires a separate freshness result.

The runtime controller observes only its local qualified trigger
and relative duration, not another port's clock or a common
view of the corridor. Relative durations measured at different
ports have the stated common bound and no unmodeled drift;
clock offsets are irrelevant. Use physical half-open reservation
intervals: earlier effects and releases precede a coincident new
start. Worst-case latencies include primitive evidence and
transport. A local controller may reserve both of its installed
ports starting on the qualified trigger, wait for the actual
transfer, and only export the new successor trigger at \(g\).
Pads cost time and hold the ports. Later jobs are not refused.
Environment progress means time reaches each \emph{finite}
event deadline; no plant adversary may stop time permanently.
A finite maximal run with pending qualified work is incorrect.
After a finite last offer and completed service, an idle run is
allowed. There is no bound on mission duration or global reset.

\paragraph{Exact physical boundary.}
For each fixed configuration, the admissible saturated offer
trace \(r_k=kP\), with all local operations taking \(g\),
forces each start at \(r_k+i g\) on link \(i\) (numbering
links from 0). It cannot start earlier than its predecessor,
and any delay leaves less than \(g\) to meet its local due
time. At \(P<g\), even two first-link operations conflict.
When \(g\le P<2g\), at an internal station the outbound
operation for job \(k\) and inbound operation for job \(k+1\)
overlap in this trace. If they need the same physical port,
no controller, including a centralized fully informed one,
can serve every admitted job under that configuration. If
\(P\ge2g\), they do not overlap; equality is admissible
because the intervals are half-open and settlement is ordered.
The two consecutive jobs on one link do not overlap for
\(P\ge g\). For a nonsaturated admissible trace and the padded
solution, jobs \(k\) and \(k+d\) differ by at least \(dP\).
For \(|d|\ge2\) the two incident intervals cannot have
positive overlap; same-job incident operations also only touch.
Thus the preceding two-job constraints are sufficient for
every trace, not just the saturated one. A local finite-state
pad-and-latch program realizes every compatible choice for
all jobs. There is at most one active job per boundary; at a
middle station, inbound and outbound for different jobs can
overlap, and each completed transfer changes that job's
actual certifier. All admitted jobs finish end-to-end exactly
\(ng\) after entry in this implementation. With finite timer
ticks or the specified local hardware timer, controller memory
is bounded by local \(g\) and the installed mode, not \(n\)
or job count. The replay's index for jobs is an evaluator label,
not a globally visible runtime counter.

\paragraph{Constructive finite summary and replacement.}
For a corridor block export the relation on its two outer link
modes: the pair is admitted iff a joint internal mode assignment
can meet the physical test. At a junction insert the exact
binary port-compatibility relation, not an independent check
that each side can operate in isolation. Compute the block
relation by Boolean relational composition, retaining a middle
witness for any entry used during synthesis. External contexts
with the same \(P,g\) and no hidden cross-block resource see only
this boundary relation. An equal relation preserves closed
realizability, and its new stored witnesses reconstruct the
replacement's controllers. If the context can pin both exterior
modes, any absent/present difference is observable.
Anonymous equality of port counts is not enough. For example,
when overlap is forced, the two outer links both pinned to
\((0,0)\) require distinct middle modes from \((0,1),(1,0)\);
each adjacent pair is separately possible, but the whole
three-link corridor is not. The state summary is at most
\(|M|^2\) bits per block for a fixed mode alphabet \(M\);
one must retain the decomposition or selector to construct
all \(n\) outputs. A linear chain of tables costs
\(O(n|M|^3)\) to compile complete summaries, or
\(O(n|M|^2)\) for an assignment on the closed instance.
Latency \(ng\) is a separate scaling statement, not encoded
as an arbitrary external deadline in the bounded Boolean
interface. A shorter or longer replacement can change a fixed
end-to-end deadline even when its relation agrees. Exact
timing reuse requires matching length or changing the
length-scaled charter. Hot replacement while work is live is
not proved.

\paragraph{Related models and checked scope.}
This is a path-constraint reduction because worst-case
saturated jobs force the relevant timed intervals. Ordinary
path constraint propagation at the same interface ties.
\href{https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2014.639}{Muscholl and Walukiewicz}
use finite rendezvous components and causal memory. A fixed
pairwise physical completion can be encoded as a local
rendezvous, but mapping these real-time offer/due constraints
and genuine private delay to a general decidability theorem
would require a separate observer-preserving encoding.
\href{https://lmcs.episciences.org/736}{Jacobs--Bloem} study
template synthesis on parameterized token networks, not these
instance-specific commissioned modes. The sole certifier for
one job is not a single global token: different jobs can be
live on adjacent links. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04561}{Bollig et al.}
study a two-process externally restricted message adversary;
real custody enablement and shared physical ports here are
not such an adversary. Acyclic geometry alone supplies no
information hierarchy.

The replay compares 11,522 mode/cadence cases with exhaustive
configuration enumeration and direct interval collision,
and exercises 140,400 modeled events up to 64 links.
Lean checks relational association and the two exact
overlap inequalities, not the whole event simulation.
This is a constructive but deliberately narrow moonshot
subclass. A failed physical latch, unbounded blackout, a
live rewiring, risk-driven pace, or arbitrary interacting
local controllers still need separate theories.
