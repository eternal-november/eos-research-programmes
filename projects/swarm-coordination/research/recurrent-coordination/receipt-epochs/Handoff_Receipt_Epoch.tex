\title{Receipt freshness for repeated custody handoffs}
\date{}

\maketitle
The model is a path of civilian service stations with a custody scope on each
edge. A work offer can wait at every edge at once, including both edges at an
interior station. Each station has one port for irreversible handoffs. Custody
ownership alternates on each edge after an actual handoff. A physically
enforced local gate admits at most one outstanding offer on that edge and
does not reopen until both endpoints separately see the handoff. Neither
the unfinished work at another edge nor a global round counter is available
to the receiving station. The functional guarantee is no duplicate or
premature handoff and eventual fulfillment of each admitted offer under the
fairness conditions below.

\paragraph{Interface.}
The current custodian witnesses a private physical clearance before issuing a
receipt. Clearing this hold does not yet transfer custody. The receipt names
its scope and carries an authenticated source identity and a finite tag. The
scope is the incident ingress, not a globally numbered identity stored in
each local control. The new custodian may queue one job upon receiving a
matching receipt in the waiting phase; later copies cannot create a second
job. When the port serves the job, the physical action transfers custody.
A gate reopening is one completed advance of this scope. A controlled relay
allows duplicate delivery only through age \(L\), measured in these advances,
not in elapsed time. The receipt contains no absolute generation; the
generation in the replay annotates provenance for verification only. This
model assumes the gate, the clearance witness and the relay age bound are
sound. It does not make them sound after controller or hardware corruption.

\paragraph{Exact tag alphabet.}
Number completed advances \(g=0,1,\ldots\) for the proof. Ignoring the
choice of initial endpoint, the authorized signer is \(g\bmod 2\).
Set \(M=\lfloor L/2\rfloor+1\) and give generation \(g\) tag
\(\lfloor g/2\rfloor\bmod M\); the gate increments its tag after every
second handoff. Equal signer and tag mean equal residue modulo \(2M\).
No strictly earlier receipt at age at most \(L\) has both the current
signer and tag, since \(2M>L\). In particular \(L=0,1\) needs just one
tag value, using signer alone. Directly reducing \(g\) modulo \(M\) can
fail: \(L=2\) gives \(M=2\), and generations zero and two coincide.
For necessity, the \(M\) even generations \(0,2,\ldots,2(M-1)\) form a clique
of same-signer alternatives: the earlier can survive to the later. If two
share a tag, replace a fresh receipt at the later decision by the retained
copy while withholding the new clearance. Receiver history up to the
decision, current phase, and input are identical. An exact receipt-only
decoder must accept one and reject the other. This lower bound allows the
receiver to have learned its complete prior local history; it excludes
extra evidence about the current clearance, and it assumes the earlier
receipt can survive without changing that prior local history.

More generally, for a fixed known signer sequence the minimum alphabet is
the maximum number of occurrences of one signer in any window of \(L+1\)
epochs. Such a window gives the clique lower bound. Greedily taking a tag
not used by that signer in the preceding \(L\) epochs gives the upper
bound. That colorability statement does not give a bounded controller for
predicting or learning an arbitrary signer sequence.

\paragraph{Uniform composition.}
Maintain one phase per edge (empty, waiting, queued, or observing), its
physical custodian, tag, two observation bits, and a controlled relay with
at most \(L+1\) live age slots. Each edge in the queued phase occurs once,
in the station FIFO of its receiving endpoint. An interior station has at
most two such jobs, and queues can name them by incident port. A handoff
consumes its job and only that job; an observation can affect only its edge.
These statements hold at initialization and are preserved by offers,
clearance, packet arrival, service and the two endpoint reads. Freshness
ensures that an admitted job was authorized by the current custodian after
clearance. Clearing and transferring are different transitions: withholding
one cannot be hidden by advancing the other. No second job on that edge can
enter during the observing phase. This gives a uniform controller and bound
on local verification state for every finite path, independent of path
length and number of rounds. Directed queues, gates and relay age slots
are part of the construction, not free information. Dropping absolute
generations preserves every projected transition and all service and safety
events; dropping actual phase or either required endpoint read does not
give the stated interface.

The environment must eventually supply clearance for each admitted offer,
deliver its genuine receipt while that offer waits, service persistently
nonempty station queues, and supply both reads after a transfer. These
are separate premises. A waiting job has at most one FIFO predecessor;
at most two service opportunities at that station suffice from nomination
to transfer. In particular, back-to-back work on a neighboring edge
cannot continuously overtake it. This argument works for indefinitely
many admitted offers. It establishes no time bound without bounds on the
four environment events. Offers made to a closed gate must be retained
externally or retried; neither progress nor a finite internal queue is
claimed for unbounded unadmitted demand.

\paragraph{A counterexample and its limit.}
Remove the age guarantee but allow one distinguishable-to-the-environment
copy to be retained alongside ordinary fresh traffic. With any finite
tag alphabet, two epochs of the same signer have the same tag along some
execution; a retained copy can reappear before the second clearance. For
this receipt-only admission interface, safety and delivery of positively
admissible offers cannot both be exact. A buffer with room for just one
persistently retained copy in addition to fresh traffic suffices for this
witness. This is not impossibility with bounded-capacity channels in general:
a different handshake can use capacity, retransmissions or explicit
purge evidence to change the admission interface. It is not a claim about
arbitrary interactive protocols or about credential forgery.

\paragraph{Comparisons and checks.}
\href{https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9293\#section-3.4.2}{RFC 9293
\(\S 3.4.2\)} addresses sequence reuse and old duplicates in TCP.
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3632}{Dolev et al.} give a stabilizing
data-link construction for a different bounded-capacity channel model.
Here the reuse clock is the completed physical transfer with both local
reads, and the authenticated signer is part of the decoder. The study
does not establish priority for modular tags or superiority over those
protocols. The direct finite product checks the same local rules, without
an operational speed or service advantage. Three Lean theorems check
tag admissibility, separation, and the exact alphabet lower bound; they
do not formalize physical effects or fairness. The independent immutable
state traversal explores the small products, and the seeded event replay
checks overlapping actual operations on longer paths. Neither finite
check by itself proves the arbitrary-path invariant.

This is a restricted recurrent baseline and obstruction for the moonshot,
not its acceptance. It does not synthesize arbitrary compatible local
controllers or handle dependencies crossing the shared station beyond
FIFO contention; it uses a trusted local two-ended gate, authenticated
receipt sources and controlled age. An exact next comparison would make
one edge's current clearance depend on another edge's still unsettled duty,
and determine which prior proof can be reused without adding another
trusted observation.
