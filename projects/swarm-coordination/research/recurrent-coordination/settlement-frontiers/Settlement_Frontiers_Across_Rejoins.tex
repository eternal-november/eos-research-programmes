\paragraph{Interface.}
A fixed issuer starts custody jobs numbered consecutively, without reset or reuse. The job retains its old load-bearing support until a successor physically accepts its load. This actual acceptance enables an authoritative, identity-bound settlement fact. The receiving gate consumes such facts to admit each job's successor use. It may not do so on physical intuition or merely because a deployment changed epoch. The issuer and gate are stable authorities; split groups may learn different subsets of the same issued and settled facts, but they cannot invent facts, issue on each other's behalf, or reset the issuer. Durable learned facts and a prefix issued upper bound are available locally after each arrival. Transport can delay, reorder and duplicate genuine facts, including across split and rejoin. There is no inference from silence. Gates need not get all facts together, and they do not poll an external history oracle. This is a documentary admission protocol behind a physical settlement interface, not a model of cryptographic implementation, storage failure, or unlimited physical capacity. A cancellation could count only with independent authority and proof that the old work can have no remaining effect; this model does not generate cancellations.

The protected behavior is exact per-ID admission and processing of valid settlement facts once delivered. Returning a late receipt must enable the associated successor permission if not already settled. An unresolved load stays on its old support. Physical support and documentary readiness can differ even after physical return, and the latter is allowed to depend on the first received receipt. Issuing a new job additionally uses the chosen backlog or reorder rule below. No bounded-time receipt or indefinite issuance is promised under arbitrary stalls.

\section{Exact compression and rejoin}
Let $U$ be the known issued upper bound, $S\subseteq[1,U]$ the learned authoritative settlement set, and $F$ the largest $f$ with $[1,f]\subseteq S$. Keep $F$ and $T=S\cap(F,U]$ besides $U$. Recover membership by testing $i\le F$ or $i\in T$ for an issued $i$. On a genuine receipt, add its ID and extend the contiguous frontier as far as the stored facts permit. A receipt below the frontier is already accounted for. This answers all exact admission and frontier-based issuance queries, not the historical audit question of which actor learned what first.

For two compatible views of the same issuer, take $U=\max(U_1,U_2)$ and start at $F=\max(F_1,F_2)$. Unite their above-frontier facts, discard those below or at the new prefix, and normalize by advancing through actual contiguous facts. The resulting triple is exactly compression of $(\max(U_1,U_2),S_1\cup S_2)$, because the two old contiguous prefixes unite to the longer one. Identical input facts therefore reassemble independently of parenthesization and duplication. The use of a common issuer and same immutable ID semantics is essential: $\max$ is not a proof that two independently minted names share one event or a common causal history. If observation of issued bounds can lag receipts, carry the whole authenticated issue prefix needed by that receipt before committing it; this checker assumes such valid facts come with known issuance.

This reassociation changes neither physical effects nor what was already observed when a decision ran. Moving a receipt across an admission decision can change that decision. Swapping an actual settlement with a conflicting new physical transfer is even stronger: new admission succeeds after actual release of a one-place support and is blocked in the reverse order. No commutation of genuine transfers is used.

\section{Exact space and its enabling assumption}
Enforce an issuer-side width rule $U-F\le B$ at authority, refusing an issue that would exceed it. Alternatively obtain the same bound from a total issue-to-observed-settlement deadline $\Delta$ (including physical acceptance and delivery) and a bound $B$ on issued jobs in any window of length $\Delta$ with the corresponding event-order convention. The oldest unresolved job must then have been issued within that window. Merging views each of width at most $B$ preserves the bound: the merged $F$ is at least the $F$ of whichever view supplies the merged $U$. An arbitrarily lagging raw view may have a different frontier; this is a closure claim for inputs satisfying the width invariant, not a promise about snapshots a transport has not delivered.

At fixed counters with $U>F$, the first hole $F+1$ is known. A bitmap of the remaining $U-F-1$ slots is sufficient. It is also necessary in the worst case. With fixed $F=0$ and $U=B$, issue the same $B$ jobs, physically settle all, and deliver any subset of receipts $2,\dots,B$ after rejoin while still delaying receipt 1. These are reached histories with identical current physical state, issued upper bound, first hole and joined mode. Two different subsets differ at some identity $j$; the same next admission query for $j$ distinguishes them. Thus exactly $2^{B-1}$ behavioral states are required at these fixed counters. In particular a new epoch or constant age timeout that forgets all these states cannot preserve exact service. The counter values themselves have a growing identifier representation; $B-1$ is informational state beyond those counters, not a constant total bit bound over indefinite execution.

A bound on the \emph{number} of unresolved IDs alone need not bound their identity information. Suppose issue is allowed with fewer than $K$ unresolved jobs and exactly one new job is issued at a time; each issue is counted unresolved until its receipt. Fix $U=n\ge2$ and keep ID 1 unresolved. A cut can retain any hole set of size $<K$ containing 1 by promptly settling all other receipts as their jobs issue. It can retain $K$ holes iff those holes also include $n$: otherwise the issuance after the last of the $K$ persisted holes would be blocked. The same sequential construction attains every allowed set, keeping only a transient slot for intervening nonholes. Hence at fixed $(U,F)=(n,0)$ the exact number of distinguishable states for $K\ge2$ is
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{K-2}\binom{n-1}{j}+\binom{n-2}{K-2}.
\]
The same single permission query distinguishes different hole sets. Already $K=3$ gives $2n-2$ states at fixed counters, and grows without bound across arbitrary issued prefixes. A smaller long-run footprint would need a stronger recurrence envelope, stronger observable completion/status evidence, or a weaker requirement than exact per-ID admission. A bounded width policy can stall with only one old hole and no physical loss; this is a cost, not an unconditional liveness proof.

\section{Replay and relation to existing work}
The finite replay checks 3,969 pairs of raw settled-set unions against compressed merge, exact frontier state counts through width 9, the sparse-hole count against an independent enumerator, and 1,750 issues across 250 split/rejoin cycles. In each repeated cycle a real old physical support stays live through rejoin while later receipts arrive first. The old permission changes only after actual acceptance and observed merge, and a duplicate does not grant twice. This execution supplies the progress schedule explicitly; it does not prove progress against infinite stalls.

The strongest direct account is already a sequence frontier plus holes and exact union. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04469}{Baquero, Almeida and Shoker} use causal stability and semantic operation-log compaction in a broader distributed replication setting. \href{https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/43864.pdf}{Akidau and colleagues} separate event time, progress estimates and handling of late records; an estimated watermark here would not be authority to clear real pending work. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01136}{Finkbeiner, Klein and Metzger} preserve unfinished temporal obligations when replacing a running implementation but do not provide this protocol's receipt grammar. The project's \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/blob/e1348964f73704212d2d8b48d5982a5ed600a2ff/projects/contextual-composition/foundations/native-whole-scope-feedback/Nonblocking_Recurrent_Assembly_Reading_Route.tex}{nonblocking recurrent assembly} keeps original commitment, read-cut and historical material as separate inputs. That richer assembly does not justify forgetting them merely because this particular fixed-issuer protocol admits a smaller exact admission state. No novelty over direct frontier/causal stability machinery, unbounded safe issuance, multiple issuing authorities, or full C2 acceptance is claimed.
