\paragraph{Modeled interface.}
The selected physics setter input in the lift replay takes a model
local vector and rotates it into world coordinates using the pose
read at consumption. The two-writer replay separately shows how
horizontal and vertical local coordinates can survive or be lost at
that seam. Those studies do not assign real-world authority. Here
consider a declared interface whose robot owner supplies a horizontal
world vector $h$ and whose lift owner supplies a vertical world
vector $v$. The operator may choose local commands before the exact
consumer rotation $R$ is known, using an observation $o(R)$.
Rotations are invertible and both contributions share the same $R$.
An aggregate downstream law requires only $R(x+y)=h+v$.
A different law with separately attributable scoped contributions
requires $Rx=h$ and $Ry=v$. In the latter the pieces are checked
before summing, not inferred from a native undifferentiated setter.
Both laws here require exact effects, not approximate tracking.
No mass, stopping, scheduling or tolerance model is assumed.

\paragraph{Exact observation quotient.}
For a given observation cell and fixed demand, a frozen aggregate
local vector exists exactly when $R^{-1}(h+v)$ is constant on the
cell. A frozen pair with the separate law exists exactly when both
$R^{-1}h$ and $R^{-1}v$ are constant there. Necessity is uniqueness
of each inverse value. Sufficiency is choosing any compatible
representative and its inverse vector or pair. For a declared family
of demands with the same observation interface these tests must
hold for each demand. Thus the signatures across all demands give
the coarsest valid partition: every coarser collapse of distinct
signatures identifies some demand with two different required
local answers. Every refinement is adequate with the same law.
The Lean result checks the pairwise inverse equivalence. Its global
interpretation follows by assigning each supported observation cell
a representative, not by assuming advance access to the true pose.

\paragraph{A sharp separation and cost.}
Let $h$ be world $x$, $v$ world $z$, each unit length, and allow
identity or the proper rotation interchanging $x$ and $z$ and
reversing $y$. Both rotations fix $h+v$. The same aggregate local
command works, while the separately attributed pair must remain
$(x,z)$ in one frame and become $(z,x)$ in the other.
No observation merging those cases can support the separate
guarantee. For the 24 proper signed axis permutations the aggregate
signature has 12 values: the omitted axis and the signs of the
two present coordinates. The separate pair has 24: the preimages
of the first and third basis vectors fix the remaining oriented
basis vector. A fixed-length single frame summary must therefore
use at least 4 bits for the aggregate issue and 5 bits for the
separate issue in that family. These are theoretical distinctions,
not measured packet costs. Enumeration confirms both counts
and compares against a direct local-vector search on 1{,}400
mode-and-demand cases.

\paragraph{Obligations on a repair.}
A prior pose sample does not automatically realize these cells:
the qualified observation must describe the pose at the setter
cut, or a certified relation must preserve the needed signature
through that cut. If a later pose changes the inverse horizontal
and vertical contributions while fixing their sum, a cached
aggregate permission remains insufficient for the scoped law.
A fresh frame observation can resolve that uncertainty in the
model but cannot establish the writer's ownership, a right to
act, or physical compliance. The native Gazebo/Open-RMF source
does not implement this labelled decomposition or a qualified
frame attestation. An equally informed direct assembler can
choose the same vectors. This is a scope/evidence separation and
not an implementation win, effect-settlement proof, or I1 claim.
