\title{A later named withdrawal needs more than the old occupancy profile}
\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle

\paragraph{Result and scope.}
Two completions agree on every occupied bay, old receipts and cutoff
time. A later grant to withdraw exactly one old shelf has opposite
effects on a named crossing. A locally qualified conditional grant
can withdraw the right shelf in either completion without revealing
the old placement to the issuer. The same-information direct policy
ties. The result is a two-bay native warehouse test and an exact
finite profile law, not independent transfer, validation of a live
sensor, or closure of the multiple-owner obligation.

\paragraph{Actors, evidence and native effects.}
In the pinned RWARE fixture, old carriers deliver distinct shelves $X$
and $Y$ and actually unload them into two authorized cells. The two
historical trajectories swap which cargo occupies which cell. Both
initial delivery rewards occur at tick~1; padded native unloads occur
at ticks~11 and~36. At the cutoff, both old carriers have cleared and
settled. Both shelf-layer occupancy masks, complete agent poses
(including headings), recorded reward sequences and timing summary
are identical. The issuer has this coarse retrospective record, not
the historical movement log, exact labels, or an omniscient view of
the shelf-index layer. A different archived observation could
preserve the necessary distinction and is not ruled out.

Two unloaded retrieval helpers approach their respective bays
before the grant cut. The model grants retrieval of $X$, not $Y$.
This does not establish a real signature system: recognition of this
authority at each actuator is varied explicitly. A qualified,
operative local label is modeled by reading the native shelf identity
\emph{at the helper's own cell}; an erased reading leaves both
identities possible. Only a recognized grant and a nonempty set of
compatible identities all covered by that grant authorize the
attempt. The source executes a native pickup, departure and
relocation, then a loaded successor attempts its independently
specified three-step route, with no alternate route in the window.
Independent work obtains an actual reward in the grant wave.
The due task is not silently exchanged for the other shelf or bay.

The conditional grant lifts $X$ and only $X$ in both old placements
when its colocated identity and authority are available. $Y$ is never
moved and can still block a due route through its bay, exactly as
the fixed task requires. Loss of authority recognition at the correct
helper or loss of its identity evidence prevents that withdrawal,
regardless of what the issuer would otherwise allow. The three
outcomes are authorized pickup, actual footprint clearance, and
subsequent paid goal; the movement and goal are checked natively.
A fixed-site attempt equipped with the same local guard
acts only when its chosen site happens to contain $X$. If it lacked
that guard, no nonempty blind choice would be lawful in both histories.
This is an information/authority comparison to a fixed-site command
class, not superiority over a dynamically interpreted grant.
An exact direct clause tests the same locally available label,
recognized permission, and storage effect, and agrees in all
28 logged cases.

\paragraph{Exact reusable profile.}
Assume a bijection $p$ from $n$ named bays to $n$ unique named
shelves, the authorized withdrawal catalog $\mathcal D$ is fixed,
and a query asks if bay $b$ would be clear after all the requested
shelves in $D\in\mathcal D$ were lawfully physically removed.
Timing, other obstructions and actuator budgets are held fixed or
out of scope for this oracle. The answer is $p(b)\in D$. The
minimal full response profile is the assignment to each bay of
the membership vector of its shelf in $\mathcal D$. If two
placements differ on a vector at some bay, a declared subset
separates them. Conversely, equal vectors at each bay give the
same answers for every query. Thus with class sizes
$n_1,\ldots,n_k$ the exact number of summary values is
\[
 \frac{n!}{\prod_i n_i!}.
\]
This is the distinct-row argument for deterministic one-way
representation applied to a named-permutation model; see the
\href{https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/073/revision/1/download/}
{primary treatment of the row characterization}.
It bounds a single stored summary servicing all those future
queries, not a live interactive protocol with fresh cell
inspection or per-query disclosure.

If a new permitted subset $D_*$ splits each old role class
of size $n_i$ into parts $m_i$ and $n_i-m_i$, each old profile
fiber splits into exactly $\prod_i {n_i\choose m_i}$ answer
classes. In an old word of roles, the $n_i$ bays occupied by a
class are already named; choose which $m_i$ have the new right.
Those choices uniquely produce the added answer row, and all
are achieved by actual permutations in the assumed family.
No split occurs exactly when the new predicate is constant
within every old role. This characterizes extra exact retained
distinctions for the fixed catalog change, not a required
violation of the old disclosure rule. In the fixture the
qualified local conditional act supplies a different interface
and need not transmit this global refinement at all. If no
legally accessible identity survives and no active local
read exists, the old archived record cannot answer the new
named question.

The finite comparator enumerates 53,963 placements across
66 small catalogs and checks the quotient and splitting counts
against explicit query rows. Lean checks the exact quotient
equivalence and the nonrecoverable split witness, as well as
the safety implication for a correctly qualified local view.
Neither software check proves a hardware sensor model,
availability of fresh reads, or safety for any assignment
outside the scoped authority and source fixture.
