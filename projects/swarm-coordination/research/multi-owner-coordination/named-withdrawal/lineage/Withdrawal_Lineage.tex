\paragraph{The issue.}
The
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/584}
{earlier two-bay replay} requires a qualified same-site label under the
new authority to retrieve only shelf $X$. The same label can have been
correct earlier and be invalid now. An identity-preserving re-seat
and an exchange both advance the generation. Neither the public
occupancy nor the generation by itself determines what may be moved.
This test is ordinary authorized source reconfiguration, not an
attack or validation of operational credentials.

\paragraph{Native evidence creation.}
Before the new grant, the old authorized holders execute zero, one,
or two paired site operations. The native step actually lifts,
moves and replaces the objects, using a temporary buffer and
clearance motions. A re-seat preserves the placement; an exchange
reverses it. For each operation both carriers have restored their
parking poses and are unloaded before the source records the
site permutation. Both choices produce the same occupancy masks,
recorded old completion times, clear agents and new cutoff
timing. The change count, when separately observed at the new
cut, is the same for the same number of events, independent of
their permutation. Intermediate native steps are real but not
known to the recipient except through declared evidence.

The delivered typed record carries the effect (identity or swap)
and the predecessor/successor generations. It does not carry an
absolute replacement placement. The test supplies those records
from the native stored identities before and after the actual
motions; it does not show that a production recorder reliably
publishes them. A consumer who may see the initial placement
profile and the complete chain can transport that profile
without a new global read. This interface does not permit a
recipient to learn data barred by its scope. In the two-identity
case the profile can equivalently be the initial bay of $X$ and
the linked operation bits; the proof needs no arbitrary cargo
history. The tests assume no unseen changes outside the declared
version interval before actuation.

\paragraph{Exact distinction.}
Write the site of $X$ as a Boolean. A trusted re-seat leaves it
unchanged and an exchange flips it. Compose the recorded effects
in generation order. An incomplete or misaligned chain is not
treated as a fresh label. For any one missing delta, keeping
all shared observations and all other deltas fixed, there are
native matched branches in which that delta re-seats and exchanges
respectively. The two terminal sites differ: any suffix of
involutive or identity steps preserves disagreement. Thus no
exact authorization can be recovered from those observations
alone. A different channel giving an absolute later site, or
a more restrictive plant law, can restore it.
Two actual exchanges, by contrast, recover the original
placement in a newer generation; requiring an unchanged counter
is conservative. Reusing a bare earlier label is sometimes
wrong and cannot be justified by eventual equality in one run.

\paragraph{Matched tests and limits.}
Across 152 native trials the complete transported proof and the
fresh same-cell check authorize the same single native pickup,
movement to a safe footprint and eventual due service, at their
respective assumed information access. The same-information
direct parity classifier agrees. In forty omitted-delta cases
two different terminal placements remain possible under the
declared source choices. Twelve stale-only decisions would
propose the protected object; these proposals are audited and
not executed. No version-only rejection is counted as a service
gain. Independent work is kept unchanged and the rightful
permit is held fixed through the scoped act and due crossing.
The Lean statements check parity, one-gap obstruction,
and reversal cancellation, not a production evidence service.
Authored finite states, no runtime authentication, sample-rate,
independent case, or R5/I1 closure claim.
