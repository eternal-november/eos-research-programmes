\title{Exact cached composition of historical authority windows}

\paragraph{Law and kernel.}
The consumer's charter stipulates one common historical cut for a combined operation. For instance, using an old access right together with an appointment to issue a successor must describe one coherent administrative context. Independently vested rights whose combination at different historical cuts is lawful are outside this charter. Freeze the causality poset and version meanings. The eligible historical cuts do not establish current authority, clearing of incumbents, or actor willingness; those are consuming checks, not historical primitives. Assume truthful disclosed bounds and complete predecessor information for the proof. An expired or withheld dependency cannot be silently turned into a known negative.

\paragraph{Interval presentation.}
Cuts are order ideals of the finite event poset, represented by prefix lengths on each authority ledger. Intersection and union of cuts are coordinatewise minimum and maximum. A grant admits cuts in the coordinate box \(\ell\le x\le h\). Let \(L\) be the downward closure of the prefixes specified by \(\ell\). Let \(U\) be the union of all cuts below \(h\), equivalently the events whose entire causal past, including themselves, fits \(h\). Then \(L\) is least above \(\ell\), \(U\) greatest below \(h\), and the grant admits precisely the lattice interval \([L,U]\), empty if \(L\not\le U\). One can replace the original grant by these two endpoints without changing any subsequent common-cut test against any other grant on the frozen history. Conversely, if singleton-cut partners are permitted as tests, every distinct interval has a different acceptance answer for some such partner. Hence the semantic equivalence is exactly equality of the admitted interval, not equality of its earliest cut alone.

\paragraph{Replacement test and sharp negative witnesses.}
For previously verified intervals \(A=[L_A,U_A]\), \(B=[L_B,U_B]\), their composition in this common-cut semantics is
\[
 \big[L_A\cup L_B,\ U_A\cap U_B\big],
\]
with the usual emptiness check. For nonempty inputs, the only new tests are \(L_A\le U_B\) and \(L_B\le U_A\). No old proof of internal compatibility is redone. A dense summary makes composition linear in the number of shared ledger coordinates once the bounds have actually been constructed; it does not bound their acquisition or validation cost. Retiring a conjunct is not computed by inverting this join: for example, intersecting \([0,1]\) with either \([0,2]\) or \([0,3]\) gives \([0,1]\), but removing the first interval gives different answers. Retain provenance or a decomposition if removal is an allowed operation. In a finite group, joint emptiness occurs iff some \(L_i\not\le U_j\), including \(i=j\). Therefore one named event required by \(i\) and excluded by \(j\) is a sufficient local explanation once those implications are known. The group can have all singles feasible but a conflicting pair, so two is sharp.

\paragraph{First broken premise.}
Take an independent reference ledger with versions \(0,\dots,m-1\). Issuer \(i\)'s grant accepts all versions except \(i\). The forbidden revision is bracketed by periods of validity when \(i\) is interior, for example one administrative rescission followed by a re-grant. Each admissible version set is a union of at most two discrete intervals. All \(m\) grants together have no witness; omitting grant \(i\) leaves exactly \(i\). Thus the bound two is lost for an arbitrarily wide family despite truthful reports and one common reference ledger. This does not mean arbitrary certificate encodings require \(m\) bits: it bounds the number of member grants in a directly unsatisfiable subfamily. The full conjunctive causal-cut algorithm from the preceding study still applies to intersected allowed sets, and selecting a contemporaneous known snapshot would avoid historical search only if it is admissible under the actual charter.

\paragraph{Civil handoff and comparison.}
In the two-owner fixture the old condition excludes \(B\)'s withdrawal, whereas the new permission needs the amendment at \(A\). In one run \(A\)'s amendment caused neither a read nor inclusion of the withdrawal, so the cut exists. In the matched run the withdrawal is a recorded predecessor of the amendment. In particular replacing the new source by an appointment with this dependency invalidates joint usability without changing its standalone eligibility. A separate consuming gate evaluates operative access and clearance. This taxonomy neither recovers physical effects nor makes the historic authorization notice timely.
\href{https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~garg/dist/dcs01-slice.pdf}{Garg and Mittal's computation slicing} already computes least and greatest satisfying cuts as steps in a general slice and treats conjunction of regular predicates, including local predicates with holes. The intermediate slice in the general case may not be an interval. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02327}{Perera, Garg and Cheney} develop causally consistent slicing with a Galois connection in a different context. The operational result here is the exact two-endpoint reusable authority export, its small negative witness under contiguous windows, and a sharp boundary with re-grant, not a faster general predicate detector. A directly factored complete-information comparator can reuse the same extremal state and ties. Transport to a changed causal history needs revalidating the extrema and their dependencies; matching version numbers is not a substitute.
