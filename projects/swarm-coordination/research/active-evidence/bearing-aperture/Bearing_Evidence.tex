\section*{When motion makes two bearing hypotheses distinguishable}
A sampled path can provide many positions and still have too little sideways extent to distinguish a mirrored pair of directions. The relevant condition is not a count of packets or poses. With one unknown phase offset and bounded per-sample error, it is the width of the circular phase differences permitted by the motion.

\paragraph{Scope and comparison.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12205}{Jadhav et al.} collect Wi-Fi channel measurements at positions along a moving receiver's trajectory. Their estimator pairs packet measurements and uses multiple positions to build a directional profile. This audit isolates an optimistic special case: exact known sampled poses, one well-defined path, known wavelength, fixed but unknown phase offset per candidate world, and a usable phase measurement at every stored pose. Phase is measured in turns modulo one. One source assumption matters: the source already binds the adopted phase path to one of two authorized bearing hypotheses. Bearings by themselves neither identify a failed vehicle nor export authority. No packet pairing, multipath suppression, decode work, delivery latency, device availability, or independent non-radio notice is inferred. A single analysis below does not extrapolate to the paper's estimated-displacement algorithm.

\paragraph{Sharp compatibility test.}
Let $d_i$ be the displacement from an arbitrary common reference pose. In a world with direction $u_j$, the ideal value at observation $i$ is $\gamma_j+u_j\cdot d_i/\lambda$ modulo one, with unknown phase offset $\gamma_j$. Observations differ from this by at most $\epsilon$ turns in circular distance, independently as a support rule rather than as a probability model. The two hypotheses share a possible record if and only if their difference phases
\[
    f_i=(u_0-u_1)\cdot d_i/\lambda \pmod 1
\]
lie in one closed arc of width $4\epsilon$, assuming $4\epsilon<1$. Indeed if an observation can arise from both worlds, their predicted phases are at circular distance at most $2\epsilon$ at each observation. Existence of an offset difference making that true is precisely the common-arc test. Conversely an arc of width $4\epsilon$ selects its midpoint as an offset difference; along each shortest arc between predictions of distance at most $2\epsilon$, the midpoint is compatible with both worlds. A causal policy cannot distinguish the worlds on that shared record. If the minimal covering arc is strictly wider, checking consistency against each candidate returns the unique one whenever at least one candidate generated the received data. This is equivalently the direct set-membership detector. Repeated copies of one phase difference cannot add discriminating extent. Unwrapped range on arbitrary circular samples is unsound: zero, one, and two complete turns can be the same observation.

\paragraph{Test using recorded motion.}
Use all available motion-capture trajectory files at receiver location L1 in the first LOS and first NLOS source configurations, declared by folder before evaluating those traces. The \href{https://github.com/Harvard-REACT/WSR-Toolbox-Dataset/tree/7c8cb67c8dff9553e39f65552c0de4bf427ba42c}{pinned data} contain five and four files respectively. The time axis is relative to the first stored pose in each file, not a fault time or an issued motion command. Choose an authored mirrored pair at $\pm30^\circ$ about the recorded $x$ axis, wavelength 0.06 m following the paper's example scale, and a stipulated phase-error bound of $10^\circ$ per observation. Since the direction difference lies on the $y$ axis and has magnitude one, the test uses the stored $y$ coordinates divided by wavelength, with no need to assume an absolute reference phase. Capture times and positions are read as exact recorded decimals.

In the first quarter second each of the nine prefixes has a minimal covering arc no greater than 0.001017 turns, far below the separating threshold $1/9$ of a turn. Some contain more than 40 stored poses. Even assigning a perfect timestamp and one idealized noisy phase sample to every stored pose cannot guarantee separating this pair on those prefixes. The first sampled prefix exceeding the threshold is between 11.104194 and 11.179158 seconds after file start. The recorded startup interval matters: this is \emph{not} a bound on intrinsic channel latency, a deadline after a command, or the observed time to get a correct bearing. The motions are physical recorded inputs; the phase errors and two hypotheses are a hypothetical control. Actual packet receptions could be a different subset and need their own time and availability proof. The calculation gives no bound between unsampled positions.

For the small phase differences in the early intervals the extent needed in an adequately sampled transverse path is over $4\epsilon\lambda=0.00667$ m, unlike motion parallel to the mirror axis, which adds no separating information for these two hypotheses in the model. Availability by a real decision cut also depends on whether the sensing movement is lawful, whether it interferes with a live duty, and whether its output reaches the decision maker. The short illustrative quarter-second read is in recorded seconds and does not convert \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/473}{PR 473}'s simulation-time fault notice into physical milliseconds.

\paragraph{Checks.}
The replay verifies all nine source blobs, uses a rational largest-gap calculation, and independently compares the circular rule to direct offset/observation enumeration on 2,379 discrete families. Lean checks real-line arc inequalities used by the proof, not the source calibration. Equal-information direct checking ties. The result is a concrete input-side negative control for R1/R3a. It is not a measured error floor for the WSR system, a finding of channel failure, or a new optimal estimator. For an end-to-end test, record actual eligible packet pairs and phases with synchronized cuts, bind the sensor output to the event and authorized act, and charge the acquisition movement and the downstream acceptance delay. No held-out mine layout, no acceptance of R1/R3a or I1.
