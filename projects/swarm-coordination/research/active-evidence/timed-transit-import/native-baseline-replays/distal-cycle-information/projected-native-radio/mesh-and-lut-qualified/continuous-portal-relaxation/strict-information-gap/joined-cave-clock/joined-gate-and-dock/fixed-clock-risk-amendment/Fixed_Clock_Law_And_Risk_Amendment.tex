\section*{Clock-dependent risk across live commitments and a cap amendment}
A single latent offset $\theta$ remains fixed for the whole mission.
The source law is unknown, but an authorized predeclared ambiguity set
contains all distributions on $n$ offset worlds with point mass at
most $1/(n-1)$. No telemetry identifying the clock phase or physical
outcome has arrived at the amendment cut. This is an ex ante regime,
not a claim about posterior risk after observing a failure.

Within the declared copied-gate family, real closure for a stamped
request $s$ of integer duration $d$ would occupy
$[s-\theta,s-\theta+d-1]$. It contacts the padded hold $[a,b]$ iff
$\theta\in[s-b,s+d-1-a]$. Instantiate the two physical pads of
PR 881, with $250$ ms closures. Space candidate offsets two
seconds apart. Translating the actual certified hold and request
together isolates each physical occurrence from all the others;
for each offset world $j$, choose the request to start within the
inner occupation in $j$ and outside the outer bracket in every
other world. No boundary contact ambiguity enters these choices.
These are repeated modeled duties, not an actual multi-mission run.

Let $A$ be the union of potential contact worlds of the irrevocable
prior commitments and $J$ that of newly chosen work. Failure is
contact in any duty; non-disjoint events on the same law are not
added as separate masses. For any $S$, the exact worst-case risk is
$\min(1,|S|/(n-1))$: a maximizing distribution leaves out a world
outside $S$ whenever possible. Admission at a cumulative cap
therefore uses $S=A\cup J$ and matches the generous direct
finite-law extreme-point baseline. In particular, when $|A|=k$,
cap $k/(n-1)$ and $J=\{j\}$, admission precisely queries $j\in A$.
All $\binom{n}{k}$ old sets are reachable by distinct irrevocable
pre-amendment authorizations; any two differ under one allowed
probe. A deterministic exact reusable summary for those future
queries needs at least $\lceil\log_2\binom{n}{k}\rceil$ bits for
this family (an enumerative code for $A$ meets it). A count is
inadequate, and this is a persistent summary bound, not a general
interactive communication lower bound. This transports the earlier
fixed-law membership idea (PR 370) through an explicit timer,
body, and effect comparison rather than claiming a new INDEX law.

If an authorized amendment binds all still-live work and raises
the stringency to $(k-1)/(n-1)$, prior risk already exceeds the new
bound and no singleton choice repairs it. If the amendment binds
only new work, with the same value of the threshold and $k\ge2$,
any one singleton is admissible under that prospective rule. The
original mission aggregate is still $\min(1,|A\cup J|/(n-1))$.
Calling the prospective exception historical compliance would
change the claim. Exhaustive tests cover both padded gates, even
$n\in[4,12]$, and all fixed-cardinality prior sets under the stated
source model. This neither discharges R4 nor estimates real cave
risk or a physical clock law.
