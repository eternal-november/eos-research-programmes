\section*{Defend timed gate reservations against an ambiguous receiver clock}
Retain the physically bound cave clocks and native radio/dock replay
of PR 885. The gate effect worker has a separate clock $g=t+\theta$,
where $t$ is the motion clock and the unknown offset $\theta$ is
constant across the modeled process replacement. No drift, clock reset,
or forged timing is admitted. The signed-off body holds are indexed in
motion time, not in gate time. The simulation uses actual time to own
the effect record; the evidence-based guard receives only the gate stamp
and the durable echo records for its clock comparison.

A motion-clock echo request at $a$, gate stamp at reception/instant
echo $g_i$, and motion-clock reception at $b$ give
$g_i-b\le\theta\le g_i-a$ by causality. No symmetric link delay is
assumed. Intersect these constraints. If the resulting interval is
$[\ell,u]$, a gate stamp $g$ and duration $d$ permit any installed
interval with aggregate support $[g-u,g-\ell+d-1]$ on the integer
motion clock. Approve only when this does not intersect any farther
body hold. When there is only the tail of the current hold remaining,
the first post-hold gate stamp certified clear at held endpoint $h$
is $h+u+1$. At one tick less, the closed inclusive hold still has
a feasible overlap. If a later hold is declared, that next stamp
must be checked against it too. The waits are to the effect scheduler,
not to the courier. A previously blocked key keeps its recorded
refusal; a separately authorized new key reschedules the actuation.

The wide calibration records the same stamps and the same round trip
under two different actual one-way delay pairs. The attempt is
labeled $h+5$ microseconds at the gate, but either takes effect just
under 50 ms before the end of the certified return hold or just over
50 ms after it. A point-stamp test authorizes both and produces a
contact in the first. The one-way delay choices and relative offset are
generated in the fixture; neither is revealed to the controller.
Each clock policy is tested over 48 runs varying the two gates, observer
availability, two gate crash cuts, two dock crash cuts, and relative
clock phase. The absolute-time calendar and every bounded guard yield
48 complete egresses, exact dock receipt and audits; the point-stamp
check halts on 24 contacts. Every run executes a separate authorized
maintenance closure. Safe branches maintain the 453.847823 s bound.

With only the wide echo, both worlds need a 49,996 microsecond wait to
subsequently authorize the gate. Replacing it with a shorter round trip
alone permits one world immediately but needs 54,996 microseconds
in the other: width by itself is not set inclusion. Keeping both
records permits the useful immediate admission without making the
other world wait longer than the old interval required. The generous
calendar using absolute effect time has the same wait outcomes on the
tested conditional worlds as the accumulated wider/narrower guard,
but supplies stronger information. No measured clock, dynamic RF
through a closed slab, or independently selected mission is claimed.
