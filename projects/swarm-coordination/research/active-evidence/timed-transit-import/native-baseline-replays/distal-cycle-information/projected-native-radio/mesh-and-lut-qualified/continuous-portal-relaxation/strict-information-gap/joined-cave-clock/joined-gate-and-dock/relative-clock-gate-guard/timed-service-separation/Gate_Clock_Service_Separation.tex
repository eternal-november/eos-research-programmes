\section*{An ambiguity can preserve egress yet defeat a separate timed gate duty}
Use the same two physical worlds generated by the fixed-offset clock
experiment and exactly the same native route, radio and SQLite effects.
Add a useful service predicate: the queued closure must start by
$h+75,000$ integer microseconds, where $h$ is the certified end of
the return-body hold in motion time. It must still last the requested
250 ms, so an equivalent completion cutoff is 249,999 microseconds
later. The crossed bodies and independent outbound maintenance are
unchanged. The evaluator reads the actual installation time and span
from the effect store. A service miss does not count as late
authorization, and no successful egress is retroactively called a
failure of motion. This deadline is an authored development
requirement, not a field measurement.

In the wide-echo worlds the gate sees the same raw calibration
timestamps and exactly the same local stamp at first release;
the modeled clock continues at the same unit rate. The operator
cannot observe the latent offset or obtain any new admission
information between that release and the deadline. The first
integer wait certified by the outer hold is 49,996 microseconds.
That certification bound need not be sharp for physical contact
within the last few microseconds of the bracket. The service
obstruction is wider and has an inner witness: to serve the
deadline in the late world, any successful controller must wait
at most 24,995 microseconds. At every such wait, the early
world's actuation lies inside the independently verified inner
return occupancy. This is an impossibility for
the declared observations even with unrestricted computation or
randomized almost-surely safe policies, not just a failure of the
coded guard. Never launching can preserve motion safety but does
not satisfy the service predicate. Other action powers or an
independent view of the crossing can invalidate the lower bound.

A shorter echo measured in the fixture distinguishes enough of the
two histories to admit the late effect immediately and wait only
as needed for the earlier one. Keeping both calibration records
matches the generous absolute-time reference in these worlds.
All three complete 48 out of 48 timely closure/egress/receipt/audit
bundles; the wide-only guard completes all egresses but just 24
timely bundles. Treating a clock stamp as absolute generates
physical contacts in 24 runs rather than resolving ambiguity.
Unlike an earlier cutoff measured only at the final dock, this
service separation consumes a changed result at a distinct
actuation interface. The fresh echo is supplied by a declared
separate timing source, not shown to be free or available on the
native RF link. Independent acceptance remains open.
