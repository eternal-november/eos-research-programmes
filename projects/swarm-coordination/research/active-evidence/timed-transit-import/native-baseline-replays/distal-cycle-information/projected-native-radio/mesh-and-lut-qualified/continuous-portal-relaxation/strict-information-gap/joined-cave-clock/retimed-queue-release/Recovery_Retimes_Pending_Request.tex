\section*{A fault repair can release a pending send at the wrong time}
The published cave scene, timed chords, native broker and exact
crash-before/after settlement from PR 877, with the physical gate binding corrected by PR 878, are held fixed.
A separately authorized diagnostic is requested while the old dock
remains registered but its pose source is unavailable. The native broker
neither processes that request nor debits its sender. The request actually
persists in the native FIFO. Using the native unregister operation removes
the dock and its endpoint bindings, but leaves the queue alone.
A subsequent dispatch, now able to get every pose, services the diagnostic
first, ahead of the protected settlement request.

Let the courier's initial five-byte report and first one-byte audit
sample dispatch at $t_0$. At $t_0+1.125$, those debits have expired.
Write $q$ for bytes charged at this dispatch to earlier eligible requests
from that courier. The settlement request (five bytes), and a separately
owed second sample (one byte) on the next eighth of a second, share the
same ledger and none of these new debits can expire by the sample.
Both run at these cuts iff $q+6\le C$, under the native rolling quota
and with the stated connectivity, unchanged recipient admission and
all non-quota conditions held as in the positive case. At $C=6$, a
two-byte pending diagnostic allows its own completion and the sample,
but blocks the retry. A one-byte diagnostic allows the retry but blocks
the sample. The same two pending bytes addressed to the removed,
now-unbound dock do not debit at all; unrelated live traffic cannot be
treated as though it too were addressed to a dead destination.

For the authored two-byte diagnostic, moving its actual enqueue until
$t_0+2.25$ lets the protected work settle on the earlier return cut
and preserves the diagnostic and both samples. Egress is at most
454.847823 s. If the request is already pending and cannot be deferred
at source, the tested compensating plan allows the initial release and
waits for the actual debit to expire before retrying at $t_0+2.25$,
with ACK and sample at $t_0+2.375$. Egress is at most 454.972823 s.
Both constructions complete all requested work in all 12 trials.
The second costs an eighth of a second; deleting the diagnostic was
not counted as success. A live broker queue is therefore a potential
future resource use even when all present accounting looks clear.
At the seam tested here, maintaining the same ledger across recovery
requires either pending-work knowledge, controlled admission at the
application, or a wait that is charged to deadlines.

Only the source FIFO and rolling accounting, endpoint removal and
the app callbacks are native. The pose failure, timely administrative
permission, staged assets, shared journal and trackable waits are the
same fixtures as before. This is not a general exact solver over all
possible trajectories or adaptive priorities, a hardware test, or
independent I2. A direct scheduler with the same admission powers can
follow the same timing and settle the same report.
