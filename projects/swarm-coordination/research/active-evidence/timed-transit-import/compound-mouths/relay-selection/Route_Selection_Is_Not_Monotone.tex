\title{A new cave relay can preempt a viable direct route}

\paragraph{Test interface.}
One newly registered breadcrumb and two distinct civil inspection
reports owed to the same dock on already-viewed Cave Circuit 01.
This is a selected source-law check using the published weighted
graph, an authored coordinate lookup that maps sampled origins to their
respective labels, and declared hardware limits. Assume fixed positions,
at most one breadcrumb at a sampled origin, and no other receiver-side
breadcrumb. A pass means that two hard RF gates permit the selected
link, not that the packet arrived or was accepted as settlement.

\paragraph{Route choice can spend an old option.}
The pinned \href{https://github.com/osrf/subt/blob/083c6a8acb0c25aa1a35e34517e628cd3b52ae77/subt_ign/src/VisibilityTable.cc}
{visibility table} scores the route through one intermediate by the
maximum of the two weighted graph distances. It uses that route if
strictly cheaper than the direct route; it keeps the direct route on a
tie. The \href{https://github.com/osrf/subt/blob/083c6a8acb0c25aa1a35e34517e628cd3b52ae77/subt_ign/src/VisibilityRfModel.cc}
{RF computation} then tests the cost cap and uses the larger
Euclidean range of the two radio legs in the relayed case, or the
direct Euclidean range otherwise. The
\href{https://github.com/osrf/subt/blob/083c6a8acb0c25aa1a35e34517e628cd3b52ae77/subt-communication/subt_rf_interface/src/subt_rf_model.cpp}
{range function} rejects a selected range above its cap, without
falling back to a displaced direct route.

For reports $A$ from tile 18 and $B$ from tile 16, both to tile 39,
the entries give (selected weighted cost, squared range in
$\mathrm{m}^2$):
\[
\begin{array}{c|rr}
 & A & B\\\hline
\text{no relay} & (10,5000)&(13,8125)\\
\text{relay at 13}&(9,5625)&(9,3125)\\
\text{relay at 42}&(10,5000)&(10,5000)
\end{array}
\]
At cost cap 10 and range 72 m, placing the relay at 13 lets $B$
through but preempts $A$'s viable direct route. The flip holds
exactly for $\sqrt{5000}\le R<75$ m under the stated assumptions.
At 42, $A$ retains the direct route at a tie and $B$ gets a relay.
The two sites are the only single-origin placements passing $B$ at
72 m among all 158 labels in the supplied graph. Independent
Dijkstra walks agree with all-pairs relaxation for all destinations
from the five relevant vertices. Lean checks the hard-gate interval
and serial activation conditions, not simulator correspondence.

The declared 72 m cap is not the stock launch's 500 m limit.
The same ordering issue has another observable in the source formula
with nominal launch parameters: from 115 to 128 through a newly
registered breadcrumb at 126, cost goes from 10 to 9 while mean
path loss increases about 1.613593 dB. This is neither a guaranteed
outage at the default nor a measured packet result.

\paragraph{A passage cut changes which repair remains possible.}
Starting from tile 13, placement at 42 takes one abstract move
normally. Remove $13$--$42$ and it takes nine moves; remove $2$--$3$
as well and it is unreachable. These are graph moves, not physical
durations. For a deadline allowing one but not nine moves, the
closure removes the site that permits both unchanged transfers.
It does not itself remove radio contact. In the remaining
single-site arm, suppose the scheduled sends are at cuts $a,b$
and the newly selected relay becomes effective at cut $k$,
including sends on $k$. Both hard gates pass exactly when
$a<k\le b$. The supervisor must distinguish the effective update
from the installation request, and the old authorized receipt from
an attempted send. If $B there is no acceptable $13$-only
schedule with those fixed sends and no extra supplier.
The \href{https://github.com/osrf/subt/blob/083c6a8acb0c25aa1a35e34517e628cd3b52ae77/subt_ign/src/CommsBrokerPlugin.cc}
{stock discovery update} keeps a newly seen static name and
pose; disappearance from the latest pose input is not a removal
operation. An equally informed direct scheduler can make the same
placement and timing choices. A routing system allowed to keep and
test the direct alternative at the hard gate is another comparator;
there is no class-wide obstruction to better radio routing.
