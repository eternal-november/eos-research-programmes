\paragraph{Exact condition and failure.}
Let the report sizes be $a,b>0$, and the configured receive
limits applying to $Q,R$ be $q,r$ respectively. An exact read
allows $Q,R$ when not yet paid and $R$ alone when already paid,
provided $a\le q$ and $a+b\le r$. Under blind $Q,R$, the paid
history accepts the duplicate only when $2a\le q$, in which case
the subsequent $R$ needs $2a+b\le r$. If $Q$ is rejected at its
receive test, its sender still spent bytes, but the shared RX
window did not get the duplicate. On the unpaid history $Q$
must be allowed by its own limit. Blind $R,Q$ requires
$a+b\le q$ to serve $A$ when unpaid, as $R$'s accepted ten bytes
are then already held. In a paid history $R$ must still fit on
top of the primary. Under the exact-read feasibility premises,
the full no-fixed-order strip is therefore
\[
             2a\le q<a+b,\qquad r<2a+b.
\]
This is for the scoped sequential menu with one authorized
retry and a required $R$ at the same native cut. No $Q$ fails
in the unpaid world; adding repeated unobservable sends cannot
improve the result while all earlier successful debits
remain live. An application treating repeated report keys as a
single completed duty does not undo their earlier receipt cost.

At $a=9,b=10,q=18,r=19$, blind $Q,R$ leaves 18 bytes in the paid
world and blocks $R$. Blind $R,Q$ leaves 10 in the unpaid world
and blocks $Q$. Lowering $q$ to 17 makes $Q,R$ work by refusing
the duplicate, while raising $q$ to 19 allows the opposite
order. Raising $r$ to 28 repairs $Q,R$ without narrowing $q$.
These are alternatives with different resource and ordering
budgets, not automatic scope extensions.
