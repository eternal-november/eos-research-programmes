\title{A weighted, source-parameterized event model for inspection and egress}
\author{}\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
The physical fixture and its finite source alternatives are explicit
input rather than hard-coded route names. An admitted local controller
starts from an actual position with reports already in durable custody
at specified forwarding slots. It chooses a movement, a reached probe,
a dispatch, or idle. Moves are directed with independently configurable
travel time and reserve cost. Probes have separate acquisition duration,
cost, local observation fields, and optional physical feed diversion.
Dispatch consumes its own duration and reserve, subject to distinct
slot identities and declared shared capacity. Loaded action models are
civil research abstractions, not a flight controller or site equipment
authorization.

\paragraph{Physical time and observed time.}
For a commitment launched at $s$ and ending at $e$, required support
must hold on the whole half-open span $[s,e)$. Movement succeeds only
if its directed arc can sustain that duration. A mid-transit closure
invalidates the tested mission; this version does not simulate detailed
in-passage braking, retreat, collision or soil dynamics. A successful
probe commits a local evidence value acquired at $e$. There is no
instantaneous movement on busy ticks. A departure releases only its
specifically modeled reservations; a different reservation of the same
physical feed can remain. A dispatch connects each attempted report
to a real stored copy, checks its slot, link and feed over the entire
span, and records actual arrival at $e$. It may commit some reports
but not others. A missing acknowledgment neither invalidates an actual
arrival nor proves an arrival. No command, accepted reservation or
signal strength is counted as delivered data.

Each declared source history includes possible physical closures, outages,
missing stock and reported observations. A signal is substituted as
evidence only at its configured cut, without revealing global truth.
Successful egress may independently require receipt evidence or actual
delivery, as two different fixtures with different stakeholder laws.
The paired test delivers both reports with missing acknowledgments and
permits physical-settlement egress; otherwise the extra
receipt-to-travel obligation fails. Such a difference is only relevant
when the real authority or safety law consumes that evidence.

\paragraph{Matching controllers fairly.}
In offline synthesis, group worlds by the controller's permitted
observation history. A candidate must choose one action in each group;
extended observations refine but never merge groups with different
histories. All physical worlds then advance their source and effects.
The alternative evaluator solves each common-history branch independently,
using the same actions, local information and model costs. The two
views do not supply any additional sensor about occult state. Finite
interval overlap is independently audited by a pointwise comparison and
checked in Lean. The effect simulator and exact reference are both
executable, but share the source definitions; agreement is not an
external certification of those definitions. Extracted policies are
replayed from local histories with no exposed scenario identifier.

Twenty-four original fixture cases preserve exact report and egress
limits from \href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/376}{the coupled-probe study}.
Seventy-two matched controller tests vary action duration, failure timing
and egress permit semantics. A separate pointwise checker validates
2,880 service, resource and acknowledgment cases, ranging across
span endpoints and actual missing copies. End-cut faults after an
effect differ from faults in its consumption interval. Unknown source
identifiers and multiple named slots on the same unit-capacity bank
cannot produce reassuring implicit availability.

\paragraph{Restricted concurrent roles.}
A separate check permits a scout and a station dispatcher to act on
different timelines. The scout acquires local evidence and follows a
source-valid egress path, while the dispatcher must pick one forward
schedule without using the scout's unshared observation. A scoped cue
is processed at its own actual arrival or may be absent; a static
default can be preplanned if every still-silent admitted history
allows it. The evaluator checks real overlap with the other crew's
feed diversion. This exposes a case absent from the one-crew model:
a relay available at the dispatch cut is lost when the other crew
begins its probe inside the forwarding span. The separate feed is
not enough if receipt of the probe evidence is presumed rather than
physically supplied. A direct time and evidence history enumeration
ties on 288 configurations. This slice uses fixed safe probe access
per branch and one possible scoped cue; it does not generalize its
completeness to free joint multi-crew control.

\paragraph{Scope and next inclusion test.}
This is the first data-driven adapter for one active controller and
already prepared durable copies. It does not yet implement simultaneous
independent crew decisions, interference derived from a radio spectrum
model, measurements during motion, or custody preparation with several
mobile workers. Those need source mechanisms if they change a mission
decision. Published mine geometry and radio diagnostics may constrain
which alternatives and intervals are physically defensible, but cannot
by themselves establish delivery or multi-relay service. Independently
selected geometry, calibrated travel times, a lawful radio/custody
adapter and the fixed matched comparison must be recorded before any
claim of held-out mission acceptance. An independently replayed validation
through an acknowledged delivery and actual egress remains outstanding.
