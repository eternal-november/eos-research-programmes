\title{When two inspections together justify a warehouse move}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

With no shared observations, two crews can each know which fast move is safe and still be unable to perform it together. Two independently purchased inspections overcome this obstruction in a finite fixture model; either inspection alone can cost more than it saves. The receipts appear only if the probe fits around work that was already promised. This is a written bound and an executable simulation, not sensor calibration or a field experiment.

\paragraph{Plant and obligations.}
A common warehouse fixture has detents $0,\ldots,2m$, with $m\ge2$. Starting from home, a scripted authorized positioning operation advances it one detent per tick before the work cut, then leaves it locked. Every final detent $j$ is reached this way. Neither crew observes that positioning history; each has only its own enclosed fixture view. Assign each detent positive prior mass $p_j$. The specified experiment is not about estimating that distribution. The old inspections at the two stations are separately promised before tick $0$. They use their own worker and enclosed service bay for $o_A,o_B$ ticks, beginning at zero; valid envelopes have $o_i\le D$. The bays cannot serve an old inspection and an active probe simultaneously. No old work can be preempted or cancelled.

A further joint move is owed by tick $D+2$. At cut $D$ both crews submit the same command from $\{F,0,1\}$, with an additional reserved-bypass command described below. The ordinary slow move $F$ completes at $D+2$ and works for every detent. Fast lane $1$ fits the exposed end carriers; fast lane $0$ fits each interior stop. Valid matching fast commands finish at $D+1$. Mismatched commands or a command to the wrong lane are inadmissible; even an interlock that prevents physical injury by denying those combinations would not deliver the joint service. The two controllers cannot postpone agreement, read the other's command, alter lane independently, or use the gate to learn and retry. Their scheduling and issue authority is fixed before the run.

\paragraph{Observations actually generated.}
The coarse enclosed readers are shifted one detent apart. Crew A reads $\lfloor j/2\rfloor$, crew B reads $\lfloor(j+1)/2\rfloor$. A probe moves the local inspection head into its own narrow end aperture and reveals only whether its corresponding end is occupied; A tests $j=0$ and B tests $j=2m$. No probe communicates its result, exposes a moving head to the other crew, or changes the detent. It does change resource allocation: it consumes $s_i\ge1$ exclusive station ticks after old inspection and records a locally unambiguous endpoint result after additional latency $\ell_i\ge0$. The only available probe window is after the old promise and before final command. With delivery before decisions when simultaneous, the exact condition is
\[
 o_i+s_i+\ell_i\le D.
\]
A duration increase in an inherited inspection can eliminate one probe without changing the detent, its views, or the other inspection. The same rule permits or forbids all pairs of probes; no invisible spare bay is assumed. Probe booking is fixed before private reads and both controllers know the schedule, not the realized remote data. Charge nonnegative $c_i$ for a booked probe, independently of whether its endpoint test is true. Units are the value of one tick saved on the joint move, not measured operating cost. Paying for untimely evidence gives no use at $D$ and cannot improve a nonnegative-cost choice; the replay restricts bookings to those that can return in time.

\paragraph{Exact agreement and value.}
Join two detents when at least one crew has identical observations there. An admissible deterministic command pair must use one common action per connected component: equality of local observations forces each edge, and agreement of the two controllers propagates equality through the graph. Conversely a local view belongs to exactly one component, so a command valid throughout that component is locally implementable. The ordinary direct proof is this component construction; exhaustive enumeration of the local tables is a separate finite reference. Randomized choices cannot improve the zero-error optimum. With fixed shared randomness independent of the detent, a common measure-one set of seeds serves all finitely many positive-mass detents; each such seed gives valid deterministic local tables. The same reasoning applies by fixing a joint realization of private coins, with no new side channel.

A's coarse cells join $(0,1),(2,3),\ldots,(2m-2,2m-1)$; B's cells join $(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2m-1,2m)$. The union is a path. A probe removes just A's first edge; B's removes just B's last edge. The uncut path includes both fast lane labels, so only $F$ is possible. From A alone only detent $0$ is separated, and from B alone only $2m$ is separated. Both together split off the two ends leaving an interior component, all of whose nodes permit fast lane $0$. Thus the minimum expected cost, in units of fallback delay plus probe costs, is the minimum of the available entries
\[
 1,\qquad 1-p_0+c_A,\qquad 1-p_{2m}+c_B,\qquad c_A+c_B.
\]
The one-inspection entries are available only at their respective timely cuts; the pair requires both cuts. In particular $c_A>p_0$, $c_B>p_{2m}$ and $c_A+c_B<1$ give a strict complementarity region: each separate buy loses to fallback but the pair wins. A myopic algorithm that permits only strictly beneficial one-step additions stops with no inspections. For uniform $m=2$ and costs $1/4$ each, the four costs are $1,21/20,21/20,1/2$. With slack $D=3$, old durations $(1,1)$, spans $(1,1)$ and delays $(1,1)$, both receipts arrive at $D$. Increasing the first old duration to $2$ eliminates the winning pair; the second probe alone still costs $21/20$, so fallback is optimal.

\paragraph{A separately reserved alternative.}
Every run has the same independent bypass lane and service equipment, separate from both observation bays. The lane can carry any detent through a reinforced cradle without inspecting its position. Its nonpreemptible older inspection occupies $[0,o_R)$. At the public booking cut the planner may give up the optional one-tick service job that would use the first free slot at $o_R$, and reserve the equipment to prepare the cradle for $s_R\ge1$ ticks from $o_R$. The optional job is offered, not owed; charging its foregone value $r\ge0$ does not release an older duty. The bypass is unavailable unless the booking was made and $o_R+s_R\le D$. It becomes ready before a coincident command cut. A matched reserved-bypass command $R$ spends the resulting lane slot $[D,D+1)$ and completes at $D+1$ without any private evidence. No reservation leaves the optional job eligible for its ordinary slot, but leaves the cradle unprepared. The slow default remains available through another route and costs one tick with respect to the fast target; reserve is therefore neither a relabeling of that default nor an implicit extra resource granted only to probe policies. Probe slots, detent distribution, old duties, safety, and joint deadline are otherwise the same for all bookings within each comparison.

The reserve-only expected cost is $r$ when ready. It has no delay loss. Adding a nonnegative-cost probe to an available reservation cannot lower a cost already equal to the route's fixed opportunity cost. Without a reservation the earlier proof is unchanged because no additional command can succeed. The exact optimum therefore adds the available entry $r$ to the previous minimum. A separate finite local-table enumeration checks that the reserve achieves zero delay even without probes, and a common event model replays all cases. For uniform $m=2$, with probe cost $1/4$ each and both timely, an on-time bypass costing $1/3$ wins over the pair at $1/2$; increasing its opportunity cost to $2/3$ makes the pair win. If the old A inspection lasts one extra tick and the older bypass inspection also makes the bypass late, no-probe slow service wins. With only A timely, its probe price $1/10$, and a bypass price $19/20$, A alone wins at $9/10$. Each comparison uses the same reached detents and exogenous work across options; prices and old durations are varied only between comparisons.

\paragraph{Pooled versus actionable evidence.}
The pair of coarse readings is injective, although neither crew sees that pair. At $m=2,j=2$, A's private cell is $\{2,3\}$ and B's $\{1,2\}$. They individually know lane $0$ fits. But iterated uncertainty reaches $0$ and $4$, where it does not fit. Complementary local data, both delivered on time, are therefore not shared data. The physically executed probes change those chains of possible local histories. Even after both probes the crews need not learn the exact detent in the interior; they only need enough common information to choose the permitted lane. An atomic observer pooling the private reads would remove the baseline obstruction, but is a different plant. Other excluded resources are implicit visibility of head motion, staggered rather than simultaneous commands, free retries and tolerance of inadmissible actions. Known, nonpreemptible work and fixed private apertures are deliberate envelope choices; this does not generalize to unknown reservations or adaptive probe booking.

\paragraph{Comparators and checks.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07525}{Moses, \emph{Relating Knowledge and Coordinated Action}}, establishes common-knowledge preconditions for simultaneous action. That is the direct reasoning here, not a novelty claim. \href{https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12738390/}{Zhong, Rossi and Shell, \emph{Planned Synchronization for Multi-Robot Systems with Active Observations}}, optimize buying observation or communication to recover joint state using a macro-action model. Our fixed simultaneous gate needs common action validity, not exact common state recovery. \href{https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/9db6faeef387dc789777227a8bed4d52-Abstract.html}{Lauri and Oliehoek, \emph{Multi-agent Active Perception with Prediction Rewards}}, study evidence fused for prediction after a sensing task; our joint service needs agreement before that kind of fusion. The scoped increment is a reached resource-bounded service example with an exact inherited-slack and nonadditive value boundary, not a new epistemic theorem.

The event interpreter executes fixture placement, acceptance and completion of both old duties, probe occupation and local delivery, and installed commands followed by timed outcomes. A separate enumeration of 11,664 paired local tables matches the agreement optimum in the five-detent case. The script checks 1,536 joint timing and resource envelopes across six fixture sizes against the proven path formula and across actual outcomes under both local tables. The uniform and blocked-slack examples and the four reservation controls are included in the replay. The bypass check enumerates an additional 4,096 paired local tables at the no-probe cut with all four commands available. No new Contextual Composition proof reuse or efficiency claim is made here.
