\title{Synthesizing a Warehouse Dispatch Policy While an Old Load Is Still in Service}

\paragraph{Result.}
An online compiler can recover a fast dispatch on every fixture configuration in a finite family, without interrupting a load already in service. On the same observations and hardware, a selector limited to a catalogue of fixed complete controller images needs $2^{n(n-1)/2}$ images to promise the same service; a scalar online replanner with fewer than $n$ observation reads cannot guarantee even one fast dispatch across every state. These are bounds for two specified interfaces, not a separation from every preinstalled algorithm. A preinstalled general compiler that specializes and validates the same writable policy registers is precisely a matched online synthesis implementation and ties.

\paragraph{Plant and inherited work.}
Civil indoor loading supports $P,Q$ are exclusive. At tick $-1$, accept an old load on $P$ with physical completion at $D$; that work continues throughout adaptation and no controller may revoke it. At time $0$ the controller chooses whether to hold the independent staging support $Q$ available through the fast decision at $D$, or admit one optional long job that occupies $Q$ through $D+1$. The shared service promise is that a qualifying new load is sent by the conservative path by $D+2$, and old work completes by $D$. The additional service option is completion by $D+1$, using $Q$ through that tick. The conservative path does not use $P$ or $Q$. There is no promise to serve the optional job; its lost throughput when holding $Q$ is one job. A failure to reach the fast service is not charged as fulfillment of the fast target. All policies preserve the old load and can meet the ordinary promise.

At time $0$ a trusted fixture authority publishes a new version and an $n\times n$ unit lower triangular Boolean matrix $A$ (arithmetic in $\mathbb F_2$). The publication arrives at all policy families at the same point. This synthetic matrix is an exact model of parity-coded fixture observations, not a sensor calibration fit. The physical latch state $h\in\mathbb F_2^n$ is hidden, unconstrained, and becomes relevant at dispatch. The rack supplies only $y=Ah$ to the actuator at $D$, not earlier. Dispatch has two choices: conservative path, or fast with a full $n$-bit configuration $x$. A fixed physical interlock refuses fast unless $x=h$ and $Q$ is actually available. Refusal supplies neither an additional observation nor a second fast attempt. The interlock gives safety even on unmodeled candidate rejection; the safety proof does not assume the compiler terminates.

\paragraph{Computation and installation.}
All families may use the same serial preparation processor with $D-1$ work quanta, and a scalar fast-phase aperture of at most $n-1$ individual latch-read operations before the one-shot dispatch; reading the vector earlier or through another port is not allowed. Work quanta are an abstract finite bit-operations model, not elapsed time measurements. We take $D=100n^3+100$. The shared rack has a configurable $n$-row Boolean policy fabric already present. A latched image consists of $n^2$ coefficient bits. When active, the fabric reads all $y$ in parallel and emits the row XORs in fixed depth $O(\log(n+1))$; the dispatch phase is sized for that common fabric. Scalar software cannot access that parallel input by executing host instructions, but all methods can use the same fabric with a certifiable image. Shared writable storage for the current and candidate authority record, scratch matrices and one incumbent and pending policy image is $O(n^2)$; for concreteness reserve $16n^2+64n$ data bits in this model (two modeled epoch values, static code excluded). For triangular inversion and validation, only two authority matrices, one inverse matrix, scratch row accumulators and an output image are required; loop indices and bit arithmetic fit the linear allowance. A catalogue using some of this memory is subject to the same total and has no larger runtime budget.

Synthesis computes a row program $B=A^{-1}$ using
\[
  B_i=e_i+\sum_{j<i}A_{ij}B_j.
\]
Every row is a newly instantiated fixed selector of a Boolean circuit for subsequent observations, not a forecast of the single current state. The checker verifies $AB=I$ against the authority record before accepting the image; since $A$ is invertible, this certifies $By=h$ for all hidden states. Each of the compiler and independent checker uses at most $n^2$ row visits, each scanning or XORing at most $n$ bits, and at most a constant number of bit reads, operations and writes per scan. With fixed padded scans and cached enable bits the two fit inside the stated $100n^3+100$ budget including an $O(n^2)$ load. This is a schedule upper bound under the modeled bit-operation budget; the replay does not time physical hardware.

The candidate is dormant before $D$. The authority can publish a replacement configuration after checking but before installation. Activation checks the candidate's immutable authority version against the current authority record atomically at the cut, along with availability of the held support; if the version changed, the certificate was rejected, or computation timed out, retain the independent fallback. The original $P$ obligation is discharged on its own schedule, and no standing $Q$ use is preempted. The installed controller may be computed earlier than $D$ but cannot interpret an unverified or mismatched model. The tested later-update cut is after the compiler's allowed work period; repeated restarts and deadlines guaranteeing success under arbitrary revisions are not asserted. If the authority can change again between commit and act without the stated atomic cut, the validation condition must be strengthened.

\paragraph{Fair baseline and exact lower bounds.}
The precompiled contingency selector may observe $A$ at the same time, may choose any of its prebuilt images (even by an ideal zero-cost selector), and has the identical actuator and work aperture. No one fixed complete image can deliver fast for two distinct matrices: on each matrix every $y$ can occur and hence its unique viable fast map is $A^{-1}$ on the whole input cube. There are $2^{n(n-1)/2}$ such maps. Thus at least that many image choices are necessary and sufficient in the selector-only class. If images are stored as separate complete coefficient records, the corresponding $n^2$ bits per record must be charged. Already at $n=4$ the full-image catalogue needs $64\cdot16=1024$ coefficient bits versus the shared $512$-bit budget, before indexing. No exponential bit-storage lower bound follows for symbolic sharing or a runtime parameter-to-image algorithm. Even an ideal catalogue cannot cover more configurations than it has distinct complete responses. A generic offline parametric implementation that uses the delivered matrix and writes a verified image during the allowed preparation phase evades the catalogue premise; classifying such use as synthesis makes its tie explicit rather than counting it against a strawman.

The bounded scalar replanner has the actual $A$, the same pre-phase work and memory, and can prepare arbitrary lookup data and adapt which $y$ coordinates to read; its action is a fixed full vector selected at the single dispatch cut. With fewer than $n$ reads there is an unread coordinate along each deterministic query branch. Flipping it preserves the branch yet changes the unique required full vector because $A^{-1}$ is injective. No universally successful scalar fast plan is available at that cut. Randomization cannot justify an almost-sure guarantee for the finite set of states: fix a seed from the common measure-one set. The conservative path is admissible. If all $n$ reads and adequate computation are allowed, scalar dispatch with the inverse attains the fast output; an oracle for that enlarged aperture is a control. The result is a one-shot read bound, not a lower bound on branch-contingent planning that can install a controller, nor a comparison against a supposed runtime inferencing advantage on the same FPGA image.

\paragraph{Preserved option and exact evidence.}
Even under omniscient planning, starting the optional long job makes $Q$ unavailable at the fast cut and removes the update's service benefit; it cannot be undone after compilation. Conversely holding $Q$ leaves it idle until the cut and charges the one optional admission independently of whether activation succeeds. There is no reopening of the old load's permission or reuse of the reserved support $P$ to hide that cost. On timeout or wrong-version delivery the old service still completes and the fallback finishes the new job by $D+2$. Starting the optional job is a valid baseline for ordinary service, not a safety violation; the strict one-tick service opportunity requires both early preservation and new verified policy.

The simulator enumerates all unit lower triangular matrices and hidden latch configurations for $n=1$ through $4$. A separate exact reference enumerates all possible latch vectors satisfying the observed equations, rather than invoking triangular inversion. The replay checks 1,098 matrix-state pairs, the distinct response count, same-image and empty-catalogue controls, the bounded view and enlarged-aperture reference, unavailable staging, timeout, invalid certificate and replaced authority. These checks are finite and do not prove the parameterized bounds, which are the counting and indistinguishability arguments above.

\paragraph{Comparison and scope.}
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s11334-022-00447-5}{Finkbeiner, Klein and Metzger, \emph{Live Synthesis}, 2022}, use LiveLTL to pass unsatisfied obligations across a synthesized update. \href{https://pldb.kirancodes.me/p/conf/icse/AmramBSY22}{Amram et al., \emph{Dynamic Update for Synthesized GR(1) Controllers}, 2022}, synthesize bridge controllers for correct online change. These are precedents, not results reproduced by this model. Here the particular increment is a concrete shared support that fallback must preserve and a finite service separation under specified decision and installation budgets, not a new logic for updating obligations. Holding a support during computation has a quantified productivity cost. No comparison against unrestricted precompiled purposes, physical timing, adversarial manipulation, new complexity class, or Contextual Composition proof saving is claimed. Empirical sensor behaviors and concurrent authority changes are outside the admitted envelope.
