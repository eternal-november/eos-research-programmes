\paragraph{Result.}
\href{https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-research-programmes/pull/354}{\#354} proves a timed placement boundary and exact message-alphabet lower bound. Its original finite harness gave each instrument a copy of the whole reservation map, even though the replacement used only its bank coordinates. This integration calls two separately specified provider modules through explicitly different sensor ports. It leaves the original receiver and physical trace verifier byte-identical. The bank provider meets the joint contract despite carrying less information than the full scan; delaying its otherwise valid output still preserves the standalone completion judgment and loses the joint one.

\paragraph{Physical and software boundary.}
At tick 0 the legacy plant admits old jobs to $r$ distinct lanes out of $n$, each reserved on $[0,5)$. At tick 1 a replace event ends new report authority of the outgoing instrument; it does not retire old work. No other old arrivals or reassignments occur before tick 5. The providers know public dimensions $n,r$ and can request exact current-idle bits via a lane-indexed read callback. The full scanner may read all lanes and reports the highest clear index. The separately coded bank supplier may request only indices $0$ through $r$ and reports the first clear index. Its callback has no duty identities, reservation map, receiver or truth about future outcomes. The plant supplies one initially admitted reservation sequence for both providers, not a solution to their new-dispatch issue.

A configured adapter wraps the selected index in the frozen clearance interval $[2,5)$ and supplies it to the unmodified incumbent only if delivered by its tick-2 decision and from the current producer epoch. Epoch is trusted provenance of the packet, not a new receiver observation; the payload and incumbent input format are unchanged. The transition traces include an otherwise well-formed old-epoch packet, which the adapter ignores. The standby negative case uses the same bank port, payload and selection discipline, but reports at tick 3. No controller gets to retry in the joint window. The standalone comparison is precisely safe service by 5 and preservation of old jobs, not equivalence of all event timestamps or lane choices.

\paragraph{Composition and judgments.}
For either timely instrument one lane in its sensed set is clear by the cut: at most $r$ lanes are occupied and the smaller provider reads $r+1$. Exact reads establish present clearance. Only the independent shared resource law, not an assertion by either instrument, lets the adapter extend present clearance to $[2,5)$. The receiver's original timing guard accepts a timely assertion covering the two-tick move, so joint service obtains by 4 without moving, interrupting or canceling any earlier reservation. The event verifier separately derives physical completion of each old job at 5. This preserves the receiver's consumed information contract, not every observation available to its source. It also need not preserve which valid lane is chosen.

The late report is still physically sound and allows a standalone dispatch at 3 and finish at 5. Its genuine absence at 2 makes the fixed joint receiver wait; a valid-looking stale packet cannot manufacture current authority. In this declared observable interface the exact one-shot boundary is the existence of a lane clear in every reachable occupancy consistent with the current readout. Necessity is that a deterministic single choice must work on all indistinguishable states; sufficiency is selecting that lane using the physical future-reservation rule. In particular, all $r$-subsets are possible without a timely report and their union covers all lanes. The original \#354 lower bound and constructive converse for timely information remain applicable. No converse for arbitrary sensing, provider replacement, or intermittent timing is claimed.

\paragraph{Cost and comparator.}
Reuse the receiver dispatch rule and proof, physical reservation laws and old-service proof. The adapter adds two local checks per considered message, current producer and arrival at or before the cut, and fixes the two public interval endpoints. The information the customer actually consumes is one configured lane label (at most $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ binary bits, excluding authenticity/framing) or no timely report; receiver reads of the raw grid are zero. Proving each implementation minimizes no cost metric: one must verify the provider's accessible read set, interpret those bits, check its returned index is a true local witness, and certify its delivery schedule separately. Those source acquisition costs are $n$ lane reads for the full scan and $r+1$ for the replacement on this defined port. These are interface construction and observation counts, not wall-clock, sensor price, cryptography overhead or run-time savings. A matched direct assume--guarantee proof checks the same contracts and reuses the same receiver proof. \href{https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~heineman/html/teaching_/CS562/p109-de_alfaro.pdf}{Interface automata} motivate the receiver admissibility condition; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12085}{information-flow guided synthesis} illustrates the distinct question of trace-level information obligations. No novelty over these baselines is asserted.

\paragraph{Replay.}
The pinned earlier source provides only reached prefix generation, the fixed installed decoder, and the independent physical trace verifier to the harness; neither of the new provider modules imports it. The audit compares 1,482 separate provider runs (three per reachable cut) and 2,964 joint and standalone resource traces. Proven counts and views are scoped to the trusted modular plant, not specifications for real hardware.
