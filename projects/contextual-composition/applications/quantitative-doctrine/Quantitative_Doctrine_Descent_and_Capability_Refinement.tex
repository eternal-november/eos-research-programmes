\begin{abstract}
A context may ask whether a command preserves safety, or how much safety it guarantees. These questions require different quotients of internal state. In a finite capability-sensitive probabilistic system with demonic uncertainty, exact descent of every lower-expectation transformer is equivalent to equality of convex hulls of projected transition distributions, together with equality of enabled interfaces. We prove closure under finite adaptive observation-based clients and give a terminating coarsest strong refinement algorithm. A robust authorization example separates exact quantitative abstraction from Boolean must-safety abstraction and from unnecessarily strict equality of transition menus. This is a finite specialization of established probabilistic-transformer ideas, not a new general doctrine for arbitrary generalized contextads.
\end{abstract}
\section{Programme connection and scope}
The programme's constructions/enrichments catalogue proposes logical doctrines and predicate transformers as a conditional extension. Its integrated acceptance dossier, section ``Contextual replacement is a native relational theorem'', fixes public views, retains private stores at intermediate leaves, and requires every client primitive to respect the proposed relation. An audit primitive that reads forgotten state can invalidate replacement. We retain this discipline: observation and admitted actions are fixed first, and clients cannot inspect a hidden implementation label.

Our context is a current state with an enabled command interface; content for a chosen command is a finite distribution of successor states, including the successor interface. Sequential substitution pushes forward and sums probability weights. We add a finite outer set of possible kernels, interpreted demonically by a lower envelope. This extra uncertainty semantics is declared explicitly, not inferred from a purported general distributive law between probability and nondeterminism. Public output events can be included in the successor state; finite retained history can be added as another finite component. We omit costs, unbounded iteration, continuous spaces, quantum structure and higher-order code. No claim is made about full abstraction, native witness equality, LPDO classification, or novelty of probabilistic bisimulation.

\section{The finite logical semantics}
Let $S$ be finite and let $E(s)$ be the nonempty finite enabled action set at $s$. For $a\in E(s)$, let $K_a(s)$ be a finite nonempty subset of $\mathcal D(S)$, the probability simplex. The demon chooses a kernel using the actual state and available past history, before that kernel's fresh random outcome is drawn. This is not a demon that sees the future outcome. Randomizing the kernel choice makes no difference to a lower expectation.
For a real postexpectation $f:S\to\mathbb R$, put
\[
 W_af(s)=\min_{\mu\in K_a(s)}\sum_t\mu(t)f(t).
\]
A quotient $q:S\twoheadrightarrow A$ has pullback $q^*g=g\circ q$. Write
\[
 C_a(s)=\operatorname{conv}\{q_*\mu:\mu\in K_a(s)\}\subseteq\mathcal D(A).
\]
We demand availability descent, $q(s)=q(t)\Rightarrow E(s)=E(t)$. Without it an abstractly legal client can be ill-typed at one representative. Exact quantitative descent means a well-defined $\overline W_a$ with $W_aq^*=q^*\overline W_a$ at all states where $a$ is enabled.

\begin{theorem}[Exact descent criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Assume availability descent. Every action's lower transformer descends for every real postexpectation on $A$ if and only if $C_a(s)=C_a(t)$ whenever $q(s)=q(t)$ and $a$ is enabled. Testing every $[0,1]$-valued postexpectation is already sufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A linear functional has the same minimum on a finite menu and its convex hull. Thus equal hulls give equal minima and define the descended transformer. Conversely distinct compact convex polytopes are strictly separated at some point: if $x\in C_a(s)\setminus C_a(t)$, there is a linear functional $\ell$ with $\ell(x)<\min_{y\in C_a(t)}\ell(y)$ (reverse its sign if necessary). Hence the minima differ. Write its coordinates as $g$; subtract their minimum and divide by their range. The range is nonzero, since constant functionals cannot separate probability distributions. These operations preserve the strict inequality and yield a $[0,1]$ postexpectation. For rational vertices a rational strict separator exists, since the strict inequality persists under sufficiently small rational perturbations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite adaptive clients]
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, every fixed finite client that chooses only enabled commands from its public observation and retained public history has the same lower expected terminal public payoff in concrete and quotient semantics, at every concrete starting state. Public randomized client choices are allowed if their kernels depend only on that same information.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Backward induction on the finite client tree. A terminal payoff pulls back through $q$. At an action node the induction hypothesis makes the continuation value constant on each public successor and retained-history fibre; the descent identity gives equality at its predecessor. Public branching selects equal continuation functions on equal histories; public randomized branching takes the same weighted sums. These steps cover the finite tree. The minimizing adversary may depend on actual state: all its available projected convex hulls are the same on a fibre. Finiteness allows the minima at successive nodes to be attained by history-dependent choices. The proof does not assert a joint coupling preserving hidden audit labels or all possible probability laws.
\end{proof}

\section{Boolean safety can erase the relevant obstruction}
For a safe set $B\subseteq A$, the Boolean must transformer is true iff every possible transition has its entire support in $q^{-1}B$. Set
$U_a(s)=\bigcup_{\mu\in K_a(s)}\operatorname{supp}(q_*\mu)$.
\begin{proposition}
The Boolean must transformer descends for every safe set iff $U_a(s)$ is constant on each enabled action fibre. This is strictly weaker than quantitative descent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Must-safety is exactly $U_a(s)\subseteq B$. Two finite subsets have identical containment tests for all $B$ iff equal (take $B$ to be either subset). The example below has equal unions of supports and distinct convex hulls.
\end{proof}
This is a distinction between Boolean certainty and quantitative expectation, not between arbitrary Boolean indicators and expectations: the expected value of the indicator of success already detects unequal success probabilities.

\section{A capability-sensitive authorization gateway}
States are $L,R,U,G$. The first three are implementations of a ready interface with command \texttt{request}; $G$ exposes the new capability \texttt{execute}, which is a self-loop. A request grants the capability with probability $p$ (moves to $G$) and otherwise retains the same ready implementation. The admissible reliability menus are
\[
 P_L=\{1/4,3/4\},\qquad P_R=\{1/4,1/2,3/4\},\qquad P_U=\{1/2,3/4\}.
\]
These probabilities describe a bounded mathematical gateway model, not a security or reliability claim about real authorization systems. The demon may choose a fresh allowed reliability each request; the hidden mode persists.

The naive public quotient identifies $L,R,U$ as ready. All three give the same Boolean must-safety tests: each request has positive probability of ready and granted. But the postexpectation $1_G$ has lower values $1/4,1/4,1/2$. Thus no single exact quantitative ready-state transformer exists.

Splitting ready into $\{L,R\}$ and $\{U\}$ repairs the defect. Although $L$ and $R$ have different transition menus, their projected convex hulls are the same interval, so they may be merged. Demanding literal menu equality would retain an irrelevant distinction. At $G$ the interface changes; pretending \texttt{execute} exists at every state would destroy the typed interpretation.

An adaptive client requests again only if still ready, and executes when granted, with at most $h$ requests. Its worst-case success probabilities are
\[
 V_h(L)=V_h(R)=1-(3/4)^h,\qquad V_h(U)=1-(1/2)^h.
\]
Indeed $V_0=0$ on ready, and $V_{h+1}(s)=\min_{p\in P_s}[p+(1-p)V_h(s)]$. Since $0\leq V_h\leq1$, the minimum uses the smallest $p$. At two requests the guarantees are $7/16$ and $3/4$. The repaired quotient exactly preserves these adaptive guarantees; the naive quotient cannot.

\section{Constructive refinement rather than an intractable invariant}
Start from a finite partition $\Pi_0$ specifying observations we refuse to merge. At each round split each current block by (i) enabled action set and (ii) equality, for each common action, of convex hulls of transition distributions projected onto the current blocks. Repeat until no split occurs.
\begin{theorem}[Coarsest strong exact refinement]
The procedure terminates after at most $|S|-|\Pi_0|$ strict rounds and returns the coarsest refinement of $\Pi_0$ satisfying availability and exact quantitative descent. For rational kernels every test is decidable using rational linear feasibility.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every strict round increases the block count by at least one, never beyond $|S|$. At a fixed point the defining conditions hold. Suppose $R$ is any stable partition refining $\Pi_0$. Inductively it refines $\Pi_k$. If two states are in one $R$ block, their projected hulls on $R$ coincide, and pushing these hulls forward to $\Pi_k$ preserves equality because linear maps commute with convex hull. Their enabled sets also coincide. They therefore remain together at the next split. Thus $R$ refines the fixed point, proving the coarsest claim. Equality of finite hulls is equivalent to every vertex of either menu belonging to the other's hull; membership is finite linear feasibility with nonnegative weights summing to one.
\end{proof}
The word ``strong'' matters. Refinement adds state distinctions needed for one-step transformer descent. It is not proved to be the coarsest equivalence of a smaller original client language, nor to reconstruct hidden states from original observations. In the gateway one split yields $\{L,R\},\{U\},\{G\}$.

A universally sound but possibly pessimistic alternative is to use the union of all projected menus in each coarse block. This enlarges adversarial choices, so lower bounds decrease. It can allow switching between hidden representatives at successive steps without a corresponding concrete trajectory. Exact descent, rather than a convenient interval bound alone, is what prevents that loss here.

\section{Checks, attribution and next boundary}
The accompanying standard-library Python checker uses exact rational arithmetic. It checks convex-hull membership by enumerating affinely independent vertex subsets and solving the augmented linear systems; verifies the refinement, unequal menus with equal hulls, all four public Boolean safety tests, the separating quantitative predicate, and six adaptive horizons; and checks that availability differences prevent a merge. Two planar triangle cases exercise non-collinear hull membership. These are finite diagnostics supporting the example, not machine-checked proofs of the theorems.

The underlying lower-expectation semantics is established probabilistic predicate-transformer mathematics \cite{morgan}. MDP state equivalence and quotient minimization are also established \cite{givan}. Our purpose is to instantiate those ideas within this programme's interface/observation discipline and identify exactly what the logical enrichment must retain. No literature novelty claim is made. The general convexification and abstraction machinery belongs to that prior theory; this note does not settle a doctrine on all the programme's constructions. A genuinely harder next obligation would specify a fibred logical semantics for changing interfaces and prove its substitution law, including partiality and observation restrictions, rather than merely adding more finite states.
