# From joint requirements to realizable interfaces

**Status:** Research direction. The cited constructions have their own stated hypotheses. The proposed law-to-interface construction and its transport theorem are open.

Contextual composition can describe how separately specified interfaces compose, and whether their actual executions and witnesses survive a comparison. The [concurrent-activity programme](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/README.md) supplies a design question that runs in the other direction: **what interfaces would make a joint requirement executable in the first place?** The desired output is an arrangement with compatible strategy witnesses, or an obstruction that explains which requirement the proposed arrangement cannot meet. There can be several valid arrangements with different distributions of freedom, information, cost, and responsibility.

## Start from the requirement and the actual wiring

Fix an assembly of histories and a requirement on its behavior. Pulling the requirement back along a deterministic assembly gives exactly the acceptable joint inputs for that assembly. An uncontrolled branch needs a specified quantifier: some outcome meets the requirement, every admitted outcome meets it, or a strategy meets it against every admitted response. These are different design problems. A requirement covering deadlines, available actions or permitted disclosures should not be substituted for the native mathematical notion of a set of admissible laws. See the Garden’s [goal doctrine and demanded law](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/goal-doctrine-and-demanded-law.md).

A shared capacity constraint illustrates the design freedom. Many splits of a fixed quota can support safe independent spending; the constraint itself grants no party a particular split. A transferable allocation preserves different opportunities from a fixed division, but needs a protocol that accounts for in-flight rights and settles late receipts. Changing the wiring also changes the demands derived from it. The design loop runs from joint requirement to local demands, checks realizability, proposes a repair, then derives the demands again.

## What existing constructions already supply

[Nonblocking History Controllers and Certified Exports](../foundations/native-whole-scope-feedback/Nonblocking_History_Controllers_and_Certified_Exports.tex) already distinguishes the actual controller view, the selected whole-plan law, the service view, and the retained incoming reserve. Its calibration equations identify a failure independent of read permission: a controller can lawfully select a label using a readable reserve bit and still change the distribution of that reserve conditional on the label. It then cannot execute the selected plan with its claimed incoming marginal unless the law is calibrated. Drawing a new reserve would change the execution.

[Native Multilimits and Selector Obstruction](../foundations/native-multilimits/Native_Multilimits_and_Selector_Obstruction.tex) gives a different failure: a witness-dependent choice of continuation need not descend through a comparison that forgets the witness. Keeping the possible continuations is not yet a strategy for choosing among them. [Residual Profiles and Admission Reflection](../feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Residual_Profiles_and_Admission_Reflection.tex) derives adequate profiles relative to specified primitive tests; it does not assert that adding a consumer leaves those profiles adequate.

The open step is to construct views, allocations, accounts, and controllers rather than supply them. A useful design procedure should distinguish a missing observation, incompatible local strategies, unavailable correlation, biased plan selection, failure of a native comparison, and a duty left without a realizable continuation. These failures call for different changes. [Designing concurrent interactions](designing-concurrent-interactions.md) develops the architecture and remaining-duty questions. [Information and capability boundaries](information-and-capability-boundaries.md) gives the separate test for prohibited powers created by an otherwise functional repair.

## What would count as a first result

For one existing assembly constructor and a finite declared class of interface changes, derive a local implementation obligation from the joint requirement. Construct a candidate with its actual strategy, calibration, observation, and ownership evidence, or return a counterexample to the proposal. Then prove that the resulting obligation and witness transport along a specified native substitution or comparison. Preserve actual branch addresses and say whether the comparison covers stipulated zero and off-policy histories as well as the execution law at the chosen prior. Equality at endpoints is insufficient.

A bounded comparison of fixed and transferable quotas would test several aspects at once. Use the same capacity invariant and positive service requirement for both designs. Retain actual custody during transfer, delayed or duplicated acknowledgment, and a duty outstanding across a handoff. Compare independent action opportunities and what happens to the duty after each transfer. Include a count-only summary as a negative control: if it merges two histories that a revised reclaim rule must distinguish, no deterministic migration using that summary alone can recover the difference. These are proposed tests of the construction, not a new quota protocol or a claim of universal synthesis.
