# Designing concurrent interactions with contextual composition

**Status:** Research direction. The constructions cited below are existing results at their stated scopes. This note proposes a use for them; it does not change the project contract or certify a new implementation.

## From interpreting a wiring to choosing one

Given an assembly of activities, contextual composition describes how the activities interact, how one result changes later inputs, and which comparisons preserve the resulting behavior. The Garden’s [concurrent-activity programme](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/README.md) asks a complementary question: which assembly should be built when activities have separate principals, resources, information and purposes? A shared aim, common observer and global scheduler are optional architecture choices. They do not define the class of problems.

The Garden’s [demanded-law note](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/goal-doctrine-and-demanded-law.md) starts with a declared joint goal and takes its preimage along the actual assembly on histories. That backward calculation determines what the wiring demands. It does not determine a division of authority or resources. For example, two parties with a shared budget can be jointly safe under many incomparable allocations. Projecting allowed joint choices onto each party and recombining those projections can overspend the budget. An allocation or coordination protocol changes what choices can safely be made independently.

This suggests a design loop. Start with the same joint requirements and several candidate arrangements. Derive the demands of each arrangement, check whether its participants can meet them from their actual views and powers, revise the wiring when they cannot, and recompute the demands. An additional message changes who can know what. Escrow or delegation changes who can act. Synchronization changes when either can act. Those changes can also create new duties to maintain a record, manage authority, recover from interruption, or finish work already promised. The comparison ought to explain these differences even if the arrangements satisfy the same headline goal.

## Reuse the project’s distinctions

- [Observable covers and local observers](../feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Observable_Covers_and_Local_Observers.tex) derives readiness and information certificates from specified profiles and actual assembly. It does not infer the assembly constraints or the joint law. [Residual profiles](../feedback-and-concurrency/observable-covers/Residual_Profiles_and_Admission_Reflection.tex) constructs a coarsest adequate profile for declared primitive tests. Adding a future consumer can invalidate that profile; maintaining or migrating the account is an additional operation. See the Garden’s [maintainable accounts](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/sufficient-state-and-maintainable-accounts.md).
- [Constructive local fairness](../feedback-and-concurrency/constructive-local-fairness/Constructive_Local_Fairness_and_Native_Completion.tex) synthesizes local supports for stationary private-randomness policies relative to a supplied decision table. The Garden’s [conditional autonomy](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/conditional-autonomy-and-coordination.md) motivates comparing the envelopes of independent action the arrangements leave open, including who can change an allocation. A static safe envelope does not implement a transfer or settle in-flight claims.
- [Calibrated online repair](../foundations/native-whole-scope-feedback/historical-diagrams-and-resources/dynamic/Calibrated_Online_Repair_Resources.tex) and [controlled recovery](../protocols/refinement-recovery/Contextual_Refinement_and_Controlled_Recovery.tex) work with actual effects, historical owners and stipulated recovery authority. An extension should ask which duties survive the change and whether any authorized continuation can still discharge them. A change to the governing obligation is a different move from a new implementation of the old one. See the Garden’s [residual obligations and revision](https://github.com/eternal-november/eos-garden/blob/f121aa5bec7415a8dbac301defb364ecddf62ecd/theories-and-designs/concurrent-activity/residual-obligations-recovery-and-revision.md).

## A useful stress test, not the organizing case

Suppose an actor must spend one of two reserves now; spending either is currently legal. A later duty needs the reserve named by a bit that is hidden now. For each value of the bit there is a successful plan, but no choice from the actor’s current view works for both values. Revealing the bit, deferring the spend, and assigning the choice to a different authority are distinct changes. Their suitability depends on who needs to act, the deadline, and what information the arrangement must protect.

A bounded version can use the [reserved-service example](../applications/reservation-service/Reserved_Resource_Service_and_Progress.tex) with explicit delayed acknowledgment and reclamation. Test whether a dependent handoff carries a single executable continuation on indistinguishable exits, with ownership and receipt identity intact. A finite imperfect-information game supplies a standard candidate calculation of the winning region ([Chatterjee, Doyen, Henzinger and Raskin](https://lmcs.episciences.org/1094)); relating that calculation to the project’s native substitution and preserving actual deadlines and obligations is open. Mixed-version replacement gives a different test: a count-only old record may be unable to migrate distinct outstanding reservations to a new position-sensitive recovery rule. Continuing old sessions until their duties are discharged can be a different admissible design.
