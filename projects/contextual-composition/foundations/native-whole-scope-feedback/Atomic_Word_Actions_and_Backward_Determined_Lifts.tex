\paragraph{Scope.} Two constructive repairs to the reviewed birth-factorization obstruction, \texttt{Finite\_Lift\_Covers\_and\_Birth\_Factorization\_Obstruction.tex}. The first retains atomic words for arbitrary finite lift covers. The second restricts the cover class so category-arrow factorizations lift uniquely and the class is stable under the reviewed progressive graded-span operations (\texttt{Progressive\_Graded\_Spans\_and\_Causal\_Lift.tex}). \section{Unrestricted finite-cover repair by atomic words}
Fix an atomic directed graph $G$ with vertices in a category $A$ and each edge assigned an actual $A$ arrow. Let $p:K\to A$ be a finite lift cover: object fibres and source-arrow lift sets are finite nonempty. Form a lift graph whose vertices over $a$ are $O_p(a)$ and whose edges over a generator $e:a\to b$ are all $u\in L_p(k,e)$, with their actual source and target. Preserve generator labels even when two generators represent the same $A$ arrow. The action base is the free path category $\operatorname{Path}G$; its identity is the empty word, not a newly sampled lift of an identity-labelled generator.

A lift of $e_1\cdots e_n$ from $k$ is exactly a composable tuple $(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ of such edge lifts. All tuples remain, including future zero-probability ones. Concatenation is the actual bijection between lifts of two adjacent words with matching intermediate lifted object and lifts of their concatenation. Empty-word lifts are singletons.

\begin{theorem}[Finite atomic-word action]
This defines an action by finite spans on the object fibres, with canonical associative concatenation and identities. Cover maps over $A$ induce maps of all lift spans commuting with concatenation. Pullback of the atomic graph, and composition of finite lift covers, preserve this construction with the corresponding retained intermediate lift records.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite nonempty edge lifts give finite nonempty word lifts by induction. Splitting a tuple at the word boundary and concatenating it are inverse. Repeated splitting retains the same tuple, proving associativity and the pentagon. A cover functor maps each lifted edge to its image and hence maps each tuple componentwise. For composed covers the intermediate edge is the image of the final lifted edge, so retaining it adds no choice. Pullback merely retains the same lifts over the pulled-back labelled edge.\end{proof}

A history-indexed whole-law choice on each edge lift set, including every zero/off-policy history and complete law labels, yields the usual product of conditional probabilities on a word's raw tuple. Admissibility and native pushforward must hold at each full history, as in the independently proved temporal theorem. Actual contexts tensor whole per-letter scope coefficients once. Primitive owner/disclosure annotations and complete unused writer grades stay attached to their letters. This is a typed temporal consumer, not a law selected by the lift geometry. Forgetting a word to its composite $A$ arrow generally loses its birth time and can miss direct lifts, exactly as in the reviewed counterexample.

This does not yet define a new graded polynomial multiplication on arbitrary nested word protocols: a single outer generator may contain inner words of incompatible lengths. The synchronized atomic subgraph theorem applies only when globally old actors, one-scope boundaries and newborn-silent paths are explicitly aligned. No silent erasure of inner events is made.

\section{A restricted class stable under progressive tuples}
Call $p:K\to A$ a backward-determined finite cover if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item each object fibre is finite nonempty;
\item for every arrow $f:a\to b$ and object $y$ over $b$, there is exactly one arrow into $y$ over $f$ (the discrete-fibration property);
\item every object $x$ over $a$ has at least one outgoing lift of $f$.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently the associated presheaf $F:A^{op}\to\mathsf{FinSet}$ has nonempty values and every restriction $F(f):F(b)\to F(a)$ is surjective. Outgoing lifts from $x$ are indexed by the finite fibre $F(f)^{-1}(x)$. Finite nonempty discrete bifibrations are examples; allowing surjective rather than bijective restrictions is essential for closure under new-position births.

\begin{theorem}[Closed backward-determined class and unique factorization]
Backward-determined finite covers are stable under arbitrary strict pullback, composition and $P_C$ for every entire collection $C$. They therefore form a closed class of left legs for progressive content-span composition. Every such cover has unique lifting of a specified factorization: for any lift $h:x\to z$ over $gf$, there is exactly one pair $(u,v)$ with $h=vu$, $p(u)=f$, $p(v)=g$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pullback preserves incoming uniqueness, finite object fibres and outgoing existence. For a composite, lift an incoming arrow uniquely through the second functor and then through the first; outgoing existence follows in the opposite order. Object fibres are finite unions of finite nonempty fibres.

For $P_Cp$, fix a target lifted tuple and a base progressive arrow into its image. Its position injection identifies every old target position; each corresponding component arrow has a unique incoming lift, determining the source lifted object and component arrow. New target positions contribute no source coordinate. Parent phases and shapes are fixed by the base arrow. Thus there is precisely one incoming lifted arrow. Object fibres are finite products. Given a lifted source tuple, outgoing existence follows by selecting a target lift for each old component using surjectivity, and any object over each new position using nonempty fibres. This works at unfinished births and at completed shapes; no finiteness of the collection itself is used.

Finally lift $g$ uniquely into $z$, obtaining $v:y\to z$, then lift $f$ uniquely into $y$, obtaining $u:x'\to y$. Their composite and $h$ are both incoming lifts of $gf$ into $z$, hence coincide; in particular $x'=x$. Incoming uniqueness also forces every competing factorization to be this one.\end{proof}

The unique-factorization map commutes with every cover functor over the same base: a functor preserves composition and the target factorization is unique. Hence direct and sequential raw lift carriers are naturally bijective in this restricted class. The earlier counterexample is excluded precisely because its isolated $b_1$ has no incoming lift of $a\to b$. In presheaf language that example was outgoing transport, whereas the present restriction determines predecessors from final objects.


\section{History-base realization of the positive class}
The surjective restriction maps need not be invertible. Their meaning is unique prefix extraction and total extension, not reversible dynamics. This gives a direct realization of the atomic-word repair in the closed class after a declared change of base objects.

Fix a set of starting vertices and retain each start as a separate root. Let $\mathcal T_G$ be the category whose objects are finite input words from those starts and whose arrows are prefix inclusions. There is at most one arrow between two such words. Let $F(w)$ be the finite set of complete lifted histories of $w$, including the initial fibre object. For the empty word this is exactly the finite nonempty initial fibre. Restriction along a prefix inclusion trims the lifted tuple. It is surjective because every omitted next input edge has a nonempty lift from every current lifted object. It is unique on each complete history by literal trimming. Thus $F:\mathcal T_G^{op}\to\mathsf{FinSet}$ is a nonempty-valued presheaf with surjective restrictions, and its category-of-elements projection is a backward-determined finite cover. This retains all raw paths and all zero paths; no probabilities have been used.

Parallel generators with equal original endpoints remain distinct letters and give different objects of $\mathcal T_G$. Even if their composites agree in $A$, no new history arrow identifies them. At each fixed finite input history the fibre is finite, though there need not be a uniform bound over histories. Evaluating these objects only at their final $A$ endpoint is a different quotient and can give infinite fibres: a single input self-loop already has histories of every finite length over the same endpoint. Hence this realization does not claim finite-cover closure over the original endpoint category. Maps of the labelled input graphs and compatible cover maps act on history objects and lifted tuples; restriction squares commute. If a proposed map erases letters or imposes input relations, its different history identification requires separate verification rather than this graph-map argument.

\section{What can and cannot descend from words}
For unconstrained probability laws on finite lift carriers, the unique-factorization bijection identifies laws on direct lifts with laws on composable lift pairs. Every joint law on pairs has a marginal on its first lift and a conditional law of the second lift at each positive first outcome. Conversely these conditional laws assemble that joint. Zero first-outcome conditional choices remain independent extra controls: this is a bijection of \emph{integrated joint laws}, not of complete all-zero kernel plans. With restricted admitted law families, closure additionally requires that the resulting marginals and conditional laws belong to those families. Unique raw factorization alone does not prove it.

Likewise, native paid descent to the quotient category requires actual coefficient and full-grade compatibility. If a direct lift $h$ is identified with $vu$, its direct context must have the supplied comparison to the chronological tensor of the two whole scope contexts, with the stated owner/admission regime; it is not automatically that tensor. A simultaneous owner product is not thereby identified with temporal composition. The full content body and unused grade maps must obey their own squares. The free-word action avoids those identifications by retaining the two events. The restricted backward-determined theorem repairs raw factorization but not these independent law/material equations.

The class also excludes autonomous nontrivial vertical response motion: a discrete fibration has only the identity arrow over an identity into a fixed target. A suspended entry corolla with multiple vertical witness arrows is therefore not automatically in it. Such a corolla belongs to the broader multivalued/path-indexed signature regime, with its law family and owner factors explicitly supplied. Thus the positive closure is substantial but deliberately restricted, not a claim about every progressive paid protocol.

Pure input pullback may be noninjective and remains within both stated constructions. This is not deterministic controller descent along a quotient: complete controls can still disagree on histories that an external map merges. Native controlled transport requires the full-history command equation, exact initial/row law pushforward and the actual owner/full-grade arrows. These distinctions keep the closed raw action class separate from an unproved complete paid-relative module.
