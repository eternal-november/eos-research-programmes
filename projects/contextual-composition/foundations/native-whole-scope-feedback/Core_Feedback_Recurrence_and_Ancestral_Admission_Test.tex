\section{One original primitive parameter}
Use a singleton world; paid identity-world steps remain genuine events. Let $A=\{a\}$, $U=\{r,\ell\}$ and $B=\{0,1,2\}$. Define one primitive system $P:A+U\rightsquigarrow B+U$. Its finite initializer data are as follows. At $a$ the singleton initializer selects a completed root with two recursive ports, both labeled $r$. At $r$ the initializer has carrier $z\in\{0,1,2\}$ and law $p=(1/2,1/2,0)$; it selects an active state with one external port labeled $z$ and, iff $z\in\{1,2\}$, one recursive port labeled $\ell$. At $\ell$ the initializer has carrier $t\in\{0,1,2\}$ with the same $p$ and selects a completed state with one external port labeled $t$ and no recursive port. All rows have their common all-raw selected-state maps.

At each active $r$ state there are two primitive paid commands. The command \emph{spawn} has carrier $x\in\{0,1,2\}$ with law $p$; it retains the old ports, emits one new recursive $r$ port, and completes the old primitive state, retaining $x$ as a private record. The command \emph{stop} has a singleton row and completes that state without emitting a new port. Completed primitive states have no active command. Passive rebase is the identity. Thus paid births can recur indefinitely through new $r$ invocations, but no newborn actor takes a paid step in its birth event.

The all-raw silent initializer relation is $a\leadsto r\leadsto\ell$, with no outgoing relation from $\ell$. In particular the zero raw alternative $z=2$ has a real silent child and is included in this finite guard. The paid transition $r\rightsquigarrow r$ is not a silent initializer edge. Confusing the two would incorrectly reject this recurrent example or incorrectly permit an infinite silent cascade.

The native compiler therefore has its actual finite initial carrier, selected configurations and complete initializer-law family at every input. Its initial $a$ tree can correlate the two complete $r$ initializer trees while keeping their required conditional marginals. At every paid event the new $r$ invocation is filled by its COMPLETE finite initializer tree, not merely by its root witness $z$.

\section{Two active owners and nested silent requests}
Consider an all-raw configuration containing two active $r$ actors, for example the positive initial alternative in which both initial $r$ witnesses were zero. Select \emph{spawn} at both in one common scope. Use a frozen driver $E$ containing the common event/boundary ticket and prescope actor/sponsor facts, with singleton public raw carrier. It does not acquire current private outcomes. For owner $i$, the expanded raw record is
\[
 (x_i,z_i,t_i),\qquad t_i=\bot\text{ if }z_i=0,
 \quad t_i\in\{0,1,2\}\text{ if }z_i\in\{1,2\}.
\]
There are $3(1+3+3)=21$ local raw atoms and $441$ two-owner atoms. The first request predecessor is $x_i$. The nested $\ell$ predecessor is the full producing chain $(x_i,z_i)$, which determines its presence, input and actual world. No sibling's current paid or initializer coordinate is included. Both initializer factors inherit the original paid owner $i$, not an invented silent paid owner.

Take independent fair $x_1,x_2$ and another fair bit $b$, and set
\[
 z_1=x_2,\qquad z_2=x_1,\qquad
 t_i=\begin{cases}\bot,&z_i=0,\\b,&z_i=1.\end{cases}        \tag{1}
\]
This describes a normalized law on the full retained carrier, assigning zero to the other atoms, not a replacement of that carrier. For either owner its complete marginal is
\[
 \Pr(x_i=x,z_i=z,t_i=t)=p(x)p(z)
 \begin{cases}1,&z=0,t=\bot,\\p(t),&z\ne0.\end{cases}     \tag{2}
\]
Thus its paid marginal is correct; its new $r$ initializer is fair conditional on its own $x_i$; and the silent $\ell$ initializer is fair conditional on $(x_i,z_i)$ wherever that predecessor is positive. This is precisely the compiler's complete-child-tree family by the ancestral-equivalence lemma. The whole scope may therefore choose (1). It has seven positive raw atoms and 434 zero atoms. In particular retained atoms with $z_i=2$ and $t_i=2$ still have actual selected states and coefficient fields.

\begin{proposition}[Concrete feedback/core compatibility]
This compiled deployment has a same-event core presentation with frozen $E$, persistent owner/private tags, declared ancestor read projections and unchanged ancestral admission, including every zero atom. Its recurrent execution never requires promoting sibling reserve into $E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each invocation's existing state is local opaque state. Its primitive command template uses that state and its own selected command, not a sibling law label. Every new request and its selected state depends on its own paid atom and its own initializer ancestry, as listed above. The local law parameter is the complete expanded marginal (2); the global law (1) remains a separately retained whole-scope plan label. Root-family membership uses precisely these ancestor tests and no extra whole-block predicate. The complete raw initializer-tree carrier and state functions are defined before law selection, so the factorization holds on zero atoms as well.

Allocate each new invocation by run-qualified event, exact primitive sponsor and persistent port/initializer path. Deeper silent children retain the same paid owner and their own ancestry. Readdressing root blocks does not change these names or the read incidences. At the next event the already installed state/actor records are prescope data; a new frozen $E$ can expose only the boundary/history data separately declared for that event. No same-event promotion is used. Applying the same construction at each finite configuration gives another finite configuration and a finite selected row. Indefinite spawn choices give genuine paid divergence; choosing stop at all remaining active invocations gives completion. Neither case calls for infinite silent initialization.\end{proof}

A complete external controller may select global scopes or joint laws using retained full histories. That does not make a primitive local template read those histories. This proposition neither quotients initial-law or whole-command-law labels nor proves descent for arbitrary controllers through root regrouping. A proposed core-local controller must additionally factor through its stated read interface at every zero and off-policy history. The compiler's native initial fold alone supplies no such selected control.

\section{Actual coefficients, terminal grade and compiler scope}
The singleton $a$ initializer has context coefficient $\mathbb R$ with identity augmentation, and every singleton \emph{stop} paid row has that same augmented unit coefficient. All terminal coefficient maps to the prescribed unit content coefficients are the supplied context augmentations (and their tensor composites when a complete initializer tree is consumed); no additional unchosen scalar or coefficient map is required. Put $K_i(x)=\mathbb R^2$ with first-coordinate augmentation for each spawn row. Put $L_r(z)=\mathbb R^2$ for $z=0,1$ and $\mathbb R^3$ for $z=2$, and similarly $L_\ell(t)$. The complete expanded owner coefficient is
\[
 W_i=K_i(x_i)\otimes L_r(z_i)\otimes
       \begin{cases}\mathbb R,&t_i=\bot,\\L_\ell(t_i),&t_i\ne\bot.\end{cases}
\]
The scope context is $W_1\times_{\mathbb R}W_2$, not a product of paid coefficients followed by a free tensor of seeds. Initial trees instead have the pure tensor of their actual initializer factors with no paid owner. Native root decomposition regroups entire owner factors and pure initial tensors, exactly as in the source compiler. All coefficient objects remain on zero atoms.

Choose the whole ordered collection $C$ with one shape $c_n$ of coefficient unit at every arity $n\ge0$. A completed primitive state uses $c_n$ at its actual ordered $B+U$ port list; unused shapes remain. Choose explicitly the WHOLE-CASE grade constructor
\[
 G(E)=C\circ(I+U\cdot E),\qquad \mu G=R_U C.             \tag{3}
\]
Its actual completed body chooses the $I$ branch at an external $B$ port and the labeled recursive branch at a $U$ port. Every other formal branch and finite shape remains in (3), including those not realized by this primitive program. The terminal coefficient map is the actual finite-tree tensor recursion. Context coefficients above are independent of these unit CONTENT coefficients. This is the separately declared whole-case branch, not an identification with the smaller colored terminal grammar of the original command-indexed theorem.

The source fixed-parameter initializer theorem applies: all raw silent initialization is well founded, common raw/selected-state/coefficient fields are supplied, and families are convex and distinguished. It supplies command-indexed initiality in its OWN native algebra category. A free global writer version requires its explicit all-formal schema library and arrows on every unused schema. Initialization adds no paid writer event or schema label. None of those statements proves initiality for arbitrary core-indexed algebras without a separately typed constructor and fold compatibility. For the heterogeneous compiler, the corresponding entire grade is $R_U(C\circ(D+I))$ when a fixed continuation $Q$ of full grade $D$ is attached; omitting that $D+I$ reserve is not justified by this example.

\section{Two exact family-admission boundaries}
\paragraph{Adding sibling conditioning changes this deployment.}
If the new $r$ initializer is instead tested fair conditional on the entire simultaneous tuple $(x_1,x_2)$, (1) fails: $z_1$ and $z_2$ are deterministic there. Own paid-plus-ancestry conditioning is therefore essential. Passing to a composite opaque paid owner and conditioning on its entire raw record is a different compiler interface, even if the selected states and raw carrier are unchanged. The successive-entry source can still map directionally into the weaker local-pair family by conditional averaging; equality is false.

\paragraph{Core locality does not eliminate extra whole-block constraints.}
Take a root initializer with two silent child requests, each having fair binary primitive family. The older recursive compiler permits every coupling of their complete child witnesses with those marginals. Now add to a core-local schema the convex block test
\[
                         \Pr(z_1=z_2)\ge\tfrac12.       \tag{4}
\]
This involves only that root's private records. It admits the distinguished independent product and diagonal law, so even product-compatible distinguished initialization does not exclude it. It rejects the antidiagonal fair law, which the child-marginal-only compiler admits. Consequently arbitrary core schema admission is not equal to the older ancestral compiler family. Equality requires a proved equivalence to the ancestral tests, or retaining (4) as actual whole-block data. One can instead encode the entire correlated pair as ONE primitive initializer carrier with its own declared convex root family; then the root-family compiler retains that family. Splitting that primitive into two child requests without retaining its block constraint changes the family. This is an admission obstruction, not merely missing notation or a coefficient issue.
