\title{Shared-scope execution through tensor clock trace: exact native interfaces}\date{16 November 2026}\begin{document}\maketitle
\paragraph{Audit and claim.} Native assembly theorem. This is a structural compiler from a selected owner/request execution interface to instantaneous countable matching trace, using finite causal unrolling and an explicit first-done restriction. It is not identification of retained state, invocation recursion and instantaneous closure, and it is not a generic contextad completion. Chronological contexts tensor entire paid scopes, even when a scope internally uses a joint limit. The terminal occurrence grade and chronological writer-history grade remain two separately typed native observations. No cross-coefficient realization arrow or tensor pairing is invented.

\section{Typed first-done restriction is an additional generator}
Define
\[
 T_n=\{h\in H_n:\ h\text{ is done and its true paid length is }n\},\qquad n\geq0. \tag{2}
\]
Because done prefixes are absorbed, this is exactly first completion at $n$, not terminal-by-$n$. A history initially done belongs to $T_0$ and to no later stratum. A blocked prefix belongs to no $T_n$ and never advances under the closed execution convention. Keep every selected raw zero atom in (2).

Use an independently typed restriction interface: an object is a raw row with a specified subset on which a stated body is defined; a map must have that subset as the exact inverse image of its target subset. Restrict weights and actual coefficient fields to the subset and retain its whole declared grade. Exact fibre pushforward then restricts because every preimage of a selected target point is selected. This is a functor on the typed subset interface. It is not in general an exact-pushforward arrow from the unrestricted row: a fair done/active prefix loses half its mass under first-done restriction. The rejected prefix, its coefficients and its recurrent observations remain a different stored construction.

Give $T_n$ its actual coefficients (1) and terminal body
\[
                     h\longmapsto (c_h,\mathbf b_h),
          \qquad |\mathbf b_h|=\operatorname{arity}(c_h).     \tag{3}
\]
Encoding (3) as a writer shape $c_h$ with ordered-list endpoint loses no data on the declared arity-preserving cells: the inverse reads the arity from the full shape and the entries from the list. World observation can be included in the external endpoint $Y$. Thus each restricted layer is a subprobability presentation of the pinned independent-coefficient $H$ interface. Active and blocked histories have not been assigned nonexistent terminal shapes.

\section{The fixed-clock compiler and actual trace isomorphism}

Its matching trace on a countable clock retains $(n,w)$ exactly when the output clock equals $n$, leaving the context coefficient and full grade unchanged. No countable coefficient tensor is formed; aggregate direct sums are unnecessary for the individual-witness presentation used here.

Define
\[
 K_X:A\times\mathbb N_0\longrightarrow Y\times\mathbb N_0
\]
by taking at $(a,n)$ the finite row $T_{a,n}$, with weight (1), coefficient $K_h$, terminal grade $C_X$ and body returning $(y(h),n,c_h)$. The same full $C_X$, including all unused shapes, is used at every clock input. \begin{theorem}[Tensor-native shared-scope clock bridge]
The process $K_X$ lies in the subprobability partial-trace domain. Its trace is natively isomorphic to the independently defined finite-done history row
\[
 \operatorname{Term}(X,\mu,\sigma)=\coprod_{n\geq0}T_{a,n},
\]
with weights and coefficients (1), body (3), and identity full terminal-grade map. The isomorphism retains every initial witness, paid whole joint, initializer witness and raw zero atom occurring in a finite first-done history. Rejected prefix data are not thereby included in the trace. Its traced mass is the probability of eventual completion. Its result is normalized exactly when that probability is one; the individual clock rows are still generally subprobabilistic, so this is not a claim that $K_X$ belongs to the normalized-clock subcategory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $H_n$ is normalized. The first-done strata through $n$ are disjoint subsets of its stopped outcomes, so their masses sum to at most one. Taking the increasing supremum proves the matching closed-mass bound. Trace has precisely the raw points $(n,h)$ with $h\in T_n$, since the output clock is $n$. The map $(n,h)\mapsto h$ has inverse $(\mathrm{paidlength}(h),h)$. It is a full raw bijection including zero points. Weights agree exactly, contexts agree up to the actual unitors inserted by cup/cap, and the entire grade map is identity on $C_X$, not its realized image. The world and ordered terminal-output body are unchanged. This proves the actual native isomorphism. The scalar mass statement follows from the reviewed path measure and the disjoint first-done events. No infinite accumulated coefficient is required.
\end{proof}

Blocked mass and infinite-active mass are distinct events in the retained prefix/path observer, even though both are absent from the finite-done trace. Initial silent divergence is excluded by the guard. The clock counts paid scopes, not primitive invocations, newly born nodes or a serial schedule: one simultaneous scope with several actors increases it once. Therefore shared world updates and their paid cost are not converted into repeated primitive execution by this compiler.

\section{The terminal-trace kernel includes zero and recurrent behavior}
The source of restriction is the original recurrent prefix/path observer, not only its omitted total probability. The terminal trace forgets globally blocked prefixes, every infinite active raw path and the early-port records on those runs. This includes measure-zero raw paths. It also does not reconstruct complete off-policy control tables. Retaining zero witnesses within each selected $T_n$ does not retain zero witnesses rejected from every $T_n$.

For a concrete witness, take the same two-atom initial carrier $\{g,b\}$ with masses $1,0$ in two systems. The $g$ state is immediately done with the same nullary terminal body. The $b$ state is unfinished, emits an early port labeled $0$ in one system and $1$ in the other, and executes the same paid self-loop forever. Use the same unit coefficients, paid writer schema and full terminal grade with the nullary done shape and any required unused shapes. Both first-done clocks have precisely the same $g$ atom at clock zero and empty later rows, with identical full declared grades. Their all-raw prefixes and zero-probability infinite paths nevertheless have different early-port observations. Thus even normalized terminating mass one does not make the trace faithful to the original open/recurrent interface. This is an observer kernel, not permission to quotient those material paths from the source construction.
