\section{Independent root-family constructor}
Let $P$ now have an initializer family $\mathcal A_P(w,u)$ rather than a single root law. Write its raw carrier $Z_P(w,u)$ and distinguished member $\nu_P$. Define $T_PX$ with exactly the previous configuration, paid-menu, raw, coefficient, world, port and terminal-grade fields. Its initial carrier is
\[
 Z_{T_PX}(w,u)=\coprod_{z\in Z_P(w,u)}\prod_{p\in O_U(\iota_Pz)}Z_X(w,u_p).
\]
A law $\lambda$ is admitted if its root marginal $\mu$ lies in $\mathcal A_P(w,u)$ and, for every positive root atom $z$, the conditional marginal of each child coordinate lies in $\mathcal A_X(w,u_p)$. The distinguished law uses $\nu_P$ and the distinguished child laws independently conditional on $z$. Initial coefficients remain $L^P_z\otimes\bigotimes_pL^X_{\zeta_p}$, on every raw atom. Paid birth expansion is unchanged: each existing paid command already includes its own particular original paid law, and the child-family constraint is conditional on that command's original paid atom. No root-law choice is inserted into a paid event. Membership permits different primitive root laws at different invocation addresses and conditional ancestral histories, as long as each local conditional law belongs to the fixed declared family. It is not a single globally selected law reused at every invocation. All raw, selected-state and actual coefficient maps remain common across every such choice. The fixed-root theorem is exactly the singleton-family restriction.

\begin{lemma}
The initial family is nonempty and convex. A common native child initializer map induces a native initial family map, and the formulas define an endofunctor on the declared fixed-$P$ native category.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The distinguished law proves nonemptiness. For $\lambda=\sum_k a_k\lambda_k$, let $\mu_k$ be the root marginal. Then $\mu=\sum_k a_k\mu_k$ lies in $\mathcal A_P$. At $\mu(z)>0$, the conditional child marginal is the convex combination of the $k$th admitted conditional child marginals with weights $a_k\mu_k(z)/\mu(z)$, omitting zero terms. Its type is fixed by the actual root atom $z$, so all summands lie in the same child family. At $\mu(z)=0$ no conditional is added. A common child raw map acts on each coordinate independently of the law; conditional marginals push into the target families. It preserves the root atom, so no further mixture is needed. Tensor and actual product functoriality give the coefficient maps, and the child initial selected-state square gives the post-square for births. Every remaining field is the unchanged fixed-root construction; none of its paid or schema formulas used a fixed initial root law. Identity and composition hold on these actual fields, not merely on laws.
\end{proof}

\section{The finite feedback carrier and fold}
Construct the same all-raw finite primitive initializer trees as in the fixed-root theorem. Define $\mathcal A_F(w,u)$ recursively by requiring the root marginal to lie in $\mathcal A_P(w,u)$ and the conditional law of each complete child initializer tree, at a positive root atom, to lie in its recursively defined family. The coefficient of a raw tree is the tensor of every primitive initializer coefficient. The guard depends on the whole raw port relation, not on which member of $\mathcal A_P$ is chosen, so it proves finiteness of the same carrier. Convexity and nonemptiness follow by the preceding calculation and well-founded induction. Distinguished laws multiply primitive distinguished probabilities over the tree.

\begin{theorem}[Root-family extension at fixed parameter]
The finite invocation system $F$ is the initial $T_P$-algebra in the declared native category, with the same command-indexed/global/flat-equation-class distinctions as the fixed-root theorem. Its initial family unfolding is exactly the constructor family above, not a selected subfamily.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Root decomposition is a bijection of full initial raw carriers. Its law test is precisely the recursive definition of $\mathcal A_F$, so it identifies the entire families, distinguished laws and actual initial tensor coefficients. The paid root decomposition is unchanged, including passive empty-block responses and complete expanded-row marginals.

For an algebra $\alpha:T_PX\to X$, define the initial raw and actual tensor-coefficient folds by well-founded recursion. Given any $\lambda\in\mathcal A_F$, its root marginal $\mu$ lies in $\mathcal A_P$. At every positive root atom, each complete child-tree conditional law lies in $\mathcal A_F$ for that input; induction pushes it into $\mathcal A_X$. Hence the recursively folded law lies in the whole constructor family $\mathcal A_{T_PX}$, and the initial field of $\alpha$ sends it into $\mathcal A_X$. This proves all-law initial admissibility, not merely distinguished-law preservation. Raw and coefficient maps at zero atoms were defined recursively before choosing $\lambda$.

The selected-state square holds on every raw initializer tree by the same recursion. Uniqueness of the initial raw/coefficient fold follows from its algebra equation on every raw root decomposition; the old prescope equations force all remaining fields. \end{proof}

\section{Exact ancestral form of the recursive initial family}
A request position is a stable address in the finite full raw initializer-tree carrier. Optional positions have an absent sentinel. For a present position $v$, its predecessor $D_v$ records the entire chain of ancestor primitive raw witnesses, the input and world, and the address. It records no siblings. These data determine presence, input, world and the finite witness carrier of that request. At the root the predecessor is just its fixed input/world. At absent positions the witness is forced to the sentinel.

\begin{lemma}[Recursive complete-subtree tests equal ancestral marginal tests]
A law on the complete finite initializer-tree carrier belongs to $\mathcal A_F$ if and only if, at every request $v$ and every positive predecessor value $d$, the conditional marginal of its primitive initializer witness given $D_v=d$ belongs to the primitive initializer family at that input and world.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use induction on the finite all-raw initialization dependency tree for the fixed input. The root test is exactly the root-marginal condition. Conditional on a positive root witness $z$, the request-predecessor tests for a particular child subtree are precisely its own ancestral tests with $z$ prefixed. By induction these are equivalent to the conditional complete child-tree law belonging to its recursive family. Conversely the recursive requirement gives that equivalence in each positive root slice. A predecessor has positive total mass exactly when its successive conditioning slices along that chain are positive; zero slices impose no conditional test in either description, but their raw coordinates remain. Correlations between different child subtrees are unrestricted by both descriptions. The induction applies to each root raw alternative, including alternatives with different child profiles; absent sentinels impose no extra initializer. Thus neither direction adds conditional independence or forgets an ancestor.
\end{proof}

The lemma does not permit replacing ancestry by merely a paid-owner tag. A random parent witness can determine request presence, input or admitted initializer law. Nor does it permit adding unrelated siblings to the conditioning predecessor: the diagonal witnesses in the random post-tightening obstruction show that such an addition changes admission. Under common native request maps whose predecessor and witness squares commute, conditional coarsening is governed by the separate weighted-fibre transport lemma. These facts provide the initial-law domain and ancestry interface needed for a later common-target proof; its actual paid and terminal-body comparisons remain separate obligations.
