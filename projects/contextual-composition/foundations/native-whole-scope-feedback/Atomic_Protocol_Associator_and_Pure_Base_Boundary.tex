\section{Atomic refinement is indexed by primitive occurrences}
A pointed content protocol may have a designated generating graph whose edges are marked silent or abstract paid. These marks carry no probability, context coefficient or chosen scheduler. For a nested activation configuration, retain its flattened tree of \emph{primitive} occurrence addresses and the entry objects and static labels at those addresses. An actor is old exactly when it is installed in this prescope configuration. Its age is not inferred from whether its enclosing composite block already exists. An entry may already expose ports; recursively installed entries at those ports are part of the old prescope configuration if installation occurred before this scope. All descendants first installed during the current scope are newborn, including descendants already exposed by their entry objects.

An admissible abstract atomic generator specifies a nonempty finite set of old primitive active actors, one selected abstract paid edge for each, one common boundary/world-arrow symbol, and passive responses for all other old actors. It also retains a finite typed request graph for births: original primitive owner, predecessor wires, existence/type/postworld data and all raw witness slots with absent sentinels. There are no laws or actual coefficients in this signature. New actors undergo only silent entry expansion in this scope; they have no selected paid edge. For nontrivial raw-dependent births this definition is applied to every typed full raw assignment separately, including zero assignments once a law is later supplied. The finite all-raw guard is an external admissibility requirement, not inferred from a positive-support observation.

Its image in the unrefined dependent protocol is the arrow carrying those parent/child paths and the appropriate entry-to-post paths. The atomic signature is \emph{retained}: different simultaneous scopes or histories that happen to have the same unrefined arrow are not identified. The refined protocol is the free path category of these atomic generators (together with explicitly declared silent/input generators and no relations unless separately specified and matched under the comparison). In particular two consecutive atomic scopes are not identified with one atomic scope having the same net occurrence injection.

\begin{theorem}[Atomic associator restriction]
The content reassociation
\[
                 (J[G])[H]\cong J[G[H]]
\]
restricts to an isomorphism of these annotated atomic generator graphs, provided both presentations retain the same primitive prescope addresses, boundary symbols and request-owner/predecessor data. Consequently it induces an isomorphism of their free path categories. It descends to categories with silent/input relation quotients only when the relation congruences are transported to each other by this reparse; unrelated independently imposed relations are not covered. The four-layer pentagon and unit comparisons preserve these annotations and the entire graded content maps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reviewed associator is a bijection of the same outer object, child objects and grandchild objects, and of every corresponding path, not merely an equality of final live lists. It therefore identifies the set of installed primitive actors before the scope and hence the old/new partition. Each selected paid edge remains the same edge at the same old primitive address; no newborn edge is inserted. A newly born outer child with live ports at its entry contributes the grandchild entry paths on both sides, because the composite entry includes precisely those grandchild entries. Those grandchildren remain newborn on both sides and cannot be selected active in this scope. Conversely every allowed annotation on either bracketing has the same inverse image by the same address reparse.

Primitive owners and predecessor wires are labels on these retained nodes and edges; they are neither recomputed from composite blocks nor enlarged with sibling coordinates. Thus the same boundary and the same request graph are preserved, including conditional absence. This proves the graph bijection, which extends freely to paths. For four layers both routes keep exactly the same primitive addresses, selected edges and entry paths, so they agree on generators and all paths. The entire terminal-grade associator and its actual content coefficient maps are the already proved full substitution associator, including unused shapes. Unary content units are identity decorations and contribute no primitive actor at all to the retained prescope signature (not even an extra inert actor). They therefore insert no paid edge or new primitive owner, so the same calculation gives their annotated unit comparisons.\end{proof}

\paragraph{Native consumption.}
When each annotated scope is supplied its independently admitted whole law and actual owner-expanded coefficient, the graph reparse induces the identity on its primitive raw witnesses, with occurrence renaming only. Its predecessor tests and any extra complete-block constraints agree. Its owner coefficient legs are the projections to the same entire owner factors followed by tensor rebracketing. The scope product universal property therefore supplies the same actual native comparison used in activation v7. The poststate and port squares hold at every raw atom by the same primitive entry maps. This is conditional on these actual supplied fields; the abstract graph alone chooses no law and provides no coefficient map. Temporal composition uses the whole chronological tensor of these scopes, not a flattening into one scope.

\begin{proposition}[Why macroblock age is insufficient]
If newborn/old status is assigned only to grouped blocks, the unrestricted content associator does not by itself certify the intended atomic no-newborn-paid condition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The unrefined dependent protocol permits an arrow in which a newly born child follows an entry-to-post path containing a paid edge. Such an arrow is legitimate as a content path but forbidden as one activation scope. An enclosing old block can contain this new child, so marking that entire block old does not detect the violation. Conversely marking an entire block new can hide which of its subactors were already installed under a different decomposition. Retaining the primitive prescope actor set resolves the former violation and prevents the latter inconsistent grouping convention. The theorem uses precisely that retained set, not an inferred block age.\end{proof}

\section{Pure maps and what remains unclosed}
A candidate pure base has small marked occurrence protocols as objects. A pure map consists of a functor, a natural order-preserving bijection of each object's occurrence positions, exact preservation of the completed marking, and the specified preservation of any retained atomic signature. Label maps may be noninjective: equality of labels never identifies positions. Send a set $S$ to the discrete completed protocol $jS$ with one unary occurrence at each object $s$. Every function $f:S\to T$, including noninjective functions, induces the corresponding pure functor $jf$, with the identity unary-position map. Thus this candidate does not delete the noninjective maps of the original Set base. Contraction or duplication of \emph{occurrences} is a different operation and is not silently supplied by a noninjective label function.

This identifies a plausible pure category but does not close the existing static-input context module. A static-input process initialized at $a\in A$ does not specify how its state, complete menu, raw/poststate fields and actual coefficients respond to a later arrow of an arbitrary input protocol $J$. Merely replacing $A$ by the set of objects of $J$ loses its arrows. Conversely declaring those arrows to be paid events would alter chronological coefficients and admission. A module on this base must independently supply moving-input actions or staged response spans for every input object, their pure reindexings, and composition compatible with the retained atomic signatures. Seed naturality/interchange cannot be presumed; the owner-aware ordered post-scope installation is weaker than an input-before-paid transport square.

The independent content module has pointed substitution, native associators and units, but these facts do not yet define both $M(J,K)$ and $N(J,K)$ as closed endomodules over this same pure category. In particular original static $N(A,B)$ is exactly recovered only at the declared event-free discrete intersection, while general progressive bodies have a richer input protocol. There is a specific variance obstruction to treating the already proved substitution as such a multiplication: $F:J\to\mathcal D_C(B)$ and $G_b:K_b\to\mathcal D_D(E)$ produce a body on $J[K]$, not on the same source $J$. Thus that theorem alone has the wrong domain to define $N(J,B)\times N(B,E)\to N(J,E)$. Adding a set-indexed entry map into $J$ restores a static initial parameter but does not provide a law choosing later paths of the latent driver $J$. The projection $J[K]\to J$ does admit the declared deterministic entry-padding transport on configurations, suggesting a dependent-span interface; random entries require retained request witnesses instead of a uniquely selected padding. Neither suggestion identifies dependent-domain composition with fixed-base multiplication. A candidate enlargement may construct graded progressive polynomial actions on protocols, but their marking, live occurrence functors, pure actions and actual mixed faces must be checked before asserting relative multiplication. The atomic associator theorem supplies one concrete compatibility needed by that construction; it is neither a normal-form equivalence nor a substitute for those missing operations.
