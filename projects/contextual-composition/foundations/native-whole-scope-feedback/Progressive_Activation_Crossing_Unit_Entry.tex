\section{Independent input and component interfaces}
Throughout the constructive theorem, context slice products are over the augmentation unit; passive context factors are precisely that neutral slice object. The independent full writer boundary is still retained. General nonunit context boundaries require their own supplied legs and are not silently included.

A finite-port protocol $J$ is a small category with a functor $P_J$ to finite ordered sets and order-preserving injections, and a completed-object marking preserved by arrows. A content decoration $F$ supplies, at each object $j$, labels in $B$ for its positions, preserved by those injections, and, at each completed object, a shape $c_j$ in the entire weighted collection $C$ with arity $|P_J(j)|$. On arrows between completed objects the ordered list and shape are unchanged. Equivalently $F:J\to\mathcal D_C(B)$ has the specified exact marking and underlying occurrence functor. The independent coefficient object at every unused shape of $C$ remains present. There is no paid law, command, world update or context coefficient in $J$ or $F$.

For every $b\in B$ let $M_b$ be a static-input unary paid context returning an $E$ value. It has opaque states, complete finite paid rows, actual augmented context coefficients, whole writer data and shared-world passive response. A state has either no revealed output or one revealed $E$ value; revelation is persistent. A completed state has exactly one revealed value. Its raw progressive observation is therefore a functor to the delayed unary interface $jE$. States can reveal before completing. Completed states are paid-inert.

The entry of $M_b$ at world $w$ is a specified state $s_b(w)$ with deterministic singleton initial raw row and unit initial coefficient. No event is paid for entry. Require, explicitly,
\[
 \operatorname{rebase}_a(s_b(w))=s_b(w')                 \tag{1}
\]
for every admitted world arrow $a:w\to w'$, with its actual port/body equality. This is a restriction, not a consequence of world-inert initialization. Terminal-world components satisfy it automatically. Nontrivial-world examples with world-independent local modes and seeds do too. Existing paid rows may have arbitrary finite correlated raw laws and nonunit augmented coefficients. Native component comparisons preserve the entry, have common raw/coefficient maps and the ordinary whole-row/port/full-writer equations; they are not merely marginal-preserving functions.

\section{The reactive actor bundle is constructed, not inferred from labels}
For a fixed world $w$ and protocol object $j$, define the live bundle state set
\[
 S_R(w,j)=\prod_{p\in P_J(j)}S_{M_{b_p}}(w).            \tag{2}
\]
A protocol arrow $u:j\to j'$ transports each old coordinate along its injection and inserts $s_{b_{p'}}(w)$ at every new position. Call this response $\rho_u$. It is deterministic, creates no paid event, and has unit response coefficient. Entry at $(w,j)$ is the tuple of all these deterministic seeds. Functoriality $\rho_v\rho_u=\rho_{vu}$ follows by separating positions already present, born at $u$, and born at $v$; every position is assigned its one corresponding seed. Repeated equal labels remain different coordinates. By (1), these responses commute with shared-world passive rebase. Without (1) this equation can fail and the construction is not claimed in this branch.

A bundle paid command chooses a nonempty subset of currently existing coordinates and an original command for each. It retains an admitted whole matching joint of their complete raw rows, the actual whole writer diagram, and the actual context product of the entire active row factors. Every unselected coordinate responds passively to that same boundary/world update. Births caused by a later protocol response do not act in this scope. All raw atoms and unused writer schemas, when globally supplied, remain. Completion of a reactive state means that $j$ is completed and every component is completed; a bundle at an unfinished $j$ can await further input even when it has no active component.

For each $u$, map an existing bundle command by transporting its active positions and declaring every newly inserted coordinate passive. Its complete raw joint and coefficient factors are unchanged, up to the stated ordered/product coherences; passive actors add no active factor. The post-square holds on old positions by identity transport and on new positions by (1). Thus $\rho_u$ has an actual all-row command transport, not just a state function. It can create live output positions, so it is a horizontal input response with port injections, not an ordinary vertical native arrow whose ports must be bijective. This distinction is part of the interface.

The observation of (2) is the ordered list of values revealed by the existing copies. Its old-position inclusions are induced by persistent component outputs and the protocol injection. If the bundle is completed, every parent position has one completed output and the whole terminal shape is $c_j$. The full terminal grade is exactly $C$, not a family of grades generated only by reached $j$. No context-to-content coefficient arrow is postulated.

\section{Separate content factor and the activation crossing}
Fix one connected component of the underlying world category. Let $H_b$ be the category generated by all states and named paid-command/raw edges of $M_b$ in that component, with passive rebase arrows quotiented to identities. This is a protocol-only quotient: the reactive context retains its original world-indexed states, named raw rows, laws and actual coefficients without quotienting them. Paid raw-edge generators remain named; only the relations generated by the specified passive-identification equations are imposed. Persistent ordered ports and the completed unary body are passive-invariant, so their observation factors through this quotient. Completion must be invariant in both directions under passive rebase, as in the pinned branch, so the marking descends as well. Equation (1) identifies the entry states at worlds joined by an arrow, hence along every zigzag in the connected component. They therefore define one actual pointed object of $H_b$. The result is an independently typed pointed protocol with unary content observation $H_b\to\mathcal D_I(E)$. Different world components are treated separately. No paid law is chosen by the protocol, and no equality of distinct actual paid coefficients is inferred from an equality of their observed protocol arrows.

The dependent protocol $K=J[H]$ of the pinned content-substitution construction has objects
\[
 (j,(h_p)_{p\in P_J(j)}).
\]
An arrow has a parent arrow $u$, paths for the retained children, and entry-to-final paths for newly born children. The actor bundle has an observation into $K$: a paid bundle command uses parent identity and the chosen raw edge at each active child; a pure input response uses $u$, identities on old children and the entry identity on new ones. This observation preserves all addressed positions and completion. The independent content substitution gives
\[
 K\longrightarrow\mathcal D_{C\circ I}(E)
              \cong\mathcal D_C(E),                   \tag{3}
\]
including the entire $C\circ I$ grade and its unit comparison, not merely actual terminal labels.

Equations (2)--(3) define the restricted crossing: static content over a moving protocol, followed by unary contexts at its revealed ports, becomes a reactive context over that protocol followed by an independent full content observation. The context is not the protocol itself: it adds component states, initial entries, complete paid menus/joints, actual coefficients and world responses; the protocol supplies only occurrences and paths. Conversely the content factor does not choose any paid joint or encode coefficients of the context process.

Native functoriality has two explicit classes. A protocol comparison preserving occurrence positions by supplied order bijections and its decoration induces the corresponding reindexing of (2); full terminal grade maps act on all completed and unused shapes. A component comparison $M_b\to M'_b$ acts on every coordinate, every raw joint by its complete pushforward, and every product coefficient by the actual cone map. Entry preservation makes these maps commute with $\rho_u$ at new positions. Old-port squares give naturality at paid edges. Full writer interpretation additionally needs the all-unused diagram functor from the structured-family interface. Port contraction/duplication, arbitrary input label changes and dynamically chosen schema maps are not included.

\section{Driving the reactive context by a paid upstream process}
Let $X$ be a paid process with a progressive protocol observation $o:e_AX\to J$. Passive rebases map to identity protocol arrows. The observation is defined on all raw paid outcomes and completed states, including zeros and unreachable states. States of the driven composite are
\[
 (x,t),\qquad t\in S_R(w,o(x)).                         \tag{4}
\]
At an initial raw witness of $X$, retain its initial coefficient and law and attach the deterministic unit entries of all currently live ports. Thus $X$ may have a general finite initial family here; only the newly activated downstream entries are restricted.

A paid command of (4) selects an upstream command or upstream passivity and a subset of existing downstream active commands, with at least one globally active participant. Choose the whole admitted matching joint of these complete rows. Let its common world arrow be $a:w\to w'$. First form the upstream post-state $x'$ and every old child active post/passive rebase at $w'$. The upstream raw transition supplies $u:o(x)\to o(x')$. Apply $\rho_u$ at $w'$ to the old child post tuple, inserting new entries there. Its context coefficient is the actual product of the original active row factors; response entries contribute only specified unit factors. The writer is the original whole active diagram. This is a completely specified one-scope operation, not sequential execution of upstream and downstream events.

Every source conditional choice is the displayed full joint. No independent resampling of existing child outcomes occurs, and no newborn child participates in its birth event. Shared-world rebase acts on the tuple; its response compatibility uses (1). Native maps act on complete raw tuples and actual product cones and commute with the selected-state and poststate equations. This verifies the actual upstream/reactive attachment interface in this restricted branch.

\begin{theorem}[Activation realizes open continuation at unit entries]
For an upstream system whose live port/body observation is $F o$, the driven construction (4), followed by (3), is the generated open substitution of $M_{b_p}$ at each live port, restricted to deterministic unit entries satisfying (1). The equality is at the complete native field level after the stated address/unit normalizations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both initial carriers are the upstream raw carrier with forced singleton child entries. Both state carriers are one upstream state and one child state at every actual persistent port. Both menus select the same old actors and original commands, admit precisely the same whole matching joints, and keep the same raw witnesses. Both update old actors at the common boundary and then install precisely the fresh child entries at the actual post-world. Their initial and paid context maps differ only by explicit unitors/product ordering. The full writer diagrams agree, and (3) records the exact old terminal shape with each unary child's value, on the same full $C$. Persistent early outputs and all raw zero states agree. Thus this is an identification with the actual operational assembly, not an inference from its final trace.\end{proof}

\section{Concrete multiplication test: two unary stages}
Let $M_b$ return $E$ and $L_e$ return $T$, both in the restricted entry class. Define $M;L$ directly by the same open activation rule at the sole possible revealed $E$ port: its states are an $M$ state with no $L$ before revelation, and the associated existing $L$ state thereafter. Initialization uses the deterministic $M$ entry and, if it already reveals, the deterministic $L$ entry. The initial coefficient stays unit. Completion requires both components complete. It reveals the $L$ output when available. Paid commands are all whole matching joints of existing active components; a fresh $L$ cannot act immediately. Rebase and seed equation (1) are inherited because both seed equations hold. Thus this composite remains a static-input unary context of the same restricted class.

There are now two explicitly defined reactive constructions over $J$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item activate one composite $M;L$ at each live $B$ position;
\item activate the $M$ copies, expose their growing revealed $E$ list, and activate an $L$ copy at each of those revelations, with all existing $M/L$ actors sharing one paid scope.
\end{enumerate}
The second route is this specified joint operational composite, not serial scheduling of an entire $M$ bundle before an $L$ bundle.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-row context multiplication comparison]
These two reactive constructions are naturally isomorphic on complete states, raw initial rows, complete paid menus, whole laws, actual coefficients, full terminal grade and the independent content factor (3). The comparison commutes with input response and all-raw successor maps. The claim is limited to the deterministic unit-entry doctrine.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At each parent position both routes retain the same $M$ state and exactly the same $L$ state when the $M$ value has been revealed. The raw state reparse is bijective even for unfinished and unreachable configurations. A paid command in either route is equivalently the choice of active primitive $M/L$ rows and a whole matching law with those marginals. In the grouped route, the law of each active composite row is its whole marginal; the outer joint retains the entire law. Restricting a flat joint to these composite blocks and retaining the original outer joint is inverse to flattening. All admitted block joints occur, so no fixed independent law is being imposed. Empty blocks are the specified passive responses. Deterministic new entries introduce neither a choice of conditional law nor a new nonunit coefficient factor.

Actual contexts on both routes are iterated finite products of the same entire active primitive factors. Their actual slice-limit associators are invertible. Initial coefficients are unit, and full terminal grades are $C$ with unary units, whose all-shape comparisons are the source unit maps. Raw posts agree because both routes apply each old primitive outcome once, then create new $L$ entries from new $M$ revelations at the common post-world. Entry before/after passive movement agrees by (1). Input responses pad the same parent positions; if an $M$ entry already reveals, both routes also supply its $L$ entry. Therefore the comparison commutes with response arrows as well as paid commands.

Naturality uses the same complete primitive raw maps, actual product-cone functoriality and the independent all-shape content maps. No actual context arrow is identified merely because its scalar image agrees.\end{proof}

For three unary stages, both association routes retain the same addressed $M/L/R$ activation chains, and a newly born downstream stage is determined recursively by any output already revealed by its deterministic entry. The chain has fixed finite length. Both routes expose the same old active primitive rows, so their flattened full laws and raw maps agree; actual context arrows are the unique same product-cone arrows. For four unary stages $M,L,R,S$, every route around the pentagon flattens the same addressed four-stage activation chains, preserves the same entire primitive paid law, and has the same actual product-cone arrow at every raw tuple. Products are actual limits over the augmentation unit, so equality of these entire-factor projections proves equality of the two native routes. This establishes the pentagon at four stages, rather than inferring it solely from a three-stage reparse. The unit context is the forced identity revelation with no paid command; deleting its forced state/raw/unit factors commutes with these same flattenings. This statement concerns these concrete unary activation composites; a full identification with all relative module/coend axioms remains to be written before naming a complete paid relative crossing theory.

\section{Discriminating probes and exact restrictions}
A binary content shape can activate two contexts which reveal at different paid times. The bundle exposes arities $0$, $1$, $2$ before its final completion, so the previous unary Set-factorization invariant does not hold. The intermediate protocol has retained the individual occurrence histories rather than replacing their growth by one revealed tuple value.

For interference, let two activated contexts have fair paid bits and matching boundary arrow. Choose the diagonal joint on the complete four-point raw carrier, retaining the two zero points. The crossing retains that joint as one command, with the actual product of its two paid coefficient factors. Independent product law is not substituted. With world-independent seeds on the free-chain world, this scope updates the world once; serialization of its two active commands updates twice. Thus the candidate is not merely a terminal-world deterministic observer construction, although its entry restriction is substantial.

The original static content module and full grades are retained. The source event-free restriction for contexts, however, is only deterministic singleton unit-coefficient entries here; arbitrary original random/nonunit context rows are not recovered by this new theorem. Their reviewed family crossing lives in a larger entry doctrine. Allowing random entries makes response to a new port a family-valued law admission rather than the function $\rho_u$; allowing nonunit entry coefficients inserts owner tensors before scope products and destroys the product-only associativity argument. Omitting (1) makes command transport across an input response fail at new passive actors. These are the first exact interfaces that must be generalized, with primitive predecessor indices and the directed owner normalization, before claiming recovery of the full family/paid theory.

A general progressive content-substitution multiplication comparison additionally has dependent input protocol $J[K]$, including child entries that already reveal ports. The separately developed content-module theorem addresses its full-grade/entry reassociation. This note does not infer the entire corresponding paid crossing diagram solely from (3); it requires a common moving-input signature and the same explicit primitive actor/joint comparison as above. Likewise arbitrary opaque paid systems need not factor into the unary activation class. These are genuine residuals, not hidden assumptions in the proved two-stage test.

\section{Exact extension tests at the response and coefficient squares}
The following tests explain which proved equations must change beyond the entry doctrine; they are not failed claims about the deterministic theorem.

First work at terminal world with unit coefficients. Suppose a protocol arrow $u$ adds one child with fair two-point initializer and $v$ adds a second such child. A response interpreted as successive fresh stochastic kernels, each required fair conditional on its entire current input state, gives the independent product on the two witnesses. In contrast the one-step response family for $vu$ with one fixed predecessor and two fair child marginals includes the diagonal law. Thus this proposed kernel interpretation cannot satisfy an invertible response-composition comparison to that all-coupling family. Its natural repair is directed admission from the stronger sequential test into the weaker common-predecessor test, with every zero raw pair retained. This does not yet construct a lax response module or its coherence.

Second, let an existing child have a fair paid outcome $Y$, and let an upstream deterministic outcome issue a fixed birth arrow $u$ in the same scope. A new child's primitive initializer has fair witness $Z$. Under owner/predecessor admission conditional only on the upstream birth outcome, the complete diagonal joint $Z=Y$ is legal: the upstream predecessor is constant, both marginals are fair, and all four raw pairs remain. An interpreter that first samples the complete old paid tuple and then uses a fresh fair kernel for $Z$ at each realized $Y$ produces only the product and excludes this legal joint. Allowing the pointwise kernel $\delta_Y$ does not solve that interpreter's own pointwise-fair admission test. The honest owner-family test is instead imposed on the integrated whole law at its declared predecessor, not at the larger observed paid tuple. Consequently a random-entry extension cannot be obtained merely by replacing each $\rho_u$ function by an independently fresh admissible kernel after all paid data are known.

Third, allow a nonunit entry coefficient $L$ owned by the upstream paid actor $X$, while an old child $Y$ also acts. A naive post-scope cost attachment gives
\[
 (K_X\mathbin{\times}_{\mathbf1}K_Y)\otimes L,
\]
whereas the owner-atomic assembly has
\[
 (K_X\otimes L)\mathbin{\times}_{\mathbf1}K_Y.
\]
In augmented vector spaces with all three factors two-dimensional and surjective augmentations, these objects have dimensions $6$ and $5$, respectively. There is a canonical directed arrow with legs $\pi_X\otimes1_L$ and $\pi_Y\otimes\epsilon_L$, with the usual unit coherences. Its equal augmentations give the actual target slice arrow. It is not an isomorphism. This is exactly the whole-owner normalization required by the earlier theory; the product-only proof of the present multiplication isomorphism cannot be reused unchanged.

Finally, without (1), a new copy inserted before an old paid command is passively rebased, while insertion after that command gives the new-world seed. A two-world arrow with mode-preserving rebase and distinct seeds at the two worlds makes these post-states unequal. Thus the response-command square itself fails, even with deterministic unit entries. The root-feedback theorem did not require (1), because it used selected-state squares at actual births instead of transporting seeds backwards through a paid event. The stronger moving-input command transport here deliberately requires the extra equation.

The three entry/cost tests and the seed test identify separate obligations: directed conditional admission, whole-law rather than sequential fresh-kernel filling, actual owner coefficient normalization, and response/paid state compatibility. The reviewed owner/request family supplies tools for the first three, but does not automatically furnish a new moving-interface module satisfying all four. This is the next construction problem, not a completed consequence of the restricted crossing.
