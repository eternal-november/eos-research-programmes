\paragraph{Audit and change of interface.}
The previous support-profile extension supplied total updates for a fixed incidence profile, but arbitrary cross-episode joining only supplied a compatibility subobject. A partial-domain span does not solve a total-update problem. The present finite-dimensional rational-linear construction represents ALL bilinear total repairs of an explicit primitive compatibility equation. It derives a normalized-feasibility criterion, retains the entire chronological coefficient in a graph object, and constructs sequential comparison maps. The resource augmentation is a native coefficient map, NOT a probability or a random seed. No scalar law, causality, private-reader permission or primitive admission is inferred from it. An introduced correction witness changes an old strict equality into a witnessed equality; this distinction is never quotiented away.

\section{Primitive diagram equations and the immutable-history obstruction}
All vector spaces are finite dimensional over $\mathbb Q$. An old historical coefficient is $X$ with augmentation $\epsilon_X:X\to\mathbb Q$. A finite proposed diagram extension has old node readouts already determined from $x\in X$, new node/witness coordinates collected into a vector space $E$, and generating edge residuals collected into $V$. Writing all edge equations gives actual linear maps
\[
 C:X\to V,\qquad B:E\to V,\qquad Cx+Be=0.\tag{1}
\]
These maps are computed from the primitive edge maps and the retained old projections, not supplied as a solved global update square. Products of edge target spaces encode the finitely many simultaneous equations. Relations between paths follow once all generator equations hold. The new node coordinates are independent LINEAR witness variables in their declared coefficient spaces. A requirement that a joint tensor be decomposable into selected marginal vectors is nonlinear and is not smuggled into this presentation. If the new node augmentations are prescribed, include their scalar equations among (1). Thus a solution satisfies the intended augmented diagram, not merely its underlying unaugmented equations. The spaces $E,V$ themselves need no augmentation. The joint witness object
\[
 H=\ker[C\ B]\subseteq X\oplus E
\]
has augmentation $\epsilon_H(x,e)=\epsilon_X(x)$ and old projection $r:H\to X$.

With no new witness coordinates, (1) reduces to $Cx=0$. Let $R$ be any augmented increment and suppose a total update $u:X\otimes R\to H$ has $ru=1_X\otimes\epsilon_R$. Then $C(1_X\otimes\epsilon_R)=0$. If $\epsilon_R\ne0$, the latter projection is surjective, hence $C=0$. Consequently no enlarged target, hidden bookkeeping or later observation can impose a nontrivial new strict equation on ALL old coefficients while preserving their old projection and a normalized increment. The failed diagonal join of the preceding manuscript is an instance. Introducing witnesses in (1) can make a different equation soluble; it cannot retrospectively prove the witness-free equality.

\section{Structural representation in a closed context category}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a symmetric monoidal closed category with the pullbacks used below. Write $[X,P]$ for its internal hom. Let $X,P$ have augmentations, and let $r:P\to X$ satisfy $\epsilon_P=\epsilon_Xr$. This prefix projection is supplied by the proposed finite diagram and its retained old nodes, before any repair is selected. Form the pullback
\[
 \mathcal R_r=I\times_{[X,X]}[X,P],\tag{2s}
\]
where $I\to[X,X]$ names $1_X$ and $[X,P]\to[X,X]$ is $[X,r]$. Give $\mathcal R_r$ its projection $\epsilon:\mathcal R_r\to I$. Evaluation defines $v:X\otimes\mathcal R_r\to P$ and satisfies $rv=1_X\otimes\epsilon$.

\begin{theorem}[Closed-category representing resource]
For every augmented object $R$, augmented maps $R\to\mathcal R_r$ correspond naturally and uniquely to arrows $w:X\otimes R\to P$ with $rw=1_X\otimes\epsilon_R$. Such $w$ automatically preserve augmentation. Normalized points $s:I\to\mathcal R_r$ with $\epsilon s=1_I$ correspond exactly to sections of $r$. No tensor-preservation of the defining pullback is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Transpose $w$ by the tensor--internal-hom adjunction. The equation on $rw$ becomes the equality between its composite into $[X,X]$ and the name of $1_X$ composed with $\epsilon_R$. This is precisely the pullback cone defining a unique arrow $R\to\mathcal R_r$ whose augmentation is $\epsilon_R$. Conversely untranspose the second pullback leg. Both operations are inverse and natural in augmented $R$. The augmentation equation follows from $\epsilon_Pw=\epsilon_Xrw$. Set $R=I$ to obtain the section assertion. The construction only uses the adjunction and the pullback universal property.
\end{proof}

This theorem holds in the ambient closed category; it does not assume that its augmented slice is itself closed. It represents the total-update interface, not the existence of a normalized point. In a general category a nonzero or epimorphic augmentation is not a substitute for a specified normalized point. The following vector-space instance derives an effective criterion and formula from the primitive linear diagram equations.

\section{A linear representing resource, without choosing a section}
Define the finite vector space and its augmentation
\[
 L_{C,B}=\{(t,T)\in\mathbb Q\oplus\Hom(X,E):BT=-tC\},
 \qquad \epsilon_L(t,T)=t.\tag{2}
\]
The constraint is linear in $(t,T)$. For $P=H$ and $r:H\to X$ this is exactly (2s): an operator $S:X\to H$ with $rS=t1_X$ has the unique form $Sx=(tx,Tx)$, and landing in $H$ imposes $BT=-tC$. Define evaluation on pure tensors by
\[
 u:X\otimes L_{C,B}\to H,
 \qquad u(x\otimes(t,T))=(tx,Tx).\tag{3}
\]
The constraint in (2) puts (3) in $H$. This is bilinear, hence induces the displayed linear map. It is augmented and obeys
\[
 ru=1_X\otimes\epsilon_L.\tag{4}
\]
No basis, augmentation section of $X$, or chosen solution of (1) is needed for this construction.

\begin{theorem}[Universal bilinear total repair resource]
For every augmented vector space $R$, there is a natural bijection
\[
 \Hom_{\rm aug}(R,L_{C,B})\ \cong\
 \{v:X\otimes R\to H\text{ linear}:rv=1_X\otimes\epsilon_R\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The correspondence sends $\phi$ to $u(1_X\otimes\phi)$. In particular $(L_{C,B},u)$ is terminal among resources with such updates, with resource maps required to preserve the actual update. The right-hand maps are automatically augmented.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first coordinate of $v(x\otimes a)$ is forced to be $\epsilon_R(a)x$. Transpose its second coordinate to the linear map $a\mapsto T_a\in\Hom(X,E)$. Since $v$ lands in $H$, $BT_a=-\epsilon_R(a)C$. Thus $\phi(a)=(\epsilon_R(a),T_a)$ is an augmented map into (2). It is the only possible map giving $v$. Conversely each such $\phi$ gives the required update by (3)--(4). The two operations are inverse and commute with precomposition by augmented resource maps. Applying $\epsilon_X$ to (4) proves augmentation preservation.
\end{proof}

This universal property represents a declared primitive equation; it does not turn every equation into a nondegenerate repair. It also does not manufacture the primitive resource $R$. An actual step must provide its own native coefficient and its map to $L_{C,B}$, or explicitly adopt $L_{C,B}$ as a new primitive coefficient. Owner keys and any cost or paid-event classification of that step remain separately declared operational fields.

\begin{theorem}[Exact normalized-feasibility and choice classification]
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L_{C,B}$ has a vector of augmentation one;
\item $\epsilon_L\ne0$;
\item $\im C\subseteq\im B$;
\item $\operatorname{rank}[B\ C]=\operatorname{rank}B$;
\item $r:H\to X$ has a linear section.
\end{enumerate}
When these hold, the normalized operators are precisely the solutions $T_0+K$ with $BT_0=-C$ and $K\in\Hom(X,\ker B)$. If $n=\dim X$ and $k=\dim\ker B$, then $\dim L_{C,B}=1+nk$ in the feasible case and $nk$ otherwise. In the infeasible case the universal resource still exists, but every one of its vectors has augmentation zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero augmentation can be rescaled to one over $\mathbb Q$. An operator $T$ with $BT=-C$ exists exactly when every column of $C$ is in the image of $B$, yielding the image and rank criteria. Such an operator supplies the section $x\mapsto(x,Tx)$ of $r$, and every section has this form. The difference of two operators takes values in $\ker B$. If a normalized operator exists, $(t,K)\mapsto(t,tT_0+K)$ identifies (2) with $\mathbb Q\oplus\Hom(X,\ker B)$; otherwise $t$ must be zero and (2) is that Hom space alone.
\end{proof}

A nonzero resource augmentation is a declared nondegeneracy condition for preservation of old data under a unit-augmentation specialization. It is not positivity, probability mass or an implementation sampler. For any normalized $\ell$, the specialization $u(-\otimes\ell):X\to H$ is a section of $r$ and therefore injective. Choosing such an $\ell$ is actual extra data, whereas the full representing resource (2) is canonical.

\paragraph{No natural normalized choice in general.}
For $r:X\oplus K\to X$ with nonzero $X,K$, every linear map $b:X\to K$ gives the automorphism $(x,k)\mapsto(x,k+bx)$ over $X$. It carries a section $(x,Sx)$ to $(x,(S+b)x)$. No section is fixed by all these automorphisms. Thus even split projections do not admit a normalized choice natural under all primitive witness symmetries. The representing resource retains these choices and their actual maps rather than pretending to select one canonically.

\section{The graph retains the kernel that joint evaluation forgets}
Evaluation (3) need not be injective. Replacing the entire chronological history by $H$ can therefore erase material belonging to the increment, even though (4) preserves the old projection. Retain instead
\[
 G_u=\{(z,h)\in(X\otimes L)\oplus H:h=u(z)\},\qquad
 c:z\mapsto(z,u(z)).\tag{6}
\]
Give $G_u$ augmentation through $z$. Projection $p:G_u\to X\otimes L$ is inverse to $c$. The joint observer is $j(z,h)=h$, and the reverse prefix restriction is
\[
 \rho=(1_X\otimes\epsilon_L)p:G_u\to X,
 \qquad \rho c=1_X\otimes\epsilon_L,
 \qquad jc=u.\tag{7}
\]
Thus $c$ is an actual TOTAL augmented insertion and preserves the entire input tensor, including $\ker u$. Joint evaluation is separately typed and may remain noninjective. The graph can equivalently be stored as the original input tensor equipped with its explicit observer $u$; it is not an assertion that the old tensor already satisfied the new equations without a witness.

For an actual resource map $\phi:R\to L$ use the graph of $u(1\otimes\phi)$, retaining $X\otimes R$, not replacing it by its image in $X\otimes L$. Therefore kernels of a supplied resource comparison are not silently erased from the source history. A full pullback $(X\otimes R)\times_X H$ instead allows all alternative witnesses over each old projection. The selected graph is its actual chosen total section; using the full pullback without the section would not specify an update.

This is an explicit enrichment of the coefficient observation interface: full tensor history PLUS a native joint observer. It is not an identification of the old genealogical joint coefficient with a chronological tensor. A previously supplied noninvertible joint comparison remains noninvertible at its observer. Recovering an old completed coefficient means applying its actual observer, not claiming the richer historical object has the same dimension or all the same equations. Only old maps that lift through the retained history/observer squares enter the corresponding comparison class; unrestricted old-map fullness or equation reflection is not established here.

\paragraph{Exact nontrivial witness.}
Take $X=\mathbb Q^2$ with $\epsilon_X(x_0,x_1)=x_0$, $E=\mathbb Q^2$, $V=\mathbb Q$, $C(x)=x_1$, and $B(e)=e_0$. Then $H=\{(x_0,x_1,-x_1,e_1)\}$ has dimension three. Resource (2) is parametrized by $(t,a,b)$ with
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}0&-t\\a&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u(x\otimes(t,a,b))=(tx_0,tx_1,ax_0+bx_1)
\]
in the coordinates $(x_0,x_1,e_1)$ of $H$. Its source has dimension six and its rank is three. For instance $e_0\otimes(0,1,0)$ and $e_1\otimes(0,0,1)$ have the same joint observer, while their difference is nonzero history. Graph (6) has dimension six and retains this difference; the reverse prefix restriction has rank two. This is a total history-preserving correction, not the preceding dimension-three constrained-domain repair.

\section{Sequential repairs and their actual composition map}
Let the first joint be $H_1=\ker[C_1\ B_1]\subseteq X\oplus E_1$ and the second joint be
\[
 H_2=\{(h_1,e_2): C_2h_1+B_2e_2=0\}\subseteq H_1\oplus E_2.
\]
For a block-matrix presentation choose a linear extension $\widetilde C_2:X\oplus E_1\to V_2$ of $C_2$; this exists in finite-dimensional vector spaces. Its values off $H_1$ have no semantic role. The simultaneous two-stage constraint on $(x,e_1,e_2)$ is
\[
 C_{12}x+B_{12}(e_1,e_2)=0,
 \quad C_{12}=\binom{C_1}{\widetilde C_{2,X}},
 \quad B_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}B_1&0\\\widetilde C_{2,E_1}&B_2\end{pmatrix}.\tag{8}
\]
Its kernel is canonically $H_2$; changing the extension gives the same solution subspace and the same equation for operators landing in it. Write $L_i$ for the stage resources and $L_{12}$ for (8).

\begin{theorem}[Directed compositional repair]
There is a canonical augmented resource map
\[
 \mu:L_1\otimes L_2\to L_{12}
\]
given on pure resources $(t_1,T_1),(t_2,T_2)$ by
\[
 t=t_1t_2,\qquad
 T(x)=\big(t_2T_1x,\ T_2(t_1x,T_1x)\big).\tag{9}
\]
Evaluation after $\mu$ equals ordered successive evaluation. These maps are unital and associative for any finite ordered chain, after the actual canonical flattening of nested witness tuples. They are not asserted invertible or surjective.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first output in (9) satisfies $C_1(tx)+B_1(t_2T_1x)=t_2(t_1C_1+B_1T_1)x=0$. Put $h_1=(t_1x,T_1x)\in H_1$. The second-stage equation is $C_2(t_2h_1)+B_2T_2h_1=0$ by the defining equation for $L_2$. Formula (9) is bilinear in the two resource arguments, since $T_2$ is linear and composition is bilinear, so defines a map on their tensor. Its augmentation is $t_1t_2$. Its evaluated witness is exactly the successive evaluation witness. For three or more stages both bracketings give the same scaled old coordinate and the same ordered tuple of witness coordinates by ordinary composition of the stage evaluation maps. The uniqueness in (5), with resource the full ordered tensor, forces equality of their resource maps. The zero-witness, zero-equation stage has resource $\mathbb Q$ with its usual augmentation and gives the unit.
\end{proof}

For HISTORICAL observations retain the full tensor $X\otimes L_1\otimes\cdots\otimes L_n$, or its iterated graph representation. The next stage's joint constraint may inspect the preceding joint through its declared observer; earlier graph kernels stay in history. The reverse restriction deletes only the latest resource by its augmentation. Finite bracketing of the graph representations gives actual isomorphisms preserving full histories, restrictions and the ordered joint observer. By contrast replacing $L_1\otimes L_2$ by $L_{12}$ uses $\mu$, which may have a kernel and is only a directed comparison. It must not be called history-preserving equality. Constraints that read additional retained historical variables must explicitly enlarge the next base beyond $H_1$; (9) does not authorize those reads invisibly.

\paragraph{Why a globally feasible repair need not stage independently.}
Take $X=\mathbb Q$, $E_1=\mathbb Q$, $C_1=B_1=0$, so $H_1=\mathbb Q^2$. Let $E_2=0$ and let $C_2(x,e_1)=e_1$. The simultaneous constraint merely requires $e_1=0$, so its global resource has a normalized operator. But the second stage demands $t_2C_2=0$ and therefore has only augmentation-zero resources on ALL $H_1$. No independent normalized second-stage resource exists. Choosing the first actual resource to force $e_1=0$ and restricting the second base to that actual interface repairs the problem. This is a different compositional interface, not an implication of global feasibility. In particular the independent tensor map (9) is not generally surjective. This is a native coefficient obstruction, not a probabilistic assertion of independence.

\begin{proposition}[Surjectivity when each whole stage is split]
If $r:H_1\to X$ and $s:H_2\to H_1$ both have sections, every normalized resource for the composite $rs$ is the image under $\mu$ of a pure tensor of normalized stage resources. Consequently $\mu$ is surjective as a linear map in this case. This does not provide a canonical factorization or an inverse.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Represent a normalized composite resource by its section $T:X\to H_2$ of $rs$. Put $U=sT$, a section of $r$. Choose any section $b:H_1\to H_2$ of $s$, and set
\[
 V=b+(T-bU)r:H_1\to H_2.
\]
Then $sV=1_{H_1}+(U-U)r=1_{H_1}$ and $VU=bU+(T-bU)rU=T$. Thus the normalized resources represented by $U,V$ evaluate to $T$ and map to it under $\mu$ by the representing uniqueness. Unit-augmentation vectors span every nonzero-augmentation vector space, so these images span the entire composite resource. The choice of $b$ and the resulting $V$ need not be natural or unique.
\end{proof}

\section{Comparison maps: what is and is not functorial}
For fixed $X$, maps $g:E\to E'$ and $h:V\to V'$ with $hC=C'$ and $hB=B'g$ induce $(t,T)\mapsto(t,gT)$, the joint map $(x,e)\mapsto(x,ge)$, and the graph comparison. Equations (3)--(7) commute by substitution. Such maps compose.

Changing the old base covariantly is different. Given $f:X\to X'$, $g:E\to E'$ and $h:V\to V'$ satisfying $hC=C'f$ and $hB=B'g$, there is no general map $\Hom(X,E)\to\Hom(X',E')$. A resource operator $T$ descends precisely when an actual $T'$ with $T'f=gT$ is supplied and satisfies $B'T'=-tC'$. If $f$ is surjective, descent is unique when $gT$ kills $\ker f$; the target constraint then follows by cancellation of the surjection. This defines a linear descent subspace of $L_{C,B}$, not necessarily the full resource. If $f$ is invertible it gives the entire canonical map $T'=gTf^{-1}$. If $f$ is not surjective, extensions and their constraint equations are additional data.

For surjective $f$, the maximal descent resource is explicitly
\[
 L^{\rm desc}=\{(t,T)\in L_{C,B}:gT|_{\ker f}=0\}.\tag{11}
\]
It has an actual augmented map to the target resource by the unique descended $T'$. The inclusion into $L_{C,B}$ need not contain any normalized resource, even when $L_{C,B}$ does. This yields a finite linear comparison-obstruction test in addition to the original repair-feasibility test. Actual descended maps compose. For two quotients, however, the sequential descent subspace can be STRICTLY smaller than the maximal subspace for the composite: a later witness map may kill an obstruction that prevented the first descent. Thus there is a canonical inclusion of sequentially descending resources into the composite's descent subspace, not an unproved equality of the maximal restrictions.

There is also a canonical contravariant restriction for $f:X'\to X$: with unchanged $E,V$, the map $(t,T)\mapsto(t,Tf)$ sends $L_{C,B}\to L_{Cf,B}$. The joint comparison $(x',e)\mapsto(fx',e)$ commutes with evaluation on the restricted input. This is restriction of the base, not an arbitrary covariant native quotient.

More generally an actual primitive repair uses resource $R$, augmentation and operator map $\tau:R\to\Hom(X,E)$ satisfying $B\tau(a)=-\epsilon_R(a)C$. A native comparison to $(X',E',R',\tau')$ supplies augmented $f,\psi$ and a witness map $g$ satisfying
\[
 g\tau(a)=\tau'(\psi a)f\quad\text{for every }a\in R.\tag{10}
\]
Together with the primitive constraint square, (10) derives the joint and graph update squares and the reverse prefix square. Identity and composition follow by substitution; no whole closed-machine square is assumed. This is the supported coefficient comparison class. It does not imply that every old coefficient arrow has a lift to the new resources.

\section{Deployment and residual obligations}
At each finite raw history a dynamic primitive may declare a finite constraint presentation (1), a native augmented resource $R$ with its operator map, and the graph insertion (6) specialized to $R$. Its full historical coefficient is the incoming coefficient tensored with that ACTUAL uniquely keyed resource. Its joint observer retains the witness and primitive node projections. Conditions (10) give local native maps; repeated finite steps give coherent prefix restrictions and actual chronological history. No infinite tensor is required. The old fixed-profile insertion can be retained as a different joint observer on the same tensor, with its established directed comparison; no equality with that joint observer is asserted.

For a complete nonblocking deployment, the raw registry must still specify actual world updates, primitive poststates, owner incidences, every zero alternative, birth/read/certificate fields, full content grade and body. None is reconstructed from a vector in $L$. Calibration, complete incoming laws, authorized service views, and executable or decentralized selection remain distinct constraints. A normalized operator can be a deterministic coefficient choice without being an authorized probabilistic controller choice. The accompanying law analysis must attach its own actual choices to every raw history.

The positive result supplies a universal total COEFFICIENT-repair interface and its exact nondegeneracy criterion, with local comparison equations and sequential composition. It does not prove total repair of the original witness-free diagonal equality, unrestricted covariant naturality, independent stage realizability, or completion of the governing feedback programme. A new mediator/correction witness is a declared change of diagram; old readouts remain separately available and any discrepancy is explicit. Historical graph kernels are retained, not identified with their joint observation.
