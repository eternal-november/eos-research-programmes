\title{Policy relations compose only laxly: the missing-middle obstruction}
\date{16 November 2026}

\paragraph{Audit.} Continuation of the conditional-family extension theorem. The result below does not prescribe target zero-history extensions. It shows precisely what composition remains available without them and gives a strict obstruction to promoting it to ordinary relational composition. This is a different test from terminal anticipation and from uniqueness of a single lift. All witnesses use unit coefficients, so actual coefficient/full-grade retention cannot remove the obstruction. The all-policy network corollary is an honest finite-horizon assembly theorem, but its operands are primitive libraries, not opaque isolated operand policies.

\section{Structural category and the policy relation}
Use the finite-horizon all-raw, full law-labelled control unfoldings of the accompanying manuscript. For this theorem fix structural skeletons, primitive maps and their full actual coefficient/grade arrows. A structural arrow $f:S\to T$ is defined on every action-and-outcome history, preserves filtration, selected actions, births, status and world/rebase/post-state squares, and includes raw maps $r_{f,h}$ on selected scope carriers. It preserves primitive component rows and maps every admitted scope law to its exact pushed admitted law, so it is defined on all off-policy law-labelled histories. It is not required to carry a source policy's selected law to the target policy's selected law. Such arrows compose coordinatewise. The ambient structural category here allows external changes of world and boundary doctrine, with actual full compatible world functors and boundary/grade maps. The fixed-world, fixed-boundary internal category is a subcategory; the boundary-collapse witness below belongs to the former, not automatically the latter. Admission subsets $A_h$ are retained object data; full-menu transport is an obligation on structural arrows, independently of selected-policy matching.

Let $P(S)=\prod_h A_h$ be the total-policy set. Associate the relation
\[
 R_f\subseteq P(S)\times P(T),\qquad
 (p,q)\in R_f\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (r_{f,h})_*p_h=q_{f(h)}
 \text{ for every raw decision history }h.             \tag{1}
\]
No existential variable for target extension is suppressed: membership fixes a whole $q$, and the earlier fibre theorem describes all such $q$ for a given $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Normal lax relational transport]
One has $R_{1_S}=1_{P(S)}$ and, for composable structural arrows,
\[
                        R_g\circ R_f\subseteq R_{gf}. \tag{2}
\]
These inclusions obey the lax associativity and unit equations in the locally ordered bicategory of sets and relations. Equality in (2) holds exactly when every conditionally native composite pair $(p,r)$ admits a middle total policy $q$ satisfying both conditional systems. Thus the obstruction is a checkable simultaneous extension problem, not failure of ordinary function composition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any witnessing middle policy $q$, pushforward composition gives
$(r_{g,fh}r_{f,h})_*p_h=(r_{g,fh})_*q_{fh}=r_{gf(h)}$.
This proves (2). Identity equations are pointwise. Both lax associativity routes are inclusions of $R_kR_gR_f$ into $R_{kgf}$, so they are equal as 2-cells in a poset. The equality criterion is exactly the existential definition of relation composition.\end{proof}

The middle-policy feasibility problem can be written explicitly, rather than hidden in the criterion. For each middle history $k$, require $q_k\in A^T_k$, all incoming equations $(r_{f,h})_*p_h=q_k$ with $f(h)=k$, and the outgoing equation $(r_{g,k})_*q_k=r_{g(k)}$. These are independent local extension sets once the structural maps are fixed. With finite histories and linear admission they are finite linear feasibility problems. The outgoing maps need not be injective, so the middle set can have more than one point even when the outer policies are fixed.

\section{A strict missing-middle witness}
Use a root observation followed by a simultaneous fair-bit scope. The source root has raw set $\{0\}$, the middle root has $\{0,1\}$ with probabilities $(1,0)$, and the target root again has $\{0\}$. Let $f$ include $0$ and let $g$ collapse both middle root atoms to $0$. Both are exact native maps of the root rows. Every primitive participant bit row is fair.

At the source history and middle history $0$, the two participants' boundary updates are constant, so the full matching carrier is all four bit pairs and every fair coupling is admitted. At middle history $1$, the participants' boundary update maps both send bit $b$ to the actual arrow $u_b$, with $u_0\ne u_1$. Consequently the full matching carrier there is exactly $\{00,11\}$, with forced fair diagonal law $D$. This is not deletion of zero atoms: off-diagonal pairs fail the primitive matching equation. At the target the boundary is constant and the carrier is again all four bit pairs with all fair couplings admitted. A change-of-world functor sends both actual arrows $u_0,u_1$ to the same target arrow. The middle-to-target raw map at history $1$ is the diagonal inclusion; all other joint raw maps are identity. The primitive post-states and maps can retain the participant bits and these arrow witnesses, with the target map applying that same world functor. Unit context/content coefficients suffice. This is the standard native situation where a boundary map identifies arrows and the target matching carrier grows.

All scope admission sets are the unrestricted marginal polytopes for their actual matching carriers. In particular $g$ transports its entire admitted menu, not merely a selected row: every pushed middle law is an admitted target fair coupling, and all full law-labelled histories map accordingly. Both $f$ and $g$ are valid structural arrows in the full-control-history category.

Let source policy $p$ and target policy $r$ select the antidiagonal fair coupling $A$. A middle policy $q$ agreeing with $p$ through $f$ must use $A$ at history $0$ and necessarily uses $D$ at history $1$. Therefore $(p,q)\in R_f$, but $(q,r)\notin R_g$, because the outgoing equation at raw history $1$ requires the pushed diagonal law to equal $A$. No alternative middle policy fixes this. Conversely $(p,r)\in R_{gf}$, since the composite tests only the source's root history $0$. Thus the pair $(p,r)$ lies in $R_{gf}$ but not $R_gR_f$.

The chosen finite horizon ends immediately after the joint scope. Earlier/root singleton primitive laws introduce no further off-policy decision coordinates, and the whole continuum of alternative chosen joint labels appears only at terminal histories. Thus these full control tables exist exactly as stated. Their integrated terminal laws can coincide, because the middle's discriminating root branch has zero mass.

\paragraph{Meaning and boundary.}
The witness proves strictness even under unrestricted all-coupling admission and full off-policy law-menu transport. The obstruction is a non-surjective raw comparison at a middle history absent from the source image. Exact pushforward native maps need not cover target raw tuples, and boundary collapse makes that failure operationally legitimate. It follows that a pseudofunctorial relational transport or a feedback fold based only on structural composability needs an additional lifting condition. The normal lax relation, or explicit middle-extension witnesses, remains valid. No independently chosen target extension has been fixed in obtaining this obstruction.

\section{A separate strict witness inside the fixed-boundary category}
Let the source root have two fair raw atoms $0,1$, and let both middle and target roots be singleton. The map $f$ collapses the source root; $g$ is identity on that singleton root. All update boundaries and worlds are fixed and constant. At the next scope, source and middle each have two fair-bit participants, all four matching tuples, and every fair coupling admitted. The second-step primitive maps for $f$ are identities. The target participants each have one raw atom with unit mass; the primitive maps for $g$ collapse each bit. These maps preserve their primitive laws, whole admissible menus and the full law-labelled histories. Unit coefficients suffice.

Choose source policy $p$ to use $D$ after root $0$ and $A$ after root $1$. Both histories map under $f$ to the same middle history, so no middle policy $q$ can satisfy both $q=D$ and $q=A$. Thus there is no $(p,q)\in R_f$. The target has its unique selected joint $r$, and the composite $gf$ pushes both $D$ and $A$ to that joint, so $(p,r)\in R_{gf}$. This proves strictness internally as well. Its mechanism is history-descent incompatibility, different from the external witness's untested middle raw branch and failed outgoing lift. Both are explicit instances of the companion fibre equations, not independent new impossibility principles.

There is nevertheless an ordinary category whose objects are pairs $(S,p)$ and whose arrows are structural $f$ satisfying (1). Composition is valid by (2). The projection to structural unfoldings is a functor, but it need not be an opfibration or fibration: a lift can be absent, nonunique, or fail the required universal property. Replacing a failed lifting claim with this category changes the advertised universal interface and must be stated explicitly.

\section{What open assembly can actually be lifted without selection}
For a finite primitive word $W$, form its open network and the entire policy set $\mathfrak P_N(W)$, including structural skeleton choices and all zero/off-policy rows. Use the supplied all-raw initialization, persistent ports, passive world rebase and scope library. No arbitrary externally supplied isolated operand policy is fixed. A parenthesization of $W$ is a presentation of the same primitive network, not an opaque implementation.

\begin{theorem}[All-policy lift of presented open assembly]
Every canonical primitive-preserving rebracketing of finite open words induces a bijection of their whole finite-horizon policy families, and actual native isomorphisms on each policy's execution rows and once-retained provenance content fibres. These provenance readdressing isomorphisms commute with the generally noninvertible directed provenance-to-terminal comparison; that directed comparison is not itself called an isomorphism. These bijections satisfy the pentagon and unit equations. They require no target extension choice: the rebracketing maps are bijective on all structural actions, raw atoms, and all-history decision indices. Their induced policy relations are therefore graphs of bijections.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The operational rebracketing theorem identifies primitive vertices, common scope diagrams, every admitted law label, all raw tuples and every post-tree, including birth initialization and passive states. Induction on depth gives a bijection of the full unselected unfoldings, not just their realized paths. It preserves distinct past diagonal/antidiagonal law choices even at off-policy histories. It therefore bijects skeletons and all conditional coordinates, with identical admitted joint laws under the tuple readdressing. The inverse structural rebracketing gives the inverse policy transport. Every pentagon route acts identically on primitive occurrence addresses and global event histories; it consequently acts identically on every policy coordinate. Empty words contribute no actor, draw or scope factor. These facts prove the family equations.

For Cartesian content with the zero-starting typed compiler, retain the complete arity-matching formal domain and apply the earlier actual directed terminal comparison, once per global event. Block/flat scope-content grafting is claimed only for the actual slice-limit branch with its passive slice-boundary units and full body factorization. Actual context maps retain the chronological tensor of the same per-event factors. Thus the pinned fibrewise coherence theorems apply without invoking a false temporal tensor--limit interchange.\end{proof}

This is a genuine policy-indexed associativity theorem for the \emph{presented network library}. It supplies all allowed shared scopes and schedules at the composite network level without selecting a coupling or fixing unknown target extensions. It is not an operation $\mathfrak P_N(P)\times\mathfrak P_N(Q)\to\mathfrak P_N(PQ)$ on isolated policies: the latter lack external passive histories and shared event alignment. Nor is it a proof that independently chosen intermediate policy families flatten bijectively. Sibling-history dependence is a distinct obstruction: a global controller may choose a child command from another sibling's earlier bit, so its child table need not be constant on local-history fibres. Zero-fibre disintegration is another: integrated joint laws determine no unique conditional row on a local history of zero marginal mass. Full retained global-history-indexed rows avoid both erasures; autonomous extraction needs separate descent data, not just a marginal trace law. The missing-middle witness shows that such a statement cannot follow merely from structural native composability and terminal law preservation.

\paragraph{Feedback residual.}
Finite horizon does not turn recursive network feedback into an initial algebra. An actual feedback theorem still needs a declared recursive structural constructor, admissible finite initialization, a fold on all structural histories, and a simultaneous conditional extension theorem for that fold. The normal lax relation gives compositional transport when middle witnesses exist, not their existence. The present advance is an all-policy lift of the available open-word assembly together with a concrete obstruction to promoting unchosen conditional transport to stronger compositional or feedback universal laws. No stronger closure is claimed.
