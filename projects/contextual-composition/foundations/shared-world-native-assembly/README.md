# Shared-world native assembly

## Result and interface

This construction treats actual shared-world updates, including nonserializable read/write histories, rather than only couplings between persistent commuting arena events. Primitive components carry independently specified passive rebase actions. A multi-active scope retains its supplied joint law, actual common world-event arrow, complete active post-states, native coefficients and writer body. Other existing copies rebase; newly born continuations initialize immediately at the actual post-world.

The generated open-network construction has multi-occurrence composition, actual coherent rebracketing and units. Its internal maps fix the world category and common raw/context/writer boundary library. The whole scope writer grade and the terminal occurrence-polynomial grade are both retained but are not identified. A restricted Cartesian-content branch supplies an actual directed comparison from once-owned scope provenance to terminal occurrence content on a common supplied timeline.

The statements concern explicit generated or indexed interfaces. They do not establish arbitrary opaque implementation refinement, an initial algebra for every shared-world system, an unrestricted single-content interaction or a generic completion of contextads. Finite diagnostics supplement written proofs; they do not certify their unrestricted quantifiers or constitute TeX compilation.

## Why both grades remain material

An actual joint writer coefficient may be a limit, while occurrence substitution tensors actor terminal coefficients. For two augmented two-dimensional factors over a common unit, these coefficients have dimensions three and four. They cannot be identified by an isomorphism. More strongly, there is no natural joint-to-tensor recovery over both primitive legs for all augmented linear maps: an augmentation-preserving shear contradicts the required natural copying map. This excludes a particular natural comparison, not arbitrary arrows or chosen nonnatural sections.

The Cartesian-content branch changes precisely the content structure needed for copying. It retains the single joint factor and uses a Cartesian diagonal in its actual arrow, rather than adding that factor twice. Its positive theorem does not make context coefficients Cartesian: two chronological joint context factors can still give dimension nine, versus seven after a different actorwise tensor/limit arrangement. Only the same globally owned context factors are compared in that branch.

The ordinary two-grade network theorem does not silently replace scope-history content by terminal occurrence content. The supplied full writer schemas include unused shapes; the terminal polynomial collection is independently full. Any stronger consolidation needs the displayed actual comparison and its hypotheses.

## Controls, initialization and native-map boundaries

A supplied global joint stays whole. Equal local marginals neither determine it nor justify replacing it with conditional independence. Native comparisons require exact pushforward of the chosen joint and actual body/coefficient squares. Internal component maps commute over identity shared boundary objects, ensuring that arbitrary componentwise families really induce scope-diagram maps. Common changes of world or boundary library are separate external comparisons.

Completion and blocking are different statuses. Finite conditional-prefix comparisons use matched command histories; normalized absorbing towers additionally require matched stopped statuses and explicit status-preserving unit padding. Context/history truncation uses context augmentations. Deleting full writer coefficients needs separate content augmentations and is not inferred from a merely monoidal content category.

A continuation may cache its birth-world. If it starts at world zero and later passively rebases to world one, its cache can remain zero; a fresh initialization at one may differ. Associativity and pure-wire balance keep the actual birth time and all pending effects. No empty wire or block introduces a paid step. When an old arena scope activates subsets `J_j` of primitive events, its event cost remains `sum_j |J_j|`, not the number of selected actors or the single scope-step count.
