\paragraph{Status and kernel.} The written finite theorem has passed independent review at the declared scope; no proof-assistant certification is claimed. This supplements, rather than changes, the pointwise scoped-control category. Averaging changes the required comparison from a conditional row square at every source history to an exact pushforward of the evaluated complete-history row. Positive-mass conditional controls are determined; zero-history controls are not. All finite raw histories and actual coefficient arrows remain. No general naturality claim for a choice of zero-history table is made.

\section{The finite source and quotient interface}
All statements are rowwise for each specified external input; distinct external input rows are not summed together. Fix finite bounded rooted history trees $H,H'$ with compatible terminal strata. At each nonterminal $h$ the source has a supplied conditional law $q_h$ on its finite successor set $W_h$, and actual coefficient $K_{h,w}$. A specified raw prefix map $F:H\to H'$ is rooted, depth preserving, preserves and reflects terminality, and maps successors by $r_h:W_h\to W'_{Fh}$. It need not push each source conditional law to a common target conditional law. Let $p(h)$ be the source prefix mass, including $p(\mathrm{root})=1$, and put
\[
 p'(h')=\sum_{Fh=h'}p(h).
\]
Suppose target successor sets, primitive rows, coefficients $K'_{h',w'}$, and body/grade maps have already been supplied. For every source edge suppose an actual coefficient arrow
\[
 \alpha_{h,w}:K_{h,w}\to K'_{Fh,r_h(w)}
\]
with the required body equation. In a joint-frontier application, the target primitive marginals are required to depend only on $h'$, and every $(r_h)_*q_h$ with $Fh=h'$ must have those marginals. This last hypothesis follows from rowwise native primitive comparisons when the target primitive inputs are determined by $h'$; it does not follow from arbitrary history erasure.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-history averaged transport]
At every target prefix with $p'(h')>0$, the formula
\[
 q'_{h'}=\frac{1}{p'(h')}\sum_{Fh=h'}p(h)(r_h)_*q_h \tag{1}
\]
defines a normalized conditional law with the specified target primitive marginals. At zero-mass target prefixes choose any admitted normalized conditional law. Such choices exist for finite joint-frontier marginals by the independent product. Then the target prefix masses are exactly $p'(h')$, and the complete leaf law is the pushforward by $F$ of the source complete leaf law. Tensoring the supplied edge coefficient arrows, or taking their finite product in the all-node product doctrine, gives an actual native map of the evaluated leaf contexts, including zero raw paths. Supplied global body/grade equations are retained on all labels, not only positive support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalization and each marginal equation are preserved by the convex mixture. For a target edge $h'\to(h',w')$, its assigned mass is
\[
 p'(h')q'_{h'}(w')=\sum_{Fh=h'}p(h)\sum_{r_h(w)=w'}q_h(w),
\]
including the zero case, since all source prefix masses in a zero fibre vanish. Induction in depth proves the prefix equation; compatible terminal strata yield the leaf equation. Along a complete source path the edge maps give the actual tensor or product coefficient arrow to the corresponding target path. Together with the leaf pushforward equality this is precisely a native original finite augmented-family arrow. No conditional row arrow $q_h\to q'_{Fh}$ is asserted.\end{proof}

The theorem is conditional on the supplied target coefficient/body data. A scalar disintegration alone cannot invent those arrows. In particular, averaging coefficients by their probabilities is neither needed nor justified: original native fibre aggregation uses the given individual arrows and the additive coproduct universal property.

\begin{theorem}[Tower coherence on positive histories]
For composable raw prefix maps $F:H\to H'$ and $G:H'\to H''$ satisfying the stated target-row hypotheses, with the same supplied final raw tree, primitive rows, body and global-grade data, and coefficient maps obtained by composition, successive averaged transport and direct averaged transport along $GF$ agree at every positive-mass target prefix. Their evaluated native maps agree under composition when the supplied edge coefficient maps compose. At zero-mass prefixes the chosen control tables need not agree, but they yield the same evaluated complete-history probabilities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At positive $h''$, substitute (1) for every intermediate $h'$ in its fibre. The factors $p'(h')$ cancel at positive intermediate prefixes. Zero intermediate prefixes contribute zero regardless of their arbitrary control. The resulting numerator is
\[
 \sum_{GFh=h''}p(h)(s_{Fh})_*(r_h)_*q_h,
\]
which is the direct formula. Actual path coefficient maps compose by tensor/product functoriality. Both maps retain the same complete raw path map $GF$, even on zero paths; only the conditional strategy representation on a zero target prefix may differ.\end{proof}

Identity transport preserves every positive-history control. Replacing all zero-history controls by independent products makes an evaluated-process identity, but generally not an identity of retained strategies: a zero-history diagonal control is changed. Quotienting strategies by agreement only at positive histories therefore changes the control kernel. The full-memory construction avoids this quotient, while the conditional-lumpability category retains pointwise control equations and thus rejects some otherwise valid averaged cells. These are three different interfaces.

\section{A bounded impossibility result for symmetric affine zero averaging}
The absence of probability weights at zero histories is not repaired by declaring uniform averaging functorial. The precise failed requirement can be isolated without claiming that all arbitrary set-theoretic selection schemes are impossible.

\begin{proposition}
Let $Q$ be a nontrivial convex segment of admitted controls, for example the segment between diagonal and antidiagonal couplings of two fair bits. There is no system assigning to every surjection of finite nonempty zero-history sets a fibre-local affine averaging operation on $Q$-valued policies which (i) fixes constant policies, (ii) is invariant under relabelling of each source fibre, and (iii) composes for surjections.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an $n$-point fibre, affine averaging of scalar coordinates on $Q\cong[0,1]$, fixing constants and invariant under all permutations, is the arithmetic mean: equal coefficients and sum one force coefficient $1/n$. Take a three-point fibre with controls $(a,a,b)$, $a\ne b$. Direct collapse gives $(2a+b)/3$. First merge the first two points and retain the third, then collapse the resulting two-point set. Fibre-locality and constant preservation give $(a,b)$ at the intermediate set, hence $(a+b)/2$ finally. The two values differ.\end{proof}

The proposition is not a no-go for every non-affine convention or extra chosen ordering. Such conventions add structure and need their own native naturality/consumer justification. It is a no-go for the symmetric affine rule that would otherwise appear to extend Bayesian averaging without retaining a measure or multiplicity on the zero fibre. Keeping fibre memory, or extra positive reference weights with their own transport law, changes this interface explicitly. A reference weight is not automatically the original probabilistic law, and cannot silently delete the original zero control distinctions.

\paragraph{Exact separation from the earlier obstruction.} In the fair erased-bit example with diagonal/antidiagonal conditional controls, (1) gives the independent coupling at the positive target continuation history after the outer bit has been collapsed. Thus arbitrary original outer maps do not prohibit every strategy transport. They prohibit the specific pointwise section transport; averaged complete-row transport is possible under the target-marginal and actual-coefficient hypotheses. At a zero target history even Bayesian averaging supplies no control table, and the stated affine/symmetry axioms cannot be completed functorially without changing retained information.
