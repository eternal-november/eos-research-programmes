Question 1: equal best values and policy associativity
====================================================
Location: opening index PI-0 -> Section 2, Proposition "Naive substitution
obstruction" (source label prop:naive; immediately followed by its proof).
Section 1 fixes the output interface: complete policies, rather than scalar
values alone. The example concerns the displayed output-indexed substitution
rule, not every possible interaction calculus.

The proof's witness is a fair bit from f, its erasure by g, and a deterministic
choice of final bit by h. One bracketing retains four bit-indexed policies;
the other retains only two constant policies. Their attainable output-one
probabilities are respectively {0, 1/2, 1} and {0, 1}. A policy-preserving
associator cannot identify those policy sets. Nevertheless every linear
terminal valuation has the same optimized value max(v(0), v(1)), because
both bracketings retain both constant policies. The scalar optimum therefore
fails to detect the missing conditional policies and intermediate law.

Governing boundary: rebracketing may preserve the bit through a common full
history arena or an explicitly typed memory interface, but cannot grant
forbidden observation. PI-1 supplies the positive retained-cut result inside
one fixed finite arena. PI-4 controls assembly when admissible local choices
are restricted. Neither result grants arbitrary opaque controllers a binary
composition operation.

Evidence separation: the written PI-0 proof covers every real terminal
valuation. The shipped associativity diagnostic reports the four-versus-two
sets and 25 rational score pairs. Those 25 checks do not establish the
universal assertion. Section 9's prospective native presentation, crossing,
and coherence obligations remain unconstructed.

Question 2: failure of optimal-policy descent after merging histories
=====================================================================
Location: opening index PI-6 -> Section 7.1, Theorem "Greedy extension and
descent" (source label thm:fibre). Its prerequisites are Section 7's PI-5;
the meaning of optimality is pinned by Section 4's PI-2.

For target history k, gather every forced image phi_h(p(h)) with Fh = k.
A compatible all-history greedy target policy must choose a control in
G_T(k) equal to every member of that forced set. PI-6 gives the complete
extension family as the product of these local constraint sets.

Under PI-5's exact row pushforward, successor, reward and terminal-value
equations, surjective control maps, and an all-history greedy source policy,
every forced choice is target-greedy. Existence is then equivalent to
agreement of the choices forced by every nonempty merged-history fibre.
Two different forced choices at a single target history obstruct descent
even if both are optimal and all scalar optimal values agree. History
surjectivity removes uncovered-coordinate freedom but does not repair
merged-history disagreement. Conditional on existence, uniqueness additionally
requires a singleton greedy set at every uncovered target coordinate.

The general product formula remains the criterion outside those sufficient
coverage hypotheses: even a single forced choice can fail to be greedy.
Root optimality is weaker than all-history greediness, because it constrains
only positive-occupancy histories. A root-optimal policy may retain arbitrary
choices at zero/off-policy coordinates, so the stronger simplified existence
criterion cannot silently be applied to it. Such coordinates remain part of
the complete policy interface and the descent equations.

Evidence separation: PI-5 and PI-6 contain written finite proofs. The shipped
native diagnostic reports finite row/control examples and the optimal
missing-middle witness (four compatible optimal composite pairs, only two
factoring through a middle policy). It is not a general native lifting
certificate. Material/interface arrows must be independently supplied;
probability equations do not reconstruct coefficient or grade maps. The
relationship to the source theorem with a fixed structural skeleton requires
the stated normal-form interpretation.
