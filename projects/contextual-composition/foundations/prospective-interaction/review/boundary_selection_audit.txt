Typing and closure
------------------
For C <= D <= E, take arrows pi:(C,u)->(D,v) and
rho:(D,v)->(E,w) in BOpt(A). Their conditions give

    E_DE^rho(w) = v = B_DE(w),
    E_CD^pi(v) = u = B_CD(v).

Hence policy-evaluation composition and Bellman composition yield

    E_CE^(pi union rho)(w)
      = E_CD^pi(E_DE^rho(w))
      = E_CD^pi(B_DE(w))
      = B_CD(B_DE(w))
      = B_CE(w)
      = u.

The composite therefore lies in BOpt(A). Empty tables are identities at
every object. All-history greedy arrows are boundary optimal, and every
boundary-optimal arrow is an Eval(A) arrow. Thus both displayed inclusions
are wide subcategory inclusions. In a forest, optimality is required at
every history of C, exactly as stated; a single designated-root condition
would not suffice for the vector endpoint equation.

Explicit suffix-restriction obstruction
---------------------------------------
Let a root r have one control with two raw successors x,y of probabilities
1 and 0, respectively, and zero immediate reward. At x, the sole action
gives reward 0 and terminates. At y, two actions give rewards 0 and 1 and
terminate. All terminal values are zero. Retain both raw successors and
all resulting terminal histories.

The complete policy choosing reward 0 at y is optimal from r, because y
has hitting probability zero. With C={r}, D={x,y}, and E the terminal cut,
it is a BOpt(A) arrow from (C,0) to (E,0). Its suffix restriction evaluates
to (0,0) on D, whereas B_DE(0)=(0,1). The suffix is therefore not
boundary optimal. Composition is union of disjoint policy coordinates,
so a factorization of this same complete table cannot replace that suffix
by another table. This validates the existential obstruction in PI-3b.

All-history greedy policies have optimal values at both x and y, including
zero-hitting and off-policy histories. The already proved greedy segment
factorization gives their optimal restrictions at every complete
intermediate cut.
