MANUSCRIPT-ONLY TECHNICAL PUBLICATION REVIEW

Manuscript: Prospective Interaction: Continuation Evaluation, Selection, and Policy Composition
Version reviewed: “Working mathematical note — 26 November 2026,” 14-page PDF
Recommendation: Major revision before publication as a standalone research note.

1. Scope, assessment standard, and principal finding

This assessment uses the designated manuscript alone. The initial assignment was a focused check of its finite mathematics; the subsequent assignment requested a complete publication assessment. Accordingly, this is a manuscript-only technical publication review, not a claim of an unprimed or strictly blind assessment. No repository files, companion program, external publications, or other review reports were consulted. Page references below are the manuscript’s printed page numbers.

The applicable publication standard is a standalone research note for an audience spanning applied category theory and mathematical control: correct statements and proofs, an identifiable contribution beyond the background machinery, adequate explanation of the intended categorical use, and evidence proportionate to the claims. No particular journal or conference was specified. A working-note series can legitimately apply a lower threshold for an unfinished research programme; the recommendation here concerns research publication.

The finite mathematics is sound under the assumptions explicitly retained in the paper. No counterexample or missing hypothesis was found in Theorems 3.2, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, 7.1, or 7.2, or in Proposition 5.2. Independent exact-rational enumeration reconstructed the principal Appendix A counts and verified the loss identity throughout that reconstructed arena. This is a bounded positive finding, not proof-assistant certification or verification of the accompanying implementation.

The principal obstacle to publication is the contribution’s positioning and demonstrated significance, rather than a broken theorem. The paper presents a coherent collection of elementary finite results and useful counterexamples, but its own account leaves the relation to its motivating interaction calculus conditional. The text needs a more precise statement of what the synthesis newly establishes for its intended audience, together with a suitably bounded programme-level conclusion. These issues can be addressed without constructing the general feedback, revision, or full context/content theory expressly excluded from scope.

2. What the manuscript establishes

The central distinction is between preserving an optimized scalar value and preserving the policies that produce outcomes. Proposition 2.1, §§2–3, pp. 2–3, makes this distinction concrete. A fair bit is generated, forgotten at an intermediate external interface, and potentially used by a later choice. One bracketing retains four tables and the other only two; the attainable final laws differ, while both optimized terminal-value functions equal the maximum of the two terminal scores. The cardinality obstruction and the all-real-scores argument are correct. This is the most economical and immediately intelligible motivation in the paper.

The repair studied in §§3–5 retains a complete policy coordinate at every decision history of a common finite arena. This includes histories behind unselected controls and zero-probability outcomes. Cut composition becomes restriction and union of disjoint coordinate sets. Evaluation composes, and a policy selected for optimality at every retained history can be restricted to optimally selected pieces. Root optimality is weaker: it constrains only histories with positive occupancy under the selected policy.

Section 6 isolates the assembly premise. For a fixed prefix hitting law and an admissible family of continuation tables, independent local maximization is exact for all independent real score tables precisely when the admissible family projects onto the full product of alternatives at positive-mass coordinates. Section 7 then distinguishes scalar value comparison from policy compatibility: control coverage suffices for equality of optimal values, while a compatible greedy target policy additionally requires agreement among controls forced by merged histories.

These results form a coherent finite package. The manuscript correctly says, “Its assembly input is a common primitive-history arena” (§3, p. 4). That qualification determines the force of the construction. It does not establish an associative operation on arbitrary opaque controllers supplied independently of that arena.

3. Mathematical correctness and proof obligations

Theorem 3.2 (§3, p. 4) is valid. A complete structural cut partitions all decision histories into those strictly before the cut and the subtrees beginning at its nodes. Restriction gives the asserted bijection and union gives its inverse. Each path has a unique cut encounter, so its probability and reward split at that point. Simultaneous attainment of suffix optima uses the product policy space, and that premise is explicitly available. The nested-cut coherence claim is justified by equality of the retained coordinate assignments, including zero and off-policy coordinates.

Theorem 4.1 (§4, pp. 4–5) is also valid. Subtracting selected-policy evaluation from optimal evaluation gives

Delta_pi(h) = delta(h, pi(h)) + sum_w q(h, pi(h), w) Delta_pi(h[pi(h), w]).

Finite expansion yields the occupancy-weighted loss identity. Nonnegativity makes the root-optimality criterion exact. The all-history product formula and the root-optimal fibre formula correctly differ at zero-hitting cut histories: their suffixes are constrained in the former and unrestricted in the latter. There is no hidden substitution of all-history optimality for root optimality.

Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 5.2 (§5, p. 6) define actual categories. Evaluation composition provides the endpoint equation for table union; empty tables provide identities. The greedy factorization identity gives closure of Opt(A), and the intermediate object for factoring an already supplied optimal arrow is indeed forced to have value B_DE(w). The non-fullness claim is witnessed by a root-optimal table with a nongreedy off-policy coordinate. Boundary-optimal arrows also compose, but their suffix restrictions can fail boundary optimality at zero-hitting histories. The paper correctly distinguishes a closure property from a restriction property, and correctly avoids inferring a fibration from factorization of an already supplied table.

Theorem 6.1 (§6, p. 7) is sharp as stated. The gap from the separated optimum is a sum of nonnegative local gaps. To prove necessity of full positive-coordinate projection, the indicator scores for a missing tuple give separated value 1, while every admissible table loses at least the smallest positive coordinate weight. A probability law ensures that the positive support is nonempty, so this minimum is legitimate. The scope limitation after the theorem matters: “The necessity result concerns arbitrary independent local scores” (§6, p. 8). A restricted family of realizable continuation valuations need not distinguish every omitted table. The text states this limitation rather than overextending the result.

Theorem 7.1 (§7, p. 8) follows by backward induction using exact pushforward of each row and pointwise reward preservation. Control surjectivity upgrades the comparison inequality to equality and gives the inverse-image formula for greedy controls. Raw-map surjectivity is unnecessary for those scalar statements because a target atom outside the image has zero probability under the mapped row. Such an atom still remains a genuine history for complete-policy questions. Fixed-policy equality requires compatibility, not coverage of unselected controls. The comparison equations and the control-surjective subclass are closed under composition.

Theorem 7.2 (§7.1, p. 9) is an exact coordinatewise compatibility calculation. Its displayed product intersects each target greedy set with all controls forced by source histories mapping there. Under control surjectivity and source greediness, Theorem 7.1 places every forced image in the corresponding target greedy set. A covered factor is therefore a singleton when the forced controls agree and empty when they do not; uncovered factors remain the full greedy sets. The existence and uniqueness statements follow.

The optimal missing-middle example (§7.2, pp. 9–10) is internally consistent. Greediness forces H = good but leaves the two L choices independent in the source, producing four compatible optimal source–target pairs. An intermediate policy requires their L labels to agree, leaving two factorizations. The reward depends on the retained U,V coordinates and is preserved pointwise by projection. Optimization therefore does not remove this obstruction. This example supports the claimed failure of strict policy-relation composition, without establishing a general native lifting theorem.

No mathematical repair to these statements is required by the checks performed. The boundary of that finding is the finite product-policy model with its stated observation, reward, and transition assumptions.

4. Contribution and programme-level interpretation: the major revision

The manuscript openly describes its work as “an explicit adaptation of dynamic programming and coordinatewise policy constraints” (§1, p. 1), and later states, “The present finite recursion is not a novelty claim relative to that literature” (§9, p. 12). These are appropriate qualifications. They also leave a research-publication obligation: identify which part of the package is the paper’s distinctive mathematical or conceptual contribution, and demonstrate what that contribution enables.

At present, the reader can identify a useful synthesis but cannot determine its precise relation to the cited alternatives from the manuscript alone. Theorem 7.2 is described as an optimizer-constrained analogue of an existing extension-fibre theorem (§7.1, p. 9); the missing-middle example is presented as an optimal version of an existing witness (§7.2, p. 9). The elementary Bellman, product, and coordinatewise arguments then carry much of the remaining paper. This review cannot establish a priority claim against unread references, and does not assert that every construction has appeared before. The issue is that the manuscript itself does not yet make its specific advance sufficiently assessable.

A revision should name a central contribution at theorem or construction level and distinguish established machinery, adaptation, and new consequences. For example, the organizing result could be the joint comparison between scalar evaluation, boundary-optimal policy composition, all-history restriction, and policy descent. If that is the intended contribution, it should be presented explicitly as such, with the exact preservation properties and failures aligned in one argument. The current sequence contains the ingredients but leaves their research significance largely to the programme motivation.

The conclusion also needs a narrower inference. Section 9 states, “It supports the specialized-structure branch of the proactad question” (p. 11). Yet the paper has not supplied an interpretation of Eval(A), Opt(A), or the comparison data in the referenced interaction doctrine. The same section correctly identifies the next obligation as “a presentation-level interpretation of this finite category of policies and its selector” (p. 12). Until that interpretation exists, the proved conclusion is that the specified finite controlled arenas admit this equipment and these obstructions. The conclusion does not yet discriminate whether the intended native interaction calculus admits the equipment with its required crossing and coherence laws.

There are two proportionate ways to resolve this. One is a tightly delimited finite-interface research note, with the programme application presented as a conditional target and the contribution framed as a precise preservation-and-obstruction package. The other is to add a small but explicit interpretation result for a specified native fragment. A complete proactad construction is not a fair publication condition for the stated finite pilot. A definite account of what the pilot contributes, and what inference its evidence supports, is.

5. Accessibility and self-containment

The elementary mathematical core is substantially self-contained. The programme-facing exposition is less so. The opening introduces contextads, interactads, a pseudodistributive law, and native coherence data; later sections use full grades, material coefficients, world arrows, calibration C1–C2, incoming-signature validity, and opaque exports. Several of these terms are external obligations rather than operands in a theorem proved here. Readers outside the referenced repository need a clearer map of their role.

A short interface table or glossary should distinguish: data defined in the paper; hypotheses imported only for a native interpretation; and operations expressly left for future work. In particular, the maps F, phi, and rho in §7 are fully defined finite data. Calling the section an evaluation of independently supplied native maps is justified only with the additional native hypothesis explicitly stated on p. 8. Keeping that distinction visible would make the theorem usable without suggesting that the finite equations reconstruct a native morphism.

The title and abstract can also foreground the finite policy-preservation problem more directly. The current abstract is accurate, including its final list of unresolved obligations, but the wider interaction and allostatic vocabulary can suggest a contribution beyond what the formal sections provide. The finite restriction is a material part of the result, not merely an implementation detail.

Section 8 contains useful boundary examples. The horizon example correctly shows that resetting continuation values changes the optimization problem; the coupling example correctly reverses the capacity choice despite equal marginals. These examples justify specific limitations. They do not provide evidence about general feedback or physiological regulation, and the text generally maintains that distinction. A revision should preserve that restraint.

6. Reproducibility and independent count reconstruction

Appendix A, pp. 12–13, reports executable diagnostics, and Appendix B, p. 13, supplies a pinned source boundary. The named program and repository claims were not inspected. Consequently, this assessment does not certify that the supplied program performs the reported checks, that the repository links reproduce the described sources, or that historical proof-assistant claims hold.

The principal arena counts can nevertheless be reconstructed from the manuscript. On the intended reading, the root has two controls and two retained bit witnesses under each. Each ensuing history has preparation/no-preparation choices, followed by work/rest choices with deterministic later transitions. Thus there are

1 + 2 x 2 + 2 x 2 x 2 = 13 decision histories,

16 terminal histories, and 2^13 = 8192 complete binary policy tables.

Work is strictly optimal at every final history. Preparation costs 1/2 and raises the final work reward by 1, giving a strict net improvement of 1/2 at every preparation history. The root action values are

Q(A) = (1/2)(3/2 + 7/4) = 13/8,
Q(B) = 1/2 + 3/2 = 2.

Root optimality fixes exactly three coordinates: choose B, prepare at the positive-mass B,0 history, and work at its selected final history. Ten binary coordinates remain unconstrained by root occupancy, producing 2^10 = 1024 root optima. Every local optimum is unique, producing exactly one all-history optimum.

An independent in-memory enumeration with exact rational arithmetic reproduced all three counts, the optimal value 2, and the loss identity for every one of the 8192 policies. This was a reconstruction from the PDF, not execution of the manuscript’s companion script. The other Appendix A enumeration totals, including the 720 rectangularity comparisons, were not independently reproduced. Their underlying finite theorems have the separate proof checks described above.

For fully self-contained reproducibility, Appendix A should explicitly name the no-preparation control and specify singleton raw carriers at the later stages. Those are the natural intended choices and reproduce every principal reported count, but leaving them implicit makes the arena description less complete than Definition 3.1 requires. This is a minor clarification, not a discovered mathematical defect.

7. Requested revision and final recommendation

Major revision is recommended for a standalone research publication. The finite proofs and examples provide a sound basis for revision. The publication threshold has not yet been met because the manuscript’s distinctive contribution and the inference to its motivating programme remain insufficiently delineated.

The essential revisions are to identify the central advance precisely; establish its relationship to the closest cited constructions at the level needed to assess that advance; distinguish the proved finite equipment from the still-conditional native interpretation in the conclusion; and provide a concise guide to the external interface terminology. The Appendix A arena specification requires a smaller clarification.

The recommendation does not rest on a demonstrated theorem failure, an externally established lack of originality, or a requirement to solve all the declared future problems. It rests on a narrower and supported assessment: the manuscript already establishes a correct finite preservation-and-obstruction package, while the argument for why that package constitutes a standalone research contribution needs further development. If revised around that contribution and its exact scope, the work has a credible form as a concise conceptual research note.
