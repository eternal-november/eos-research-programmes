\section{A nontrivial native kernel and the universal-limit obstruction}
Take $E=F=k^2$ with augmentation $\epsilon(e_0)=1$, $\epsilon(e_1)=0$. The augmented product is
\[
 E\times_kF=\{(x,y):\epsilon(x)=\epsilon(y)\},
\]
of dimension three. The tensor has dimension four. Formula (1) gives
\[
 e_0\otimes e_0\mapsto(e_0,e_0),\quad
 e_1\otimes e_0\mapsto(e_1,0),\quad
 e_0\otimes e_1\mapsto(0,e_1),\quad
 e_1\otimes e_1\mapsto0.                                  \tag{6}
\]
The kernel is exactly $k(e_1\otimes e_1)$, not a unit artifact. More generally, for $r$ such factors, tensor dimension is $2^r$, product dimension is $r+1$, and the kernel is spanned by basis tensors with at least two $e_1$ factors, of dimension $2^r-r-1$. The comparison is not always even surjective: for two copies of $k$ with zero augmentation it is the zero map from their one-dimensional tensor to their two-dimensional augmented product.

This loss occurs for genuinely shared, not merely independent, effects. At a binary frontier take two fair raw bits with the chosen diagonal law $q(0,0)=q(1,1)=1/2$, retaining mixed raw tuples at zero, and give each node coefficient $k^2$ as above. The tensor evaluator retains (6) at every raw tuple. On an auxiliary row with the same raw tuples, law, body and full grade, use coefficient $k^2$. The two augmented maps sending $e_0$ to $e_0\otimes e_0$ and $e_1$ respectively to $0$ or $e_1\otimes e_1$ give distinct actual native row arrows into that chosen tensor joint. Their composites with the product comparison agree. This is a concrete native-map distinction lost by that comparison; it is not a claim that these auxiliary maps factor into primitive per-node maps or that the entire causal evaluator is faithful.

Finally, the tensor object with its augmentation-induced projections cannot be the universal coefficient product in this example. An ordinary product would be uniquely isomorphic to $E\times_kF$, but (6) is not an isomorphism. Equivalently, the displayed two distinct augmented maps have identical component projections, violating the uniqueness clause for a product cone. The same context example embeds in any chosen raw law, including the shared fair-bit law, so scalar marginal correctness cannot repair it. Thus the tensor family retains source coefficient directions precisely by not claiming the universal all-coupling/native-limit property served by product coefficients.
