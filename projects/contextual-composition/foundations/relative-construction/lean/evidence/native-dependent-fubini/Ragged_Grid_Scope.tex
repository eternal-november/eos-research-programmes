\section*{Ragged two-axis structural interchange}
Fix addresses $x,y$ and a continuation list $B_y$ varying with $y$. The
row source has order $x,y,b$ and the column source has order $y,x,b$,
where $b$ is a choice from $B_y$. The additive transpose maps whole
$(x,y)$ cells. No quotient by equality of their resource objects or
cardinalities is performed. The independently defined gathering routes
both yield the tensor of the entire $x$ sum with the nested $y,b$
sum. The transpose is invertible, including empty axes, and its
naturality is proved even for arbitrary whole-cell maps that are not
tensors of maps. Resolving each ragged cell to a gathered tensor, then
using the rectangular result, proves the ragged comparison.

The mathlib adapter builds the needed binary and nullary mixed diagrams
by inversion of the existing forward distributor and absorber diagrams.
The ordinary braided middle-four is not assumed involutive in an
arbitrary braided category: its involutivity here uses additive symmetry.
There is no assumption that the additive unit is initial.

Typed additive circuits derive transport through a surrounding tensor
context from its local lax symmetric monoidal compatibility. They
preserve the tagged leaf list up to permutation, retaining a genuine
zero-labelled leaf and distinguishing it from syntactic zero.
The interpreted circuit agrees on actual arrows with the semantic
transpose used in the ragged grid. The context-lifted comparison starts
at a flat addressed sum and compares the two ragged gather schedules
followed by an actual whole-sum arrow. For the reverse-function
instance the two flat entry charts may be chosen independently:
regrouping and transposition provably preserve the decoded raw
address plus token. Duplicate-free here concerns addresses only.

The generic flat theorem still uses the column chart obtained by the
explicit verified transpose of the row chart. The corresponding
path-independence theorem for independently chosen flat entry
charts in every rig is not formalized. Neither is the identification
of both original four-level binary native Fubini routes from their
chosen finite native source, nor all-arity assembly and coherences.
