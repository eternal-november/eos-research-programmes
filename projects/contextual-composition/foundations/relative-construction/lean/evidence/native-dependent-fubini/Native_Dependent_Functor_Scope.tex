\section*{Functoriality of ordered native dependent accumulation}
The literal unary dependent word map preserves identities and
composition. Input composition is performed on the original parent
native map and on each matching original continuation native map.
Each decoded opaque whole-fibre cell reassembles to that actual
composition; no opaque cell is split into per-occurrence inputs.

At a fixed final occurrence the sequential native cell is obtained
by grafting each original first-stage addressed fibre chart into
the second-stage chart. The second continuation is constant on the
first parent fibre, so its opaque cell can be moved before the
opaque first parent cell by multiplicative bifunctoriality and
right-gather naturality. The remaining grouped block exchange is
proved on the actual additive sheets. The separately assembled
one-step coefficient, with composed parent and child input maps, is
expanded by the standard native finite partition maps and reduces
to the same parent-major coefficient. The two raw order circuits
are identified by distinct addressed additive coherence. This
proves equality of actual whole cells and of the native arrows.
The same maps preserve the augmentations by the prior collapse
square, so their identity and composition laws are equalities
in the augmented native category.

Each presentation has distinct parent addresses and distinct local
child addresses. Resource labels may repeat. Carrier maps may be
noninjective. Empty parent and child rows and globally empty words
are handled without choosing a default element; padding consumes
only the given additive coherence and nullary gather maps. The
supplied lax braided factor structures must have the original
binary and nullary gathers. The proof does not assume that the
additive unit is initial or that opaque cells out of it are unique.

This proves the augmentation, identity, and composition parts of
unary dependent accumulation in the specified ordered native
presentations. It does not yet package arbitrary order-independent
finite carriers, dependent associativity and unit naturality, the
horizontal binary functor laws, or the all-arity augmented Fubini
comparison and its regrouping, unit, and permutation compatibilities.
