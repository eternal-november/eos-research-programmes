\section*{Independent review: categorical telescope extension}
Verdict: accepted as a discrete-state instance of the displayed telescope extension comparison, not as a proof of the general extension theorem. At manuscript revision \texttt{458ff36a}, lines 929--953 distinguish this comparison from the free-monad and strict-algebra claims. None of those latter claims is needed or concluded here.

At each initial state, the value presentation pairs an existing native chain morphism with a payload arrow at a common endpoint. The iterate presentation independently builds arrows from a payload arrow at zero and a grade arrow paired with a recursively built tail at successor. Both compose with the native fibre operations. The zero and successor hom equivalences decode these constituents; cross-length maps and successor maps changing the next state are impossible. In particular, the singleton computation retains both independent inputs: replacing either hom family by its mere inhabitation would destroy the product-hom equivalence in general.

The forward and backward object maps are the previous pack and unpack. Their independently recursive arrow maps preserve composition; induction gives heterogeneous arrow roundtrips over the already proved object equalities. Functor extensionality then identifies each composite with the identity propositionally, not by definitional reduction. The resulting equivalence is not assumed. Each pointwise payload-change functor acts on the terminal payload morphism without changing the grade components. The forward comparison commutes by induction, and the backward square follows using the two inverse equalities. This is not a naturality assertion about transformations between payload-change functors. Base, grade objects and payload objects share a universe; hom universes are independent. No base reindexing, general grade change, normal cleavage, abstract coproduct universal property or strict telescope-algebra equivalence is established.
