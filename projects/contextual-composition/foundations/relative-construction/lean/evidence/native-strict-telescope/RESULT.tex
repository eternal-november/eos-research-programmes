\noindent\textbf{Exact interface.}
Fix an ordinary category \(B\), a concrete split indexed system \(P,S\) in the repository's common size bound, and its primitive actual span
\(B\xleftarrow{\ell} U\xrightarrow{r}B\).
On each primitive carrier take a normal cleavage of \(\ell\), carried as data. The chosen cleavage need not be its canonical split choice; no composition law for these chosen lifts is imposed. A map of span carriers is an actual apex functor commuting strictly with both feet and preserving \emph{all} cartesian arrows, not necessarily the chosen lifts. A strict algebra is such a functor \(a:\Tel U\to U\) satisfying the \emph{actual} native equations
\[
  a\eta_U=1_U,\qquad a\mu_U=a\,\Tel(a).
\]
The equations are literal functor equalities. The recovered algebra category uses these component equations directly. No extra identification with a chosen bundled \texttt{Monad.Algebra} across all carrier presentations is asserted. Lean checks an isomorphism of ordinary categories
\[
  \AA(B)\ \cong\ \CC(B)
\]
on these concrete carriers. The right side uses strict unit and product with strict associativity and units, and maps with identity unit and multiplication comparisons. The functors fix the carrier and chosen cleavage and fix each map's apex functor in its original direction. The split-indexed presentation is kept as part of the carrier, not quotiented by equivalence of presentations. Both round trips are equalities of objects and functors, not just isomorphisms in the fibres. Not claimed: an arbitrary fibration without a given split-indexed presentation, invariance of equality under an equivalence of presentations, an enhanced EM 2-category, maps with merely invertible comparisons, or the remaining broader issue \#105.

\paragraph{Restriction and construction.}
Restrict \(a\) to length zero, and to length two with the actual strict matching chart, to obtain the unit and binary map. Testing the actual multiplication equation on the full outer-two domain yields preservation of native binary concatenation. The strict section of \(a\) by the singleton inclusion then reflects the native left/right/associativity laws, including tests with inputs of lengths \((0,1),(1,0),(2,1),(1,2)\). Conversely, build an action by the empty-case unit, literal singleton identity, and the ordered native comb on the last cut. The concatenation equation is proved by induction on the right block using exact head/tail transport and the given ordinary strict contextad laws. Applying that result to the actual outer lengths, not to object lists, derives the native two-level algebra equation. Its head--tail specialization proves that the implemented left fold equals the source's right-comb evaluation on every diagram. Uniqueness is first shown on all such diagrams using the native singleton/tagged concatenation; restriction of those equalities to each length summand and full functor extensionality give equality on the coproduct, including its maps.

The cartesian estimates are independent of the strict equations. Comparison to a chosen lift is by an actual vertical isomorphism over its fixed incoming base arrow, from the universal factorization property. In a strict matched product a cartesian arrow has two cartesian components, and conversely; the proof does not infer cartesianness just from component object values. The exact native cut is an isomorphism of categories over both legs, so induction extends preservation of cartesian arrows from the unit and binary operations to the comb. Restriction uses the actual tag inclusions and the native binary chart. Reindexing in the input base is never inverted.

\paragraph{Maps and discrimination.}
\verb|StrictTelMap| applies a given strict two-legged apex functor to each actual primitive, through the native cut/rejoin charts, including on directed homs. Identity and composition are checked by the two native cut projections, not posited. From an actual strict algebra-map equality, restrict to lengths zero and two to recover the two comparison equalities. Conversely, the exact monoid-map equalities extend through every cut by induction; they give the original strict algebra-map square. The ordered categories and their inverse functors are assembled in \verb|StrictTelCategories|.

The positive probe uses the directed base \(\mathbb N\) in its order category and a constant, nonthin one-object grade category with additive natural-number morphisms. On the actual two-leaf native arrow moving from \(0\) to \(4\), labels \((2,3)\) evaluate to \(5\); an actual strict algebra map doubling the labels gives \(10\) on the other side of the native map square. The base arrow has no reverse and distinct payload labels remain distinct. The negative probe uses the same directed nonthin data but each primitive advances its right endpoint by one; the real length-zero word at \(0\) has endpoints \((0,0)\). No action strict over both feet can send it to a primitive, already before asking for algebra equations. Hence the recovery does not make arbitrary native spans into algebras, suppress the nullary field, or identify all arrows by their base image.

A separate supplemental probe (not in the ordered official suite) chooses two normal cleavages of the same native split-indexed carrier with different lift sources over the same noninvertible base arrow. The identity span map preserves all cartesian arrows but not these choices. The flip choice is also not split: reindexing successively along \(0\to1\to2\) gives a different chosen object than reindexing along the composite. This demonstrates why the theorem keeps the split indexed data and the independently chosen normal cleavage distinct.
