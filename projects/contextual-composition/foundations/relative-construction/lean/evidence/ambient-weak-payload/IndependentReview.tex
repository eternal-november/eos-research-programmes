\section{Objects and morphisms actually accepted by the code}
The manuscript specifies an ordinary base, a split indexed presentation, the contravariant total category at each base object, and two object formulas using directed legs. Its pseudomonad sentence is stronger than those formulas; the subsequent restriction to representables is stronger in a different direction. Fix the Lean size indices as in \code{AmbientBicategory.Obj}: the payloads are ordinary $B^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Cat}$ functors regarded as pseudofunctors. The homs are full induced hom categories: pseudonatural transformations as arrows and modifications, not only invertible modifications, as 2-arrows. Neither arbitrary weakly indexed \emph{objects}, a strictification theorem for them, nor a change of base is supplied here. The asserted strictness concerns the induced bicategory and its block actions, not pseudonaturality squares of its weak arrows.
   
For $S$ a split grading and $n\ge0$, \code{AmbientBicategory.action} constructs a mathlib \code{StrictPseudofunctor}, rather than a rule on objects supplied with a promise of functoriality. On a weak payload map $f$ one encodes $f$ by its cartesian functor over $B$, extends it over a grade block, conjugates by the block chart, and decodes. The two inverse identities are not merely on objects: restriction to a fibre identifies the component functors, and reading a cartesian lift identifies the moving-base comparison. On an arbitrary modification $t:f\Rightarrow g$, \code{coCell} inserts its possibly irreversible fibre component over a base identity. The naturality calculation uses both weak squares. Projected verticality allows the resulting transformation to descend to a modification; invertibility of $t$ is not an input. Faithful reading of the grade word plus terminal edge proves preservation of both whiskers. Identities and composition on maps and 2-cells supply the remaining fields. Extensionality on objects, maps \emph{and} 2-cells gives $K_0=1$ and $K_n;K_1=K_{n+1}$. These are equalities of bundled strict pseudofunctors, not definitional coincidences of displayed object lists.

For a native cartesian operation $r:i\to j$, cartesianness is a condition on the grade map, not invertibility of its endpoint leg. In \code{AmbientMateCalculus.factor} the terminal projection of the chosen comparison, followed by the cartesian tip, agrees up to equality transports with the image of the source tip. Equality on projected grades and cancellation against the cartesian tip establish compatibility with an arbitrary modification, identity and composite. This instantiates, rather than assumes, the three obligations in \code{AmbientPseudonaturality.assemble}. Substitution of native insertion and tensor yields genuine mathlib strong transformations $\eta:1\Rightarrow K$ and $\mu:K;K\Rightarrow K$. Identifying $K_2$ with $K;K$ changes types; treating this as unmarked definitional reduction would be wrong. \code{nativeMultiplication} identifies the tensor with the previously constructed strong multiplication, not just its object formula.

\section{What the native laws do and do not realize}
For a native cell between fixed operations, \code{AmbientNativeModification.iso} satisfies a square in the \emph{payload} arrow by a grade-and-cartesian-factor calculation. It is an invertible modification in the pseudofunctor hom category, not just an isomorphism for each chosen payload. \code{AmbientScheduleRealization.realize} vertically composes these modifications. For two native schedules on the same boundary, equality after this realization is equivalent to equality of the composite native grade arrows on every row. In the reverse direction take the constant $B$-valued observer, decorate each row by its outgoing endpoint, then project back to its grade. An arbitrary individual observer need not have a point above every row. The signed $BC_2$ probe has a coherent native triangle and a failing native pentagon; the same separation holds for the \emph{vertically realized} schedules. This is not an example of coherent native input breaking a horizontal pentagon.

At each $X$ the comparisons actually have boundaries $\eta_{KX};\mu_X\cong 1$, $K\eta_X;\mu_X\cong1$, and $\mu_{KX};\mu_X\cong K\mu_X;\mu_X$. Here the native comparison triple is input; matching its three component boundaries by total functor equalities is proved. These cells are not yet a triple of \emph{globally} natural modifications between the displayed composites. \code{AmbientHorizontalNaturality.Left}, \code{Right} and \code{Assoc} are propositions with genuine horizontally composed boundary squares. \code{viaNative} proves a reduction: transport a native natural square only after identifying the source and target comparison isomorphisms; it does not manufacture either identification. Weighted identity has a cast comparison for every weak map; insertion does not. Identification of serial mates for outer/inner insertions and contractions, and in the associativity case of both composite boundary mates, is still required.

Write $a=\mu_{K^2X},b=K\mu_{KX},c=K^2\mu_X,d=\mu_{KX}, e=K\mu_X,t=\mu_X$ in travel order. The horizontal pentagon has long route $adt\to bdt\to bet\to cet$ and short route $adt\to aet\to cdt\to cet$. The short middle step is the inverse of the multiplication square at $\mu_X$, right-whiskered by $t$. It is neither a cast nor a preselected canonical native cell by definition. The triangle compares the associator followed by $K$ applied to the left unit component, against the right unit component at $KX$, both on the actual shared source. The code retains structural isomorphisms suppressed by this notation. Neither horizontal pasting equality is proved generically in the frozen candidate. The typed generator criterion consumes two actual-path reading certificates and a native law; matching endpoints, arbitrary invertible fillers, and selecting the last seam after seeing the result are not certificates.

Before $t$, \code{AmbientDisjointFrames.disjointLeft} and \code{disjointRight} identify the \emph{whole encoded functors} $ae$ and $cd$ with the two native disjoint contractions. In particular these are not just endpoint-object equalities. The demanded arrow comparison is \code{AmbientExchangeDemand.Law}: on every decorated four-grade row the actual intervening isomorphism must equal the prescribed native exchange up to the indicated endpoint equality transports. Applying $t$ to the equation is a proved forward implication, not a faithful-reflection theorem. The signed central probe uses a different model from the moving-colour exchange probe.

The grade purity of a weak mate is indexed by the chart in which that mate was built. At multiplication-on-multiplication the payload itself is an iterated block; its inner grades become part of the output grade after flattening. Thus purity of the outer grade projection in \code{ContextadWeakWeight.weakGrade} never licenses a cast between the flattened two-grade objects. A forward route for the general identification is to carry the actual square through the flattening charts, identify the head and terminal-leg projections, and use the directed cartesian factorization of \code{ContextadDisjointCut.exchangeUnique} for the native, not an arbitrary, terminal comparison. Agreement of full endpoints is already available and should not be reproved as if it settled those projections.
\section{Independent discriminating controls}
\paragraph{Weak and directed are independent tests.} Over the chain of natural numbers take the one-object payload monoid $\mathbb N\times C_3$, with component functors equal to the identity. The moving gauge and flat gauge have different comparison on $0\to1$. Every modification from moving to flat changes component between those two objects; the explicit one has first coordinate one and is irreversible. The actual bundled $\eta$-naturality field accepts it. Reading insertion at its directed discharge gives terminal phase $+1$ for the inverse of the normalized mate and $-1$ for the forward map. The separate $BC_2$ fixture gives bit one whereas a cast gives zero; the non-cast property survives the transfer to the actual bundled $\eta$. In a different directed example \code{DirectedAll} shows a chosen noninvertible modification stays noninvertible under the induced action at \emph{every} block length, including zero, by evaluating on a populated row. These tests do not infer invertibility of a modification from that of a weak arrow's naturality squares.

\paragraph{An equality of types is not an identified square.} Generic elimination of the strict-functor equality in \code{BundledMaps} gives the object-cast normalization of components and heterogeneous equality of the actual forward comparison arrows for both transferred operations. For the target of unit naturality, \code{AmbientReview.identityTarget} in \code{UnitTargetMate.lean} identifies the real identity square with the exact bend of the weighted-identity mate, using the strict unitors. There is no corresponding proof here for the source serial mates. At the thin, moving-grade observer in \code{HorizontalAtThin}, the actual triangle and pentagon both hold, as do all three naturality predicates for every incoming weak arrow; parallel raw arrows into that observer are unique. Yet the actual left unitor changes the black grade over zero and hence is not a cast. This tests inhabitation and the reason for it, not generic coherence.

\paragraph{Exchange at four grades, before multiplication.} At the original moving-colour fixture the two encoded functors actually land at different objects (white versus black in their last grade). The inequality concerns the actual $4\to2$ frames, not merely a disagreement of native route names. Consequently the actual intervening component on that row cannot be an equality cast. Yet this thin observer satisfies the \emph{entire} prescribed exchange law: in each parallel hom the comparison is unique after transporting endpoints. After final multiplication, the two formerly distinct objects actually agree; no nonfaithfulness assertion about parallel 2-cells follows from this object calculation.

The stronger control changes only the payload to constant $BC_2$. \code{EncodeIso} evaluates a genuine ambient 2-cell with both chart functors and proves identity and composition, giving a hom functor. \code{AmbientReview.exchangeIff} expresses the open rowwise law equivalently as equality of its image of the real interchange natural isomorphism with the native natural isomorphism conjugated by the two fixed functor equalities. Under the doubled observer the unequal endpoint objects persist, but postcomposition with a natural central bit flip produces another \emph{natural isomorphism of those same raw functors}: its whole grade arrow agrees on every row and its terminal bit is flipped on every row. This construction applies to the actual candidate and to the prescribed native one. It refutes uniqueness from endpoint functors, grade arrows and invertibility, not equality of the two chosen candidates. No global naturality in the payload is asserted for the alternative.

The checked object/morphism controls separate these obligations instead of declaring them impossible.
