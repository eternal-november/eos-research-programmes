\section*{Independent review: sensitivity of actual recovery to a change of transport}
Scoped pass. For equivalences $P,Q$ and parallel transports $(H,J),(I,K)$ with forward comparison isomorphisms $a,b$, the compatibility hypothesis on transformations $h:H\to I$ and $j:J\to K$ identifies the two forward pastings, with inverse comparison components. At every object $y$, applying $Q^{-1}$ to $j_y$ and then the new recovery equals applying the old recovery and then $h_{P^{-1}y}$. Here inverse means the chosen inverse functor. Neither side transformation is assumed invertible. The proof expands the actual recovery component of PR 165, uses naturality at the inverse counit, substitutes forward compatibility, and uses inverse-unit naturality. This is not ordinary naturality of a fixed recovery at variable cocone objects: both parallel transports and their comparisons may vary. Two such changes paste because the intermediate comparison and its recovery agree exactly.

The endpoint specialization uses the original input and output choice isomorphisms and both lift equations. It does not require invertible lift arrows. The cocone choice components assemble naturally on all cocone maps by naturality of extension on the factor-choice arrow. In both concrete instances the forward premise is discharged by existing theorems, not newly imposed. For targets the parameter is any natural transformation of target functors, not only a natural isomorphism. Neither instance substitutes a different inverse equivalence or strictifies the localization comparison.
