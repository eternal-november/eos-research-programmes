\section*{Positive-length payload extension: scope and proof obligations}
Fix the existing split endpoint data on a possibly nondiscrete base. The positive native power of length $n+1$ has two proved leg functors. Over a specified source $A$ take the actual strict fibre of the source leg, and compose its inclusion with the destination leg. The Grothendieck total of an ordinary category-valued payload along this latter functor retains noninvertible terminal payload arrows.
\par The comparison of strict fibres uses surjectivity on objects of the earlier apex decoder; an arbitrary essentially surjective functor over the base would not justify this step. Lifting along the destination equality compares both weighted totals, strictly compatibly with ordinary payload change. The global weighted functor lies over the base and its square with the two fibre inclusions commutes strictly. It preserves and reflects the strongly cartesian factorization property, including test arrows over arbitrary further factors in the base. This does not choose lifts.
\par In the shared universes of the earlier iterate library the independent decorated fibre maps fully faithfully and surjectively on objects to the weighted chain fibre. Unpacking an arrow uses its identity-lift equation to rebase the square without dropping its final arrow. Identity and composition are those previously fixed in the independent model. Consequently the evaluation of a positive presented span power is pointwise equivalent to the positive iterate. Ordinary payload change gives a natural isomorphism of the two composite functors for each transformation; no separate coherence theorem for composites of these isomorphisms is claimed.
\par Not proved: a comparison for a chosen native split cleavage across all base maps as a presheaf morphism, the zero-length case or coproduct assembly, flattening, strict algebras or global recovery. The native apices themselves are the reviewed presentations of the sequential products, not literal equality with all choices of strict pullback.
