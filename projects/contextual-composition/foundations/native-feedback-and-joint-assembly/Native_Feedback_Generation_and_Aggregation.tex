\paragraph{Status and target.}
This draft characterizes which native finite-measure one-cells are generated by finite instantaneous feedback from the normalized source. It uses the literal raw witnesses and the complete global writer grade, not only the matrix interpretation. The written proof has passed independent review at this scope; no mechanized verification is claimed. The result is a semantic generation theorem at the declared writer scope, not a free-trace universal property, a polynomial-content extension, or completion of the general feedback contract.

\paragraph{Integrated claim.} Normalized finite feedback generates exactly the atom-bounded one-cells. The locally full bounded hom categories are dense in the full finite-measure hom categories, and every process has an explicit native aggregation coequalizer preserved by the existing operations. These are different categorical dimensions: density uses all bounded native cells, not only cells proved generated by normalized feedback. Consequently the result is a precise generation-and-reconstruction theorem, not a free compact completion theorem.

\section{Pinned source and exact interface}
Use the independent presentations and finite-endpoint native writer bicategory of \emph{Finite-measure native feedback with retained writer grades}, repository path
\path{projects/contextual-composition/foundations/finite-measure-native-feedback/Finite_Measure_Native_Feedback.tex}, blob
\texttt{51517c496be9ed6a31c1faa96ae82a95fbe98171}.
The source has finite endpoint sets, but arbitrary raw witness sets with finitely supported nonnegative weights. Context and writer coefficient categories are symmetric monoidal. At input $a$, a one-cell $f:A\to B$ has raw set $W_a$, weight $p_a$, actual context coefficients $C_{a,w}$, one global writer grade $Q=(O,(D_o)_{o\in O})$, and body
\[
 W_a\longrightarrow B\times O,\qquad w\longmapsto (b_a(w),o_a(w)).
\]
Every label of $O$, including every unused label, is retained. A native cell has actual witness and coefficient maps and a single actual global writer map; effects are preserved by exact pushforward. Denote this bicategory by $\HM$ and its normalized sub-bicategory by $\HN$.

Horizontal composition retains witness pairs, with individual weight the product of the two individual weights. Independent tensor does the same on independent coordinates. For $F:A\times U\to B\times U$, finite-wire feedback retains exactly the raw pairs $(u,w)$ whose output loop value equals $u$; their weights and context coefficients are unchanged, and the entire writer grade of $F$ is unchanged. These are the operations of the pinned source. In particular feedback does not merge witnesses having equal external output.

Call a one-cell \emph{atom-bounded} if $p_a(w)\leq 1$ for every individual raw witness. This concerns individual weights, not total mass. Let $\Ha$ be the sub-bicategory with all endpoint objects, the atom-bounded one-cells, and every native cell between those one-cells. The full choice of cells is declared; it is not inferred from one-cell generation.

\section{The invariant and the exact positive converse}
\begin{lemma}[Atomic invariant]
Normalized one-cells are atom-bounded. Atom-bounded one-cells are closed under horizontal composition, independent tensor, finite-wire feedback and native isomorphism. The compact cups and caps lie in this class. Hence $\Ha$ inherits the compact structure of the pinned source.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An individual nonnegative summand of a normalized measure is at most one. The individual weights of composite or tensor witnesses are products of numbers in $[0,1]$. Feedback only restricts and reindexes individual witnesses. Cups and compatible cap witnesses have weight one; incompatible cap carriers are empty. A native isomorphism has a bijective raw map with a native inverse, so its pushforward equation preserves the weight of each individual witness. The structural native isomorphisms and feedback laws already proved in the pinned source therefore restrict to this class. No statement of closure under arbitrary outgoing native cells is made.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact one-cell feedback generation]\label{thm:generation}
For a one-cell $f$ of $\HM$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f$ is atom-bounded.
\item There are a finite loop set $U$ and a normalized native one-cell $F:A\times U\to B\times U$ such that $\operatorname{Tr}_U(F)$ is natively isomorphic to $f$.
\item Up to native isomorphism, $f$ is generated from normalized one-cells by horizontal composition, independent tensor and finite-wire feedback.
\end{enumerate}
The construction in (ii) leaves the global writer grade exactly equal to $Q$, retains all raw zero-weight witnesses, and recovers every context coefficient of $f$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The atomic invariant proves (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i), and (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii) is immediate. Suppose (i).

First suppose every $W_a$ is empty. Take $U=\varnothing$ and the vacuously normalized process $F:A\times\varnothing\to B\times\varnothing$ with global grade $Q$. There are no input rows and no raw or body data to supply. Its feedback has empty raw carrier at every $a$, and still has exactly $Q$. This includes empty endpoint or writer-label cases; if $B\times O$ is empty, the supplied body of $f$ forces every $W_a$ to be empty.

Otherwise $B\times O$ is nonempty; choose $(b_0,o_0)\in B\times O$. For each $a$, let $S_a=\{w:p_a(w)>0\}$. Each $S_a$ is finite. Since $A$ is finite, choose a finite set $U$ with at least two elements and with $|U|\geq |S_a|$ for every $a$. Choose injections $j_a:S_a\to U$, a distinguished $u_0\in U$, and a fixed-point-free permutation $s:U\to U$. Extend $j_a$ to $\ell_a:W_a\to U$ by assigning every zero-weight witness to $u_0$.

At input $(a,u)$ define the raw carrier of $F$ as the disjoint union
\[
 V_{a,u}=\{w\in W_a:\ell_a(w)=u\}\amalg\{d_{a,u}\}.
\]
The desired witness $w$ has weight $p_a(w)$, coefficient $C_{a,w}$, and body $((b_a(w),u),o_a(w))$. There is at most one positive desired witness in this row. Consequently
\[
 m_{a,u}=\sum_{\ell_a(w)=u}p_a(w)\leq 1.
\]
Give the dummy $d_{a,u}$ weight $1-m_{a,u}$, unit context coefficient, and body $((b_0,s(u)),o_0)$. Use exactly $Q$ as the global writer grade; its label $o_0$ and coefficient $D_{o_0}$ already exist, so no extra writer labels or coefficient objects are added. Every row has total weight one and finite support, even when its raw carrier contains infinitely many zero-weight witnesses. Thus $F$ is a legitimate normalized native process.

On feedback every desired witness survives because its output loop value is $u$, while every dummy is removed because $s(u)\ne u$. The raw bijection
\[
 (u,w)\longmapsto w
\]
from the closed carrier to $W_a$ preserves individual weights and the full external body. Equip it with identity context coefficient maps and the identity map of the entire global grade $Q$. Its inverse sends $w$ to $(\ell_a(w),w)$ and has the same coefficient and grade identities. These are mutually inverse native cells. No zero-weight witness of $f$ has been removed and no unused writer label has been forgotten.
\end{proof}

The finite endpoint condition is used to choose one finite loop set accommodating all row supports. The theorem is not asserted for arbitrary input sets with unbounded support complexity. It also does not normalize the closed process: its total mass can exceed one, although each individual atom remains bounded by one.

\begin{proposition}[Strict native generation boundary]
The singleton raw process of weight $r>1$ is not generated from normalized processes by these operations up to native isomorphism. Therefore $\HM$ is strictly larger than the generated one-cell class, even though its weighted-matrix interpretation may identify such a process with a feedback-generated one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The singleton violates the atomic invariant. For example, the closed two-point identity has two raw witnesses of weight one and behavioral total mass two. It has a mass-preserving raw collapse onto a singleton of weight two, but no native inverse: an inverse would have to be an inverse of a two-to-one function. Thus behavioral equality does not supply native generation.
\end{proof}

\section{Native cells: a positive lifting statement and an exact excluded case}
\begin{proposition}[Compatible dilation of a nonempty-label cell]
Let $\alpha:f\Rightarrow g$ be a native cell between atom-bounded one-cells with the same finite endpoints $A,B$. Write its raw maps as $r_a:W_a\to W'_a$ and its writer map as $(v,\beta):Q\to Q'$. If $B\times O$ is nonempty, then $\alpha$, not merely its source and target, is the feedback of a normalized native cell on a common finite loop set, up to the native isomorphisms of Theorem~\ref{thm:generation}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose loop assignments for the target witnesses as in the theorem, and assign each source $w$ to the loop state assigned to $r_a(w)$. A target row contains at most one positive target witness. The exact pushforward equation yields
\[
 \sum_{\ell'_a(r_a(w))=u}p_a(w)
 =\sum_{\ell'_a(w')=u}p'_a(w')\leq1.
\]
Thus both source and target admit normalized dummy completion in each row with exactly the same missing mass. The source row may contain several positive desired witnesses; only the row total bound is required for this construction. Choose a source dummy label $(b_0,o_0)$ and a target dummy label $(b_0,v(o_0))$, and use the same fixed-point-free loop permutation. Extend $r_a$ by sending the source dummy to the target dummy. Use the original context coefficient maps on desired witnesses and the identity unit coefficient map on dummies; retain the full original writer map $(v,\beta)$. These data satisfy the exact row pushforward and full body equations. Feedback removes all dummies and retains exactly the original native cell, including its actual coefficient maps.
\end{proof}

This proposition is not an assertion of simultaneous dilation for every arbitrary diagram of cells, nor an extension to unrestricted changing endpoints. Its one-cell source need not have nonempty raw carriers; only an existing body label is needed to normalize the dummy rows.

\begin{proposition}[Empty-label obstruction to common normalized dilation]
There are native cells between atom-bounded one-cells which are not a single fixed-loop trace of a normalized native cell when source and target global writer grades are required to remain unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $A=B=1$ and terminal context and writer coefficient categories. Let $f$ have empty raw carrier and global label set $O=\varnothing$. Let $g$ have a singleton raw carrier of weight zero and singleton global label set $O'=1$, with its unique body. The empty raw function and the unique global label function $\varnothing\to1$ form a native cell $f\Rightarrow g$: both pushforward measures are zero and the body equation is vacuous.

A normalized dilation of $f$ on a nonempty loop $U$ retaining $O=\varnothing$ cannot exist. Each input row would need a normalized measure but its body would land in $(B\times U)\times\varnothing=\varnothing$, forcing the raw carrier to be empty. If $U=\varnothing$, the feedback of any dilation of $g$ has empty raw carrier, so cannot be natively isomorphic to the singleton raw carrier of $g$, even though that singleton has weight zero. This proves the claimed impossibility.
\end{proof}
The obstruction is specific to fixed-loop normalized dilation with unchanged full grades. It does not establish that the cell cannot be assembled from more general terms and additional cell constructors. In particular the one-cell theorem is not silently promoted to a presentation or freeness theorem for all native cells.

\section{Relationship to the general extension problem}
This theorem supplies a coefficient-independent numerical invariant within this finite-measure writer doctrine at the actual raw-witness interface and an exact converse for the stated native writer class. It distinguishes three operations: normalized feedback generation, which gives precisely atom-bounded one-cells; native mass-collecting quotients, which can increase individual atomic weights; and behavioral erasure, which forgets the distinction. A quotient or scalar-extension route to the whole finite-measure doctrine must declare that additional operation and prove its compatibility rather than attribute it to feedback alone.

The construction preserves all actual coefficients and global grades, but does not add general polynomial occurrence content or infer shared-effect compatibility. The finite product-wire feedback here is instantaneous matching, not delayed execution or a sum-interface iteration operator. Recurrent semantics, native shared interaction, universal target maps and the full v0.2 structural completion remain separate obligations.
\paragraph{Size convention for reconstruction.} All raw sets, coefficient categories and writer families are taken small in a fixed universe, with hom sets and presheaf constructions formed in a larger universe. Bounded-probe indexing diagrams are small at the latter level.

\section{Explicit native coequalizers}
A copy refinement $e:S\to X$ has the same global writer family, a surjection
$e_a:S_a\to W_a$, coefficient $C_{a,e_a(s)}$ at $s$, identity coefficient
and writer maps, body pulled back from $X$, and weights $q_a$ with
$\sum_{e_a(s)=w}q_a(s)=p_a(w)$. The following construction uses finite
nonempty fibres, though the coequalizer argument itself only needs
surjectivity and the displayed finite-support weight equations.

Put $R_a=S_a\times_{W_a}S_a$, with coefficient $C_{a,w}$ at $(s,t)$,
the same writer family, the body of $w=e(s)=e(t)$, and weights
\[
 r_a(s,t)=\begin{cases}q_a(s)q_a(t)/p_a(w)&p_a(w)>0,\\0&p_a(w)=0.\end{cases}
\]
Both projections $d_1,d_2:R\rightrightarrows S$ are native, with identity
coefficient and writer maps. Their row weights have finite support.
The raw set is the set-theoretic kernel pair. It is \emph{not} asserted to
be the categorical kernel pair: different couplings may prevent that
universal property.

\begin{lemma}[Native aggregation coequalizer]
The cell $e$ is the coequalizer of $d_1,d_2$ in the full hom category
$\Hh(A,B)$, including actual context arrows and all global writer data.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The marginal weight equation is immediate when $p_a(w)>0$; when it is zero,
all $q_a(s)$ in that fibre vanish by nonnegativity. Let $k:S\to Y$ equalize
$d_1,d_2$. Its raw map is constant on every fibre of $e$. Its actual context
arrows are also equal on that fibre, since the two relation arrows have
identical source coefficient and identity coefficient maps. Thus both
descend uniquely to $X$. Retain the full writer map of $k$ unchanged.
The descended body square holds by surjectivity. Its weight equation is
\[
 \sum_{w:h(w)=y}p_a(w)
 =\sum_{s:k(s)=y}q_a(s)=p^Y_a(y).
\]
These data give the unique native $h:X\to Y$ with $he=k$.
This includes empty rows and all unused global writer labels; no writer map
is inferred from its action only on labels reached by the body.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded coequalizer presentations]
Every $X\in\Hh(A,B)$ has such a coequalizer presentation
$R\rightrightarrows S\to X$ with $R,S\in\Bb(A,B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every $w$ put $n_w=\max(1,\lceil p_a(w)\rceil)$ and replace it by
$n_w$ copies of weight $p_a(w)/n_w$. A zero-weight raw witness has one copy,
not zero copies. This produces $S$ of finite support with atoms at most one.
For the relation above, $q_a(t)\leq p_a(w)$, hence
$r_a(s,t)\leq q_a(s)\leq1$ whenever the denominator is positive.
The zero case is already bounded. Apply the preceding lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Compatibility with the existing operations}
These coequalizers are stable under either horizontal whiskering, independent
tensor with a fixed process, and fixed-wire feedback. The claim is preservation
of the displayed coequalizer, not that its image is the particular
conditional-independent relation that would be reconstructed from scratch.

For right whiskering, the new quotient sends $(s,z)$ to $(e(s),z)$.
The output of $s$ determining the continuation fibre is constant on each
$e$-fibre, so this formula is well typed. The whiskered relation connects
$(s,z)$ and $(t,z)$ for every pair $e(s)=e(t)$. Consequently equality on
that relation is precisely constancy of the raw map and actual coefficient
arrow on each quotient fibre. Its common coefficient is
$C_{a,e(s)}\otimes E_{b(e(s)),z}$. The same descent proof applies.
Left whiskering uses $(z,s)\mapsto(z,e(s))$ and its corresponding common
coefficient. Tensor uses the identical argument with an independent second
input. In all cases the full new global writer family and its entire map
are retained.

For feedback, the quotient has fixed input loop coordinate $u$ and the body
square preserves its output loop coordinate. Therefore a fibre is either
entirely loop-consistent or entirely loop-inconsistent. Restricting the
quotient and relation to consistent witnesses is still a surjection and
still connects every pair in a retained fibre. The coequalizer proof applies
without change. This does not cover arbitrary changes of the loop interface.
Taking products of two copy refinements also gives a copy refinement and
connects all pairs in the joint fibre. Thus using two presentations does not
silently require one process to remain fixed.

\section{A stronger reconstruction theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Hom-category density]
In fixed universes, the inclusion $\Bb(A,B)\hookrightarrow\Hh(A,B)$ is
dense: the restricted nerve
\[
 X\longmapsto\Hh(-,X)|_{\Bb(A,B)}
\]
is fully faithful. In particular bounded probes together with their actual
native arrows determine all native maps between arbitrary finite-measure
processes. This is not density after forgetting coefficients or writer data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the bounded presentation $e:S\to X$ above. A natural transformation
$\tau:\Hh(-,X)|_{\Bb}\to\Hh(-,Y)|_{\Bb}$ sends $e$ to a map $k:S\to Y$.
Naturality for $d_1,d_2$ says $kd_1=kd_2$, so the native coequalizer provides
a unique $h:X\to Y$ with $he=k$.

For arbitrary $u:Z\to X$ with $Z\in\Bb$, construct a bounded $L$ whose raw
points are pairs $(z,s)$ with $u(z)=e(s)=w$. Its coefficient is the coefficient
of $z$, global writer family is that of $Z$, and body is that of $z$.
Give the pair weight
\[
 \ell(z,s)=\begin{cases}p_Z(z)q_S(s)/p_X(w)&p_X(w)>0,\\0&p_X(w)=0.\end{cases}
\]
Because $q_S(s)\leq p_X(w)$, each atom is at most $p_Z(z)\leq1$.
The projection $c:L\to Z$ is a surjective copy refinement (including all
zero-weight pairs). There is a native map $l:L\to S$ using the context
arrow and global writer map of $u$. Its weight equation follows by summing
over $u(z)=w$, and $uc=el$ as full native cells. The copy refinement $c$ is
epimorphic by the coequalizer lemma. Naturality gives
\[
 \tau_Z(u)c=\tau_L(uc)=\tau_L(el)=\tau_S(e)l=hel=huc.
\]
Cancellation of $c$ proves $\tau_Z(u)=hu$. Thus $\tau$ is induced by $h$,
uniquely. Conversely postcomposition by any native $h$ is natural.
\end{proof}
The ordinary canonical density-colimit formulation is equivalent: $X$ is
the colimit of all bounded processes mapping natively to it. The argument
does not require an existing native pullback of $u$ and $e$; the chosen
conditional coupling above merely supplies a surjective refinement square.

\section{Relative uniqueness of aggregation-preserving extensions}
Fix a hom category $\Hh=\Hh(A,B)$, its bounded full subcategory $\Bb$,
and a locally small target category $E$, in the declared universes.
Let $\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{cp}}(\Hh,E)$ be the full subcategory of
$\operatorname{Fun}(\Hh,E)$ consisting of functors that preserve every
finite-fibre copy-refinement coequalizer constructed above. This condition
requires that the image cocone be a coequalizer in $E$; it does not require
$E$ to have all coequalizers. It includes copy refinements other than the
canonical ceiling-based splittings. Natural transformations are unrestricted.

\begin{theorem}[Relative extension uniqueness]
Restriction along $\Bb\hookrightarrow\Hh$ gives a fully faithful functor
\[
 \operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{cp}}(\Hh,E)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Fun}(\Bb,E).
\]
In particular, a natural transformation between two such extensions is
determined by, and always extends from, its bounded components. A natural
isomorphism on bounded objects extends uniquely to a natural isomorphism.
This is a uniqueness and transformation-extension theorem, not existence
of an extension for an arbitrary functor $\Bb\to E$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $F,G$ preserve the specified coequalizers, and let
$\alpha:F|_{\Bb}\Rightarrow G|_{\Bb}$. For each $X$ choose a bounded
presentation $R_X\rightrightarrows S_X\xrightarrow{e_X}X$.
The arrow $G(e_X)\alpha_{S_X}$ equalizes $F(d_1),F(d_2)$, by naturality of
$\alpha$ on the bounded relation object and by $e_Xd_1=e_Xd_2$.
Since $F(e_X)$ is a coequalizer, there is a unique arrow
$\alpha_X:F(X)\to G(X)$ such that
\[
 \alpha_XF(e_X)=G(e_X)\alpha_{S_X}. \tag{*}
\]
For bounded $X$ choose its identity copy refinement; its raw relation has
one point above each point, with the original weight and identity maps.
Thus this definition agrees with the supplied component $\alpha_X$.

Suppose $u:Z\to X$ with $Z$ bounded. The bounded refined-lift construction
from the density proof gives $L\in\Bb$, a finite-fibre surjective copy
refinement $c:L\to Z$, and $l:L\to S_X$ satisfying $uc=e_Xl$ as full
native cells. The relation object for $c$ is bounded: its weight at a
relation pair is at most the corresponding source weight in $L$.
The functor $F$ preserves this copy-refinement coequalizer, so $F(c)$
is epimorphic. Using (*) and naturality between bounded objects gives
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_XF(u)F(c)
 &=\alpha_XF(e_X)F(l)
  =G(e_X)\alpha_{S_X}F(l)\\
 &=G(e_X)G(l)\alpha_L
  =G(u)G(c)\alpha_L
  =G(u)\alpha_ZF(c).
\end{align*}
Cancellation proves naturality for every arrow with bounded source.

For arbitrary $h:X\to Y$, precompose the desired naturality equation
with the epimorphism $F(e_X)$. Naturality for $he_X:S_X\to Y$ and (*)
give
\[
 \alpha_YF(h)F(e_X)
 =G(he_X)\alpha_{S_X}
 =G(h)\alpha_XF(e_X).
\]
Hence the components form a natural transformation. Any extension of the
original $\alpha$ must satisfy (*) by naturality, proving uniqueness and
independence of the chosen presentations. This also proves preservation of
identities and composition by the extension procedure. Applying it to the
inverse of a bounded natural isomorphism gives the inverse everywhere.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Application to feedback and assembly.}
Fixed finite-wire trace, either horizontal whiskering, and tensor with a
fixed process preserve the specified copy-refinement coequalizers by the
preceding fibre-saturation arguments. Thus, among functors preserving those
coequalizers, each is determined up to unique extension of a specified
natural isomorphism by its action on bounded objects. In particular, the
finite-measure trace is not an arbitrary independent operation on the
large-atom part: its bounded restriction together with aggregation
preservation determines it at this functorial strength.

This statement does not assert that bounded objects are projective over
copy refinements. The proof uses a further bounded refinement of a proposed
lift, not a direct lift. It also does not make the restriction functor
essentially surjective, provide a free compact bicategory, or identify all
feedback notions. Coherence for varying endpoints, higher comparison cells,
joint-effect admission and recurrence must be proved at their own interfaces.

\subsection{Finite-arity reconstruction and equation detection}
\begin{theorem}[Finite-product corollary]
For $1\leq i\leq n$, let $\Bb_i\hookrightarrow\Hh_i$ be bounded inclusions
of the hom categories considered above. Let
$\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{sep.cp}}(\prod_i\Hh_i,E)$ be the full
subcategory of functors preserving every specified finite-fibre
copy-refinement coequalizer in each variable separately, with arbitrary
objects fixed in the other variables. Restriction induces a fully faithful
functor
\[
 \operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{sep.cp}}(\prod_i\Hh_i,E)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Fun}(\prod_i\Bb_i,E).
\]
Thus natural transformations extend uniquely from bounded tuples, natural
isomorphisms extend as isomorphisms, and equations between natural
transformations hold everywhere if they hold on bounded tuples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $n=0$ restriction is the identity, and for $n=1$ this is the preceding
theorem. For positive finite $n$, start with a natural transformation on
$\prod_i\Bb_i$. Extend it in the first variable while fixing a bounded
tuple of the remaining variables. The single-variable theorem supplies
unique components natural in that variable. They are also natural in an
arrow of any remaining bounded variable: the two sides of the required
naturality square are natural transformations between functors of the first
variable that preserve the specified coequalizers, and their restrictions
to $\Bb_1$ agree by the original naturality. Single-variable faithfulness
therefore equates the two sides.

Repeat this argument for the second variable, allowing arbitrary first
variables and keeping the later variables bounded. Naturality in each
already extended variable follows in exactly the same way: its naturality
square has two sides that are transformations of the variable currently
being extended, and equality on its bounded restriction forces equality
everywhere. After $n$ steps all variables are arbitrary and the resulting
components form a natural transformation. At every step uniqueness follows
from the single-variable theorem, so restriction is full and faithful.
An inverse bounded natural transformation extends, and faithfulness forces
its composites to be the identity transformations. Finally faithfulness
reflects equality of transformations, proving equation detection.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact coherence consequence.}
For finite-arity expressions whose associated functors have been checked
to preserve copy-refinement coequalizers separately in every independent
variable, their natural comparison maps and equations are reconstructed
from bounded tuples. This is a criterion for extending a specified
comparison or checking a specified equation, not an automatic assertion
that every expression has the required preservation. In particular,
repeating a variable, inserting a shared-choice operation, or passing to
higher-dimensional structure requires a separate preservation and typing
check; none is licensed merely by the finite-product corollary.

\section{Boundary of the characterization}
This gives a feedback-stable aggregation presentation and exact hom-category
reconstruction, not a claim that every cell of $\Bb$ is feedback-generated from normalized cells. The first part characterizes precisely its one-cells. The density theorem here uses the locally full bounded subcategory, including cells not proved to be generated by normalized feedback. Nor does it identify $\Hh$ as a free compact, regular or exact
completion. In particular, inverting copy refinements would identify old
normalized raw presentations, such as a fair two-point witness and its
one-point erasure, violating the retained-witness interface. The operation is
adjoining or using directed coequalizer cocones, not inversion.

For a cocomplete target category $E$, ordinary left Kan extension provides a
canonical extension of $F:\Bb\to E$ by the bounded-probe colimits, and its
restriction is $F$. Additional hypotheses are required for this extension to
preserve the selected coequalizers or the bicategorical feedback/assembly
operations. The density theorem alone does not supply those hypotheses.
Shared-effect joint admission, arbitrary polynomial content and recurrent
semantics remain separate obligations. No priority or mechanized-verification
claim is made.
