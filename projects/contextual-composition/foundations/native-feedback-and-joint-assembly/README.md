# Native feedback generation and joint assembly

[Native feedback generation and aggregation](Native_Feedback_Generation_and_Aggregation.tex) characterizes the finite-measure writer processes generated by normalized finite-wire feedback: every individual retained raw witness has weight at most one. The bound is not a bound on total mass. Every finite-measure process has an explicit bounded aggregation coequalizer, and the bounded hom categories are dense. Functors preserving finite copy-refinement coequalizers, their transformations and separately preserving finite-arity equations are determined by bounded data. One-cell generation does not imply generation of all native cells, and the result is not a free compact or exact completion.

[The joint-coupling span bridge](Joint_Coupling_Span_Bridge_With_Global_Writer.tex) gives a locally faithful oplax bridge from normalized augmented native processes to all-coupling spans, retaining full global writer grades and changing pure endpoints. Conditional bind selects a Markov component; the target also admits other couplings. Its feedback requires a complete law supported on pathwise loop equality. This differs from unnormalized finite-measure feedback: the same native process can have normalized dilations with empty and nonempty span feedback. No extension agreeing with the normalized bridge can preserve the two feedback operations invertibly.

These results relate specified feedback and interaction doctrines without identifying them. Arbitrary original polynomial content, occurrence substitution with shared interference, recurrent semantics and the general [v0.2 target](../../contracts/Native_Feedback_and_Interaction_Extension_Contract_v0_2.tex) remain open.

The written arguments were independently reviewed. The standard-library checks can be run with `python check_bounded_refinement.py` and `python check_joint_coupling_span.py`; they use exact rational arithmetic for finite diagnostics, not proof-assistant verification. No TeX compilation, Lean certification or novelty claim is asserted.

The constructions build on [finite-measure native feedback](../finite-measure-native-feedback/README.md), [native multilimits](../native-multilimits/README.md), and the [joint-limit boundary](../native-coefficient-envelopes/README.md).
