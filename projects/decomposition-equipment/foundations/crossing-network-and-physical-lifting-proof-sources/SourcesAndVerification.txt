The Python check files and accompanying JSON results supply executable examples
and bounded cross-checks. Both the development checks and independently written
checks are included. Verification.txt identifies the packaged runs and their
outcomes; it is the place to consult for exact verification metadata rather
than inferring coverage from a filename or a passing summary.

Running the checks

Extract the entire archive, keep its directory structure, and run this command
from the extracted package root:

    python3 checkrunner.py

The runner uses Python 3 and its standard library. The included JSON files
record the packaged results; a fresh run checks the delivered programs in the
reader's environment. Consult Verification.txt for the runner's output and
comparison conventions. These are finite checks, not a general-purpose proof
assistant or an implementation of unrestricted network synthesis.

What verification does and does not show

The checks exercise quotient and coverage conditions, bounded direct-map and
partial-operation comparisons, witness symmetries, interface transport, actual
crossing equations, compiled record identifications, and selected synthesis
obstructions and repairs. Exhaustive enumeration inside a bounded instance is
not exhaustive coverage of all finite instances. Independent AI proof review
is distinct from independently written programs, and neither is human peer
review or formal proof-assistant verification. No test totals or universal
correctness claim should be inferred beyond the recorded run descriptions.

Provenance and integrity

SourceProvenance.json identifies the source snapshots and delivered copies.
Where their hashes differ, the recorded delivery changes are editorial only:
they do not constitute additional mathematical results or computational tests.
SHA256SUMS records delivered-file hashes for integrity checking. The
reviewed_source_sha256 field marks a source snapshot recorded in the independent
review manifest; delivered-file hashes identify the actual
packaged bytes. These serve different purposes and need not be equal after
editorial cleanup. Neither kind of hash certifies mathematical correctness.

The notes acknowledge standard mathematical baselines rather than claiming
priority for their general mechanisms. The equipment note cites Shulman's
Framed bicategories and monoidal fibrations for the companion/conjoint
framework. The plain-language summary lists further primary-source comparisons.
Those comparisons provide context, not a comprehensive novelty certification.
