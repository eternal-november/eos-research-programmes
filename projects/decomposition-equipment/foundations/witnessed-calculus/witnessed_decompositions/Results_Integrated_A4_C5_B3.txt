## 2. The independent generators-and-relations construction

### 2.1 The generating graph

Objects are all raw finite S-trees with output W.

A **graft edge** inserts one primitive corolla at a specified external leaf,
with matching output component. A nullary insertion creates no descendants at
that leaf. A **positive equation edge** e:T→U consists of a rule name in (1.1),
a well-typed one-hole context, and the ordered metavariable substitution that
realizes that contextual rewrite. Add a formal inverse e⁻¹:U→T. Matching is
encoded by this actual syntactic data, not by interchangeable proof objects;
identical rule/address/substitution data denote the same edge.

Take finite composable edge words. Quotient by the least category congruence
containing the relations below: this is the ordinary algebraic construction
of a category presentation, with objects unchanged. It exists as a quotient
of a set of finite words. Nothing in its definition mentions D, evaluation,
semantic tuples, or equality of images in a target category.

### 2.2 Raw relations and abbreviation R(T,F)

Impose the inverse relations only for equation edges, not for graft edges.
Impose B1's raw history relations: identities and interchange of adjacent
incomparable inserted vertices. B1 then gives a well-defined abbreviation

    R(T,F):T→T[F]

for any raw forest, independent of its legal elementary execution order.
Conversely these relations identify exactly histories for the same raw forest.
In particular, writing F⋆H for entrywise substitution after splitting H at the
descendant occurrences of F,

    R(T,id)=id_T,
    R(T,F);R(T[F],H)=R(T,F⋆H).                           (2.1)

These are consequences of the known raw category presentation. They are not
relations that compare forests by their eventual semantic values.

### 2.3 Ordinary and mixed relations

The ordinary relations O are the 18 local peak equations in §3, all their
well-typed context/substitution instances, the disjoint-redex squares, and the
variable-overlap squares. A variable-overlap square transports the inner
rewrite to the unique retained occurrence of that variable. Each variable
occurs once on each side, so this involves neither duplication nor deletion.

For each contextual positive equation e:T→U and each elementary graft A at a
leaf of their common ordered frontier, impose the mixed relation

    e;R(U,A)=R(T,A);e[A].                                (MOut)

Here e[A]:T[A]→U[A] is the *same old equation occurrence*, with its variable
subterms and outer-context leaves grafted as prescribed. This edge exists
syntactically because the rule is occurrence-linear and frontier-preserving.
The equation is not an assumed naturality of a semantic comparison.

For each **bare identity-leaf template** l→r in (1.1), each ambient tree T,
and each matching external leaf i of T, impose

    R(T,i:l);T[e]_i=R(T,i:r).                            (MIn)

All other entries of these two forests are identity leaves. R(T,i:l) and
R(T,i:r) are legal multi-step raw histories of the two template trees. The
middle T[e]_i is the one equation edge in the newly inserted template. There
are seven template families with finite labels for fixed N,K,M, in arbitrary
ambient T and i. In particular, MIn is NOT initially declared for arbitrary
semantically equal forests. Its contextual and substituted extension is proved
in §4.2.

Denote the resulting presented category by C=Comp(S,E;W).

### 3.2 Coherent normalization theorem

Choose a deterministic reduction strategy, for example the first applicable
rule in a fixed preorder of addresses and rule names. Let

    ν_t:t⇒t↓

be its finite positive reduction path; for an irreducible t this is the empty
path. Termination proves finiteness, and inherited confluence identifies its
endpoint with t↓.

**Lemma 3.1.** Modulo O, every two positive paths from t to t↓ are equal.

**Proof.** Use well-founded induction on the contextual termination measure
of t. If t is irreducible both paths are empty. Otherwise compare first edges
e:t→a and f:t→b. If they are identical, the tails are equal by induction at a.
If different, §3.1 supplies an O square

    e;p=f;q,                  p:a⇒v, q:b⇒v.

Normalize v. The paths p;ν_v and the original tail from a both end at t↓:
all terms are reducts of t and have the same normal form. Their source a is
strictly smaller than t, so induction identifies them. The analogous claim
holds at b. Thus the first full path equals e;p;ν_v=f;q;ν_v, equal to the
second full path. The induction applies even when p or q is empty. No assertion
that arbitrary paths are already equal is used. ∎

Consequently, for every positive equation edge e:t→u,

    ν_t=e;ν_u.                                             (3.1)

Both sides are positive normalization paths. Since equation edges are
invertible, the inverse version follows by multiplying (3.1) by e⁻¹.
For an arbitrary pure equation zigzag z:t→u, repeated substitution of (3.1)
and cancellation gives

    z=ν_t;(ν_u)⁻¹.                                         (3.2)

The right side is defined exactly when t↓=u↓. Conversely, a pure equation
zigzag implies pi(t)=pi(u), and therefore equality of normal forms by (1.2).

**Theorem 3.2 (ordinary path coherence).** In the pure equation subcategory,
there is a morphism t→u exactly when pi(t)=pi(u), and any two such morphisms
are equal using O and inverse relations. Its canonical morphism is

    κ(t,u):=ν_t;(ν_u)⁻¹.                                  (3.3)

It satisfies κ(t,t)=id and κ(t,u);κ(u,v)=κ(t,v). This is proved coherence,
not an axiom identifying all parallel equation paths and not an inference
from object confluence alone.

## 4. Deriving the full mixed compatibility

### 4.1 Outer equation paths whiskered by arbitrary forests

For an elementary graft A, MOut is a defining relation. For a forest F,
choose a B1 elementary execution A_1,…,A_s. After an equation e:T→U,
the same execution is possible at the common ordered occurrences on both
sides: old external positions agree, and each insertion creates the same new
ordered typed positions. Apply MOut successively. Raw relation (2.1) then gives

    e;R(U,F)=R(T,F);e[F].                                (4.1)

If a graft position lies outside the context's rewrite subtree, e[F] is the
same disjoint old occurrence. If it lies inside a metavariable subtree, that
subtree has simply received its descendants. There is no frontier position
inside an actual nullary u; a nullary graft instead removes its chosen external
position on both sides. Thus the induction includes all nullary cases without
inventing an occurrence.

Changing the elementary execution of F changes neither raw arrow by B1; the
equation paths e[F] agree by §3.2 (indeed here the contextual edge data give
the same grafted rule occurrence). Taking inverses in the equation direction
derives (4.1) for e⁻¹. Concatenation proves, for any pure equation path z:T→U,

    z;R(U,F)=R(T,F);z[F].                                (4.2)

The endpoints of z[F] are T[F],U[F]. This construction uses the canonical
shared occurrence order, not a choice matching equal component labels.

### 4.2 Inner equation instances in arbitrary forest entries

**Lemma 4.1.** Suppose F,F' differ by one contextual equation step in one
original entry i. Let z:T[F]→T[F'] be the corresponding equation edge in the
whole tree. Then

    R(T,F);z=R(T,F').                                    (4.3)

**Proof.** Write the changed entry as C[l[σ]]→C[r[σ]], where C is a
one-hole raw context and l→r is a bare declaration template. Cut out that
redex occurrence, leaving a single identity leaf of its output component.
Let F^0 be the forest whose i-th entry is C[x] and whose other entries are
unchanged from F. This is a genuine raw forest. Put B=T[F^0], and let j be
the distinguished descendant leaf at that hole. No other leaf is identified
with j, even if its component is equal.

Graft bare l at j, then graft σ into its template variable leaves, using
identity entries at all other leaves. Call this latter forest H. On the r
side use the corresponding forest H'; l and r have exactly the same ordered
template frontier, so H and H' are the same specified tuple in that common
frontier, with identity entries outside the hole. B1 composition gives

    R(T,F)=R(T,F^0);R(B,j:l);R(B[l]_j,H),
    R(T,F')=R(T,F^0);R(B,j:r);R(B[r]_j,H').

MOut for the bare equation inside B and the arbitrary forest H, already
proved in §4.1, reads

    B[e]_j;R(B[r]_j,H')=R(B[l]_j,H);z.

Insert this equality into the first factorization. Then bare MIn at B,j gives

    R(T,F);z
      =R(T,F^0);R(B,j:l);B[e]_j;R(B[r]_j,H')
      =R(T,F^0);R(B,j:r);R(B[r]_j,H')
      =R(T,F').

This proves the contextual and arbitrary-substitution case from the seven
bare-template families. It did not posit equality of semantically equal
forests. It remains valid for σ with nullary entries: the corresponding
external positions disappear on both sides, and no rule variable is duplicated
or removed as a syntactic occurrence. ∎

An inverse forest-entry equation satisfies the reverse equality by invertibility.
Applying Lemma 4.1 successively proves (4.3) for a path in one entry. Applying
it at the finitely many original entries, in their fixed order, proves:

**Corollary 4.2.** If F_i and F'_i are pointwise E-equivalent, then there is a
pure equation path z:T[F]→T[F'] with

    R(T,F);z=R(T,F').                                    (4.4)

Every such z is κ(T[F],T[F']) by Theorem 3.2. In particular the result is
independent of chosen entry paths and their interleaving. For an empty original
frontier the statement is the equality of empty forests and identity paths.

## 5. Independent comparison functors and exact commuting squares

Define P_Q on objects by T↦pi(T). On a graft edge inserting g at leaf i, its
arrow tuple has pi(g) at i and identity operations at all other original
positions. On a general raw arrow it is (pi(F_i)). On a positive or negative
equation edge it is the identity tuple, regarded between the equal quotient
operations pi(T)=pi(U).

These generator images have the required endpoints by strict preservation of
substitution by pi. We verify every relation before forming the induced functor:

* Raw identity/composition relations become the B2 identity and entrywise
  forest-substitution laws, with the same descendant occurrence split.
* Equation inverse relations become identity composed with identity.
* Every O relation has only equation edges and maps to the same identity
  tuple at its common source operation; source/target profiles agree.
* Both sides of MOut map to the full tuple (pi(F_i)), in the same original
  occurrence positions. An equation changes neither that profile nor the
  individual profiles of F_i.
* In MIn, the two tuples agree outside i and have entries pi(l),pi(r) at i.
  Those entries are equal by the declared A3 equation. Thus the complete
  tuples agree; this argument does not equate arbitrary tuples having a
  common total composite.

Therefore P_Q:C→Ref(Q,W) is a well-defined functor. Define

    P_D=Ref(Phi)∘P_Q:C→Ref(D,W).

Its raw forest action is (ev(F_i)); its equation action is the identity tuple.
Let J:Raw(S,W)→C be the identity object map and raw arrow inclusion. The two
comparison equations are, on both objects and actual arrows,

    P_Q∘J = Ref(pi)∘(Raw≅Ref(F(S))),
    P_D∘J = rawEvaluation(ev),
    P_D   = Ref(Phi)∘P_Q.                                (5.1)

For example, at F:T→T[F], both sides of the second equality are the original-
slot tuple (ev(F_i)), with the same entire entry profiles. Preservation of
composition on either route is exactly

    ev(F_i[H_(i,j)]) = ev(F_i)[ev(H_(i,j))].              (5.2)

This equality is the inherited strict-calculus law at the canonically split
pair (i,j), not merely equality of endpoint operations. The identity equation
is equally entrywise. Equality-transport proofs add no new tuple data.

## 6. Mixed path normal forms and complete hom equality

### 6.1 Moving all equations to the right

**Lemma 6.1.** Every finite mixed path p:T→U is equal in C to

    R(T,F);z,                z:T[F]→U pure equation.      (6.1)

**Proof.** Induct on the finite word p. Start with the identity forest and
empty z. Appending an equation edge concatenates it to z. If appending an
elementary graft A:U→U[A], use (4.2):

    R(T,F);z;R(U,A)
      =R(T,F);R(T[F],A);z[A]
      =R(T,F⋆A);z[A].

Here A is read on the common frontier of T[F] and U, and F⋆A splits A by
the actual descendants of the original entries. This produces the next
factorization. The induction covers inverse equation edges by (4.2). ∎

By §3, the final z in (6.1) is necessarily κ(T[F],U).

### 6.2 Canonical entrywise forest

Write F↓ for the forest with (F↓)_i=(F_i)↓. It has exactly the same separately
specified entry profiles as F. Corollary 4.2 and ordinary coherence give

    R(T,F);κ(T[F],U)
      =R(T,F↓);κ(T[F↓],U).                              (6.2)

For clarity, the path α:T[F]→T[F↓] supplied by Corollary 4.2 satisfies
R(T,F);α=R(T,F↓). Ordinary coherence yields
κ(T[F],U)=α;κ(T[F↓],U), which proves (6.2).

Thus for fixed raw endpoints T,U, a canonical arrow representative has shape

    A(T,U;F↓):=R(T,F↓);ν_(T[F↓]);(ν_U)⁻¹,              (6.3)

where the two middle normal-form objects are literally the same. The data
of this representative are an ordered family of normal terms, one at EACH
original source occurrence. The endpoint condition is pi(T[F↓])=pi(U).

### 6.3 The exact equality theorem

**Theorem 6.2.** Let p,q:T→U be arbitrary mixed paths. Compute their normal
families F↓,H↓ by §§6.1–6.2. Then the following are equivalent:

1. p=q in the independently presented category C;
2. F↓_i=H↓_i at every original occurrence i of T;
3. P_Q(p)=P_Q(q) as full semantic refinement arrows;
4. P_D(p)=P_D(q) as full semantic refinement arrows.

**Proof.** (2) makes the canonical representative (6.3) identical, proving
(1). A well-defined functor gives (1)⇒(3) and (1)⇒(4). If (3) holds, full
tuple equality says pi(F_i)=pi(H_i) at every original i, after the same
canonical source-profile transport. Equation (1.2) gives F_i↓=H_i↓, proving
(2). The identical argument with ev gives (4)⇒(2). No cancellation of the
outer T operation is used. ∎

The theorem proves, rather than defines, the presentation's relation on paths.
A normal entry retains its output component, individual frontier and length,
ordered cut partition, complete frontier arrow string, and monoid value.
Consequently equal total composites, equal total monoid products, or equal
component labels at distinct positions are not criteria for arrow equality.

### 6.4 Explicit hom inverse and equivalence

Given b:P_Q(T)→P_Q(U), use the exact-profile canonical representative

    F_i=N(Phi(b_i)).

The defining semantic endpoint and strictness give
pi(T[F])=pi(U), so κ(T[F],U) is defined. Set

    H_Q(b)=R(T,F);κ(T[F],U).                             (6.4)

Then P_Q(H_Q(b))=b entrywise. Conversely, Theorem 6.2 implies
H_Q(P_Q(p))=p for every p:T→U. These are explicit two-sided hom maps.
There is no assumed categorical lifting property: each endpoint is derived
from the given tuple equation and inherited semantic completeness.

Every Q operation has a raw representative and every D witness w has N(w).
Therefore P_Q and P_D are full, faithful and surjective on object isomorphism
classes; indeed their object functions are surjective as maps of sets. They
are not injective on objects: distinct raw trees such as the two binary
associativity groupings have the same quotient value. Hence they are
**equivalences**, not raw-object isomorphisms.

The analogous H_D uses F_i=N(b_i). Define S:Ref(D,W)→C by S(w)=N(w) and
S(b)=H_D(b) at these endpoints. Its identity law follows directly from
N(identity)=the identity leaf, raw identity, and κ(T,T)=id. For composable
b:a→a', d:a'→a'', the composite S(b);S(d) has, by the mixed-normalization
calculation, entrywise class

    N(b_i)[N(d_(i,j))].

Its normal term is N(b_i[d_(i,j)]) by inherited substitution soundness and
completeness. Formula (6.3) therefore equals S(b;d). This proves composition
on full arrow data, rather than only on their endpoint witnesses.

The equation P_D∘S=id is literal on operations and tuples. The maps

    η_T:=ν_T:T→N(ev(T))

form a natural isomorphism id_C⇒S∘P_D. For p:T→U both p;η_U and
η_T;S(P_D(p)) have the same full tuple under P_D, so Theorem 6.2 (now proved)
identifies them. This use of established faithfulness is a proof of coherence,
not a hypothesis. Its inverse is (ν_T)⁻¹. At canonical objects ν is empty,
and P_D(η_T) is the identity tuple. Thus both triangle compatibilities hold.

Finally let C_nf be the **full subcategory** on actual irreducible trees.
Every such tree is the uniquely labelled N(w), and ev(N(w))=w. The restriction
P_D:C_nf→Ref(D,W) and S have inverse object and arrow maps, hence are an
actual category isomorphism. No quotient of the object set is silently added
to the original completion.

## 7. Effective basis and terminating arrow equality algorithm

For fixed finite tables N,K,M the colour and primitive-generator sets are
finite, as are the bare equation labels and the 18 critical shapes. All
remaining infinitude is in finite terms, contexts, addresses, forests and
words. Enumerate these by total constructor count, then finite lexicographic
code. Check component/profile equalities, graph partitions and monoid/category
tables directly. This enumerates all well-typed Rcat, O, MOut and MIn instances.
The classification in §3 and derivations in §4 prove that no unbounded
context or forest case is omitted by that effective schema presentation.
Nothing asserts that the entire category or all arities are finite.

A direct arrow decision algorithm takes a finite generator word and raw
endpoints:

1. Validate each generator's actual source/target, rule instance and occurrence
   address in sequence. Reject ill-typed words, mismatched whole components,
   illegal nullaries, or wrong repeated-leaf positions.
2. Initialize one identity term F_i at every original leaf of T. For an
   equation edge, leave this family unchanged. For a graft edge at a current
   leaf, use the lengths and full ordered profiles of the current F_i to locate
   its unique descendant pair (i,j), and insert that primitive corolla in F_i.
   This is the constructive induction in Lemma 6.1. Current raw word endpoints
   are tracked separately and validated, so an equation cannot invent a graft
   address. When there are no original leaves there can be no graft edge.
3. Normalize each final F_i with the inherited terminating algorithm. Return
   the full normal family as the key at the fixed endpoints T,U.
4. Compare two keys entrywise, including profiles/labels. By Theorem 6.2 the
   keys agree exactly when the arrows are equal in C.

The procedure processes a finite word and finitely many terminating term
normalizations; it does not enumerate equation paths or search for a semantic
lift. Optional canonical proof words can be produced from the constructions
in §§3–6. The existence of those derivations is what licenses the faster key
algorithm; the key was not used to define C.

## 8. Necessity and a precise minimality statement

Let B be the free category on the same graft/equation generating graph, with
equation inverse relations and Rcat imposed, but no O or mixed relations yet.
The generator assignment to Ref(D,W) is already a functor P_B:B→Ref(D,W).
Let K_loc be the category congruence **generated syntactically** by O, MOut
and MIn. Sections 2–7 prove

    K_loc = kernel congruence of P_B on every fixed hom. (8.1)

Equation (8.1) is a theorem: soundness gives one containment and the complete
normal-form proof gives the reverse. It was not the definition of K_loc.

Now consider any candidate B/R on this SAME generating graph, where R is a
specified set or effective schema of local relations, each sound under P_B.
No full/faithful comparison, cancellation, or lifting condition is included
in this candidate class. Soundness is the independently checkable statement
that each proposed relation has equal full tuples. The induced P_R is always
full and essentially surjective: the path (6.4) exists already as a word in B,
and its endpoint follows from the term presentation, regardless of R.

**Theorem 8.1 (local necessity and sufficiency).** The comparison P_R is
faithful, and hence an equivalence, if and only if every displayed O, MOut,
and MIn equation holds in B/R.

**Proof.** If P_R is faithful, the two sides of each listed local relation
have equal P_R-images by the explicit checks in §5, hence must be equal in
B/R. Conversely, if those local equations hold then K_loc is contained in the
congruence generated by R. Soundness of R and (8.1) give the reverse
containment. Thus the congruences agree and Theorem 6.2 applies. ∎

This characterizes a sufficient and necessary **local equational entailment**
criterion, not a decidable test for arbitrary alternative R. Effective
enumerability of an arbitrary presentation does not make all its entailments
decidable. The particular complete basis K_loc has the separate effective
equality algorithm of §7. Nor is every listed family claimed independent:
a family derivable from the others is honestly redundant, not a new obstruction.

## 9. A uniform obstruction when forest-entry compatibility is omitted

Let C_minus be the same completion with all O and MOut but **without MIn**.
For every raw forest F define |F| as the sum of its primitive-vertex counts.
For an elementary graft its contribution is 1; for either equation edge its
contribution is 0. Give a finite mixed word the sum of these contributions.
This defines a functor

    wt:C_minus→B(N,+),                                   (9.1)

where B(N,+) is the one-object category with natural-number endomorphisms:

* Equation inverses and every O relation have weight 0.
* Raw composition has |F⋆H|=|F|+|H|, because every primitive vertex of the
  second forest is inserted once at an actual descendant leaf. Independent
  history interchange therefore preserves the sum.
* The two sides of MOut use exactly the same inserted elementary corolla,
  so both have weight 1; its derived arbitrary-forest version has weight |F|.

These verifications include graph labels, units and repeated occurrences;
wt deliberately ignores them without changing the count argument.

Suppose the parameter class has a component W. For every integer k≥0 let
v_k=d_1^k(x_W), with v_0=x_W. The mixed word

    ℓ_k=R(x_W,v_k);(D0 at the root repeated k times)       (9.2)

is an endomorphism of x_W in C_minus. Its weight is k, so ℓ_k≠ℓ_j for k≠j.
Nevertheless its full semantic refinement tuple is the singleton identity:
ev(v_k)=identity_W. Therefore P_minus is not faithful and has infinitely
many spurious endomorphisms above that one identity arrow. This holds for
EVERY finite commutative M, including the trivial monoid, every N admitting
W, and every nonempty K. In particular it holds on the entire required
nondegenerate graph family, not only a unary toy: insert v_k at either of the
two nonempty repeated middle occurrences of the six-vertex four-leaf example
and keep the other original entries identity. The same invariant gives
unbounded spurious loops at that mixed-interface object.

The obstruction is exactly failed pointwise equation compatibility for newly
grafted material. MIn for D0 makes R(T,i:d_1(x));D0=id_T; iteration removes
all ℓ_k, and §§4–6 show that the complete repair removes exactly semantic
tuple equalities and no others. In particular it does not identify unequal
local monoid-value tuples merely because their products agree. This supplies
a repaired positive theorem on the same full unbounded parameter family,
not an obstruction substituted for C5's mandatory positive presentation.

## 10. Other discriminating boundaries and examples

### 10.1 A precisely specified object-only quotient fails

A thin reachability quotient whose objects are semantic witness classes and
whose homs merely say that some raw representatives refine has at most one
arrow per fixed pair. It cannot recover the present Ref(D). For example use
the six-vertex four-block cut with grades (a,id_1,id_1,b) and M=Z/3 additively,
source value 0. At its four original occurrences compare unary local-value
tuples (0,0,0,0) and (0,1,2,0), leaving all block partitions unchanged. Both
are arrows to the SAME full target witness of total value 0. They differ at
each of the two repeated middle occurrences and are unequal by B2 tuple
equality and Theorem 6.2. The repaired completion retains both. Thus this
named object-only candidate fails on witness information, not on a change
of whole or target profile.

### 10.2 Ordinary localization needs path coherence

Before O or mixed relations, the free groupoid on equation steps retains
a nontrivial loop at d_1(d_n(x)) obtained by the D0 edge followed by the inverse
of the distinct DA edge. Both edges have the same target d_n(x). This reduced
edge word is nonempty, hence is not the free-groupoid identity, although its
semantic tuple is the identity. Row 7 of O fills exactly that peak. This is
a diagnostic for ordinary localization without coherent relations; no claim
is made that row 7 remains independent after *every* mixed relation is added.

### 10.3 Rotation really identifies internal structure

The two distinct raw binary groupings c(c(x,y),z) and c(x,c(y,z)) have the
same retained three-block full witness but different internal vertices and
raw prefix order. R gives their coherent equation isomorphism in C. Refining
different original occurrences of an outer binary cut yields distinct tuples
even when this rotation makes their final operation classes equal. That is
exactly the difference between the B1 thin raw category and the completed
semantic category, not artificial chronology arrows.

### 10.4 Rotation, units and scalar aggregation do not imply movement

For a concrete undeclared-equation separator, take M={1,e}, e²=e. Remove both
DL and DR, retaining R,UL,UR,D0,DA. Interpret every component by the set
{0,1}; every binary cut by Boolean OR; u by 0; d_1 by the identity function;
and d_e by the constant-0 function. This is a typed multicategory model with
all component carriers equal but their types retained. OR is associative and
unital, and the two unary functions satisfy the prescribed monoid action, so
all retained declarations hold. At inputs x=0,y=1,

    c(d_e(x),y)=1,          d_e(c(x,y))=0.

Thus the DL equation is not derivable from those retained declarations.
The full graph-cut semantics equates the terms because both retain total
value e and the same full cut. This negative control shows why the mixed
object presentation needs the movement equations; it is not another proof
of their already inherited positive completeness.

### 10.5 A witness-collapsing parameter map need not be faithful

Let h:M→M' be a monoid homomorphism with s≠t but h(s)=h(t). At any binary cut
of value 1, take the two unary-entry tuples (d_s,d_t) and (d_t,d_s). They have
the same target full cut of value st=ts but are unequal tuples. After h, their
entries agree. This works for arbitrary commutative monoids, not only groups
or cancellation models; the six-vertex repeated-component context embeds it
without empty principal blocks. Compatibility of the comparison with h
therefore cannot mean that every h induces an equivalence or reflects arrows.
The separate parameter-transport proof must preserve this distinction.

## 2. Explicit full semantic hom sets

### 2.1 Admissible ordered runs

Fix the SAME output component W=(G,f:A→B) and objects

    a=(U_1,…,U_k; f_1,…,f_k; m),
    b=(V_1,…,V_l; g_1,…,g_l; m').

The source profile has interfaces A_0,…,A_k. For a vector
lambda=(l_1,…,l_k) of nonnegative integers with sum l, put
s_i=∑_(h<i) l_h. Run i is the ordered interval of target indices
s_i+1,…,s_i+l_i. Call lambda admissible if all the following hold:

1. The union of the actual sets V_j in run i is U_i. Thus every V_j of that
   run lies in U_i. Empty source blocks and empty target blocks remain actual
   entries; an empty V_j is not deleted.
2. The complete subpath of the target profile in run i has endpoints
   A_(i−1),A_i, and its composite arrow is f_i. This requires equality of
   the actual intermediate endpoints and arrows, not merely a common outer
   composite. For l_i=0 the empty path exists at a single interface, so
   A_(i−1)=A_i, f_i=id, and condition 1 forces U_i=empty.

The condition is decidable from the supplied finite data. For l_i>0, full
run arrows g_j, not just their composite, are retained as the child profile.
For each admissible lambda, the only possible underlying cut at original
slot i has blocks

    ( i_(U_i)^(-1)(V_(s_i+1)), …,
      i_(U_i)^(-1)(V_(s_i+l_i)) )                         (2.1)

in the coordinates of G|U_i, and that full subpath of (g_j). Here inverse
means the inverse bijection [|U_i|]→U_i, only on subsets V_j⊆U_i. No
inverse arrow in K is used.

### 2.2 Hom-description theorem and inverse maps

There is a canonical bijection

 Hom_Ref(D)(a,b) ≅
   ⨿_(lambda admissible) { (n_1,…,n_k)∈M^k : m·∏_i n_i=m' }.       (2.2)

The disjoint-union label lambda is retained. Homs across unequal W are empty
in the total disjoint-union category, even if the graphs agree.

Proof. A semantic arrow is a tuple t_i with output X_i and a[t_i]=b. Let l_i
be the length of its full profile and n_i its value. The substitution profile
law identifies the profile of b with the ordered concatenation of these full
profiles, so their lengths sum to l and determine the indicated runs. The
arrow endpoint equality compares actual ordered partitions: the run's lifted
blocks equal exactly the corresponding V_j, and their union is U_i. Its arrow
string compares to the entire string g_j, with the child output grade f_i.
Thus lambda is admissible. Injectivity of i_(U_i) forces (2.1); each child's
remaining independent datum is n_i. The value part of the endpoint equation
is precisely m·∏n_i=m'. This defines the forward map without forgetting an
entry's profile or local value.

Conversely, take admissible lambda and n satisfying that equation. Define
t_i by (2.1), the full run path, whole X_i=(G|U_i,f_i), and n_i. Its blocks
partition [|U_i|]. By (1.1), restricting its whole to the j-th block gives
the j-th target component graph. Its full run path has composite f_i.
For a zero-length run the conditions give exactly the allowed nullary
component (empty,id), so t_i is a legitimate nullary of value n_i. The tuple
is therefore typed. Substitution lifts its blocks back to V_j, returns the
full target arrow string, and has value m'; hence a[t_i]=b. Reading lengths
and values returns the original data, while reconstructing a tuple from its
lengths/values returns each of its forced blocks and arrows. These two maps
are inverse as actual tuples. QED.

This proof establishes equality of arrows, not merely equality of their
endpoint operations. It retains empty-run labels n_i, which are not recovered
from later descendant entries because a zero-length run has no descendants.

Boundary cases. If k=0, lambda is the empty vector and its sum is zero, so
l=0; the value condition is m=m'. Thus a nullary object has only its identity
arrow, even though there are many nullary objects with different values. If
l=0 and k>0, every source occurrence must be (empty,id) at the appropriate
interface, and the full tuple of k nullary values remains arrow data. An
empty graph carrying a nonidentity grade never passes this test.

For finite M, each fixed hom set is finite and is effectively enumerated by
all weak compositions of l into k parts (with the k=0 case above), testing
admissibility and all M^k value vectors. In particular

 |Hom(a,b)| = (# admissible lambda) ·
             |{ n∈M^k : m·∏n_i=m' }|.                         (2.3)

This formula is not a theorem that frontiers reconstruct wholes or that all
finite-degree cut structures have unique factorization. W is fixed and
retained throughout.

### 2.3 Composition, identities, and nonthinness

Let alpha:a→b have data (lambda,n_i), and beta:b→c have data
(mu,r_j), where mu=(h_1,…,h_l). At original source slot i, the composite
has

    L_i = ∑_(j in run i) h_j,
    N_i = n_i · ∏_(j in run i) r_j.                         (2.4)

Its local blocks are the final c-blocks in the resulting concatenated run,
pulled back into U_i; its interface string is that same full final subpath.
To prove this, apply B2's actual tuple composition t_i[u_(i,j)]. The ordered
slot-expansion identifies its final positions with pairs (i,j), and (1.1)
identifies their lifted subsets. The output of the run's grade multiplication
is f_i by category associativity; its decoration is exactly (2.4). The total
endpoint value is

 m·∏_i(n_i·∏_(j in run i)r_j)
   = (m·∏_i n_i)·∏_j r_j = m'·∏_j r_j,

using the commutative monoid laws. This proves the formula on arrow DATA.
For l_i=0, both sums and products over its descendants are empty, so L_i=0
and N_i=n_i. A nullary label survives composition and is not replaced by 1.

Identity has l_i=1, the singleton unchanged block/path, and n_i=1 at every
slot. Formula (2.4) proves both units entrywise. For three arrows, both
parenthesizations identify the same ordered triples of descendant slots;
their lengths and products agree by finite-sum associativity and the monoid
laws. Therefore the formula itself gives the same B2 category, not a different
endpoint-only quotient category.

For example, at an object with two original slots in additive Z/2 and source
value 0, unary identity cuts at those slots with values (0,0) and (1,1) give
distinct parallel endomorphisms. Both total values are 0; their individual
entries differ. The same example can be inserted at the two nonempty repeated
middle components of the six-vertex four-slot mixed-interface cut, leaving
values 0 at the other slots. This is nonthinness at fixed endpoints, not merely
two different-target refinements. In the idempotent monoid {1,e}, a source
value e has 2^k different unary-value endomorphisms for a fixed k-slot profile:
e·∏n_i=e for every vector, while (2.2) keeps every vector distinct. These
are consequences of a standard scalar factor, not independent novelty.

## 3. Strongest product and typed-pullback baseline

### 3.1 The profile and scalar calculi

Fix the SAME component quiver J of pairs (G,f). Let Prof(W) consist of all
full typed paths p from W.source to W.target, with profile p. There is exactly
one Prof operation in each such fibre, including the empty path only when
these endpoints coincide. It does not require the path's grades to compose
to W.grade; that restriction belongs to Cut, not Prof. Identity is the
singleton path (W), and substitution is ordered typed path expansion. A1's
path laws give its units and associativity.

Let Scalar_M(W;p)=M for EVERY such admissible full path p, independent of
whether a graph cut exists. A scalar operation is the pair (p,m). Set

    1_W=((W),1),
    (p,m)[(q_i,n_i)] = (expand(p,q), m·∏_i n_i).             (3.1)

These are actual fibres over complete paths and fixed W. Both units follow
from the monoid unit and empty/singleton products. Associativity compares the
same ordered triple occurrence indexing and the product of the outer,
intermediate, and innermost values. Associativity and commutativity of M allow
the two displayed nestings to be rearranged to the same finite product. Thus
Scalar_M is a lawful fixed-base Calc. The forgetful map Scalar_M→Prof sends
(p,m) to p and is strict. Its nullary fibres at arbitrary loop components
cause no enlargement of Cut's nullary fibres in the pullback below.

Let Cut=FlatCut(N,K,{1}), with its whole, partition, grades and full path.
The profile map Cut→Prof is strict. Construct a fibrewise pullback P: at W,
pairs (a_0,(p,m)) satisfying profile(a_0)=p, with identity and substitution
coordinatewise. Their profile agreement is preserved because both maps are
strict. Equality witnesses are proof-irrelevant and are not extra operations.

### 3.2 Explicit isomorphism of calculi

Define

    F:D→P,      F(a_0,m)=(a_0,(profile(a_0),m)),
    B:P→D,      B(a_0,(p,m))=(a_0,m), using profile(a_0)=p.  (3.2)

These maps preserve fixed output W and every full profile. They are inverse
by reading the same actual partition, graph, arrows and M-value. In particular
at a nullary component outside (empty,id) both D and P have an empty fibre,
because Cut has no such witness; the scalar factor alone cannot create one.
The identity pair in P maps to D's identity value 1 and unchanged block. For
substitution, both routes produce the same lifted blocks and full expanded
arrow string, and (3.1) produces exactly m·∏n_i. Hence (3.2) is an actual
strict isomorphism

    D ≅ Cut ×_Prof Scalar_M,                                (3.3)

not just a cardinality identity or an analogy. The whole retained graph is in
Cut and is not reconstructed from the scalar factor or the leaves.

### 3.3 Refinement preserves this typed pullback

Define the categorical pullback of Ref(Cut) and Ref(Scalar_M) over Ref(Prof)
explicitly. Its objects are pairs of operations with the same fixed output
and the same complete profile. A morphism is a pair of refinement tuples with
EQUAL images as Ref(Prof) arrows. This equality means, at every original source
slot, equality of the ENTIRE child profile, not just the concatenated target
profile. It therefore retains the same length/segmentation at that slot, its
component list, and all intermediate endpoints/grades. Equal endpoint
profiles alone do not define this pullback.

Map a D-object to its Cut/scalar pair. Map a D-arrow (t_i) to the pair of
tuples (underlyingCut(t_i)) and ((profile(t_i),value(t_i))). Their images in
Ref(Prof) agree entrywise, so this is a pullback arrow. The coordinate endpoint
equalities are exactly the underlying-cut and scalar-value coordinates of the
D endpoint equality. Conversely a compatible pair of tuples has identical
child profiles slot by slot, and pairing each cut child with that scalar child
by (3.2) gives a typed D tuple. Its two endpoint coordinates give the D
endpoint equation. The two assignments are inverse on objects and arrows.

Identities pair the same original singleton profiles and value 1. Composition
in both factors uses the SAME profile splitting precisely because the child
profiles agree; coordinatewise composition is therefore (2.4). This proves
an isomorphism of actual categories

 Ref(D) ≅ Ref(Cut) ×_(Ref(Prof)) Ref(Scalar_M).               (3.4)

A naïve Cartesian product would allow incompatible segmentations/child
profiles and is incorrect. The needed compatibility is nevertheless ordinary
pullback/category-of-substitutions structure; replacing a naïve comparator
by the correct pullback removes any claim that scalar labels create a new
kind of semantic refinement theory. Equations (2.2)–(2.4), many parameter maps,
and the multiplication fibres below are standard consequences of this
explicit decomposition.

## 4. Parameter maps and strict semantic comparisons

Let theta=(F,h,ι), where F:K→L is any functor, h:M→M' is any monoid
homomorphism, and ι is a bound inclusion N≤N'. The component map is
(G,f:A→B)↦(G,Ff:FA→FB). On a full witness, keep G and all actual subsets U_i,
apply F to every intermediate object and individual arrow f_i, and apply h
to the retained value m. This is well typed: F preserves the full composite
f, and graphs/induced restrictions are unchanged. Identities and nullaries
are preserved because F(id)=id and h(1)=1. For substitution, both routes
lift the same subsets, concatenate the same image strings, and have value
h(m·∏n_i)=h(m)·∏h(n_i). Thus theta_D is a strict base-changing calculus
map, with the slot numbering 1,…,k unchanged even when F identifies objects
or arrows.

The Ref map sends an entire arrow tuple (t_i) to (theta_D(t_i)). In (2.2)
coordinates it keeps its length vector lambda, maps every individual grade
in every run by F, and sends n_i to h(n_i). Its target equation is the image
of m·∏n_i=m'. Distinct tuples can become equal when h or F is noninjective;
no faithfulness is claimed. Formula (2.4) proves identity/composition
preservation entrywise, including zero-length runs.

Identity parameter maps act literally as identities. Composition applies
composite functors/homomorphisms entrywise, so it is literally composition on
objects and arrow data. The product maps of §3 commute on the nose: forgetting
a scalar and then applying F is the same cut, and forgetting a cut then
applying (F,h) is the same scalar-profile pair. Their Ref pullback comparisons
likewise commute on every full tuple, not merely on endpoint objects.

Ordered carrier relabeling may separately use a specified increasing
bijection j:[r]→[r'] (or between explicitly ordered finite carriers). It sends
G to the transported edge relation and U to j(U). Its restricted bijections
satisfy j∘i_U=i_(jU)∘j_U by uniqueness of increasing enumeration. This
identity proves all nested block/substitution comparisons. On standardized
initial-segment carriers an increasing bijection forces r=r' and is the
identity; the more general wording is a coordinate comparison, not an
unannounced graph-permutation symmetry. Arbitrary F is a comparison map, not
an invertible interface change. Only parameter isomorphisms yield inverse
maps. None of these statements supplies arbitrary groupoid-action descent,
noninvertible relational restriction, or virtual-equipment structure.

## 5. Syntax, completion, and coherent normal sections under parameters

This section uses the independently proved syntactic completion theorem,
not a full/faithful assumption. Write nu(t):t→N(eval t) for its coherent
chosen pure-equation normalization path; nu of a normal term is the identity.
Its key conclusions are: every two pure equation paths with the same endpoints
are equal under the LOCAL O relations, and every mixed Comp arrow is uniquely
determined by its full original-slot tuple of normalized forest entries.

### 5.1 Generator maps and all relation families

The raw syntax map theta_S sends each cut label to the same graph/subsets
with the image arrow string, u_A to u_(FA), and d_m^W to
 d_(h(m))^(theta W), WITHOUT prematurely deleting d_1. It maps identity leaves
to identity leaves and preserves every occurrence slot and graft. Therefore
it maps raw generators and their B1 composition/interchange relations to
raw generators and relations. It sends each of the seven equation templates
to the SAME target template instance: R uses F's composition preservation;
UL/UR use its identities; D0/DA use h's unit/product; DL/DR keep the cut label
and move the image value. This statement remains true if h(m)=1: a target
D_1 vertex is still a legitimate raw syntax vertex before normalization.

Consequently equation inverses and contextual instances map to corresponding
target paths. Each explicit O critical join word maps to the same word at
the image parameters, including degeneracies; the target O family includes
these instances. Disjoint and variable squares map to themselves because no
occurrence is deleted by theta_S. MOut maps to its target outer-equation
whiskering square. Bare MIn maps to its target insertion/template relation;
its derived arbitrary-context/forest version follows from those same mapped
relations. Hence this is an actual functor theta_C:Comp→Comp' on the
INDEPENDENT presented categories. No target semantic equality was used to
define its action or validate the relation basis.

The raw quotient map theta_Q is well-defined by the same template check and
the least-congruence universal property. Its composition is strict because
raw label maps and quotient representatives compose strictly. The actual
comparison maps satisfy on objects and arrows

    theta_C J = J' theta_Raw,
    P_Q' theta_C = Ref(theta_Q) P_Q,
    P_D' theta_C = Ref(theta_D) P_D.                         (5.1)

For raw arrows this equality applies theta to every original forest entry;
for equation edges both sides are the identity tuple after the same profile
transport. Generator equality and functoriality prove equality on arbitrary
words. The inherited quotient/semantic square Phi' theta_Q=theta_D Phi holds
on every represented tree by evaluation of mapped primitive generators, and
surjectivity of pi gives all quotient objects. Its arrow version is then
entrywise the same equality. Thus (5.1) is not only an object-level diagram.

### 5.2 The normal section is coherent, not literally invariant

For a full semantic object w define the isomorphism

    alpha_(theta,w) = nu'(theta_S N(w)):
      theta_S N(w) → N'(theta_D w).                         (5.2)

The endpoint is correct by evaluation preservation. If h kills a nonidentity
value, theta_S N(w) can contain a d_1 vertex whereas N'(theta_D w) does not.
Thus literal equality of the two raw objects is FALSE in general; (5.2) is
the required equation isomorphism.

Let S:Ref(D)→Comp be the explicitly constructed canonical-object section:
S(w)=N(w), and S(b_i) inserts N(b_i) at the original slots then follows the
coherent equation path to N(w'). For any semantic arrow b:w→w',

 theta_C(S(b)); alpha_(theta,w')
   = alpha_(theta,w); S'(Ref(theta_D)(b)).                   (5.3)

Proof without circular hypotheses. The left mixed path has normalized forest
entries N'(theta_D b_i), because theta_S N(b_i) evaluates to theta_D b_i.
For the right path, MOut moves alpha_(theta,w) past the raw insertion, and
MIn normalizes the inserted entries; it has exactly those same full original
slots and normalized entries. Their remaining pure equation paths have the
same endpoints and agree by O coherence. The proven path-normal-form theorem
therefore gives (5.3). This is also a consequence of the already proved
faithfulness of P_D', not an assumption of it.

For the identity parameter alpha is identity. For composable theta,eta,

 alpha_(eta theta,w)
   = eta_C(alpha_(theta,w)); alpha_(eta,theta_D w).          (5.4)

Both sides are pure equation paths from eta_S theta_S N(w) to
N''(eta_D theta_D w), so the independently proved O coherence gives equality.
No higher unmentioned coherence axiom is needed; repeating (5.4) makes all
associative compositions agree because category composition is associative.
The normalized parameter map between canonical full subcategories is

  on arrows b: alpha_(theta,w)^(-1); theta_C(S(b));
               alpha_(theta,w'),

which equals S'(theta_D b) by (5.3). It is therefore strictly compatible with
identity/composite parameter maps when indexed by the full semantic objects.
This is the exact sense in which normalization commutes with transport.

## 6. Complete local factorization fibres and their limits

Fix a full witness w=(G;(U_1,…,U_n);(f_1,…,f_n);m), n≥3, and a proper
contiguous interval [i,j] with 2≤j−i+1≤n−1. Write

    U = U_i ∪ … ∪ U_j,
    h = f_j⋯f_i : A_(i−1)→A_j,
    K_w=(G|U,h).

Consider the actual F1-shaped substitution map: its domain is the disjoint
union over ALL components K of outer witnesses with profile obtained by
replacing that subpath by K, paired with inner witnesses from the original
interval subpath to K. The map substitutes the inner witness at that one
outer occurrence and uses identity witnesses at every other outer slot.
No interval-cut axiom or simplicial structure is assumed.

### 6.1 The structural factors are forced

For a pair mapping to w, equality of the expanded partition forces the
outer distinguished block to be U. Its induced graph therefore forces the
graph of K to be G|U. The interval's full grade string has composite h, so
the output grade of the inner operation forces K's grade to be h; its
endpoints are already A_(i−1),A_j. Thus K=K_w, including actual arrow and
whole graph. The other outer blocks are U_1,…,U_(i−1),U_(j+1),…,U_n in
order. The inner blocks must be i_U^(-1)(U_i),…,i_U^(-1)(U_j), and their
full arrows are f_i,…,f_j. Formula (1.1) proves all input component equalities.
Conversely these structural outer/inner cuts are valid and substitute to the
underlying full witness. They keep all cross edges in G and all edges of
G|U, including edges between interval blocks.

Only the outer and inner values, call them a and b, are free. The substitution
value is ab, so there is an explicit bijection of the FULL fibre

    Fact_[i,j](w) ≅ { (a,b)∈M² : ab=m }.                    (6.1)

The forward map reads these two values. The inverse equips the forced cuts
above with them. The preceding uniqueness proves both inverse laws. This is
a fibre over a fixed retained whole AND partition, not an inference of G from
its pieces. Consequently its failure of injectivity cannot be blamed on
cross-edge loss.

### 6.2 Exact local uniqueness boundary

The map is surjective for every m, since (1,m) is a solution. If M is trivial,
(6.1) is a singleton for every witness. If M is nontrivial, choose m≠1.
The pairs (1,m) and (m,1) are distinct elements of the fibre; hence that
witness has at least two distinct factorizations. For every nonempty K and
N≥3 this can be demonstrated with three nonempty blocks and any retained
ordered graph at one interface, using identity grades. The mandatory mixed-
interface six-vertex model supplies a further nondegenerate instance. For
smaller N, empty blocks also provide n≥3, but are not the principal example.
If no objects/witnesses exist, a universal uniqueness assertion is vacuous;
we do not claim that vacuity forces M trivial.

Thus, in every nonvacuous stated family containing such interval witnesses,
ALL these local factorization maps are bijective iff M is the one-element
monoid. This is a sharp LOCAL boundary, not the full F1 recognition theorem.
For a particular m the answer is exactly its multiplication fibre, not a
blanket group formula. For example in {1,e}, the fibre of 1 is {(1,1)}, while
the fibre of e is {(1,e),(e,1),(e,e)}. In a finite group the fibre of m has
|M| elements. These elementary counts are consequences, not significance
claims or substitutes for the actual maps.

### 6.3 Unary, nullary, and chosen-shape iteration

Unary D(W;(W)) is precisely M, with only 1 the identity. The designated
nullary fibre D((empty,id_A);empty) is precisely M, with the chosen u_A
having value 1; all other nullary fibres are empty. Thus the nontrivial
M enrichment fails normalized-unary and unique-nullary hypotheses that a
stronger recognition theorem may require. No extra nullary is silently
redeclared an identity, and valid nonidentity unary witnesses are not removed.

If one separately defines a one-slot or full-interval substitution map in the
same way, its structural intermediate is respectively that slot's component
or W and the same multiplication-fibre calculation applies. These are not
additional proper intervals in (6.1). An inserted empty-interval operation
would require a designated (empty,id) component at a specified boundary and
an explicitly inserted empty outer block; it is additional unit data, not
an interval assumed by the theorem. We do not infer its coherence from (6.1).

For a FIXED planar substitution-tree shape whose vertices are semantic
operations, and a fixed full frontier witness w, the same descendant-union
argument determines each intermediate graph as the restriction to the union
of its descendant external blocks, and its grade as their ordered composite.
Nullary vertices force empty/id and unary vertices the same component. If
this shape is well typed, has q≥1 vertices, and every vertex's ordered children
are prescribed, all underlying vertex cuts are forced. Its complete value
fibre is then

    { (m_v)_(v in vertices) ∈ M^q : ∏_v m_v=m }.             (6.2)

Proof: apply (1.1) down the shape; the root whole is retained, each child's
support is the union of its descendant leaves, and the displayed structural
cuts flatten back to w by induction. Substitution multiplies precisely all
vertex values. Reading values and equipping those forced cuts are inverse
maps. For a vertex-free shape, only the actual identity operation is realized:
one external leaf equal to W and value 1. Formula (6.2) is NOT extended to
q=0 without that boundary condition.

Regrouping a chosen collection of factors multiplies their values; it can
forget their individual placements. This statement is about an externally
fixed shape and its fibre, not a canonical choice of factors or a bijective
comparison among all cut shapes. No simplicial identities, coherent chosen
cut operators, all-degree 2-Segality, CULF, F3 equivalence, or objective
incidence descent follow from these calculations alone.

### 6.4 Relation to operations, equations, and refinement tuples

A4's object presentation places all values at the root normal form: DA
aggregates, DL/DR move a value through a cut, and D0 removes only value 1.
Consequently it deliberately identifies different internal placements with
the same total value in a SINGLE operation. Equation (6.1) identifies exactly
why that operation normal form cannot uniquely recover the two factor values.
It does not identify different total values.

In Ref(D), by contrast, a tuple consists of separate operations at the original
source slots. The equations normalize the internal placement WITHIN each
entry. They do not move a value between different entries merely because
the resulting total m·∏n_i is unchanged. The bijection (2.2) and the Comp
arrow-normal-form theorem keep the n_i and child profiles separately. This
is why the Z/2 (0,0)/(1,1) example remains parallel and unequal, while two
presentations of either entry itself can become equal. A local cut of the
entry can again have fibre (6.1); no stronger cut uniqueness is restored by
retaining the tuple. These are precise inputs and boundaries for F1–F3,
not acceptance of those obligations.

## 1. Typed calculi as ordinary colored multicategories

Let A be any accepted fixed-base path-typed nonsymmetric calculus. Take its
colors to be the COMPLETE components W=(A,B,x∈J(A,B)), not interface names.
For a finite ordered list of colors (X_1,…,X_n) and output W, set its operation

## 2. The standard monoidal envelope and exact opposite-slice formula

Define Env(A) as the following category. Its objects are finite ordered lists
of colors, whether or not the whole list itself is a composable path. An arrow
from a list q to a list p=(X_1,…,X_k) consists of an ordered division of q into
k consecutive sublists q_i and an A-operation b_i:q_i→X_i for each i. Empty
q_i are allowed when that operation exists. The division is arrow data. The
empty target list allows only the empty source and empty tuple.

Identity uses singleton sublists and the color identities. To compose an
arrow q→p with an arrow r→q, split the second tuple by each q_i and substitute
its entries into b_i. The resulting ordered division of r and tuple are
actual data. A1/B2's pair/triple occurrence identification proves the unit
and associative laws. Concatenation is a strict monoidal product on objects
and tuples. This is the usual free strict monoidal category on a plain
multicategory, not a new construction. Leinster gives it in *Higher Operads,
Higher Categories*, §2.3, pp. 56–57; the plain multicategory conventions of
§§2.1–2.3 permit colors and arities in the nonnegative integers.
[Primary source, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305049).

Fix the singleton list (W). An object of the ordinary slice Env(A)/(W) is
exactly one operation a:p→W. A slice arrow from a':q→W to a:p→W is a forest
b:q→p with a∘b=a'. In the opposite category it is an arrow a→a', precisely
the full occurrence tuple required by Ref(A;W). The two assignments simply
read the same data, including each q_i and its segmentation; they are inverse.
Identity/composition on both sides is the forest operation just specified.
Consequently

    Ref(A;W) ≅ (Env(A)/(W))^op.                            (2.1)

The formula includes zero-input child operations, repeated identical colors,
and the full output color W. It is not a thin reachability construction.
Nonthin semantic refinement is therefore already standard forest/slice data.
No theorem that slices commute with arbitrary quotients is inferred from (2.1).

## 3. Equation groupoids and the standard forest-with-isomorphism category

semantic equalities. In every full profile/output fibre form the groupoid
G whose objects are raw trees and whose arrows are contextual equation paths
modulo the LOCAL O relations. The complete ordinary-coherence proof in
Completion_Comparison §§3.1–3.2 gives an arrow t⇒u exactly when t and u are
E-equivalent, and that arrow is unique. This uniqueness was proved from the
local basis and termination, not imposed as a semantic-kernel definition.

Substitution extends to these groupoids. On objects it is raw forest graft.
Given an outer equation path and a tuple of inner equation paths with matching
profiles, A3's substitution-stable congruence gives an equation path between
the substituted terms. Ordinary O coherence makes its class independent of
all chosen paths and interleavings. Define substitution of morphisms by this
unique class. Identity, composite, operadic unit and associativity laws follow
because the corresponding object grafts agree by A1 and each parallel
morphism pair is already equal in G. This makes G a groupoid-valued colored
plain multicategory. It includes genuine nullaries; it is not a
constant-free/uncoloured simplification.

# A convergent mixed presentation of decorated typed graph cuts

Status: complete mathematical proof draft for non-author review. This supplies A4's semantic normalization dependency; it does not declare the whole C1/C5 or A4 obligation discharged.

## 1. Parameters and strict restriction convention

Let K be an arbitrary finite category, M an arbitrary finite commutative monoid with identity 1, and N a natural number. The statements below hold for every N; the principal certificates use N >= 6. Represent a graph by a simple undirected edge relation on [r]={0,...,r-1}, r <= N. Graph equality is equality of r and the edge relation, not unlabelled isomorphism.

For U subset of [r], let i_U:[|U|] -> [r] enumerate U increasingly. Define G|U on [|U|] by pulling edges back along i_U. For V subset of [|U|], write U[V]=i_U(V). Then

    i_(U[V]) = i_U composed with i_V,
    (G|U)|V = G|(U[V]).                                      (restriction)

These are literal equalities of increasing maps and ordered graphs: both enumerations are the unique increasing bijection onto U[V]; checking the two graph edge relations then gives the second equality. Disjoint unions of subsets below are set unions, not disjoint graph unions: all edges in the retained whole remain available.

The component (G,f) has interfaces A,B when f:A -> B in K. A profile is a full ordered list ((G_1,f_1),...,(G_n,f_n)) with f_i:A_(i-1) -> A_i. Intermediate objects and arrows remain part of that list even for empty graphs or identity arrows.

## 2. The independently defined semantic calculus

A flat witness from that profile to (G,f) is

    (G; U_1,...,U_n; f_1,...,f_n; m),

where (U_i) is an ordered partition of [r], allowing empty blocks, G_i=G|U_i, f=f_n ... f_1, and m in M. It retains G itself and the actual subsets U_i. For n=0 it exists only when r=0, A_0=A_n=A and f=id_A; its value m is arbitrary. Thus an empty graph with a nonidentity loop arrow has no nullary witness, and an empty graph with unequal endpoints has none either. For n=1 the partition is ([r]), f_1=f, and its possible witnesses are exactly the M-values. Identity is the unary witness with value 1.

Given an outer witness with blocks U_i and value m and, at each separate occurrence i, an inner witness on G|U_i with blocks V_(i,1),...,V_(i,k_i), arrow string (g_(i,j)), and value n_i, define substitution by

    blocks: (U_1[V_(1,1)],...,U_1[V_(1,k_1)],
             ...,
             U_n[V_(n,1)],...,U_n[V_(n,k_n)]),
    arrows: concatenation of the g_(i,j),
    value:  m times product_i n_i,
    whole:  the unchanged G and f.

Typing follows from (restriction) and composition in K. A nullary refinement of an occurrence is possible only at (empty,id_A), so deleting that occurrence's arrow preserves the composite and all remaining boundaries. The two unit laws follow from whole-set restriction and identity factors. Associativity follows from i_U i_V i_W = i_(U[V[W]]), equality of the lexicographically ordered triples (i,j,k), category associativity, and commutativity/associativity of M. Commutativity is genuinely used: outer-then-inner flattenings can order factors n_i,p_(i,j) differently. Since no substitution changes the outer graph and each inner graph is a restriction, every bound r <= N is closed. This is not a depth truncation.

All choices are finite in each fixed profile and output. Empty blocks allow unbounded arity even for fixed N. This elementary calculus construction is included only to avoid circularly assuming C1/C5; no all-degree cut theorem is imported.

## 3. Finite signature and exact local rules

The signature has the following generators; identity leaves are not primitive generators.

- For each graph G, ordered bipartition (U,V), and arrows a:A -> B, b:B -> C, a binary generator c_(G;U,V;a,b) with inputs (G|U,a),(G|V,b) and output (G,ba).
- A nullary u_A with output (empty,id_A).
- For every component W and every m in M, a unary generator d_m^W:W -> W in the multicategory sense. This unary notation does not mean an interface arrow.

All binary and nullary generators have semantic decoration 1; d_m has semantic value m and identity underlying cut. There are finitely many colours and generators for fixed N,K,M. Terms are the A1 occurrence-linear planar presentations on this signature. Every displayed metavariable occurs exactly once on both sides of every rule. The u_A is a nullary term and has no external occurrence. An identity leaf of colour (empty,id_A), in contrast, is an external occurrence and never matches u_A.

Suppress only uniquely recoverable cut labels when writing c(x,y). The seven rule schemas are:

    R:   c(c(x,y),z) -> c(x,c(y,z))
    UL:  c(u_A,x)    -> x
    UR:  c(x,u_B)    -> x
    D0:  d_1(x)     -> x
    DA:  d_m(d_n(x))-> d_(mn)(x)
    DL:  c(d_m(x),y)-> d_m(c(x,y))
    DR:  c(x,d_m(y))-> d_m(c(x,y)).

The exact typing of R is essential. Choose an ordered triple partition (U,V,W) of the whole G and composable a:A_0 -> A_1, b:A_1 -> A_2, c:A_2 -> A_3. On the left the outer partition is (U union V,W), with arrows (ba,c), and the inner partition is (i_(U union V)^-1 U,i_(U union V)^-1 V), with arrows (a,b). On the right the outer partition is (U,V union W), with arrows (a,cb), and the inner partition is (i_(V union W)^-1 V,i_(V union W)^-1 W), with arrows (b,c). The child colours agree by (restriction), and the output arrows agree by category associativity. Every well-typed left-nested binary pair uniquely supplies these data by lifting its inner blocks into the outer whole. No intermediate arrow is guessed from a composite.

UL is only the generator labelled by (empty,[r];id_A,f) applied to (u_A,x). UR is only ([r],empty;f,id_B) applied to (x,u_B). They do not remove a leaf of empty graph colour, an empty block carrying a nonidentity arrow, or an arbitrary nullary primitive from another signature. DL and DR change no cut label; the new decorator is at the cut's output colour. DA and D0 act at a single colour.

Rules apply in every well-typed one-hole tree context. Finite tables for K and M, finite adjacency matrices, and finite subset lists decide all typing and matching tests. R computes its replacement using unions, increasing enumerations, and category composition. Its matching never asks to solve an equation for missing factors. Thus matching and one-step enumeration are decidable, with finitely many reducts for every term.

Every rule preserves the ordered frontier, endpoints, output colour, complete flat partition and full frontier arrow string. Decoration preservation for DL uses (m p)q=m(pq); for DR it uses p(m q)=m(pq), explicitly using commutativity. R uses associativity; unit rules use the identity. Hence evaluation is invariant under reduction and under the parallel substitution congruence E generated by the unoriented rules. This does not assume equality in this model defines E.

## 4. Termination in arbitrary contexts

For a term t define four natural numbers:

1. B(t): number of binary cut vertices.
2. A(t): sum, over binary vertices v, of the number of binary vertices in v's left subtree; unary decorations are ignored for this count.
3. H(t): sum, over decoration vertices d, of the number of binary strict ancestors of d.
4. D(t): number of decoration vertices.

Order tuples (B,A,H,D) lexicographically. This is well-founded on N^4.

- UL and UR strictly lower B by one, regardless of changes to later coordinates.
- R preserves B. Writing B(x)=b_x, B(y)=b_y, its two affected contributions to A change from (1+b_x+b_y)+b_x to b_x+b_y; A decreases by 1+b_x. All other internal contributions are unchanged. At ancestors of the redex, subtree B is unchanged, so their contributions are unchanged too. H may increase, which is harmless after the strict A decrease.
- DL and DR preserve B and A and move exactly one decoration across exactly one binary ancestor. All other decorations keep their binary ancestor counts, so H decreases by one. This remains true within every context.
- DA preserves B,A. If its redex lies under h binary ancestors, it removes one decoration at that cut-depth, so H decreases by h. If h=0, H is unchanged and D decreases by one. Decorations inside x have unchanged binary ancestor counts.
- D0 has the same H,D calculation as DA.

These calculations prove strict lexicographic decrease for every contextual step, including all unit and decoration values. Therefore there is no infinite rewrite sequence. Neither raw vertex count nor decoration depth alone would prove this theorem.

## 5. Canonical witnesses and irreducibles

For a flat witness w with n blocks, construct a term C(w) ignoring m:

- n=0: u_A. This case has whole (empty,id_A).
- n=1: the identity leaf at (G,f). It is not u_A, even if G is empty and f=id_A.
- n>=2: let V=U_2 union ... union U_n and b=f_n ... f_2. Use the binary cut of G with partition (U_1,V), arrows (f_1,b), left identity leaf at (G|U_1,f_1), and right child recursively C(G|V; i_V^-1 U_2,...,i_V^-1 U_n; f_2,...,f_n).

Define N(w)=C(w) if m=1, and N(w)=d_m(C(w)) otherwise. This is well-typed by (restriction), has exactly the original ordered frontier, and evaluates to w by induction on n. In particular it supplies surjective generation, including all nontrivial decorated nullaries. N(w) is irreducible: C(w) has no internal nullaries for n>0, no decorations, and no left-nested binary cut; its left identity leaves cannot trigger unit elimination. The optional root decoration is nonidentity and unnested.

Conversely let t be irreducible. No decoration can have a decoration parent (DA), nor a binary parent (DL/DR), and no identity-valued decoration can occur (D0). Thus there is at most one decorator, at the root, with value different from 1. Remove it temporarily. A nullary u_A can have no binary parent (UL/UR); if the remaining term is not itself u_A, no nullary occurs. Any binary left child would trigger R. Consequently the remaining term is either a single identity leaf or a right comb of identity leaves.

For a right comb with fixed whole and flat evaluation w, the root's left block is U_1 and its right block is the union of the remaining blocks. Its left arrow is f_1 and its right arrow is their composite, because each child term is well-typed. The remaining graph and all restriction embeddings are then forced; induction determines every internal cut label. The leaves, including repeated types, remain the ordered occurrences of w's profile. Its root decorator, if any, is exactly w's total m. Hence

    irreducible(t) implies t = N(eval(t)).                     (NF-injectivity)

This argument does not recover a whole from isolated pieces. It starts from the retained whole and actual partition. Two partitions with identical induced graphs generally give different cut labels and different normal forms.

## 6. Global confluence and presentation completeness

Termination gives an irreducible descendant of every term, for example by repeatedly taking the first redex in a fixed traversal. Semantic invariance and NF-injectivity show that every such descendant is N(eval(t)). Thus all reduction paths from t have the same terminal term. If t reduces to a and b, terminate from each: both reach N(eval(t)). This proves confluence without assuming any external critical-pair theorem.

For parallel presentations s,t, the following are equivalent:

    s E t;
    N(eval(s)) = N(eval(t));
    eval(s) = eval(t).

The first implies the third by semantic soundness of every generating equation and substitution. The third implies the second by the explicit N construction. The second implies the first because each term reduces using equations in E to that same normal form. Evaluation of N(w) is w, so equality of normal forms implies equality of semantics as well. Thus this is soundness AND completeness for the independently declared local equations, not a quotient defined to have all semantic equalities.

A normalization algorithm can use terminating rewriting or the direct independent two-stage computation t -> eval(t) -> N(eval(t)). The theorem proves their equality and that the direct output is E-equivalent to t. Comparing finite graphs, subsets, arrow strings, and M-values decides E. No claim about arbitrary additional equations follows.

## 1. A genuinely mixed nonconfluent control

Delete DL from the seven-rule presentation, leaving all other rules and a nontrivial commutative monoid element m != 1. On three well-typed identity leaves x,y,z, the term

    c(c(x,d_m(y)),z)

has two reductions. Applying DR at the inner cut gives

    c(d_m(c(x,y)),z).

Applying R first gives

    c(x,c(d_m(y),z)).

Both are irreducible under the modified rules: the surviving decoration on a left child prevents the relevant rotation and there is no DL rule. They are different terms. The graph labels can be those obtained from any three-block partition, and the arrow string can be (a,id_1,b) in the three-interface chain 0 -> 1 -> 2. Choose a graph with three nonempty blocks to avoid relying on empty/unit degeneracy.

This is a finite, well-typed, semantically sound but nonconfluent mutated presentation. The original full presentation joins this exact overlap (row 12). It demonstrates why testing only associativity would miss a real defect. It does not disprove the original theorem or prove a global undecidability result by itself.

## 2. Nontermination control

Add the reverse of R for each typed rotation. For any admissible three-leaf cut there is a two-step directed cycle

    c(c(x,y),z) -> c(x,c(y,z)) -> c(c(x,y),z).

Both steps are parallel and semantically sound; soundness does not imply termination. The reverse increases A by 1+B(x), so the frozen reduction order rejects this orientation. ## 3. Exact unary-word reduction

Input: a finite monoid presentation P = <Sigma | u_i=v_i, 1 <= i <= r>, with u_i,v_i in Sigma*, and two words u,v. Empty words are permitted.

Choose a fixed interface A and one component W:A -> A. For each letter a in Sigma supply one primitive unary witness alpha_a with profile (W) and whole W. Supply no binary or nullary primitive generators. The free operation fibre F(S)((W);W) consists exactly of finite unary chains; its identity leaf represents the empty word. Define

    T(epsilon) = identity_W,
    T(a_1 ... a_k) = alpha_(a_1)(alpha_(a_2)(...alpha_(a_k)(identity_W)...)).

This is an effective bijection Sigma* -> F(S)((W);W), with effective inverse obtained by reading the unique root-to-leaf label sequence. In particular T(pq)=T(p)[T(q)], with substitution at the single leaf, and no other tree shapes or arities are introduced by this signature.

Declare exactly the parallel equations T(u_i)=T(v_i). Let E be their least substitution-stable equivalence congruence. Let ~_P be the least monoid congruence generated by u_i=v_i. Then

    u ~_P v  if and only if  T(u) E T(v).                     (*)

Proof from words to terms: each defining word replacement p u_i q <-> p v_i q is a declared tree equation with suffix T(q) substituted at its leaf and prefix T(p) as its outer unary context. Reflexive, symmetric and transitive word derivations therefore map to E-derivations.

Proof from terms to words: transport ~_P along the bijection T. It is an equivalence, contains all the declared tree equations, and is stable under every allowed substitution/context because ~_P is stable under left and right concatenation. By minimality, E is contained in this transported relation. This proves the reverse implication without assuming that one model happens to be complete.

If an algorithm decided equality for all finite typed unary witness presentations, apply it to this effectively constructed presentation and the two finite terms. By (*) it would decide equality in every finitely presented monoid, contrary to Post's theorem. Moreover, Post's proof produces a fixed finite Thue system with undecidable equivalence to a fixed word. Its unary translation is therefore a single finite witness presentation with undecidable operation equality; this is stronger than merely saying there is no uniform algorithm across presentations.

Nothing in this reduction asserts that A4's finite commutative multiplication-table fragment has undecidable equality. There M itself is finite and explicitly known, with all mixed rules and the proven reduction order. Here the quotient monoid of a finite presentation can be infinite, and no convergent orientation is supplied. Adding unused interfaces or other disconnected sorts does not remove the obstruction, so it is not a loophole caused by using one interface in this LIMITATION proof. The principal positive certificate still has several interfaces as required.

## 2. Direct published theorem for the ordinary O relations

Samuel Mimram, *Rewriting techniques for relative coherence*, LMCS 21(3:7)
(2025), §3.2: Definition 3.14 requires a confluence diagram filled by the
declared coherence relations, not merely a common reduct. Proposition 3.16
upgrades termination plus this local confluence to confluence; Lemma 3.13
supplies weak normalization. Proposition 3.19 compares zigzags with chosen
normalization paths, and Proposition 3.20 yields equality of parallel
zigzags. The setting is an abstract 2-ARS presenting a groupoid.
[Published paper, pp. 7:18–20](https://lmcs.episciences.org/16103/pdf).

**Exact instantiation (reviewer's translation):** objects are well-typed raw
trees; steps are contextual instances of the seven oriented rules; coherence
cells are the specified 18 joining diagrams and disjoint/variable squares.
The inherited well-founded measure supplies termination. An exhaustive local
branching classification and actual joining words supply local *filled*
confluence. Thus O's pure equation-path conclusion is standard once these
hypotheses are checked. Typed trees and nullaries are allowed as abstract
states; no untyped/cartesian encoding is needed. Directed graft arrows are
not invertible equation steps, so this application does not prove the mixed
comparison. Earlier correspondence cited correct v1 numbers 36, 39, 40;
the published numbers above supersede those for final citation.

Guiraud–Malbos, *Polygraphs of finite derivation type*, Theorem 4.3.2, likewise
says that generating confluences of a convergent 2-polygraph form a homotopy
basis. Its exact statement concerns 2-polygraphs presenting categories, not
only monoids. A typed-tree-to-polygraph translation would still need checking;
the abstract result above avoids that unnecessary gap. Neither result says
that confluence without chosen commuting fillers proves path coherence.
[Version 2, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1402.2587v2).

## 3. Exact standard semantic category, including colors and nullaries

Leinster, *Higher Operads, Higher Categories*, §2.3, pp. 56–57, constructs the
free strict monoidal category F(P) of a plain multicategory P: its objects
and arrows are ordered finite sequences, and its arrows are forests of P
operations. The construction is left adjoint to the underlying-multicategory
functor. Plain multicategories here have arbitrary object/color sets and
arities in the nonnegative integers, including zero.
[Primary text, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305049).

**Exact interpretation (reviewer's translation, not a quoted theorem):** encode
the path-typed calculus as a colored plain multicategory by assigning empty
operation sets to inadmissible profiles. For fixed output color W,

    Ref(P;W) = (F(P)/(W))^op.

A slice object is an operation a:p→(W). A refinement from a to a' is a forest
b:q→p with a b=a', precisely the opposite slice arrow a'→a. Its forest
retains each child's whole profile and includes zero-input children. Thus
semantic nonthinness and original-slot segmentation are already ordinary
forest/slice data. This identification does not show that taking a slice
commutes with an arbitrary presentation quotient; that is not an acceptable
shortcut for B3's independent local presentation.

## 4. Closest explicit categorical-operad construction and its limits

Dominik Trnka, *Integration of a categorical operad*, published TAC 45(3)
(2026), Definition 5 and Proposition 9 (arXiv v2), defines 1-cells by an
ordered forest plus an internal operation morphism, and 2-cells by
entrywise operation morphisms compatible with that terminal morphism.
Composition substitutes the forests and transports the terminal morphism.
Proposition 9 factors 1-cells into internal and substitution parts. Theorem
26 identifies these integrations with split-fibered operadic 2-categories.
**Its stated scope is uncoloured, nonsymmetric, constant-free Cat-operads:**
arities are positive and the indexing maps are surjections.
[Primary version, Definitions 1/5, Proposition 9, Theorem 26](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12399v2).

**Comparison, not an unproved application:** reversing the 1-cell orientation
matches forest-then-equation paths; entrywise 2-cells match changes within
grafted entries. The frozen family needs colored profiles, empty fibres and
arity zero, so this paper cannot simply be cited as its full theorem.
Moreover, a construction described using all operation morphisms does not
establish that seven bare MIn schemes and elementary MOut generate exactly
the needed identifications. That local-to-general claim still requires the
author's proof. Failure of this source's hypotheses is not evidence that
colors or nullaries are new mathematics: §3's standard envelope already has
both.
