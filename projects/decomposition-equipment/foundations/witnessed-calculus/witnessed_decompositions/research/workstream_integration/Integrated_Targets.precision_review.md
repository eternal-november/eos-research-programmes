## 2 Frozen mathematical core

### 2.1 Interfaces and profiles

Fix a set or type of interfaces $I$ and a directed family of component types $J(A,B)$ for $A,B\in I$. An ordered profile is a composable path

$$p=(J_1:A_0\to A_1,\ldots,J_n:A_{n-1}\to A_n).$$

A whole $W$ for this profile belongs to $J(A_0,A_n)$. A signature supplies a set of primitive witnesses $S(p;W)$. A witness is not required to reconstruct $W$ from the component types alone. Two different wholes may admit witnesses with the same input profile; two witnesses may have the same profile and whole.

The default foundational development permits nonempty profiles. A separately tested nullary extension permits the empty path at $A$ only with output $W\in J(A,A)$. Such a witness is additional supplied data. It does not assert that every loop component is a unit, that every interface has a chosen unit component, or that a component with unequal endpoints can be the output of an empty profile.

### 2.2 Presentations and evaluation

A presentation is a finite planar rooted tree. Its edges carry component types; a vertex carries a primitive witness with the matching ordered incoming edges and outgoing edge. The identity presentation on $W$ is a single edge with no primitive vertex. It is not a new primitive unary witness. Every external leaf has its own occurrence position, even when multiple leaves carry exactly the same component type.

Substitution replaces each leaf occurrence by a presentation whose output is that occurrence's component. The new frontier is the ordered concatenation of the substituted frontiers, with the required interface identifications. The main laws are left unit, right unit, and associativity for simultaneous occurrence-wise substitution. There is no exchange, symmetry, contraction, copying, or permutation rule unless introduced in a separately named extension.

Let $F(S)(p;W)$ denote the operation fibre of free presentations with frontier $p$ and whole $W$. Semantic evaluation maps these presentations into another typed witness calculus. Evaluation may identify different trees. Neither equality of frontiers nor equality after evaluation implies equality of presentation trees.

### 2.3 Equations

A relation may equate only parallel operations: the same ordered frontier, the same intermediate interface data, and the same whole. The presented calculus $F(S)/E$ is the quotient by the least substitution-stable congruence containing the declared equations. It is not the quotient by all equalities observed in one selected model.

Arbitrary equational theories are not promised normal forms or decidable equality. A named decidable fragment and a separate limitation result are mandatory. A proof may be unexpectedly short and still fully succeed: the acceptance floor constrains mathematical strength and scope, not labor expended.

### 2.4 Refinement

A raw refinement $T\to T'$ is a leaf-indexed forest $F$ with $T'=T[F]$. Composition is forest substitution. Its data retain the internal tree structure added at each original leaf, not the chronology in which independent grafts were performed. Two event sequences that merely interleave independent grafts are not automatically different refinements.

For an evaluated calculus $M$, the analogous semantic refinement category has objects $a\in M(p;W)$ and arrows given by an occurrence-indexed tuple $b_i$ satisfying $a'=a[b_i]$. The tuple, including its individual frontier lengths and types, is arrow data. Semantic equality of the composite does not erase which original occurrence was refined.

The program will prove the precise relation between these categories. It will not assume that identifying presentation objects automatically produces the correct quotient category.

### 2.5 Independent additional layers

The following structures are additional layers, not assumptions of the minimal core. The full virtual-equipment layer is nevertheless a central mandatory outcome of this program:

- transport of interfaces along isomorphisms or general maps;
- a full virtual equipment with restrictions and universal units, plus a distinct ordinary-equipment representability/completion analysis;
- exact assembly of a whole from independent pieces;
- chosen coherent cuts, factorization uniqueness, and a 2-Segal nerve;
- observations, measurement protocols, or identifiability assumptions.

An extension must state its added data, prove its laws, and give the exact comparison with the core. An equipment completion may add horizontal components and cells, so its full identity-boundary part need not literally equal the original core. No extension may silently redefine a legitimate nonreconstructive primitive cut as invalid.

## 3 Completion rules

### 3.1 Obligation classes

Each obligation below has an identifier, claim or question, inputs, required artifact, discharge rule, tests, dependencies, and current status.

**M means must pass.** A constructive proof, model, or specified verification is required. An obstruction to an M obligation exposes a defective baseline and triggers an amendment decision; it does not count as completion.

**B means bounded theorem or obstruction.** A positive theorem may discharge the obligation. A negative branch must instead identify a natural, explicitly bounded class; prove the failure and isolate the failed hypothesis; and provide either a useful repaired positive theorem with a nontrivial model or a sharp impossibility boundary for that class. It must compare with the relevant existing construction. Finding one tiny counterexample or writing that a problem is hard does not discharge a B obligation.

**G means governance or integration gate.** It requires the stated evidence, not a mathematical theorem.

All obligations start OPEN. An artifact may be recorded as submitted or verified without being accepted. ACCEPTED requires the evidence and reviewer requirements below. Observed seed artifacts do not automatically discharge broader claims.

### 3.2 Distinct completion predicates

**Core achieved:** A1–A3, B1–B2, the core-facing model certificates, and every mandatory Lean claim in Section 13 are accepted. This does not mean all research obligations are complete.

**Full virtual equipment achieved:** E1–E3 and their dependencies are accepted, including the universal presentation, coherent change of choices, and positive recovery on original profiles. A Rel/Span interpretation alone does not qualify.

**Ordinary equipment specialization achieved:** The positive representability/completion theorem in E4 is accepted for its named class, including actual universal composites and coherence. This does not assert that every virtual model is ordinary or that every legitimate witness is exact.

**Program investigated to closure:** Every obligation has its prescribed positive or explicitly allowed negative outcome, and all governance gates pass. A failed attempt, an open mandatory theorem, or an unapproved weakening prevents this status.

**Intended scientific scope achieved:** In addition to investigation closure, non-author review confirms that the integrated result packages meet their generality, comparison, sharpness, and anti-shortcut floors. If the ambitious claims all reduce to known results, trivial enrichments, or elementary counterexamples, the report must say the intended scope was not achieved, even if definitions and code are correct. It must propose a material amendment or report the shortfall, never fabricate novelty.

Creating this proposed contract satisfies none of these mathematical predicates. The final verdict must also distinguish a wholly positive program from closure containing certified obstruction branches.

### 3.3 Evidence levels

- **L:** Lean-checked theorem, with the exact statement, source location, dependency closure, and axiom report.
- **P:** complete mathematical proof that a competent independent reviewer can verify without filling a central gap.
- **C:** executable computation with stated finite domain and independent or redundant checking. Computation alone does not establish an unrestricted theorem.
- **X:** explicit counterexample with all types and laws checked, together with the required scope analysis.

Every theorem must list its assumptions and quantifiers. A conditional theorem is accepted only as conditional. A missing assumption is not cured by silently narrowing a theorem after testing it. In particular, loss of cross edges from isolated pieces is not a failure of 2-Segality when the simplicial data retain the whole and its cut. A negative bridge must identify either failure to define coherent simplicial maps or a specified nonbijective comparison in an actually defined simplicial object.

### 3.4 Minimum passing work and managed-task handoffs

For every obligation, the minimum passing deliverable must already reach its intended depth. An author may not substitute a narrower toy case, a weaker theorem, a familiar corollary under renamed hypotheses, or a code-only result and rely on broad wording to obtain acceptance. The explicit floors and nonpassing shortcuts below are part of the contract, not optional advice.

Before dispatching an attempt, a non-author reviewer must identify the cheapest technically compliant solution and ask whether it would satisfy the intended mathematical goal. If not, tighten the target class, integration requirement, or proof obligation before the work starts. The reviewer records the proposed cheap solution and the clause that excludes it. This check is mandatory even when the individual task looks easy.

Each task or agent handoff must carry the contract version, obligation ID, complete acceptance clause, frozen inputs and hypotheses, nonpassing shortcuts, dependencies, required evidence, and the permitted theorem/obstruction branch. A handoff that says only “prove the theorem” or “implement the structure” is insufficient. Subdelegation preserves these clauses. A successful build may pass implementation review while failing scientific-scope review.

Acceptance has two distinct dimensions. **Investigation closure** records a complete positive or allowed negative account of the stated question. **Scientific acceptance** additionally requires the promised generality, substantial integration, prior-art distinction, and nondegenerate model coverage. A proof attempt that shows a proposed contribution was already known may close that investigation, but does not silently satisfy the program’s desired significance. It prompts a scope decision or an honest shortfall report. Hours, lines of code, numbers of definitions, tests, and new terminology are never substitutes.

### 3.5 Minimum nonvacuity

The mandatory model suite must jointly include at least three distinct interfaces; genuinely composable mixed-interface profiles; repeated occurrences of one identical component type receiving different substitutions; primitive witnesses of arity at least two; nested trees with at least four ordered leaves; competing binary groupings; parallel distinct witnesses where the selected model permits them; a nonempty, nontrivial equational theory; and different wholes with identical ordered restricted pieces because cross-piece information has been lost.

An empty signature, a single terminal model, a one-object example, or a table containing only identities may be a sanity check but cannot be the principal certificate. Numeric thresholds are coverage minima, not a substitute for mathematical substance. Raising their size without adding a phenomenon does not increase research credit.

### 3.6 Integrated result acceptance

The following bundles prevent a technically correct but shallow collection of isolated deliverables from passing:

- Witness presentation and refinement: A4, B3–B4, C5. Require sound-and-complete semantics, effective reasoning in the selected fragment, and the comparison of refinement arrows, not only ordinary tree associativity.
- Full decomposition equipment: D1–D3, E1–E5. Require the complete virtual theory, universal presentation and original-profile recovery, with ordinary representability and exactness treated separately.
- Coherent cuts and incidence: F1–F4, I3–I4. Require quotient/witness-sensitive recognition, a positive all-degree typed model, structural lifting limits, and the incidence/interchange consequences.
- Observation and recovery: G1–G3, I2, I5. Require explicit recoverable information and lower bounds, mixed context/cut minimization, and structural compatibility or noncommutation of repairs.
- Modular reasoning: I1 together with B3, D3, and E2. Require conservative combination across genuinely shared interfaces and transport of the accepted certificates.

Every bundle must connect to the common parameterized models, identify its strongest established baseline, and be used by at least one other bundle or a concrete reasoning task. Definitions, standard corollaries, and elementary negative controls remain valuable infrastructure but do not separately discharge a bundle's scientific floor. These are conjunctive acceptance requirements, not a menu from which an implementer may pick the easiest result.

### A4 Decidable fragment and limits

**Class and claim:** M. Establish one useful, nontrivial decidable equational fragment and an explicit boundary beyond which decidability is not asserted.

**Inputs:** A3; a finite, typed, occurrence-linear rewrite specification with decidable matching and a stated reduction order. ## 5 Workstream B Refinement and semantic comparison

### B1 Raw refinement category and occurrence coherence

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the raw leaf-graft refinement category, prove its category laws, and characterize its thinness and antisymmetry for the selected planar syntax.

**Inputs:** A1; leaf-indexed forests and typed grafting.

**Artifact:** Category construction; unique recovery of the graft forest when valid; a proof of the tree-prefix characterization; and a theorem identifying finite graft-event partial orders and showing that complete histories for the same forest differ precisely by interchange of independent events. **Discharge:** L for the category laws, typed forest composition, and core occurrence correspondence. P or L for the full prefix and chronology comparison. If raw refinement is a poset, the result must say so; artificial parallel arrows must not be added to make it look richer.

**Tests:** Repeated labels at different positions; independent grafts in both orders; a dependent graft that cannot be interchanged; nullary grafts when enabled; and a failed attempt to refine an existing internal vertex rather than a leaf.

**Dependencies:** A1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove raw thinness and antisymmetry as well as the category and chronology comparison. A free category of execution traces, a reachability relation with lost forest data, or category laws proved only after erasing occurrence positions does not pass.

### B2 Evaluated refinements and comparison functors

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the semantic refinement category for a general target calculus and prove that interpretation induces a functor from raw refinements.

**Inputs:** A2, B1, and an interpretation $F(S)\to M$.

**Artifact:** Explicit objects and arrow tuples; the evaluation functor; its identity and composition laws; and sufficient hypotheses for reflecting or preserving the distinctions the report claims.

**Discharge:** L for the basic construction and functor laws where representation permits; P for the general comparison. **Dependencies:** A2, A3, B1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Construct actual arrow tuples and a functor for arbitrary target calculi, then distinguish the nonthin semantic example from raw prefix refinement. Showing equal evaluations of two trees or naming a category without arrow composition does not pass.

### B3 Quotients and comparison coherence

**Class and claim:** B. Determine when forming the presented witness calculus and then its refinement category agrees with an explicitly specified categorical quotient or completion of the raw refinement category.

**Inputs:** A3–A4 and B1–B2; the interacting C5 presentation family and an independently specified categorical completion. The relations must have a finite or effectively enumerable basis under the selected hypotheses, rather than being defined as every equality true in the intended semantic target.

**Artifact:** A precise comparison construction, a sufficient criterion for agreement, and a necessity analysis within the named class. **Discharge:** Either a comparison theorem covering a nontrivial presentation class, or an obstruction showing exactly why the proposed quotient fails together with a repaired construction and positive theorem, or a sharp impossibility boundary for the stipulated class. A statement that quotients are subtle is not a result.

**Tests:** Associativity equations; an equation that identifies different internal structures; a witness-collapsing semantics; and a case where quotienting objects alone fails to account for arrow witnesses.

**Dependencies:** A3, A4, B1, B2, C5. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The comparison must cover an unbounded presentation family with genuine witness identification and characterize which information its arrows retain. A quotient of objects alone, one associative-tree example, or assuming the comparison is full/faithful as a hypothesis does not pass.



### C1 A finite typed graph-cut family

**Class and claim:** M. Build a model family that simultaneously exhibits nontrivial interfaces, repeated occurrences, nonreconstructive cuts, and competing refinement trees.

**Inputs:** A1–A3. Use finite simple graphs with explicitly ordered vertices, a bound $N\geq6$, and a finite category with at least three objects and nonidentity composable arrows. A three-object chain is an admissible starting case.

**Discharge:** P for the family and all calculus laws; L for a typed nontrivial fragment; C for the finite test instance. The report must identify exactly which data are stored in a witness, in a whole, and in a leaf. It must not erase the whole graph while relying on it in the proof.

**Dependencies:** A1, A2, A3. **Status:** OPEN.

### C2 A relation-based interface model

**Class and claim:** M. Develop a second model with genuinely different semantics and explicit boundary changes.

**Class and claim:** M. Give a semantic presentation theorem for the parameterized typed graph-cut family, then identify exactly how finite witness decorations change it. This is the central link between the free syntax, the models, and the independent-cut theory.

### F1 Cut criterion and its distinction from substitution

**Class and claim:** M. Specify the additional structure that allows an evaluated operation to be cut coherently along intervals, and prove that ordinary substitution laws alone do not supply it.

**Inputs:** A typed witness calculus $M$ and a finite or locally finite set-valued setting. The starting candidate is bijectivity of a factorization map for each proper contiguous interval:

$$\coprod_K M(p/K;W)\times M(p_{[i,j]};K)\longrightarrow M(p;W).$$

Here $p/K$ replaces the selected subpath by the single component $K$, and the map is the corresponding substitution. All intermediate boundaries and the exact interval range must be specified.

**Artifact:** An exact criterion with normalized unary and nullary/unit conditions where needed; a theorem relating unique interval factorizations to coherent cuts; and a counterexample showing why generic free or presented calculi do not automatically satisfy the criterion.

**Discharge:** P. The candidate must be corrected if it is too strong, too weak, or missing unit hypotheses; corrections are recorded through the amendment process before acceptance. It is not enough to name an axiom 2-Segal and then restate it as the theorem.

**Tests:** Multiple factorizations, no factorization, two incompatible cuts, and missing unit data. Check all boundary cases separately.

**Dependencies:** A3, B2, B3, C1, C5. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove a structural recognition criterion tied to C5 and B3, including units and coherent iteration. Naming the desired property as an axiom, or observing that a nontrivial monoid label has two factorizations, does not pass the research package.

**Tests:** Both triangulations of a quadrilateral; pentagon compatibility; a noninjective ordinary Segal comparison showing that different wholes share the same pieces; outer restrictions that discard cross-block edges; empty-block degeneracies; nonidentity interface arrows; and repeated component occurrences. **Dependencies:** C1, F1. **Status:** OPEN.

## 13 Formalization boundary and current seed

The mandatory Lean slice is intentionally bounded. It covers the typed path/profile and tree representation; occurrence-indexed substitution and frontier laws; the fibre-level nonsymmetric multicategory laws; the genuine free extension theorem; the core parallel congruence and quotient-substitution construction; raw refinement composition; basic semantic comparison; selected invariants; and a nontrivial typed model or rewrite fragment. If completing this slice reveals a foundational representation defect, the defect must be repaired rather than concealed by moving the theorem to prose.

The full virtual-equipment presentation/reconstruction theory, ordinary-equipment completion analysis, interface descent, recognition/2-Segal comparison, all-degree graph-cut proof, model classifications, and observational limits may be discharged by complete pen-and-paper proofs with finite executable support. Full Lean formalization of double categories, groupoids, simplicial objects, or infinity-categories is not part of this baseline. Optional formalization is reported separately and cannot inflate the mathematical completion percentage.

The specifically verified seed currently contains output-indexed typed trees, occurrence-wise substitutions, grafting, both unit laws, an associativity statement through composed substitutions, a correspondence with structural leaf positions, and ordered frontier/grafting results. It also includes examples of different binary groupings with equal typed frontiers, repeated component occurrences receiving different replacements, and nullary loop witnesses. The final seed verification passed for these specific claims. The accepted artifact ledger must attach its exact declarations and build evidence; this narrow verification is not acceptance of broader obligations.

The seed's operation fibre does not yet by itself provide the complete fibre-level multicategory composition/coherence package. A recursive evaluation uniqueness theorem is not yet the free multicategory universal property. None of A1–A4, B1–B4, or the whole program is declared discharged on this basis. The final ledger must attach the exact checked declarations, source hashes, and review findings rather than relying only on this progress summary.

### 13.1 Explicit deferred scope

“Full” means complete at the defined classical virtual-equipment level, including its morphisms, transformations, universal presentation, coherence, and the stated ordinary-equipment branch. It does not mean every conceivable enrichment.

The following remain a visible backlog, not hidden omissions or mandatory outcomes: arbitrary symmetric or parallel wiring; causal feedback, traced, or cyclic extensions beyond what a chosen model actually supplies; continuous or stochastic dynamics; physiological identification or claims about real systems; full infinity-categorical generality; and complete Lean mechanization of all equipment, descent, simplicial, and observational theorems. Each would need its own definitions, hypotheses, acceptance floor, and authorization before becoming an active commitment. The planar path core does not silently acquire those capabilities.



## 15 Stage gates and stopping decisions

### Gate 0 Baseline approval

Freeze the core definitions, obligation versions, candidate model conventions, formalization boundary, and effort assumptions. Before an affected proof attempt begins, append its exact quantified target, model/morphism class, comparison maps, and any allowed obstruction alternative. In particular, resolve the strict/coherent E2 universal-property convention, the I1 common-interface embedding class, the I2 allowed observation/probe family, the I3 target quotient span, and the I4 output structure. These are explicit pre-proof specification gates, not permission to choose an easy class after seeing results. No choice may reduce the minimum floors without a material amendment. This document is the proposed baseline. Approval of it is distinct from authorizing every later phase to run unattended or incur external costs.

### Gate 1 Foundational viability

Require A1–A3 and the basic parts of B1–B2, with verified nontrivial examples from C1. Decide whether the representation supports the intended multicategory and quotient universal properties. If it does not, stop the dependent claims and repair or amend the core. A compiling recursive tree datatype is not enough.

### Gate 2 Model and novelty viability

Require the principal models, nonreconstruction theorem, decidable-fragment result, and the mature baseline comparison. State what remains mathematically substantive beyond established multicategory and restriction-species machinery. If only a renaming remains, report that outcome and seek a direction decision rather than certifying the original research ambition.

### Gate 3 Extension viability

Evaluate D–F against their bounded classes. Require the positive typed finite 2-Segal model regardless of whether the broad recognition claim succeeds. Require the full E1–E3 virtual-equipment construction and universal presentation, not merely a positive specialization. E4 ordinary representability/completion and E5 faithful exact assembly may use only their specified bounded obstruction branches. Unsupported infinity-categorical generalization cannot rescue an unsuccessful finite claim.

### Gate 4 Synthesis and identification

Require semantic and observational distinctions and all I-workstream mechanisms to agree with the proved comparisons and counterexamples. Check that no reconstruction, CULF, exactness, or empirical claim has been strengthened in the exposition beyond the theorem.

### Gate 5 Independent acceptance

Run H1–H3. Completion requires the full accepted ledger, not a selectively summarized list of successes. The final report must state the actual assurance level, including whether review was agent-managed and whether optional external human review occurred. It may not imply an external certification that was not obtained.

Each gate produces a short decision record: continue under the frozen plan; repair within scope; propose an amendment; or stop with a precise negative outcome. No gate permits silently deleting a hard obligation.

## 16 Amendment and failure protocol

Every amendment receives a new version, rationale, affected obligation IDs, old and new claims, dependency impact, and a revised effort estimate. Obtain approval before materially weakening acceptance conditions, changing a target class, dropping a required comparison, or expanding authorized scope. Routine stronger proofs, equivalent formulations with unchanged scope, and in-scope implementation repairs do not require repeated user decisions. Whenever possible, fix criteria before observing the outcomes they will judge.

An unexpected counterexample may motivate an amendment after discovery, but it may not be used to relabel the original claim as passed. Preserve the original version and its FAILED or SUPERSEDED status, the counterexample, and the reason for the replacement. A replacement theorem is a new obligation with its own review. Changing from M to B, shrinking a model class, dropping a unit law, weakening faithfulness, or excluding a difficult boundary case is a material amendment.

Maintain a failure register containing false conjectures, failed proof strategies that reveal a genuine issue, counterexamples, rejected encodings, and failed negative controls. Keep a separate backlog for optional extensions and unresolved questions. Backlog items do not count as discharged obligations and do not become implicit promises of future execution.

Progress reporting must distinguish definition complete, proof drafted, formalized, tested, independently verified, and accepted. A tool log or author assertion cannot promote its own result to accepted. Preserve all negative results needed to understand why the final theory has its stated hypotheses.
