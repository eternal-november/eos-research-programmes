# Matched anonymous FIFO deadline obstruction and slack repair

Status: restricted two-world result under the stated physical and information premises. This is not an impossibility claim for every payload model or every anonymous FIFO device.

## 1. Physical experiment and legal information

Fix Q>0 and 0≤γ<Q. There are two addressed independent, work-conserving, nonpreemptive FIFO servers, one per address, with fixed service time Q and zero delivery lag. The initial calibration state is empty at a Q-grid time. Every request admission/release, including calibration, uses the atomic clocked API at a time in Qℤ. No fractional-grid request release, cancellation that preempts service, speed modification, origin-bearing acknowledgment, queue-load read, payload reread, or direct role-load read exists. An admission can be chosen adaptively; arbitrary serial, partial, concurrent, and repeated calibration is allowed. All calibration time, requests, and computation are charged, and the argument does not impose a finite preset number of probes. Policies must be executable (no infinitely many actions before a finite clock boundary).

Each invocation samples an independent fair bit, independent of all other invocation bits, policy randomization, and queue timing. It provides exactly one callback when service completes. Its observable packet is `(completion timestamp, payload bit)`. The raw stream orders packets even at equal timestamps. A fixed unknown parameter π is either (1,2) or (2,1), and orders the two callbacks when one completion on each server occurs at the same timestamp. No role or invocation identifier is carried. With service Q, each server completes at most one job at a timestamp. The two worlds differ **only** in π. Request inputs cannot select or influence payloads. A singleton callback has no observable rank coordinate other than being the sole callback; it does not reveal the tie arbitration rule.

At exogenous mission time 0, two specified fresh service jobs become available, one for each address and distinct physical role. Earlier calibration invocations cannot substitute for them, and neither service payload may be prefetched. A completed service job loads its bit into its corresponding physical role-load latch; these are the two mission loads. The task is to complete both specified jobs and deliver the correct ordered vector of their loaded bits, with the authorized interlock pulse, by D=Q+γ. The physical bridge stipulates that a wrong vector/pulse is unsafe; incomplete work, absence of a valid pulse, and late completion are failures. Aborting is not success. Known post-read reading/computation/actuation capability suffices within γ (γ=0 is the idealized instantaneous case). The original hardware in both worlds already provides safe role-load retention and isolation through at least 2Q+γ plus any stipulated final physical tail; holding a completed first load until the repaired deadline requires no hardware or safety-capability change. This bridge, including retention, isolation, two distinct role-specific loads, and the meaning of the pulse, is a separate engineering premise, not inferred from anonymous callbacks.

Policies may guess, act before reading, abort, wait, leave calibration backlog, and randomize. They cannot evade the independent work requirement by predicting bits for jobs they never execute. A controller that outputs early is not assumed to fail merely because it guessed: the proof grants it its best possible interpretation and still bounds success. If an early physical pulse itself causes a hazard, counting that hazard can only strengthen the bound. No additional uncontrolled actuator output or analog observation is available. Multiple candidate pulses cannot repair an incorrect pulse: any wrong pulse is already a failure, so the proof may retain a putative successful run’s valid mission vector without assuming a one-action policy.

## 2. Observable filtration and complete interaction law

Let H_t consist of the public clock, all of the controller's own admissions/actions, ordered received `(time,bit)` packets, and any fixed public task parameters through t. Include the policy's private random tape R when conditioning on its decisions. A policy is any measurable nonanticipating action kernel relative to this information (with admissible grid releases). In particular it may act on partial callback prefixes, when the implementation permits that, and may choose its calibration length adaptively.

**Interaction-law lemma.** For every such policy, its complete raw calibration transcript has the same law under π=(1,2) and π=(2,1). The same statement holds for raw service interaction before any role-dependent physical outcome is revealed.

Proof: conditional on the public action history, every FIFO service start and completion time and every number of packets due is determined by the action schedule and Q, not by π or any payload. Pending invocation bits not yet reported are fresh independent fair bits conditional on the public history. A singleton emits a fair bit. At a tie, either π order emits an ordered pair of independent fair bits, with exactly the same conditional law. This remains true one packet at a time within the pair: the second bit is independent of the first and of any intervening policy action. Policies have the same next-action kernel at equal histories. Induction over clock/action/report events therefore gives equal finite transcript laws. Equivalently, couple the worlds by assigning the same fresh fair bit to each public report position; at a tie those bits attach to opposite invocation identities, while each world's per-invocation law remains independent fair. The coupled public actions and transcript are identical. This extends to adaptive stopping and all executable interaction histories by the usual cylinder-event construction. The mission loads themselves are deliberately not part of H_t.

Consequently no calibration statistic, even one using arbitrary singleton or asynchronous probes, has a different distribution between the two π worlds. Under the equal prior on π, its posterior remains equal after every calibration history. This is a property of this iid witness subclass, not a universal assertion about finite identification under arbitrary payload laws. Calibration involving a dangerous role-dependent interlock outcome would be a different observation/experiment and is not provided by this diagnostic callback API.

## 3. Grid/backlog lemma (including pre-zero calibration)

Starting empty on the Q-grid, all starts and finishes are on that grid: induction uses `start=max(release, previous finish)` and `finish=start+Q`. Thus at time 0 a server is either available for its fresh service job or its next possible service-job start is at least Q. A calibration completion exactly at 0 leaves no positive residual delay; any positive work ahead of the service job delays its start by at least Q. Queueing a calibration request ahead of that service job at 0 has the same effect.

Because D<2Q, completion of each specified fresh job by D requires its start to be 0. Its release cannot precede availability 0, so it must be admitted at 0 with no positive work ahead. Thus **every successful execution** admits and starts both fresh jobs at 0, and both finish/report at Q. Any late admission or positive pre-release backlog yields incomplete mission work at D and is a failure, regardless of guesses or pulses. Jobs behind a current service job cannot provide extra reports before D. Pre-zero jobs finishing exactly at 0 can provide additional calibration bits, but their complete history has the same π law. Neither aborts nor hypothetical preemptive actuator decisions alter the nonpreemptive service recurrence.

The integer-grid hypothesis is essential here. Off-grid service times could create residual calibration workload smaller than γ and separate the mission callbacks inside the deadline. For the variant service time kQ, k a positive integer, the same proof gives D=kQ+γ and the same one-quantum repair below.

## 4. All-policy risk lower bound

Let failure include missed, incomplete, abort, late, or unsafe outcomes as above. For any one uniform policy P that is not told π, write R_12(P) and R_21(P) for its failure probabilities in the two worlds, including fresh bits and its own randomization.

Couple the two worlds using the interaction-law coupling and the same policy random tape. Let E be the event that both mission jobs start at 0; E is fixed before their fresh reports and is identical in the coupled worlds. On Eᶜ, neither world can succeed by the grid/backlog lemma. On E, write (X,Y) for the ordered pair of report bits at Q. These are independent fair bits, independent of the pre-Q history and E. The required ordered role vector is (X,Y) in world 12 and (Y,X) in world 21.

Grant the policy the entire raw transcript through D before it chooses its vector/pulse; this only enlarges its information relative to early actuation, sequential reading, or aborting. No other reports between Q and D can originate from later jobs, and any permissible actions in that interval reveal no role binding. When X≠Y, a single chosen vector cannot be correct in both coupled worlds. When X=Y, success in both is possible. Thus conditional on E, the sum of the two success probabilities is at most

    2·Pr[X=Y] + 1·Pr[X≠Y] = 3/2.

Since Pr(E)≤1, the equal-prior success probability is at most 3/4. Therefore

    (R_12(P)+R_21(P))/2 ≥ 1/4,
    max{R_12(P),R_21(P)} ≥ 1/4.

This covers policies that intentionally do no work, decline to answer, release late, act prematurely, use calibrated randomization, or retain backlog. Those policies cannot turn missing work or a refused pulse into success. In particular no policy uniformly guarantees zero-risk completion in this two-world class. Actual pre-action role-load observations or permitted calibration outcome feedback would invalidate the information premise and require a different theorem.

**Sharpness in this subclass.** Start both jobs at 0, flip an independent fair coin to guess π, assign the ordered callbacks accordingly, and issue the computed vector/pulse by Q+γ. For either true π, error occurs exactly when the priority guess is wrong and the two payload bits differ: probability (1/2)(1/2)=1/4. Thus the two-world minimax failure risk is exactly 1/4. This is a mathematical risk comparator, not a safety recommendation.

**Hard-safety versus combined loss.** A uniform policy with zero probability of a wrong physical vector/pulse in either world cannot pulse on a differing-bit transcript: both worlds have the same positive transcript law and require different vectors there. Hence its completion probability is at most 1/2 in each world. The bound is attained by starting both jobs at 0, pulsing `(X,X)` when X=Y, and safely refusing when X≠Y. Its hazard risk is zero but its counted missed-or-unsafe failure probability is 1/2, not 1/4. The equal-bit case is a concrete joint-compatibility acceptance without full identity acquisition; the differing-bit case demands refusal under hard safety. The randomized 1/4 comparator instead allows hazard risk and must not be advertised as a hard-safe policy.

**Informed oracle.** An otherwise identical controller told π starts both jobs at 0, maps their ordered Q-time callbacks to roles using π, and actuates by D. It completes with zero error for every realized payload pair on the same hardware and budget. It knows no future bits and performs the same physical service work.

## 5. Matched repair: one extra clock quantum, no tags

Change only the task deadline to D'=2Q+γ, using the original hardware's already supplied safe retention/isolation horizon through that time and the final physical tail. From an idle production boundary, admit job 1 at 0 and job 2 at Q. Their callbacks occur at Q and 2Q, respectively. Distinct timestamps and the known schedule assign each packet to its job regardless of π and regardless of payload values. Store the first bit, read the second, and issue the correct role vector/pulse within γ. Both actual fresh jobs complete; risk is zero for all payloads. No identification of π, callback tags, payload pilots, or extra service invocation is needed.

This schedule uses the same fresh-job availability, two independent queues, clocked API, and fixed service durations. If calibration has been performed, pay it and drain it before time 0; the constructive repair need not perform any calibration. For service kQ, use releases 0 and Q, callbacks kQ and (k+1)Q, and deadline (k+1)Q+γ.

Among feasible extensions with deadline still strictly below 2Q (for the Q-service fixture), the obstruction remains because a positive distinct start offset costs at least Q. We do not claim Q is the smallest real-valued increase from Q+γ under arbitrary faster-than-γ computation; only that adding one quantum suffices and every deadline below 2Q retains the lower bound.

## 6. Scope and relation to joint compatibility

Both π worlds are actual physical instances of the broader anonymous FIFO class with a strong zero-jitter margin; server partition and duration can already be known. The negative is not an inability to learn the queues. It is a budget/capability obstruction to identifying the fresh role binding even after unrestricted charged callback calibration. On differing payloads the two physical bindings give conflicting role vectors; on equal payloads they agree, illustrating why full binding identification is stronger than required mission-output identification. The separated-time repair removes binding ambiguity by control, not by discovering a tag or changing the payload law.

This is the standard indistinguishability/two-hypothesis decision argument applied to an explicitly costed raw FIFO interaction and an independently stipulated mission. It does not establish a novel universal learning lower bound or a physical interlock hazard law. The constructive acquisition/compatibility results remain essential alongside this deliberately small matched boundary.
