\title{Law and Possibility Repair for Dependent Protocols\\[5pt]
\large A compositional finite repair of the all path observation gap}
\date{1 November 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The probability-law interpretation of the O1 mixed construction preserves
finite probability and dependent composition, but cannot preserve universal
claims over all retained raw histories. A zero-weight branch can change
not only cost but the number and ownership of the continuations. We make
this obstruction exact and repair its semantic bridge without banning
zero weights or returning to full raw-history identity.

The repaired observation is a pair $(\mu,S)$: one finite probability law
and a finite nonempty set of joint possible outcomes containing its
positive support. We prove its monad laws, an occurrencewise tree and cost
composition theorem, compatibility with every admitted O4 constructor
and ordinary-data frame, and natural forgetting to the old probability
semantics. It retains precisely the full joint law and all predicates of
joint outcomes interpreted universally over raw histories; it still
forgets raw multiplicity and witness identity.

A probability coupling together with candidate possibility coverage is
proved equivalent to the existence of an original O1 paired-history span
whose candidate projection is raw-history-surjective. Positive coupling
pairs plus zero-weight fillers give the constructive converse. This
criterion composes through result-dependent bind and separated batch,
and applies to the parameterized destructive-diagnostics family of O6.
An independent presence-enriched coefficient semiring realizes the repair
inside the O1 input theorem. Its native maps deliberately reject old
non-surjective zero-branch insertions. Its two-sided native spans are not
confused with the asymmetric original-O1 refinement certificates.

The result is finite, parameterized and genuinely dependent. It is not a
solution of the general probability--nondeterminism distributive-law
problem, a continuous-support theorem, a full-abstraction result, or a
claim of historical novelty. The requested deliverable is TeX source;
compilation and proof-assistant certification are not assumed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and its exact increment}\label{sec:scope}
O7 of the active contract~\cite{Contract} asks for an obstruction to an
actual positive theorem or semantic bridge, followed by a useful proved
repair connected to O1 and a selected application. We test the bridge
from retained O1 executions to the O3 finite probability semantics, in
the O4 language and O5 observation regime~\cite{O1,O3,O4,O5}. The
plausible overextension is:
\begin{quote}
Because the bridge preserves the full joint probability law and every
admitted composition, it also preserves all-raw-path specifications,
including universal resource budgets.
\end{quote}
This statement is false. The failure is information loss and unsound
observation transfer, not failure of the original interaction law.

O3 already exhibited the zero-weight cost obstruction and named retention
of raw paths as a repair. O5 already proved that a candidate-surjective
paired span transfers all-path bounds. Those results are reused, not
reclaimed. The new increment is a smaller compositional law-and-possibility
quotient, its exact finite span-recognition theorem, an independent O1
coefficient realization and a genuinely dependent application. Neither
repeating the old two-row example nor saying ``retain the paths'' would
suffice.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded obstruction and positive repair]\label{thm:main}
Fix a finite-signature instance of the O3--O4 protocol language, the finite
planar causal-tree monad $T$, and finite vector execution costs
$C=\mathbb N^d$, $d\geq1$. Interface sets may vary; all primitive witness
fibres and all trees have finitely many occurrences. Keep complete typed
leaf states during composition and the admitted continuation discipline
of Section~\ref{sec:raw}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item On the parameterized diagnostics subfamily of
Section~\ref{sec:application}, no interpretation factoring through the
full joint finite law can decide every all-raw-path upper budget.
This remains true although the law interpretation preserves pure maps,
occurrence bind and batch, and although an offending inclusion is an
actual cost-exact old O1 native square.
\item The carriers
\[
 MX=\{(\mu,S)\in\Dfin X\times\Pfin X:\supp\mu\subseteq S\},
 \qquad KX=M(C\times TX)
\]
carry the explicit compositional structures proved below. The map from
raw executions to $K$ preserves every admitted typed constructor,
substitution and ordinary-data framing. Probability forgetting is a
natural monad morphism to the old finite-law execution semantics.
\item Equality in $K$ is exactly agreement of the full joint law and
of every universal predicate of the retained joint outcome. It need not
imply equality of raw witnesses, multiplicities or native cells.
\item A finite probability coupling supported on the required
body/state/cost relation, together with candidate possibility coverage,
is equivalent to the existence of an original O1 paired-history span
with a surjective candidate-history projection, a cost-exact reference
leg and a cost-lowering candidate leg. These existence judgments are
closed under the admitted dependent constructors and compose vertically.
\item The presence-enriched semiring $\E$ of
Section~\ref{sec:rig} supplies an independent O1 input realization of
law and possibility, with a strong forgetful rig map to the old
probability--cost input. All original raw objects lift with every raw
row marked present, but precisely the surjective original native maps
lift between these all-present objects. Thus not all old cells survive
as exact repaired comparisons.
\item The repaired branch includes all admissible finite diagnostic
kernels, including zero weights, arbitrary legal result-dependent arities
and resource partitions, and a nonidentity family of covered audit
implementations. The explicit two-hole zero branch is correctly rejected
as an all-path refinement of its one-hole-only counterpart.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The theorem is not a claim about arbitrary raw-history-dependent
continuations, unrestricted private-state erasure, infinite trees,
continuous kernels, unbounded recursion or general concurrency. Its
positive class is the same finite typed family, not a finite list of
exceptional examples. Ordered costs other than $\mathbb N^d$ can replace
$C$ in the direct $K$ construction whenever addition is commutative and
monotone. The specific O1 coefficient realization below uses the inherited
min-plus vector rig.

\section{The retained model and the observation interface}\label{sec:raw}
An O4 interface $F$ has finite public configurations $b$, a declared
bundle of owned token identities and kinds $\Delta_F(b)$, and semantic
private states for those actual tokens. Write $\widehat F$ for its full
state carrier. A tree leaf is labelled by an element of $\widehat F$,
not just a public return value. Distinct leaf occurrences own disjoint
subsets of the actual input inventory, as required by the type system.
A token name in ordinary data is an alias, not an additional owner.

At an input $a$, a retained computation has a finite nonempty carrier
$\Omega_a$, normalized weights $p_a(h)\geq0$, finite costs $c_a(h)\in C$,
and a body $\theta_a(h)\in T\widehat F$. Zero-weight rows are genuine
members of $\Omega_a$. The joint outcome and raw quotient are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-quotient}
 o_a(h)=(c_a(h),\theta_a(h)),\qquad
 \qobs_a=(\mu_a,S_a)
 =\left(\sum_{h\in\Omega_a}p_a(h)\delta_{o_a(h)},\ o_a[\Omega_a]\right).
\end{equation}
Thus $S_a$ records a joint cost, causal tree, typed postconfiguration and
needed private state. It is not a set of separate marginal observations.
The fixed O5 normalization may remove declared private unary events when
comparing implementations. Its tree-multiplication and occurrence
compatibility are inherited from O5. Private stores are not erased
between calls; a declared public observation may erase them after closing.

For a prefix witness $h$ with leaves $b_j$ indexed by its actual address
set $\leaf(\theta_a(h))$, old O1 composition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-bind}
\begin{split}
 \Omega_{P;R}(a)&=\coprod_{h\in\Omega_P(a)}
       \prod_{j\in\leaf(\theta_P(h))}\Omega_R(b_j),\\
 p(h,\mathbf k)&=p_P(h)\prod_jp_R(k_j),\qquad
 c(h,\mathbf k)=c_P(h)+\sum_jc_R(k_j),\\
 \theta(h,\mathbf k)&=\theta_P(h)[\theta_R(k_j)]_j.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
An empty product is a singleton of weight one and cost zero. Equal-valued
leaves at different addresses are different occurrences and make separate
choices. No common-output-label sampling or implicit sharing is used.

\paragraph{The continuation restriction.}
Admitted continuations depend on the retained typed leaf input, including
private semantic state where relevant, and not on discarded raw witness
labels. This is the O3--O4 discipline, not a new program restriction.
Two raw prefixes with the same joint tree and leaf states have identical
continuation kernels and possibility choices at corresponding addresses.
Without this condition the quotient in~\eqref{eq:raw-quotient} need not
compose: a continuation could distinguish two hidden raw labels that it
has already identified. Full state is semantic bookkeeping; clients do
not thereby obtain a private-state oracle.

The original coefficient rig and native squares remain explicit. Put
\[
 \Cost=(\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\})^d,\quad
 c\longrightarrow c'\ \Longleftrightarrow\ c\geq c',\qquad
 \mathcal V=(\mathbb R_{\geq0})_{\rm disc}\times\Cost.
\]
Direct sum is $(p,c)\oplus(q,e)=(p+q,\min(c,e))$ and tensor is
$(p,c)\otimes(q,e)=(pq,c+e)$. For endpoint maps
$u:\widehat E\to\widehat E'$ and $v:\widehat F\to\widehat F'$, a native
square has functions $r_a:\Omega_a\to\Omega'_{u(a)}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old-native}
 p'_{u(a)}(y)=\sum_{r_a(h)=y}p_a(h),\qquad
 c'_{u(a)}(y)\leq\min_{r_a(h)=y}c_a(h),\qquad
 Tv\,\theta_a(h)=\theta'_{u(a)}(r_a(h)).
\end{equation}
The empty minimum is $\infty$. ``Cost-exact'' means equality of source
and target costs on every source witness; it does not mean that $r_a$
is surjective. Distinct functions $r_a$ remain distinct native cells.
Neither $\qobs$ nor any equality of its values is claimed to retain all of
those cells faithfully.

\section{The precise law only obstruction}\label{sec:obstruction}
For a raw presentation $P$ and predicate $A\subseteq C\times T\widehat F$,
define
\[
 \All_A(P)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       (\forall h\in\Omega_P)\ o_P(h)\in A.
\]
In particular $\All_B(P)$ denotes $c_P(h)\leq B$ for every raw $h$,
coordinatewise. This is different from the almost-sure condition
$\mu_P\{(c,t):c\leq B\}=1$.

\begin{theorem}[No law factoring decision for all raw budgets]
\label{thm:obstruction}
For every pair of integers $1\leq\ell<H$ in the legal finite
diagnostic construction of Section~\ref{sec:application}, and every
$B$ with $\ell\leq B<H$, there are well-typed finite programs $P,Q$ with
the same input and output interfaces such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item their full joint causal-tree, typed-result and cost probability
laws are equal;
\item $\All_B(P)$ holds and $\All_B(Q)$ fails;
\item $P$ has a cost-exact original O1 native square to $Q$ with
identity endpoints, and the law bridge sends it to equality;
\item the extra raw branch of $Q$ has two actual resource-separated
continuation holes instead of one, and both children are executed.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, for every set $Y$, every map $F$ from joint finite laws to
$Y$, and every function $d:Y\to\{0,1\}$, the identity
\[
 d(F(\Law(R)))=\mathbf1_{\All_B(R)}
\]
cannot hold for every program $R$ in this family. In particular no law
factoring compositional interpretation with an exact all-path decision
map can satisfy that identity, even if it preserves all the pure and
composition operations already preserved by the O3 law bridge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $P$ retain only the low branch of probability one and final cost
$\ell$. Let $Q$ retain the same branch together with the well-typed high
branch of probability zero and final cost $H$. The two joint laws are
the same point mass at the completed low outcome; the high tree contributes
zero mass even though its shape differs. The raw cost conditions give
(b). The inclusion of the low raw row has correct weight on its image;
the missing high target row has mass zero and satisfies its cost inequality
against the empty minimum $\infty$. The body equation holds on the low
row. Thus~\eqref{eq:old-native} holds and this is a cost-exact old square.
Occurrence composition with the typed continuations gives (d) and the
same square on completed executions. Finally $F$ and $d$ receive the same
argument on $P$ and $Q$ but would have to return different truth values.
\end{proof}
This theorem rules out a specified stronger preservation claim, not
existence of the old semantics, of a different observation semantics, or
of a general interaction law. The counterexample preserves the old pure
embedding and operations; it changes the information demanded from their
semantic image. Requiring strictly positive raw weights would remove this
particular obstruction, but would discard permitted finite tables and is
not the repair chosen here.

\section{A finite monad of one law and its possible outcomes}\label{sec:M}
\begin{definition}
For a set $X$, let $MX$ be the pairs in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For
$f:X\to Y$ define
\[
 Mf(\mu,S)=(f_*\mu,f[S]),\qquad
 \eta^M_X(x)=(\delta_x,\{x\}).
\]
If $k(x)=(\nu_x,S_x)\in MY$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-bind}
 (\mu,S)\mathbin{\gg=_M}k
   =\left(\sum_x\mu(x)\nu_x,\ \bigcup_{x\in S}S_x\right).
\end{equation}
The first sum is over the finite positive support of $\mu$; its extension
by zero to $S$ is harmless. Only $k$ on $S$ is needed.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The law and possibility monad]\label{prop:M}
The preceding formulas define a commutative monad on $\Set$. Both
projections $\pi_D:M\to\Dfin$ and $\pi_P:M\to\Pfin$ are monad
morphisms. The canonical independent product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-product}
 (\mu,S)\boxtimes(\nu,R)=(\mu\otimes\nu,S\times R).
\end{equation}
The empty product is $(\delta_{()},\{()\})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pushforward preserves total mass and takes support into the image of
support, so $Mf$ is well defined. In~\eqref{eq:M-bind} the law is
normalized. Its positive mass at $y$ comes from some $x$ with
$\mu(x)>0$ and $\nu_x(y)>0$; then $x\in S$ and $y\in S_x$.
The union is finite and nonempty. The two unit identities follow from
Dirac laws and singleton unions. If $l(y)=(\rho_y,R_y)$, both
associativity sides have law and possibility set
\[
 \sum_{x,y}\mu(x)\nu_x(y)\rho_y,
 \qquad \bigcup_{x\in S}\ \bigcup_{y\in S_x}R_y.
\]
The finite sums can be reassociated and the unions are equal. These
identities also prove the projection equations. The two orders of
independent bind give the product law and $S\times R$, up to the
specified swap; symmetry of real multiplication and Cartesian products
proves commutativity. Naturality, associativity and units of the products
follow by the same finite-tuple bijections.
\end{proof}
Equivalently, $M$ is the support-compatible submonad of the componentwise
product of the finite probability and finite nonempty powerset monads.
This is standard elementary monad machinery. The chosen $S$ may strictly
contain $\supp\mu$: a zero-weight possible outcome is precisely the
additional datum of interest.

The meaning of $S$ is the image of retained raw verification possibilities.
It is not an operational scheduler, and a member of $S\setminus\supp\mu$
is not asserted to occur with positive probability. There is one specified
law, not a convex family of alternative laws. Arbitrary nondeterministic
choice with all of its usual interaction axioms is not being added.

\section{Occurrence dependent tree and cost composition}\label{sec:K}
Let $T$ be the finite planar tree monad over the fixed ranked event
signature. Its unit is a leaf and its multiplication is grafting. Write
$UX=C\times TX$. The unit of $U$ is $(0,\eta^T x)$; its multiplication
adds the outer cost and the costs on all actual inner leaves, and grafts
the inner trees. Commutative addition and the bijection
\[
 \leaf(t[t_i]_i)\cong\coprod_{i\in\leaf(t)}\leaf(t_i)
\]
prove the unit and associativity laws of $U$. This construction does not
replace a tree's actual positions by its set of leaf values.

Given $k:X\to KY$ with $k(x)=(\nu_x,R_x)$ and an outcome
$z=(c,t)\in UX$, let $I=\leaf(t)$ and write $x_i$ for its leaf labels.
Define the graft-and-sum map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graft-map}
 g_z:\prod_{i\in I}UY\longrightarrow UY,
 \quad ((c_i,t_i))_i\longmapsto
          \left(c+\sum_i c_i,\ t[t_i]_i\right).
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 L_k(z)=(g_z)_*\bigotimes_{i\in I}\nu_{x_i},\qquad
 R_k(z)=g_z\left[\prod_{i\in I}R_{x_i}\right].
\]
For $I=\varnothing$, this is the point law and singleton possibility at
$(c,t)$, not an empty possibility set. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K-bind}
 (\mu,S)\mathbin{\gg=_K}k
   =\left(\sum_{z\in UX}\mu(z)L_k(z),\
               \bigcup_{z\in S}R_k(z)\right),
 \qquad \eta^K(x)=(\delta_{(0,\eta^T x)},\{(0,\eta^T x)\}).
\end{equation}
The sum and union only use finite carriers.

\begin{theorem}[The occurrence composition law]\label{thm:K}
These formulas make $K=M U$ a monad. They give a distributive law
$\lambda:UM\Rightarrow MU$ by occurrencewise traversal. They are
natural in leaf relabelling and preserve the complete joint cost/tree
outcome. In particular the law includes sample-dependent arities,
nullary trees and equal-valued distinct occurrences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $L_k(z)$ is normalized; its support lies in $R_k(z)$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:M}. A finite nonempty union of the latter is
finite and nonempty. Thus~\eqref{eq:K-bind} is well defined.

For the left unit the outer tree has one occurrence, outer cost zero and
one possible outcome. For the right unit every child is the single leaf
with cost zero and unit mass. The unit equations for tree grafting prove
both $K$ unit laws, in both law and possibility coordinates.

For associativity retain the following complete data before taking any
image: an outer outcome $(c,t)$; for each outer occurrence $i$, a middle
outcome $(c_i,t_i)$; and for each leaf $j$ of $t_i$, an inner outcome
$(c_{ij},t_{ij})$. Both parenthesizations of three-stage composition
range over exactly these finite dependent choices. In the possibility
coordinate every declared choice is retained, even if an outer or child
probability is zero. In the law coordinate the unaggregated weight is
\[
 \mu(c,t)\prod_i\nu_{x_i}(c_i,t_i)
                  \prod_{i,j}\rho_{y_{ij}}(c_{ij},t_{ij}).
\]
The total cost is $c+\sum_i c_i+\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}$. The final tree is
$t[t_i[t_{ij}]_j]_i=(t[t_i]_i)[t_{ij}]_{i,j}$, using the addressed
leaf bijection. Reassociation and finite distributivity identify the
weights after summing all presentations of an identical final outcome;
the same choice bijection identifies the image sets. Empty factors obey
the stipulated singleton convention. Hence the $K$ associativity law
holds exactly, not only up to equality of probabilities after dropping
cost or tree shape.

For completeness, $\lambda_X$ takes $(c,t)$ with each leaf labelled by
$(\mu_i,S_i)\in MX$ to the independent product choices pushed along
$(x_i)_i\mapsto(c,t[x_i]_i)$. It satisfies all four Beck equations:
\begin{align*}
 \lambda\circ\eta^U_M&=M\eta^U,&
 \lambda\circ U\eta^M&=\eta^M_U,\\
 \lambda\circ\mu^U_M&=M\mu^U\circ\lambda_U\circ U\lambda,&
 \lambda\circ U\mu^M&=\mu^M_U\circ M\lambda\circ\lambda_M.
\end{align*}
The first two equations are respectively the one-leaf product and the
product of singleton Dirac choices. The third equation compares nested
outer/inner trees: both sides have the addressed choices $(x_{ij})_{i,j}$,
the same sum of deterministic costs and the same grafted tree. Their
products and Cartesian products coincide. For the fourth equation, a
leaf carries a law-and-possibility object whose outcomes are themselves
such objects. Both sides first select an intermediate pair at each
address and then a value from that pair. Finite distributivity gives the
same sum of products of weights; distribution of finite Cartesian
products over unions gives the same possible tuples. An empty address
set has one empty tuple on both sides. Relabelling leaves commutes with
every selection and graft map, proving naturality. The induced composite
monad bind is exactly~\eqref{eq:K-bind}.
\end{proof}
This theorem derives the needed interaction rather than assuming a
law that already includes it. It does not assert that $M$ itself is a
cartesian monad or that arbitrary monads distribute over $T$.

\begin{corollary}[Natural probability forgetting]\label{cor:forget}
Let $K_DX=\Dfin(C\times TX)$ with the same occurrence formula using
only probabilities. The map $\pi_D:K\to K_D$, $(\mu,S)\mapsto\mu$,
is a natural monad morphism. The possibility projection similarly maps
$K$ to the occurrence monad $K_PX=\Pfin(C\times TX)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both unit equations are immediate. Taking either coordinate of
\eqref{eq:K-bind} gives its target bind formula. The relabelling equation
is the corresponding pushforward or image equation.
\end{proof}

\section{The typed interpretation and data frames}\label{sec:interpretation}
\begin{theorem}[Compositional raw quotient]\label{thm:interpretation}
For every admitted finite O4 command $t:E\Rightarrow F$ and every full
input $a\in\widehat E$, the $K$ interpretation obtained from primitive
raw tables satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interpretation}
 \den{t}_K(a)=
 \left(\sum_{h\in\Omega_t(a)}p_t(h)\delta_{(c_t(h),\theta_t(h))},\
       \{(c_t(h),\theta_t(h)):h\in\Omega_t(a)\}\right).
\end{equation}
This identity is preserved by pure return, primitive operation,
result-dependent case, occurrence bind, separated batch, pure
substitution, open-command substitution and the finite rank-decreasing
procedure invocation of O4. It commutes with the ordinary-data framing
of O2.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a primitive it is the definition. Return has one raw witness of
weight one and cost zero, so gives $\eta^K$. Case selects the same public
branch from the same full typed input; it does not condition a law on an
unobserved event. For bind, the raw choices are exactly
\eqref{eq:raw-bind}. Prefixes with the same retained joint outcome use
the same continuation family by the continuation restriction. Their
weights can therefore be aggregated before or after taking the child
products. Finite distributivity gives the law in~\eqref{eq:K-bind};
taking the image of all raw tuples gives its union of Cartesian-product
images. Duplicates are collapsed only after this joint calculation.
Zero-weight prefixes and children remain in the second coordinate.

Separated batch takes the product of the child raw carriers conditional
on the actual full input, then adds child costs and places their trees
under the declared $\mathsf{Fork}$ constructor. Formula
\eqref{eq:M-product} followed by that deterministic map gives exactly
its joint law and image. Disjoint ownership is supplied by typing;
product laws do not turn initially correlated token states into
independent states. Pure substitution relabels the input or output and
commutes with sums and images. Open-command substitution uses the bind
and case identities at the substituted occurrence. Procedure invocation
is proved by induction on the strictly decreasing finite rank and then
on its body. Ordinary data captures do not hide owned capabilities.
These are the O4 constructors; no recursion principle beyond those
inductions is invoked.

For an ordinary data frame $Z$, let
\[
 f_z:C\times T\widehat F\longrightarrow C\times T(Z\times\widehat F),
 \qquad f_z(c,t)=(c,T(b\mapsto(z,b))(t)).
\]
The framed interpretation is $M f_z(\mu,S)$. Both ways of framing a
bind put the same $z$ at every final leaf and leave costs unchanged.
Return gives the frame unit equation. Relabelling $z$ and a leaf commutes,
so frame naturality and the associativity/unitor comparisons follow by
the same leaf equations. On raw objects the witnesses and coefficients
are unchanged. This is exactly O2's external data action, not copying of
capabilities or mutable stores. A nullary tree has no leaf that can retain
$z$; no frame-information reflection is asserted.
\end{proof}
After a command closes, any declared deterministic observation map $o$
can be applied to both coordinates by $Mo$. Its possibility set then
preserves all predicates of that observation, not predicates of private
information that $o$ deliberately discards. Applying $\pi_D$ gives the
old O3 finite law and hence its established finite-discrete QBS inclusion.
There is no new general QBS support or measurable-selection theorem.

\section{Exactly what the quotient preserves and forgets}\label{sec:information}
\begin{proposition}[Observation completeness for all joint predicates]
\label{prop:predicates}
For every raw presentation $P$, every joint outcome predicate $A$ and
$\qobs(P)=(\mu_P,S_P)$,
\[
 \All_A(P)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_P\subseteq A.
\]
For two presentations over the same joint outcome set, $\qobs(P)=\qobs(R)$ if
and only if their full joint probability laws agree and their universal
truth values agree for every such predicate $A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first equivalence is the definition of image. If the pairs are
equal, all the observations agree. Conversely equality of laws gives
$\mu_P=\mu_R$. If $S_P\neq S_R$, a point in their symmetric difference
is excluded by a predicate containing the other set, which distinguishes
the universal truth values. Hence the sets must agree.
\end{proof}
Thus $\qobs$ is precisely the quotient by the declared observation
equivalence. Any representation preserving and reflecting all these
observations must distinguish different $\qobs$-values. This is a bounded
observation statement, not a universal categorical completion. In
particular it is not minimality for scalar budgets alone: the truth
values of all scalar upper bounds see only the maximum cost, not the
entire set of joint outcomes.

A raw family with two equal-outcome rows of masses $1/3,2/3$ and a
single equal-outcome row of mass one have the same quotient. Splitting
an atom into several raw indices, raw multiplicity, unused provenance
and the distinct functions implementing native maps are still lost.
They remain available in the separate O1 presentation and certificate
layer when needed.

\begin{proposition}[Exact equality via two covered raw spans]
\label{prop:exact-span}
Let $P,R$ be raw presentations over the same full endpoint carrier and
joint outcome set $Z=C\times T\widehat F$. Their repaired observations
are equal if and only if they admit an original O1 span whose two raw
projections are surjective and cost-exact, and whose bodies have equal
full joint outcomes. At this fixed observation level every $MZ$-value
has a canonical raw representative with carrier $S$ and weights $\mu(s)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\qobs(P)=\qobs(R)=(\mu,S)$. Retain every pair in the finite fibre
product
\[
 H=\{(w,v):o_P(w)=o_R(v)\}.
\]
Both projections are onto because the image sets agree. Give a pair
with common outcome $z$ weight
\[
 \Gamma(w,v)=
 \begin{cases}p_wq_v/\mu(z),&\mu(z)>0,\\0,&\mu(z)=0.\end{cases}
\]
For a positive $p_w$, summing $q_v/\mu(z)$ over the matching fibre
is one. For $p_w=0$ the row is zero, including when $\mu(z)=0$.
Thus the marginals are $p,q$. Give each pair its common cost and tree.
All native equations hold, with exact costs on both legs; zero pairs
are retained to ensure coverage. Conversely push the common weights
along the legs to obtain equal laws, and use the two surjections and
body equalities to obtain equal image sets. The canonical representative
uses each $z\in S$ once with its specified cost and tree; its normalized
weights are $\mu(z)$, including zero values.
\end{proof}
This proposition is at a fixed full observation interface, after any
specified compatible normalization. It does not silently identify
different private endpoint types; those require the related-state
construction in the next section.

\begin{proposition}[The exact direct-cell boundary]\label{prop:direct-boundary}
For a cost-exact original native square~\eqref{eq:old-native}, let
$\bar v(c,t)=(c,Tv(t))$. Then, at each input,
\[
 \bar v_*\mu_a=\mu'_{u(a)},\qquad
 \bar v[S_a]\subseteq S'_{u(a)}.
\]
Equality of repaired observations after this transport holds exactly
when $\bar v[S_a]=S'_{u(a)}$. Raw surjectivity of $r_a$ suffices, but is
not necessary when missing raw rows duplicate outcomes already covered.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The native mass and exact-body equations give the pushforward law.
Every source outcome appears as its target outcome, giving inclusion.
The definition of $\qobs$ then makes the extra equality condition necessary
and sufficient. A surjective raw map covers all target outcomes; a
nonsurjective map can still cover them if distinct rows have the same
joint outcome.
\end{proof}
For cost-lowering maps the joint exact-cost laws need not be equal.
Their graph instead gives the inherited monotone-cost coupling, with
the precise body/state relation specified below. The repair does not
turn that order into equality. In particular the ghost insertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} cannot be sent to equality by a semantics
that also reflects all joint universal predicates.

\section{Possibility sensitive refinement and exact span recognition}
\label{sec:refinement}
Fix candidate and reference full endpoint carriers, related owned-token
private states, and the common normalized public event signature of O5.
The lifted outcome relation $\mathcal R$ holds between
$x=(c,t)$ and $y=(d,u)$ when $t,u$ have the required equal public tree
shape, corresponding leaves have related full typed states, actual
capability identities and kinds correspond as declared, and $c\leq d$.
Matching trees have the same addressed leaf occurrences. These are the
specified comparison conditions, not arbitrary consequences of having
some native cell. Write the candidate first.

\begin{definition}[Law coupling and candidate possibility coverage]
\label{def:refinement}
For $(\mu,S)\in K\widehat F_P$ and $(\nu,R)\in K\widehat F_Q$, put
\[
 (\mu,S)\preceq_{\mathcal R}(\nu,R)
\]
if both of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item there is a finite probability coupling $G(x,y)$ of $\mu,\nu$
whose positive support lies in $\mathcal R$;
\item for every $x\in S$ there exists $y\in R$ with $x\mathcal R y$.
\end{enumerate}
The second condition is candidate possibility coverage. It does not
require positive coupling mass on zero-weight pairs, and it does not
require every reference possibility to have a candidate partner.
\end{definition}

If predicates $A_P,A_Q$ are compatible in the sense that
$x\mathcal R y$ and $y\in A_Q$ imply $x\in A_P$, then reference
universal validity transfers to the candidate. Indeed, for each candidate
possibility choose its reference partner and apply that implication.
Every common coordinatewise upper cost bound has this property. The
coupling condition separately supplies the old O5 probabilistic
conclusions. Neither condition is substituted for the other.

The next criterion is fibrewise. At one chosen related input, the input
relation carrier in its diagram can be that singleton. If the hypotheses
hold at every pair in a finite related-input carrier, the constructed
fibres assemble into the displayed full endpoint square.

\begin{theorem}[Exact marked original O1 span criterion]
\label{thm:span}
Let $P,Q$ be finite original O1 raw presentations at a related full input,
with outcome maps $a:\Omega_P\to C\times T\widehat F_P$ and
$b:\Omega_Q\to C\times T\widehat F_Q$. All raw rows, including
zero-weight rows, are retained. Then
\[
 \qobs(P)\preceq_{\mathcal R}\qobs(Q)
\]
if and only if there exists a finite common paired-history presentation
$W$ with native endpoint-square legs
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 &W:E_{PQ}\Rightarrow F_{PQ}&\\[-2pt]
 {}_{(\pi_P^E,\pi_P^F)}\swarrow&&
          \searrow_{(\pi_Q^E,\pi_Q^F)}\\[-2pt]
 P:E_P\Rightarrow F_P&&Q:E_Q\Rightarrow F_Q
 \end{array}
\]
such that every common raw history has the specified paired public body
and related leaf states, its cost is the projected reference cost, its
candidate cost is no greater, and the candidate raw projection is
surjective. The legs are in the original probability--cost O1 model;
the candidate-coverage mark is extra certificate data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that Definition~\ref{def:refinement} holds. Write $p_w,q_v$
for the raw weights and $\mu,\nu$ for their outcome laws. As in the
O5 raw-lift lemma, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-lift}
 \Gamma(w,v)=
 \begin{cases}
 G(a(w),b(v))\dfrac{p_w}{\mu(a(w))}\dfrac{q_v}{\nu(b(v))},
       &\mu(a(w))\nu(b(v))>0,\\[5pt]
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For $p_w>0$, sum first over the raw reference fibre of each outcome $y$.
Its conditional factors $q_v/\nu(y)$ sum to one, so the coupling
marginal gives
\[
 \sum_v\Gamma(w,v)
    =\frac{p_w}{\mu(a(w))}\sum_yG(a(w),y)=p_w.
\]
If $p_w=0$, every summand is zero. The other marginal is symmetric.
Thus the positive-pair carrier is normalized and covers each positive
candidate raw row. Each positive pair is in $\mathcal R$.

Now take each candidate raw row $w$ not yet covered. Necessarily $p_w=0$.
By candidate possibility coverage, $a(w)$ has a partner $y$ in the raw
image of $Q$ with $a(w)\mathcal R y$. Choose a raw $v$ above $y$ and
adjoin $(w,v)$ to the common carrier with weight zero. This is a finite
choice, made for the actual input under discussion. Distinct duplicate
candidate rows are covered separately, even when their joint outcomes
are equal. Adding the rows changes neither marginal and gives a
surjective candidate projection.

Every retained pair has matched tree addresses and related leaves, hence
a paired tree over the state-relation carrier. Give it cost $c_Q(v)$.
The two witness functions are projection to $w$ and to $v$. Their
probability equations are exactly the marginals. Their body equations
are the two paired-tree projections. For each reference fibre the target
cost equals every common cost in that fibre. For each candidate fibre
the target cost is at most every common cost, hence at most their minimum.
An empty target fibre has mass zero and its cost inequality holds against
$\infty$. These are precisely~\eqref{eq:old-native}. Nonnegative common
costs and total mass one give the inherited global augmentation. This
constructs the required original O1 span.

Conversely push the common probability weights along the pair of raw
projections and then along $(a,b)$. The native marginal equations give
a coupling of $\mu,\nu$, and the stipulated paired-body and cost
conditions put its support in $\mathcal R$. For any candidate outcome
$x\in S_P$, choose a raw $w$ above it. Surjectivity provides a common
history above $w$; its reference projection provides a raw reference
outcome $y\in S_Q$ with $x\mathcal R y$. This proves candidate
possibility coverage.
\end{proof}
The conditional formula~\eqref{eq:raw-lift} and the native cost argument
are inherited from O5 and reproduced to show the exact hypothesis match.
The new part is the converse use of semantic possibility coverage to
adjoin all needed zero fillers, giving the equivalence. The statement
concerns existence of a finite certificate, not a natural canonical
choice of every filler or equality of all chosen spans. A particular
previously supplied span may omit zero histories; the theorem constructs
an adequate span from the semantic conditions.

Candidate possibility coverage is weaker than raw surjectivity of a
\emph{specified direct map}. Nevertheless it is equivalent to existence
of a suitable raw-surjective \emph{span}, because zero fillers can cover
all duplicate raw rows. These two assertions do not conflict.

\begin{theorem}[Contextual closure of the repaired criterion]
\label{thm:contextual}
Consider the complete finite O4 context class and tokenwise private-state
relations of O5. Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(L\arabic*)}]
\item the joint initial state laws have a relation-supported coupling,
and every declared candidate initial possibility has a related reference
initial possibility;
\item at every related full method input, not just positive-probability
reachable inputs, the primitive outcome pairs satisfy
Definition~\ref{def:refinement};
\item every other client primitive satisfies the same relation and
coverage conditions, and the public observations, normalizations and
ranked code interpretations are the fixed ones used by the comparison;
\item disjoint owned bundles restrict and reassemble the state relation,
and continuation bodies respect the retained typed interface.
\end{enumerate}
Then every admitted finite open context preserves the repaired criterion.
Every closing context has the O5 law coupling and candidate possibility
coverage, and hence transfers all compatible all-raw-path predicates,
including coordinatewise upper costs. Its certificate is an actual
occurrence-composed original O1 span with surjective candidate projection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The law part is the O5 conditional coupling induction, not a new
uncorrelated-product argument. For precision, at a paired prefix retain
its complete related private leaf states. At each actual matched address
use the continuation coupling for that input pair; multiply these
conditional child matrices and then mix with the prefix coupling. When
summing one side, each child's marginal is that side's original kernel
at its actual input. Finite distributivity therefore gives the required
marginal law. Equality of public tree shape and monotonicity of cost
addition give the supported relation. This is the O5 calculation with
exactly the same occurrence indices.

For the possibility part, fix any candidate possible prefix outcome.
Coverage chooses a matching reference possible prefix. Fix any candidate
child outcome at each of its actual addresses, including zero-weight
choices. By the inductive coverage premise choose a related reference
child at each matched address. Their finite tuple is a declared reference
choice; the grafted tree has related leaves and its cost dominates the
candidate's sum. Thus every candidate composite possibility is covered.
If the prefix is nullary, there is exactly one empty tuple on each side.

At the raw-certificate level apply the same construction to an arbitrary
candidate raw prefix and each of its raw child histories. The local span
surjections supply paired lifts. Their tuples are precisely the O1
occurrence carrier~\eqref{eq:raw-bind}, so the composite candidate
projection is surjective. Its reference cost is the sum of reference
costs; its candidate leg is cost-lowering. The O1 marginal and grafting
calculations give native endpoint squares, not merely abstract couplings.

Return is diagonal. A public case selects corresponding branches.
Separated batch applies the same finite-tuple construction to disjoint
children conditional on the complete paired input, and retains the
reserve. Substitution and framed ordinary data preserve the selected
relations. Induction on body structure and decreasing invocation rank
handles procedure calls, aliases and all the bounded callbacks admitted
by O4. Finally the initial coupling mixes the law part, and initial
possibility coverage supplies a partner for every candidate raw starting
point. At no stage is an initially correlated joint state law replaced
by a product of its marginal token laws.
\end{proof}
The stronger premises are justified by the stronger conclusion: without
candidate coverage, Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} refutes the conclusion.
They are independently checkable local finite conditions. They are not
the desired global all-context theorem smuggled into its hypotheses.

\begin{proposition}[Vertical composition of existence judgments]
\label{prop:vertical}
The repaired relation is reflexive at identity state relations and
transitive under composition of the specified state relations. Together
with Theorem~\ref{thm:contextual}, this gives compositional existence
judgments and proof-relevant original O1 certificates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use diagonal law couplings and identity possibility witnesses for
reflexivity. For couplings $G(x,y)$ and $D(y,z)$ with common middle law
$\nu$, retain middle outcomes and give $(x,y,z)$ weight
\[
 J(x,y,z)=
 \begin{cases}G(x,y)D(y,z)/\nu(y),&\nu(y)>0,\\0,&\nu(y)=0.\end{cases}
\]
Summing the other endpoint yields the original adjacent coupling; after
summing $y$ this couples the outer laws. Zero middle mass forces the
associated marginal fibres to be zero, so no undefined division is used.
Compose the possibility partners existentially, including zero outcomes.
State-relation composition and transitivity of cost order give the outer
relation. For related inputs and leaves, retain the needed middle state
witnesses before gluing. Disjoint tokens permit their reassembly. Finally
Theorem~\ref{thm:span} realizes the outer judgment by a native marked
span. Alternatively the horizontal constructions already give explicit
native occurrence certificates.
\end{proof}
This is not a strict double category of chosen conditional couplings,
a canonical horizontal--vertical interchange theorem, an equipment, or
a faithful image of every O1 parallel cell. Only the stated existence
judgments and explicit witnesses are claimed.

\section{An independent presence enriched O1 coefficient input}
\label{sec:rig}
\begin{definition}
Let
\[
 \E=\{(p,b):p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0},\ b\in\{0,1\},\ p>0\Rightarrow b=1\}.
\]
Define
\begin{align*}
 (p,b)+(q,e)&=(p+q,b\lor e),&0_{\E}&=(0,0),\\
 (p,b)(q,e)&=(pq,b\land e),&1_{\E}&=(1,1).
\end{align*}
The nonzero coefficient $\epsilon=(0,1)$ records a declared possible
outcome of probability zero. It is different from the absent coefficient
$0_{\E}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The coefficient realization]\label{prop:E}
$\E$ is a commutative semiring. Finitely supported normalized
$\E$-valued distributions on $X$ are exactly $MX$. The projection
$\varphi:\E\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$, $(p,b)\mapsto p$, is a unital
semiring homomorphism realizing probability forgetting.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If a sum has positive real component, one summand is positive and its
flag is one, so the resulting disjunction is one. If a product has
positive real component, both factors are positive and both flags are
one. Hence the operations are closed. Associativity, commutativity,
units and absorption are the componentwise real and Boolean identities.
Boolean conjunction distributes over disjunction, as real multiplication
distributes over addition, proving distributivity.

Given $(\mu,S)$, assign coefficient $(\mu(x),1)$ to $x\in S$ and
$(0,0)$ elsewhere. Its finite coefficient support is $S$ and its sum is
$(1,1)$. Conversely such a normalized coefficient distribution has total
real mass one, a finite nonempty flag-one set $S$, and positive real
support inside $S$. These operations are inverse. Coefficient
multiplication and summation give the product and bind formulas already
proved for $M$. The projection preserves all four operations and units
componentwise.
\end{proof}

Use the discrete semiring category $\E_{\rm disc}$ and put
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal V}=\E_{\rm disc}\times\Cost.
\]
Its direct sum and tensor use the semiring operations with minimum and
addition on costs. Its units are $(0_{\E},\infty)$ and $(1_{\E},0)$.
The global augmentation of a finite family requires total coefficient
$(1,1)$ and minimum cost at least zero; it is not an all-path budget.
Every original executable raw row is lifted with flag one, even if its
probability is zero. A formal flag-zero row permitted by the ambient rig
is an absent or dummy summand, not an extra raw execution counted by
our all-path assertions.

\begin{theorem}[O1 input match and strong forgetting]\label{thm:O1-input}
The terminal-grade causal-tree content and
$\widetilde{\mathcal V}$ satisfy the independent hypotheses of the
O1 theorem. Their occurrence mixed construction restricts to an
all-present raw execution fragment, closed under return, bind, batch,
pure framing and the finite O4 syntax. Forgetting flags is a strong
comparison of the independent rig inputs and their induced mixed
constructions. On joint outcome observations it is
$\pi_D:K\to K_D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the same ambient base $\Set$ and admissible class of all set maps
as O6 A. Identities, pullbacks and composition stay in that class, and
$T$ preserves membership trivially. Proposition~\ref{prop:E} with the
min-plus cost rig gives an independently supplied coherent symmetric rig
category. The finite tree monad is polynomial, with the unit and
multiplication cartesian by the one-leaf and addressed grafting
bijections. Its content-grade fibres are terminal; the occurrence display
is $\id_T$. The covariant transport, cartesian grade lifts and their
Beck--Chevalley comparison are the unique terminal-grade maps. The unit
and accumulation occurrence equations are exactly the leaf-address
identities. These checks precede and do not assume the desired mixed
composition or a proposed comparison certificate. The O1 independent
criterion therefore supplies its full law on native maps and endpoint
squares.

Within the actual execution fragment every row flag is one. A finite
tuple of actual child choices has product flag one; a prefix together
with that tuple also has flag one. A nullary child product has the
multiplicative unit flag one. Return and the typed constructor induction
therefore keep the fragment all-present. Aggregating equal outcomes uses
disjunction, so its flag is one exactly when some raw outcome represents
it. The coefficients consequently reproduce~\eqref{eq:raw-quotient}.

The map $\varphi\times\id_{\Cost}$ is strict on the displayed sums,
products and units and is therefore strong as a rig functor. Leave the
content input and its display maps unchanged. Every occurrence product,
fibre sum, unit and accumulation diagram is sent to the corresponding
old diagram, since the real component of each coefficient is unchanged.
Thus the O1 parameter comparison is strong, including changing-endpoint
squares and the pure embedding. Applying it to observed coefficients
is exactly forgetting $S$ while retaining $\mu$.
\end{proof}
The old independent O1 theorem is the structural source of the retained
native calculus; the direct finite $K$ proof supplies the smaller
observation semantics. We are not asserting a new general theorem for
arbitrary graded contentads, nor revisiting the deferred display/codisplay
branch.

\begin{proposition}[The native preservation cost]\label{prop:E-native}
An original native map between fixed all-present raw objects lifts to
$\widetilde{\mathcal V}$ if and only if its raw witness function is
surjective in every input fibre. Cost and body conditions are unchanged.
Consequently the old ghost insertion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} has no all-present enriched lift.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The new coefficient equation at a target row $y$ adds exactly
\[
 1=\bigvee_{r(h)=y}1.
\]
An empty disjunction is zero; thus this equation holds precisely when
the fibre is nonempty. With that condition, the real-weight equation is
the original one and all other native equations are unchanged. The
missing zero target row of a ghost insertion has an empty fibre and
violates this equation.
\end{proof}
Marking every actual row present is a constructorwise program lift, not
a semiring section in the opposite direction: the assignment
$p\mapsto(p,1)$ sends the old additive zero to $(0,1)\neq0_{\E}$.
No strong rig embedding of all old coefficients or cells is asserted.
The strong rig map proved above is the forgetful direction.

Objects and zero-weight programs are retained. The loss is specific old
native arrows as exact presence-preserving comparisons, not the old
programming operations or probability laws. Flag forgetting retains
the same witness functions, and is faithful on these native hom-sets;
it is generally not full. No conservative recovery of every old cell
is claimed.

\paragraph{Why the two span notions are different.}
An enriched native span with two all-present endpoints forces both raw
projections to be surjective. Theorem~\ref{thm:span} only requires the
candidate projection to be surjective, and correctly lives in the
original O1 model with a coverage mark. For example, a candidate with
law $\delta_t$ and possibility set $\{t\}$ refines a reference with the
same law and possibilities $\{t,u\}$, where $u$ has a different public
tree and zero mass. The candidate's single possible tree is covered;
the reference's unused $u$ need not be matched. A paired enriched span
with both projections onto cannot exist if its body relation requires
equal public trees and the candidate has no $u$. Cost zero on all rows
already suffices for this distinction. No asymmetric refinement theorem
is falsely presented as an enriched native span theorem.

\section{A local reflection and its lax reconstruction comparisons}
\label{sec:reflection}
Fix retained endpoint carriers $A,B$, exact cost/tree observations and
the all-present enriched raw objects. Restrict morphisms to cost-exact
native maps with identity endpoints. Call this category
$\mathcal C_{\rm ex}(A,B)$. It still has raw witness functions and may
have nontrivial parallel maps. Let $\mathcal D(A,B)$ be the discrete
category of realizable input-indexed pairs $a\mapsto(\mu_a,S_a)$ on
$C\times TB$. Here ``realizable'' means a finite-fibre ambient raw
presentation; it does not assert definability by an O4 source term.

The quotient gives a functor
$L:\mathcal C_{\rm ex}(A,B)\to\mathcal D(A,B)$: enriched maps are
surjective and cost-exact, so preserve both coordinates. Define
$R:\mathcal D(A,B)\to\mathcal C_{\rm ex}(A,B)$ by the canonical
representative with carrier $S_a$ and coefficient
$(\mu_a(z),1)$ on row $z$.

\begin{theorem}[Local observation reflection]\label{thm:reflection}
At these fixed endpoints and this restricted map class, $L\dashv R$,
$LR=\id$, and $R$ is fully faithful. Its unit is the canonical native
collapse of each raw history to its joint outcome. Reconstruction has
coherent comparisons
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lax-reconstruction}
 R(g)\circ R(f)\longrightarrow R(g\circ_K f),
\end{equation}
which are cost-exact and all-present enriched native maps. They are
generally noninvertible. Thus reconstruction is lax with respect to
occurrence composition, not a strong recovery of raw presentations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an object $H$ and a pair family $d$, body and cost preservation force
any map $H\to R(d)$ to send raw $w$ to its joint outcome
$(c_w,\theta_w)$. There is at most one such function in each fibre.
It is surjective and mass-balanced exactly when $LH=d$. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C_{\rm ex}}(H,Rd)
  =\begin{cases}\{\text{canonical collapse}\},&LH=d,\\
                \varnothing,&LH\neq d,
    \end{cases}
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(LH,d).
\]
This bijection is natural; the nonempty map is forced on each raw
witness. It proves the adjunction, and $LR=\id$ gives the stated
counit and full faithfulness.

The raw composite of $R(f)$ and $R(g)$ has, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:interpretation}, observation $g\circ_K f$. Applying
its unit collapse gives~\eqref{eq:lax-reconstruction}. For three
composable arrows, identify the two raw bracketings by the canonical
O1 occurrence isomorphism. Both coherence routes are cost-exact
all-present maps from that raw object to the same canonical representative.
The hom-set just proved is a singleton, so the routes agree. The same
argument proves unit coherence and compatibility with a fixed separated
batch constructor and ordinary-data framing.

For noninvertibility take a unary event primitive with two raw outcomes
of the same leaf and event shape, costs zero and one, each of probability
$1/2$. Compose it with an identical primitive. The two independent choices
$(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ have the same two-event tree and total cost one.
The composite raw representatives have two such histories, whereas the
representative of the composite has one cost-one row of mass $1/2$.
The comparison aggregates them and is not bijective. Empty capability
bundles suffice for this ambient structural example.
\end{proof}
This is a family of local reflections onto discrete joint-observation
classes, with the displayed controlled composition comparisons. It is
not a global equivalence of the original O1 bicategory with a discrete
semantics, nor an adjunction on arbitrary cost-lowering maps or differing
private endpoint carriers. Those require the distinct relational
statements of Section~\ref{sec:refinement}. The underlying raw presentation
category retains its nontrivial parallel maps, but $L$ sends parallel maps
to the same identity in the discrete target and is not faithful in general.
A canonical representative packages entire joint outcomes into raw rows;
it need not be generated by the same allowed primitive signature or have
a physical implementation. There is no compiler or executable-realization
claim for $R$. The positive branch instead interprets the unchanged
well-typed programs, independently of whether their canonical ambient
representatives are source-definable.

\section{The dependent diagnostic application and its positive family}
\label{sec:application}
This is a new boundary instance within O6 Case A, not a revision of the
previously reported O6 numerical experiment~\cite{O6}. It retains that
family's synthetic destructive-specimen model, actual token identities,
result-dependent capability kinds and disjoint returned bundles. The
numbers below isolate the O7 distinction; no empirical instrument or
human medical claim is made.

\subsection{A legal one hole and two hole screen}
Initially own
\[
 \{r_0:\mathsf{Screen},\ r_1:\mathsf{Reserve},\ r_2:\mathsf{Reserve}\}.
\]
Screen consumes $r_0$, costs one and has two declared raw rows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Result & Weight & Public constructor & Actual returned holes\\
\midrule
low & $1-p$ & unary $\mathsf{screen.low}$ &
$\{r_1:\mathsf{Confirm},r_2:\mathsf{Reserve}\}$\\
high & $p$ & binary $\mathsf{screen.high}$ &
$\{r_1:\mathsf{AssayA}\}$ and $\{r_2:\mathsf{AssayB}\}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $p\in[0,1]$. The low hole runs confirmation, consumes $r_1$ and
costs two. In the minimal instance it returns pass with the actual
remaining owner $r_2:\mathsf{Discard}$; discard costs zero and consumes
$r_2$. The high left hole runs $A$, costs three and consumes $r_1$; the
high right hole runs $B$, costs five and consumes $r_2$. Their result bits
have parameters $a,b\in[0,1]$. Every completed leaf owns no resource.
All declared raw branches are typed, even at probability zero.

The controller is the actual dependent term
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
screen ; case actual hole tag of\\
\hspace*{1em}low: confirm ; discard ; return low report\\
\hspace*{1em}high left: A ; return A bit\\
\hspace*{1em}high right: B ; return B bit
\end{quote}
It does not run both high methods on a copied bundle. The two calls fill
different holes with different owned resources, and their costs add.
The low branch has one continuation hole owning two survivors; the high
branch has two holes of different capability kinds. This is genuine
result dependence in both arity and ownership. Ordinary earlier data may
be framed into the holes; neither owner is duplicated.

Let $\tau_L$ be the completed low tree and let
$\tau_H^{ij}$, $i,j\in\{0,1\}$, be the four completed high trees with
the actual two reported bits. The repaired completed value is
\begin{align}
 \mu_{p,a,b}
   &=(1-p)\delta_{(3,\tau_L)}
      +p\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{Ber}_a(i)\operatorname{Ber}_b(j)
                           \delta_{(9,\tau_H^{ij})},\label{eq:app-law}\\
 S&=\{(3,\tau_L)\}\cup\{(9,\tau_H^{ij}):i,j\in\{0,1\}\}.
 \label{eq:app-may}
\end{align}
All bit rows here are represented, so this $S$ is unchanged when any
parameter becomes zero or one. Its elements have distinct displayed
public trees.

For the obstruction put $p=0$ and $a=b=1/2$. Compare the full raw screen
with the pruned screen whose only represented row is low. Their exact
joint completed laws are both $\delta_{(3,\tau_L)}$. The full presentation
has five possible outcomes and raw maximum cost nine; the pruned one
has one possible outcome and maximum three. Thus the external all-raw
bound three is true only of the pruned program. A budget premise has not
filtered the high branch out of the type system or execution table.

The old native inclusion maps the low row to low. Its high target fibre
is empty, sums to zero and satisfies the inherited empty-minimum cost
convention. The inclusion persists after the typed bind. In the enriched
coefficient model, its target high coefficient $(0,1)$ cannot be obtained
from the empty source sum $(0,0)$. The repaired semantics likewise
separates the two sets. The full program does not refine the pruned one
under the repaired body/cost relation: its high possibility has no
reference tree partner. The reverse candidate-to-reference comparison
is valid and needs only low coverage.

The scalar costs can be varied independently. For any integers
$1\leq\ell<H$, keep screen cost one, give confirmation cost $\ell-1$,
discard cost zero, and high assays costs $H-1$ and zero. The same legal
resource transitions give completed costs $\ell$ and $H$, proving the
parameter range used in Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}. Both high
operations still execute and consume their actual tokens. Alternatively,
for any $n\geq2$, the O6 A schema can return $n$ disjoint assay holes
after screening, each of cost $h\geq1$, versus one low disposal-bundle
hole of cost zero. The two completed costs are $1$ and $1+nh$. No fixed
arity or numerical accident is needed.

\subsection{A result dependent continuation whose zero branch also matters}
The low confirmation can have two represented outcomes: pass of weight
$1-q$ returning $r_2:\mathsf{Discard}$, and uncertain of weight $q$
returning $r_2:\mathsf{RescueD}$. Rescue costs seven and consumes $r_2$.
The continuation branches on the actual response; rescue after pass is
not merely unnecessary but unavailable at the returned kind. With both
rows represented, for all $p,q,a,b\in[0,1]$ the possible completed costs
are three, ten and nine, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-costs}
 \mathbb E[c]=(1-p)(3+7q)+9p,
 \qquad \max_{\text{all raw histories}}c=10.
\end{equation}
In particular setting $q=0$ does not remove the represented uncertain
history. The minimal obstruction above uses a genuinely one-row
confirmation table, not this two-row table with its second probability
merely set to zero. Distinguishing absence from zero mass is the purpose
of the repair.

More generally, for every finite raw prefix $h$, let $W_i(h)$ be the
maximum represented continuation cost at its $i$th actual typed leaf.
Then finite nonempty raw fibres give the exact scalar recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worst-recurrence}
 W=\max_h\left(c_h+\sum_{i\in\leaf(\theta_h)}W_i(h)\right).
\end{equation}
For vector costs, take maxima coordinatewise; the result is the least
coordinatewise upper bound and need not be attained by one common path
in all coordinates. Formula~\eqref{eq:worst-recurrence} follows by taking
the image of all occurrence tuples in~\eqref{eq:raw-bind}. Nonempty finite
choices attain each scalar child maximum; nullary trees use the empty
sum. The dependence on the actual prefix and leaf state is retained.

These bounds are external verification observations, not new runtime
oracles. A declared zero-weight raw branch is not asserted to have
positive chance, nor does the formal model certify that it is physically
executable. The theorem states precisely what the chosen retained
semantics can and cannot establish.

\subsection{A nonidentity covered implementation family}
Keep the full parameter class of O6 A: arbitrary finite inventories,
finite public and private quality states, finite menus, response-dependent
resource partitions and capability-kind changes, arbitrary normalized
finite real or rational tables including zeros, finite cost vectors, and
arbitrary admitted finite adaptive O4 clients. Initial qualities may have
an arbitrary specified joint correlation. Primitive continuations can be
any legal finite kernels, not just the deterministic terminals above.

For every candidate method raw outcome $y$ at full input $x$, choose an
independently supplied finite nonempty audit carrier $A_{x,y}$ and a
normalized conditional law $\alpha_{x,y}$ on it. The reference retains
rows $(y,a)$ with weights and costs
\[
 p_{x,y}\alpha_{x,y}(a),\qquad
 c_{x,y}+\delta_{x,y,a},\qquad \delta_{x,y,a}\geq0.
\]
The public result, causal constructor, surviving quality and resource
partition are the candidate's; optional redundant private state and
private unary audit nodes obey the same O6 A relation and the fixed O5
normalization. The audit may depend on the result and input; it is not
assumed independent of all private correlations. Candidate and reference
initializations have the O5 joint law coupling and, for an all-path
claim, candidate initial possibility coverage.

\begin{theorem}[Covered diagnostic replacement with raw zeros]
\label{thm:application}
For every such parameter instance, the candidate refines the reference
under Definition~\ref{def:refinement} in every admitted finite O4 context.
Every reference all-raw compatible predicate transfers to the candidate.
The result allows zero outer and audit weights, changing arities and
resource kinds, and noninvertible audit aggregation and cost decrease.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At each full related input the projection $(y,a)\mapsto y$ is
mass-balanced because $\sum_a\alpha_{x,y}(a)=1$. It preserves the
specified public tree and related survivor states. Its candidate cost
is at most every reference fibre cost. The carrier $A_{x,y}$ is nonempty
for every represented $y$, including $p_{x,y}=0$; hence the projection
is onto every candidate raw row. It is an actual native endpoint square,
noninvertible when an audit fibre has multiple rows or overhead is
strictly positive. Marking all rows present also gives an enriched map
in this particular direct-projection example.

Equivalently pair $(y,a)$ with $y$. The weights give the local coupling;
the nonempty audit fibre gives possibility coverage independently of
positive mass. The state relation restricts and reassembles on the same
disjoint token identities. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:contextual} with the
specified joint initialization. Its occurrence products run the actual
number of children on each branch, preserve all zero-row lifts, and
satisfy the inherited O1 native equations. Thus the conclusion holds for
all admitted contexts, not just for the numerical controller.
\end{proof}
This supplies a positive repair, rather than a negative example followed
by a refusal to compare any programs. The audit expansion and direct
projection were already part of O6 A; O7 adds their complete
law-and-possibility interpretation, exact span criterion and explicit
zero-sensitive preservation account. The independently specified O6
two-stage XOR screen also falls under the repaired criterion when its
actual outcome possibilities are covered. Equal probability laws alone
do not certify arbitrary zero-sensitive implementation compression.

\section{Existing theory and source credit}\label{sec:sources}
The project dependencies have distinct roles. O1 supplies the independent
rig/content criterion and the full retained native calculus; O2 supplies
the ordinary-data framing; O3 supplies the common raw/law interpretation
and already identifies zero-path information loss; O4 supplies the
bounded dependent typed syntax; O5 supplies conditional cost couplings,
the finite raw lift, the contextual induction and the sufficiency of
candidate coverage; O6 A supplies the diagnostic family and audit
implementation pattern~\cite{O1,O2,O3,O4,O5,O6}. This dossier proves the
smaller compositional observation repair, its exact finite recognition
and local reconstruction statements, and the explicit enriched-input
specialization. It does not relabel the old ingredients as new results.

Finite-distribution support as a finite-powerset monad morphism is an
established elementary construction, explicitly discussed by Fritz,
Perrone and Rezagholi~\cite{FPR}. Our possibility coordinate is a chosen
finite superset of that support, so it can retain declared zero-weight
outcomes; the support morphism itself is not new. Their richer
topological results do not automatically extend the present raw-possibility
interpretation to continuous spaces. No topological support is being
identified with all declared raw paths.

Varacca and Winskel~\cite{VW} study substantive interactions between
probability and nondeterminism, including indexed valuations and
alternative combination constructions. Goy and Petri\c{s}an~\cite{GP}
use weak distributive laws to recover convex-powerset structure. Those
are important baselines, not unsolved generic constructions that the
simple pair $M$ displaces. Here a single fixed probability law accompanies
an additional may-set; there is no set of scheduler-dependent laws and
no claim of freely adjoining arbitrary nondeterministic choice while
retaining all its expected equations. The construction is therefore not
a counterexample to or solution of the general distributive-law
obstructions studied there.

The proofs needed for this finite specialization are given directly.
The primary sources establish context and credit rather than hiding a
missing hypothesis match. No exhaustive priority review has been
performed for this exact carrier, coefficient semiring or local
reflection; no publication novelty is claimed.
