\section{What the robot learns}

A repair robot enters a workshop whose controls have undocumented side
effects. It needs to route a connector correctly while doing two required processing jobs on that same
connector in the fixture. The model permits either job order. A safe test can reveal that two jobs have a predictable \emph{combined}
effect, even when neither job is predictable on its own. The robot can then
do both jobs and compensate their combined disturbance before releasing the
connector. It need not first identify every hidden interaction.

This useful discovery has a sharp limit. If the robot is interrupted after
the first job, the certificate for the pair does not authorize release.
Finishing the second required job may restore a certifiable state, but it
costs time and consumes real work authority. A theorem about compensation
does not undo the first job, create an actuator, or make waiting safe
forever.

The result below answers one exact question:
\begin{quote}
When does finite evidence about a stipulated family of hidden interactions
justify a reusable compensated interface for the work actually performed?
\end{quote}
The algebraic answer is a row-space test. The operational answer additionally
requires a current physical lease, a guarded release, accounted work tokens,
and enough time. We prove the finite instance and identify the precise
existing theorems that propagate its local proof. The workshop is a motivating
interpretation of a simplified finite machine, not a physical robot
demonstration or a general method for discovering arbitrary world dynamics.


\section{The finite machine and the exact discovery theorem}
\label{sec:model}

Fix a dimension $m\ge2$, a finite time horizon, a finite collection of
individually identified work tokens, and one machine epoch $e$. A hidden
vector $\theta\in\F^m$ is fixed throughout that epoch. All additions below
are in $\F$. No probability distribution on $\theta$ is assumed.

One owned station token contains the shared routing latch, gate, and
controller state. Loading a fresh real connector with desired route
$x\in\F$ sets its private latch to $s=x$. During the pending phase, an
addressed work operation $j$ performs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
 s\longmapsto s+\theta_j,\qquad
 v\longmapsto v+e_j,
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the parity vector of the \emph{actual} work history, initially
zero. It also consumes its particular work token, emits that job's receipt,
and incurs its declared duration. Required jobs must be done after loading
the real connector. Their tokens, receipts, order, and elapsed time are
retained separately from $v$: cancellation modulo two does not cancel work
or its resource cost. The invariant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
                  s=x+\theta\mathbin{\cdot}v.
\end{equation}
There is no production readout of $s$ or $\theta$.

\paragraph{What is supplied rather than learned.}
The family \eqref{eq:work}, truthful addressed event accounting, stationarity
within an epoch, and the connection between the final latch and routing are
assumptions. Calibration has a shielded dummy mode: load a dummy with latch
zero, pulse each selected address once, read its result, and drain it before
returning to Idle. A probe $q\in\F^m$ therefore returns
$b=\theta\cdot q$. With unit durations it costs $|q|+3$ actions, including
load, read, and drain. Calibration tokens are distinct from productive work
tokens. Safe calibration, the correction actuator, gate retention, and a
physical exclusive-control lease are supplied capabilities, not conclusions
of the observations.

For $k$ probes in the same epoch, retain the ordered evidence
$E=((q_i,b_i))_{i=1}^k$ and write
\[
 A\theta=b,\qquad
 \V_E=\{\eta\in\F^m:A\eta=b\},\qquad L_E=\row(A).
\]
The actual machine witnesses $\V_E\ne\varnothing$. Inconsistent evidence
instead marks the interface invalid; an empty version space never certifies
a command by vacuous truth. Bounds on time, tokens, and evidence length make
each chosen instance a finite transition system.

\begin{theorem}[Exact task relative identification]\label{thm:identify}
For every such finite instance with $\V_E\ne\varnothing$ and every actual
work vector $v\in\F^m$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a single correction $d\in\F$ satisfies $\eta\cdot v=d$ for every
$\eta\in\V_E$;
\item $v\in L_E$;
\item some $\lambda\in\F^k$ satisfies $\lambda A=v$.
\end{enumerate}
When these hold, the correction is uniquely $\delta_E(v)=\lambda b$,
independent of the choice of $\lambda$. When they fail, two machines agree
on every recorded probe but require different corrections after this work.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v=\lambda A$, then $\eta\cdot v=\lambda(A\eta)=\lambda b$.
For two witnesses $\lambda,\lambda'$, choose $\eta_0\in\V_E$ to get
$(\lambda+\lambda')b=(\lambda+\lambda')A\eta_0=0$.
Conversely, $\row(A)=(\kerA A)^\perp$. If $v\notin\row(A)$, choose
$n\in\kerA A$ with $n\cdot v=1$. Both $\eta_0$ and $\eta_0+n$ satisfy the
evidence and their evaluations on $v$ differ. This is standard
finite-dimensional row-space and null-space duality.
\end{proof}

Thus evidence exposes a partially defined linear interface
$\delta_E:L_E\to\F$, rather than necessarily identifying $\theta$.
To support every context vector in a specified subspace $U$, probing a
basis of $U$ suffices. At least $\dim U$ independent scalar measurements
are necessary to have $U\subseteq L_E$. This counts measurements in this
noiseless linear model, not acquisition time or universal learning complexity.

\paragraph{The guarded production protocol.}
The controller retains the epoch, evidence, actual history, remaining work
tokens, target, mode, and clock. Its modes include Idle, Pending, Sealed,
Finished, Abort, and Expired. Load and Work implement the transitions above. A
\textsf{Seal} request requires all mandatory jobs complete, consistent
current-epoch evidence, and a checked witness $\lambda A=v$. Its effect
is $s\mapsto s+\lambda b$, and it retypes the station to Sealed. Even a
zero correction has a positive declared duration. Only Sealed can
\textsf{Commit}; commit releases the connector and returns the route bit.

The seal guard also checks that the actual physical lease and gate-retention
bounds cover \emph{both} seal and commit through final routing. Completed
work has no outstanding delayed latch writes, no work is admitted in Sealed,
and the supplied lease excludes other interference throughout the tail.
A software token alone does not establish these hardware conditions. A
current tag is insufficient if control can be revoked after checking it.
The unit-duration Commit below includes final physical capture; there is no
unaccounted post-Commit arrival tail.

Rejected requests cost one controller tick, emit a public rejection, and
preserve the physical latch, work tokens, mode, and evidence, except for
the clock-expiry transition described below. A rejected
request is not an instantaneous free operation: its clock advance consumes
remaining retention time. Hold is available only in Pending, costs time,
and is permitted only within its supplied retention horizon. An authorized, timely abort safely disposes of
the pending connector if that primitive is supplied, but yields mission
failure. No safety claim is made for waiting beyond the holding horizon or
for an unavailable or too-late abort. Crossing the holding bound without
Commit or Abort enters a public terminal Expired mode with a saturated
clock; no physical safety assertion is made there.

\begin{theorem}[Guarded correctness]\label{thm:production}
For every $\theta\in\V_E$, every admitted finite execution of this protocol
returns $x$ at each accepted Commit. It preserves actual work-token
accounting. This holds for public adaptive work orders, not only a fixed
batch. It is partial correctness, not a guarantee that every execution
commits or meets its deadline.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Load establishes \eqref{eq:invariant}; each actual Work preserves it by
\eqref{eq:work}. Its token is consumed once and its receipt retained.
At an accepted Seal, the new latch is
$s+\lambda b=x+\theta\cdot v+\lambda A\theta=x$.
The sealed phase and physical lease preserve that value through Commit.
Hold and rejection preserve the latch and work state while accounting for
time. Abort terminates without a successful routing output. Expired is terminal and cannot produce an accepted Commit. These are all
declared transitions, so induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

If $v\notin L_E$ and no further informative production observation is
available, no controller selecting one correction from this public history
can guarantee a correct release for all consistent machines. The two
machines in Theorem~\ref{thm:identify} have the same observations and need
opposite corrections. Random selection does not give zero-error correctness
in both. This obstruction concerns the specified decision state: further
permitted work or measurements can change it.



\section{A paid successful mission and its interruption boundary}
\label{sec:mission}

Take $m=3$ and probe $q=(1,1,0)$. The answer is
$b=\theta_1+\theta_2$ and leaves four of the eight machines possible.
Neither individual coupling is determined, and $\theta_3$ remains unknown.
The mission requires one fresh job $\mathsf A$ at address 1 and one fresh
job $\mathsf B$ at address 2 after loading the connector. It supplies one
token for each and fixes the total deadline at $T=10$ \emph{before}
calibration. All action durations are one. The absolute holding bound is $H=11$,
and the physical lease covers the entire mission through capture. Seal
reserves the two ticks needed for Seal and Commit, with completion no later
than both $T$ and $H$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.57\linewidth}rr@{}}
\toprule
Actions & Duration & Finish time\\
\midrule
Dummy load, $\mathsf A$, $\mathsf B$, read, drain & 5 & 5\\
Real Load$(x)$ & 1 & 6\\
Required Work$(\mathsf A)$ and Work$(\mathsf B)$ & 2 & 8\\
Seal with $\lambda=1$ and correction $b$ & 1 & 9\\
Commit & 1 & 10\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The real work ends with $v=q$, so its correction is the measured $b$.
Both jobs are complete and the route is $x$ at time 10 for every hidden
machine and either work order. This proves positive completion separately
from guarded correctness. The five calibration actions, two real work
actions, loading, correction, and release are all paid.

For the illustrative answer $b=1$, the retained alternatives are
\[
 \V_E=\{(0,1,0),(0,1,1),(1,0,0),(1,0,1)\}.
\]
Their individual coordinate marginals are all $\{0,1\}$, but their joint
constraint fixes the pair's disturbance to one. Replacing the joint set by
the Cartesian product of its marginals loses the useful certificate. Used
honestly, this replacement is conservative but incomplete; pretending that
isolated success implies contextual safety is a different, unsound move.

\paragraph{Why isolated success does not suffice.}
For every $\theta$, loading and immediately releasing with $v=0$ returns
$x$. Nevertheless, a surrounding job at an address with $\theta_j=1$
changes the latch. In the required two-job mission, machines with
$\theta_1+\theta_2=1$ send an uncorrected connector to the wrong route.
Before informative calibration, the same mandatory workflow admits final
disturbances zero and one, while all pre-release public production
observations agree. No choice of correction gives uniform zero-error
completion. Moving the jobs before Load, adding a production sensor, or
providing a direct reset changes this comparison and is not silently allowed.

\paragraph{The interrupted prefix.}
After only $\mathsf A$, $v=(1,0,0)\notin\operatorname{span}\{q\}$.
Both values of $\theta_1$ remain possible. The pair's certificate is not a
certificate for this prefix. Performing the remaining required $\mathsf B$
job restores $v=q$; Work, Seal, and Commit then need three units. They
finish on time from the scheduled time 7, but not if fewer than three units
remain or $\mathsf B$ is unavailable. Repeating $\mathsf A$ would need a
new token and would still omit the required $\mathsf B$ job. This repair
completes real work; it is not free undo. A timely safe abort may avoid an
incorrect release but is not mission completion: an abort begun at time 10
finishes at $H=11$, whereas one begun at 11 is too late.

\paragraph{The physical lease is essential.}
For $\theta=(0,1,0)$ the measured correction is one. After both jobs and
Seal, the latch is $x$. One unauthorized $\mathsf B$ pulse between Seal and
capture changes it to $x+1$. The evidence remains mathematically true, yet
the connector is misrouted. The supplied lease excludes this trace; a
point-in-time certificate and a software token do not.

\paragraph{Evidence has a validity boundary.}
Adding truthful rows in the same epoch shrinks $\V_E$, enlarges $L_E$, and
extends $\delta_E$; an old coefficient witness can be padded with zeros.
Withdrawing rows can shrink the certified domain: removing the only $q$
row invalidates the paired-work correction. The controller must use any
remaining valid witness, acquire new evidence only where the physical
protocol permits it, or refuse/abort. It does not erase completed work.
Withdrawal after Seal conservatively invalidates the Sealed token: only
a timely authorized Abort is then available, not a second correction or
Commit. Known reconfiguration is allowed only while quiescent and retypes
the station into a new epoch, invalidating old receipts. Contradictory evidence
invalidates the interface. Arbitrary silent drift is excluded, not magically
detected by the guard.


\section{What connects the two programmes}


\subsection{A common witness before any abstraction}

For a fixed work word $j_1,\ldots,j_n$, use variables
$\theta,s_0,\ldots,s_n,v_0,\ldots,v_n$ and the Boolean factors
\begin{align*}
 A\theta&=b,&s_0&=x,&v_0&=0,\\
 s_{i+1}&=s_i+\theta_{j_{i+1}},&&
 v_{i+1}=v_i+e_{j_{i+1}}\quad(0\le i<n).
\end{align*}
Further factors impose the actual resource, gate, clock, seal, and commit
rules. Every occurrence shares the \emph{same} $\theta$ through equality
wires. A physical run satisfies these factors. Conversely, a satisfying
assignment fixes its initial machine and determines each next state by the
transition equations; induction gives exactly a physical run with the
specified work occurrences. Thus the joint boundary relation is the
operational support, not an arbitrary gluing of local marginals.
Conditional on a separately admissible actual work word, its algebraic
disturbance relation is
\[
 K_E(v,d)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \exists\theta\;(A\theta=b\ \land\ d=\theta\cdot v).
\]
The full operational boundary additionally retains the word's resource,
time, and gate-admissibility factors; $K_E$ alone does not make every $v$
physically realizable. This is a possibility relation, not a probability
kernel. Evidence $b$ is
observed, not a control that physically selects a hidden machine.

The existing Boolean cylinder construction \cite{Cylinder} may now be
applied to these established factors: retain all shared assignments $z$ in
$X_N=\bigoplus_z\bigotimes_i M_{F_i}$. Its preparation/test pairing is
$\bigvee_z\bigwedge_i F_i$, the physical relation just proved. The existing
comparison span $X_N\leftarrow U_N\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{im}(K_!)$
normalizes the generated preparation submodule while preserving its pairing.
Only complete shared boundaries are normalized. The operational
factor-to-run proof above is the instance-specific step; the older theorem
does not discover the physical factors for us.

\subsection{Exact decoding only for the declared observations}

The existing exact-record criterion \cite{Decoder} requires
\[
 \overline P(q(a),z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists w\;(P(a,w)\land r(w)=z)
\]
at \emph{each fixed concrete source} $a$. Equivalently, concrete sources
with the same code must have the same sets of encoded complete records.
The operative requirement is source-fixed lifting, not merely existence
at some compatible source. Complete occurrence records retain their common
witness; two individually possible answers need not be jointly possible.

For guarded production, the full-state relation in the next subsection
proves exactly this stability for the retained public histories, outputs,
costs, and resource changes. Deterministic steps descend by their explicit
transition checks; composing records retains the intermediate state and
its common $\theta$. The existing exact-decoder and finite generated-test
theorems can then be used for those supplied step and cut instances. They
do not authorize extra hidden-state observations or arbitrary crossing
tests that the declared test language does not contain.

This abstraction is \emph{not} exact for unrestricted future probes when
$\theta$ is forgotten. At a fixed machine a new probe has one answer;
the union over compatible machines may contain two. For example the
$q=(1,1,0), b=1$ evidence permits both answers to the probe $(1,0,0)$,
but no fixed machine produces both. That union is useful epistemic support,
not a source-fixed exact decoder. Discovery and certified production have
different observation boundaries.

\subsection{Local simulation followed by the actual native span}

Fix $\theta\in\V_E$. Compare the wrapped physical station $P_\theta$ with
an ideal guarded station $Q_E$ that retains the same target, parity,
ordered events, work receipts, remaining tokens, epoch, clock, lease checks,
rejections, and costs, but omits the hidden latch and predicts the accepted
route as $x$. Relate Pending states by agreement of every public field and
$s=x+\theta\cdot v$, and Sealed states by agreement of public fields and
$s=x$. Initial, finished, aborted, and expired states have their corresponding
matching public fields; Expired makes no physical safety assertion.

Load establishes this relation, Work preserves it, and Seal and Commit
preserve it by Theorem~\ref{thm:production}. Hold, rejection, and timely
abort preserve their stated relation and exact public costs. Every other
admitted client primitive must do likewise; an extra latch reader or
unlogged actuator would fail this premise. The station's shared private
state remains under one owner, so no disjoint-store rule is used.

Each deterministic local table has one outcome on each side. Coupling the
two outcomes with weight one gives the required O5 local matrix, with equal
public constructor shapes, related private leaves, and equal costs.
Initialization provides the corresponding point mass; pure syntax uses
only public data. These verify O5 L1--L4 for this finite signature, rather
than assuming a theorem from endpoint agreement. O4 typing
\cite{O4} supplies legal finite plugging and owned-token discipline;
it does not supply physical exclusion.

O5's raw-lift construction \cite{O5} turns the local coupling into the
\emph{native} common-witness span, not an informal simulation arrow. For
a deterministic step, the common retained carrier is the single paired
history of weight one and its actual charged cost. Its two witness
projections satisfy the probability pushforward, body commutation, and
cost equations; both legs are cost-exact here. O5's occurrence composition
then retains the dependent continuation witnesses and sums their costs.
For fixed $E$ and epoch, the resulting open contextual theorem applies to
every admitted finite typed production client, including public adaptive choices and legal reuse
with fresh tokens. It supplies contextual closure of the local proof,
not the time bound of Section~\ref{sec:mission}. The evidence-withdrawal
and reconfiguration rules above are separate boundary protocols, not
quietly added to this fixed-evidence contextual theorem.

This argument is pointwise in $\theta$ and retains the same machine across
calls. It never resamples the coupling or invents an epistemic prior. A
probabilistic extension would need the existing full joint initialization
and local coupling hypotheses; an all-declared-possibility claim would
also need the stronger coverage premises. Neither extension is needed for
this deterministic mission.


\section{Relation to existing methods}

The row-space criterion is elementary linear algebra, and learning a useful
interface is not itself new. Angluin's exact-learning framework obtains
information from an expressly supplied query and counterexample teacher
\cite{Angluin}. Here the family of possible dynamics and the exact safe-probe
oracle are supplied instead; the finite observations do not establish that
the real world belongs to this family.

The closest architectural baseline is learning assumptions for compositional
verification \cite{Cobleigh}. That method already learns an environmental
assumption sufficient for a particular composition and can stop before
learning the entire weakest assumption. Our closed-form experiment-driven
instance should therefore not be described as a new assume--guarantee
principle. Its contribution to this application is to make the retained
joint evidence, physical authority, full-state coupling, and paid mission
boundary explicit in one small worked case.

Abstract separation logic supplies frame rules under semantic locality
conditions \cite{LocalAction}. Those conditions do not follow from an
isolated successful call. The work actions here intentionally change shared
state; a shared invariant or a verified compensated composite is the right
proof object, rather than a claim that the actions are disjoint.

Runtime assurance likewise needs an actual safe alternative.
Black-Box Simplex assumes an initially permanently safe command sequence
and a correct decision mechanism \cite{Simplex}. Assumption-based monitors
also require care over which state is observable, and rejecting an action
does not establish mission completion \cite{Assumptions}. In this finite
model the gate, bounded holding safety, exact observations, and lease are
supplied operational premises. None is inferred from the learning lemma.


\section{Evidence and limits of this result}

The general finite claims have the hand proofs above. The accompanying
independent programs exhaustively check the row-space criterion on bounded
finite instances and replay the paid mission and adversarial variants.
They cover all nonempty affine evidence fibres through $m=5$ (83,802
fibre/activity cases), 64 complete paid mission runs, and 96 interrupted
runs. Forged, stale, and withdrawn evidence and forbidden production
operations have explicit negative tests. Precise scopes and receipts are
supplied alongside the source; finite regression is not a proof for all $m$. Existing formal backbone declarations
are cited at their exact scope, not represented as a new proof-assistant
verification of this workshop model. This document is supplied as
uncompiled TeX; no rendered-layout or compiled-PDF claim is made.

What is established is a useful, partially identified compensated interface,
a successful paid mission, an obstruction outside the learned row space,
and a source-level route from one retained joint witness to all admitted
finite production contexts. What is not established is automatic discovery
of the dynamics class, safe experiments in an unknown physical world,
noise tolerance, hidden-drift detection, unbounded waiting, unrestricted
concurrency, or universal mission completion. A new physical realization
would require validating the supplied gate, lease, sensor, actuator, and
model assumptions; it is not an obligation silently added to this example.
For this bounded application, the existing machinery plus elementary
linear algebra and the explicit instance proofs suffice. The example
demonstrates no need for a new generic categorical theorem or a new
research contract.
