TYPED DECOMPOSITION MOTIVATION AUDIT
25 October 2026

MAIN CONCLUSION

Several proposed motivations make good mathematical sense, but they ask different questions. The strongest practical one is not simply that systems have decompositions: it is that a decomposition identifies enough interaction information to justify isolation, local reasoning, and safe replacement. The strongest intrinsic one is whether an independently specified system can be recovered and characterized from selected decomposition data. Backwards proof refinement is an unusually clear test case, but its elementary tree encoding is already standard.

I recommend deepening the operational isolation/replacement question first if practical significance is the priority. Use proof refinement as a sharply specified alternative or diagnostic specialization. Keep intrinsic reconstruction as a distinct foundational track rather than letting a successful application imply it. This is a recommendation about which questions deserve investigation, not a claim that the current construction answers them.

The current contract should not be discarded. It already contains much of the technical infrastructure and important safeguards. If these motivations become completion criteria, the cleanest arrangement is a small prospective governance amendment plus a separately approved motivation/suitability companion. No contract or acceptance status has been changed by this audit.

1  WHAT THE EXISTING CHECKPOINT ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES

A1–A3, B1–B2, and C3 retain their recorded acceptance at their exact scopes. C1 and C2 have verified local models but outstanding downstream requirements. The integrated A4/C5/B3 package has verified local technical results, not full contractual or scientific acceptance. B4 and the later D/E/F/G/I obligations were not activated or discharged by that checkpoint. References below to those obligations describe targets, not achieved results.

The integrated results are not empty: they provide explicit occurrence-sensitive refinement comparisons, local coherence and normal forms over an unbounded parameterized family. But the strongest comparison identifies standard scalar enrichment, profile pullback, rewriting coherence, and operator/opposite-slice machinery. No further mathematical increment was established.

That finding neither cancels the program nor proves the later questions trivial. Equally, discovering a useful new motivation cannot retrospectively turn that scientific shortfall into a pass. Technical correctness, usefulness, and novelty must be assessed separately.

2  THE CENTRAL DISTINCTIONS

A decomposition witness can establish that pieces fit a whole according to specified rules. It need not establish that they behave independently, that an environment can execute them, or that their interaction satisfies a desired property.

Four questions must therefore be separated:

• Admissibility: is this a permitted decomposition or coupling?
• Execution: what happens when the components and environment interact?
• Suitability: does that interaction satisfy the selected property?
• Replacement: can a different component be substituted while preserving it?

A model–comodel or runner construction may supply execution semantics, and may be internal to the semantic equipment rather than merely an external interpretation. But an execution pairing is not by itself a suitability theorem. The property and the admissible environment class must be specified independently of the desired conclusion.

There is also a decisive limitation in the current refinement construction. T → T[F] retains the same output whole W and expands its decomposition. It does not compare an implementation C with a different C′, or a whole W with a redesigned W′. If W already means the complete semantic system, retaining W makes behavioral preservation tautological. Safe redesign requires a separate same-interface implementation relation and a theorem lifting that relation through the allowed contexts.

3  THREE SMALL EXAMPLES THAT SEPARATE THE MOTIVATIONS

Identical pieces need not give identical interaction

Suppose two components each emit either 0 or 1. Their separate possible outputs are {0,1}. One joint system permits only (0,0) and (1,1); another permits all four pairs. The marginal components are identical, but an equality monitor always succeeds in the first system and can fail in the second.

This illustrates a real reason to retain coupling witnesses. It does not yet establish a new theory: ordinary relations already represent the distinction. The substantive question is whether the proposed calculus yields reusable, compositional criteria for retaining exactly the interaction information needed by a specified task.

Compatible overlaps need not glue globally

For binary variables A, B, C, require A=B on one overlapping subsystem, B=C on another, and A≠C on the third. Every shared single-variable projection is {0,1}, so the local models agree on overlaps. Nevertheless, no global assignment exists.

Thus reconciling partial models requires more than compatible local views. A pushout of presentations or a formal common refinement is not automatically a globally realizable system. This example is a diagnostic boundary, not a novel impossibility result.

Backwards subgoals need not be weaker

To prove A∧B, proving A and B is jointly sufficient. But choosing one backwards disjunction rule changes the goal A∨B into A, which is stronger rather than weaker. With B assumed and A unrelated, the original goal is immediately provable while the chosen subgoal is not.

The correct general interpretation is “a witness turns solutions of these subgoals into a solution of the goal,” not “every subgoal is an individually weaker theorem.” Rule choice, failure, backtracking, and termination belong to proof search, not merely to grafting proof trees.

4  AUDIT OF THE PROPOSED MOTIVATIONS

Isolation and local to global reasoning

This is a strong motivation with both intrinsic and operational readings. An algebraic island may have useful closure, normal forms, or exactness absent from its surroundings, without any external measurement. Behavioral isolation instead asks when an interface captures enough interaction for local certificates to imply a global guarantee. The D2 and E5 targets already address interface transport and exactness under substitution, but this is not yet a behavioral assume–guarantee theorem. “Well behaved” must name a property, such as protocol safety, rather than mean simply “has a valid witness.”

A credible target is a checkable boundary/coupling criterion that transfers local certificates through arbitrary permitted finite decomposition trees, together with a classified failure when interaction information is omitted. The criterion must not simply require checking the whole global property again. Existing interface and contract methods are the comparator, with the same information available to both approaches. In particular, composing open assumptions/guarantees is not the same question as decomposing a fixed closed specification. [Abadi–Lamport, Conjoining Specifications](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/abadi-conjoining.pdf).

Environment suitability and safe replacement

This is the clearest operational test of the preceding motivation. Freeze the execution semantics, property, environment class, and local implementation certificate. Then ask whether that certificate implies property-preserving replacement in every environment in the class, and whether certificates compose and survive the claimed interface changes.

The runner connection is real: Ahman–Bauer study modular resource-providing layers and runtime/operating-system relationships. That supports a candidate interpretation, not an existing bridge to this calculus. Architectural occurrence-linearity is not the same theorem as linear use of runtime resources. [Runners in Action](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11629v2).

I2 already targets contexts, cut probes, and observation equivalence. A3/B2 provide substitution and refinement infrastructure. The missing bridge is to independently meaningful implementation behavior. A contextually defined equivalence is not enough by itself: the useful result connects it to local information that can actually be checked.

Intrinsic reconstruction and distinctive equipment properties

There are two legitimate ambitions here. One takes admissible decompositions, couplings, and refinements as structural semantics in their own right; this does not require an external observation functor. The stronger ambition asks whether that structure determines an independently specified system, and which decomposition profiles arise. These require different acceptance tests.

E2 calls for reconstructing a selected semantic equipment from a supplied rich local presentation, including additional vertical/restriction/unit data. G2 targets minimal retained information. Neither automatically reconstructs a whole from restricted decomposition observations. A witness that already stores the whole cannot demonstrate that the pieces determine it.

A serious target fixes independently motivated elementary probes, witnesses, boundaries, morphisms, and the intended equivalence. It then proves faithful recovery and, if claimed, recognition of realizable profiles—or exact failure fibres and a necessary augmentation. “Use every probe” or “include the entire object” would evade the question. Nerve/density results are a demanding baseline: Arkor–McDermott treat relative-monad algebras under specific hypotheses, including exact virtual equipment structure, not arbitrary reconstruction of arbitrary wholes. [The nerve theorem for relative monads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01281).

Backwards sequent calculus

This motivation is sound when stated proof-relevantly. Take complete sequents as colors, inference-rule instances as generators, and open subproofs as leaves. Grafting then models filling proof obligations; an identity leaf is an unresolved goal, not a proof. This directly uses the existing core, but does not yet require meaningful interfaces or an equipment.

A more substantial target is contextual proof refinement: validations, renaming and substitution, repeated goal occurrences, exact proof equality, and transport of partial-proof certificates. If later subgoals depend on earlier answers, ordinary independent leaves do not suffice; one needs a telescope or equivalent dependency structure. Sterling–Harper already supply dependent refinement semantics and distinguish refinement rules from tactics by naturality. [Algebraic Foundations of Proof Refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05215).

Logical cut, filling an open proof leaf, graph cutting, and cut elimination are different operations or theorems. The current associativity and graph-cut results do not establish logical cut elimination or successful proof search. A useful specialization must supply its own soundness/completeness correspondence and a concrete certificate-reuse or search benefit.

Reconciliation of decompositions or partial models

This is plausible but presently bundles different questions. The contract targets raw/presented refinement comparison in B3, interface descent in D3, theory combination in I1, and cuts versus substitution in I4. These overlap with reconciling different presentations, but do not automatically solve gluing partial systems on overlapping scopes, or finding a common refinement preserving particular guarantees.

Choose the problem first: conservative theory amalgamation, common refinement, or semantic gluing. Each needs its own compatibility notion and obstruction. The three-variable example explains why a blanket promise would be false. Existing sheaf-theoretic work already distinguishes local compatibility from global realization in its specified measurement setting. [Abramsky–Brandenburger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264).

Abstraction and changing resolution

This is already substantially represented in G2, I2, and I5: retained information, contextual observable realizations, and simultaneous repairs for substitution, cuts, and transport. It should not be added again under a new name.

New scope would arise from a particular operational guarantee, approximate error bounds, stochastic dynamics, or intervention semantics. Exact observation preservation does not imply preservation under interventions or arbitrary feedback. Those extensions need their own semantic definitions and remain outside the current authorization. Existing open-Markov coarse-graining preserves specified steady-state boundary behavior under its hypotheses; it is not arbitrary transient preservation. [Baez–Courser](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11343).

Mechanistic or causal isolation is stronger still: observationally identical systems can react differently to an intervention. This motivation is coherent, but requires explicit interventions and their translation through abstraction. It cannot be inferred from passive observations or a convenient partition.

Computation and discovery

A4 and B3 have locally verified decidability/comparison results; the unactivated I2 and I5 targets include minimization and stabilization. These are genuine computational ingredients or proposed targets, not completed general-purpose algorithms. They do not yet imply faster reasoning, efficient proof search, or discovery of useful modules.

Distinguish computing with a supplied decomposition from discovering or optimizing one. An efficiency claim needs an input representation, cost model, output task, and strongest fair comparator. A discovery claim also needs an objective and admissible search class. A bounded executable example establishes feasibility, not a broad algorithmic advantage. Existing compositional algorithms already obtain guarantees under precise decomposition and solution-space assumptions; those are the baseline to beat or use, not consequences of the word compositional. [Althaus–Bumpus–Fairbanks–Rosiak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05575).

5  WHEN EQUIPMENT STRUCTURE WOULD EARN ITS PLACE

The useful possibility is two-sided interface change that interacts coherently with decomposition witnesses, restrictions, units, refinement, and assembly. But naming these operations is not proving their universal properties. Standard virtual-equipment theory already supplies the surrounding framework. [Cruttwell–Shulman, A unified framework for generalized multicategories](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2460v3).

For a selected application, compare with a plain multicategory, indexed semantics, or the relevant operational model. Ask what restriction/assembly or certificate-transport theorem the equipment formulation supplies or organizes, with all information retained in the comparator. An equivalent alternative formulation does not invalidate the work; unnecessary extra machinery does weaken the practical motivation for requiring it.

A meaningful distinctive-property target would characterize a decomposition-generated class among general equipments using independently checkable hypotheses. It must not define that class by the desired conclusion. If the selected application needs only lighter structure, that is a useful finding—not completion of the existing mandatory E1–E3 obligations and not permission to delete them silently.

6  WHICH INVESTIGATION SHOULD COME FIRST

For practical significance, I would first deepen isolation plus certified replacement. It most directly tests whether interfaces and retained witnesses carry the right information for reasoning about a system, rather than merely preserving its syntax.

The prospective target should cover a nontrivial parameterized semantic class, meaningful internal interaction, nested decomposition, and the relevant interface changes. A finite request/reply-machine family with hidden state could expose these issues, but should not be selected merely because it is easy. A small pilot would test the specification; it would not by itself discharge the general motivation.

Before an attempt, freeze:

• Independently defined systems, execution, the desired property, and any observations needed for the selected query.
• Component/interface/environment classes and all claimed quantifiers.
• A local certificate language, its promised contextual guarantee, and the intended benefit.
• A positive theorem and a permitted negative branch with sharp scope or a useful repair.
• Equally informative baseline translations and adverse examples.
• What counts as feasibility, mathematical adequacy, practical usefulness, and novelty.

A positive outcome could be a compositional certificate theorem with a meaningful completeness boundary and demonstrated reuse across decompositions. A negative outcome could classify why the permitted boundary information is insufficient and identify its minimum repair. Merely proving type correctness, or choosing an environment where the desired behavior happens, passes neither.

If the intended purpose is instead “semantics intrinsically encoded by decomposition,” prioritize intrinsic adequacy; reconstruction is the stronger version when recovery of an independently specified system is intended. If it is reasoning about proofs, select a named calculus and concrete proof-certificate task. These are substantive alternatives, not cosmetic application choices.

7  HOW THE COMPLETION CRITERIA SHOULD CHANGE IF YOU CHOOSE TO PROCEED

Keep four separate judgments:

1. Technical correctness: is the exact theorem true and its evidence trustworthy?
2. Intrinsic adequacy: does the structure characterize the intended phenomenon without encoding the conclusion by stipulation?
3. Practical usefulness: what independently specified reasoning task does it help settle, and compared with what?
4. Novelty attribution: background, specialization, integration, new theorem/implementation, or unresolved?

Known mathematics can satisfy a practical objective. A genuinely new theorem can fail that objective. Neither result changes the historical checkpoint automatically.

The minimal prospective changes concern §§1/1.1, 3.2/3.4, and Gate 2: clarify the selected purpose and separate these judgments. Add the independent semantic/task freeze to Gate 0 and separate verdicts to H1/H3. C4 should carry the fair practical baseline. G3 should retain its synthetic-example role unless explicitly strengthened; one synthetic application is not general suitability evidence. If §3.6 gains mandatory application criteria, record that as a material strengthening.

A companion contract can then specify the selected operational, intrinsic, or proof-theoretic investigation while reusing existing obligations. Do not activate every attractive motivation, duplicate I-workstream targets, or weaken old criteria after seeing the results. Any changed target class, dependency, effort estimate, or deferred extension follows §16.

Gate 2 should not mean “some ingredients are known, therefore stop.” It should ask what substantive question or useful capability remains. The current scoped shortfall still requires an explicit direction decision; this audit supplies options, not that approval.

BOTTOM LINE

The most promising significance lies in theorems connecting decomposition information to something independently meaningful: recoverability, global guarantees, safe replacement, or proof refinement. The current program contains much of the requisite infrastructure but does not yet establish those bridges. That is a reason to sharpen the goal, not to inflate novelty or discard verified work.
