Reconstructing a component from crossing decompositions
A relative reconstruction theorem for finite witnessed systems
26 October 2026

Abstract

Two workable divisions of one system can conceal incompatible internal explanations.
They may agree on every possible input and output, and every event count, while
assigning different meanings to the same supposed component when another branch changes.

We now have a necessary and sufficient criterion for recovering one missing component in
a four-component layout with crossing row and column groupings. The other components,
larger subsystem descriptions, and actual whole-event matching are fixed. First
reconstruct an intermediate component using explicit local checks and permutation
equations. Then test whether it leaves the parallel branch unchanged and remains the
same component at every parallel signal value. These conditions are computed from
supplied event records, not assumed from the desired conclusion.

When all relevant interfaces expose witnesses, the result is unique up to unique
compatible renaming. A complete fallback classification covers empty interfaces,
alternative matching choices, and genuine symmetries of invisible events. The
reconstructed components also have precise transport laws for surrounding context and
compatible interface changes.

This establishes a bounded but genuine crossing reconstruction theorem. It does not
settle arbitrary crossing families or physical implementation languages. Separate sensor
examples show why those next steps matter: mathematically sufficient information need
not be available through permitted hardware.

1 What has been reconstructed

The target is relative reconstruction: recover a concrete decomposition of a specified
whole, compatible with decompositions already supplied, rather than construct some
system with the same abstract behavior.

Three levels must be separated.

  Support asks which endpoint pairs can occur.
  Multiplicity asks how many distinct witnesses realize each pair.
  Coherence asks how those witnesses match across the supplied decompositions.

Success at one level does not guarantee success at the next. For one missing component
with the other three fixed, the result has two stages: solve a two-sided interpolation
square, then test the additional context-independence needed for a genuine four-corner
crossing. A three-piece factorization alone would not establish the second stage. Both
results can require a prescribed abstract support for the missing component: they
compute a realization fibre over that candidate rather than silently changing it.

Here is the smallest useful separation at the elementary-square stage. Suppose each of
its four boundary spans has two witnesses and every interface has one value. All 24
bijections between the two four-witness wholes agree on support and multiplicity. Yet
only four permit a common middle component. The other twenty fail solely because of the
supplied matching. Replacing that matching would change the reconstruction problem, not
solve it.

The systems are spans, not graphs. The incidence diagram used to compute the answer is a
diagnostic representation extracted from their witnesses, not an assumption about the
physical system's ontology.

2 The elementary witnessed square

A finite span L from A to B is a finite set of witnesses, each carrying an A endpoint
and a B endpoint. Distinct witnesses may have the same endpoints. Composing L with U
retains every compatible pair (l,u) whose shared endpoint agrees. This is pullback
composition.

Fix four spans

  L : A to B       R : C to D
  V : A to C       W : B to D

and an actual endpoint-preserving bijection

  theta : V;R -> L;W.

The semicolon means composition in reading order. Thus theta identifies the witnesses of
two decompositions of one A-to-D whole. It is part of the input, not a choice the
reconstruction may revise.

We seek a span U from B to C and bijections

  alpha : L;U -> V       beta : U;R -> W

satisfying, for every compatible triple,

  theta(alpha(l,u),r) = (l,beta(u,r)).

This equation says that the same intermediate witness u explains both sides of the
supplied matching. All endpoints, boundary witnesses, and theta stay fixed.

A compatible isomorphism between two solutions is a bijection of their U witnesses
preserving endpoints, alpha, and beta. Keeping these isomorphisms gives the solution
groupoid: it records alternatives and their automorphisms rather than forgetting them to
an existence verdict.

If an abstract relation Ubar between B and C is prescribed, require additionally that U
has a witness over (b,c) exactly when (b,c) belongs to Ubar. The relative fibre is the
full subgroupoid satisfying this requirement. Abstract support equations are necessary,
but the theorem below supplies the additional witness-level conditions.

3 A constructive rectangle criterion

Build a bipartite incidence diagram. Its left vertices are witnesses v of V; its right
vertices are witnesses w of W. Whenever

  theta(v,r) = (l,w),

insert an edge from v to w labelled by the pair (l,r). Retain parallel edges and all
witness labels.

Write L_b for witnesses of L ending at b, and R_c for witnesses of R starting at c.
First suppose every L_b and every R_c is nonempty. The theorem says:

  A coherent U exists exactly when every connected incidence component
  is a complete labelled rectangle with the prescribed L_b and R_c
  coordinate sets. If it exists, the solution groupoid is contractible.

Concretely, a rectangle must have one common B endpoint b and one common C endpoint c.
Each left vertex must carry one consistent l label, each right vertex one consistent r
label. The left vertices must be indexed bijectively by L_b and the right vertices by
R_c. Every left-right pair must have exactly one edge, with the corresponding labels.

Reconstruction is explicit: introduce one U witness for each such rectangle. Given l and
that witness, alpha selects the unique left vertex labelled l. Given that witness and r,
beta selects the unique right vertex labelled r.

Why is this sufficient? The rectangles partition all vertices, so alpha and beta are
bijections. Their edges express exactly the required coherence equation. Why is it
necessary? Any proposed intermediate witness u generates the entire rectangle of pairs
(l,u,r). Different u values cannot share vertices because alpha and beta are bijections.
These rectangles therefore must be precisely the connected components already present in
the input.

Every solution witness is forced to correspond to its incidence component, proving
uniqueness of the compatible isomorphism. This concerns the fixed witnessed boundary
data, not all decompositions of the underlying whole.

Failure has a short finite certificate: an incompatible endpoint, a changing vertex
label, a missing or repeated required edge, or an incorrect label fibre. The method
reads the supplied matching rather than enumerating all conceivable intermediate spans.

4 The obstruction can be presented by permutations

The rectangle condition has a useful algebraic form. After endpoint and vertex-label
consistency pass, group left vertices by their l label and right vertices by their r
label. For each pair (l,r), theta induces a bijection

  m_lr : V_l -> W_r.

For a fixed endpoint pair (b,c), all these sets have the same finite size, possibly
zero. Choose base labels l0 and r0. Transport a witness from V_l0 through four matchings
around a label square. The resulting permutation of V_l0 is

  h_lr = m_l0r^(-1) m_lr m_lr0^(-1) m_l0r0,

where the rightmost map acts first. Reconstruction succeeds exactly when every h_lr is
identity. Only (|L_b|-1)(|R_c|-1) permutations need checking at each endpoint pair. A
witness moved by one of these permutations is a compact obstruction certificate. The
permutations are derived from theta; no arbitrary group labels are added.

For a tiny genuinely global matching defect, let l and r each range over {0,1}, and give
every label fibre two witnesses, called sheets 0 and 1. Set three of the four m_lr to
identity and set m_11 to the swap of the two sheets:

                 r = 0       r = 1
  l = 0          identity    identity
  l = 1          identity    swap

Every individual matching is a bijection, and all local endpoint and label checks pass.
But transport around the square swaps sheet 0 with sheet 1. One cannot consistently
declare which middle witness is "the same" across all four positions. The incidence
diagram is an eight-cycle rather than two separate four-edge rectangles.

This answers the presentation question positively in this finite witnessed setting: the
obstruction is a finite list of explicit permutation equations, preceded by elementary
typing checks. It is not merely a statement that some unspecified obstruction class
vanishes.

The separation is exact: with two left and two right labels, one middle witness gives 4
coherent matchings out of 24; two middle witnesses give 288 out of 40,320. In the latter
case 576 matchings pass the local checks; half still fail the permutation test. The
proof companion derives a general factorial counting formula.

5 Empty interfaces retain choices and symmetry

Visibility is substantive. The elementary square theorem also classifies all empty-side
cases.

If L_b is nonempty but R_c is empty, a witness of U_bc is seen only through alpha. The
remaining problem is a typed tiling: partition the relevant V witnesses into copies of
prescribed L fibres. Its finite nonnegative count equations are

  sum_b |L_ab| n_bc = |V_ac|.

Actual tilings can be nonisomorphic, but their compatible automorphisms are trivial
because each copy is observed. There is a dual classification when only R_c is nonempty.

If both sides are empty, U_bc can be any finite set. An n-element choice has the full
symmetric group on n letters as its automorphism group: these are genuine unobserved
witnesses.

The complete solution groupoid combines the forced visible part, the one-sided tilings,
and these finite-set groupoids. Prescribing Ubar requires zero witnesses outside its
support and positive counts inside it. This preserves all alternatives and symmetries
instead of obtaining uniqueness by discarding unseen data.

6 From interpolation to a genuine crossing

Consider four component occurrences R,T,Q,S, with only T unknown:

      R ----> T
              |
              v
      Q ----> S

R feeds T; T and the neighboring branch Q jointly feed S. The supplied groupings are

  {R,T} | {Q,S}       and       {R,Q} | {T,S}.

Their four intersections are the four component occurrences, so these cuts genuinely
cross. Q need not be an identity or an unused component.

Write R:A to B, Q:K to L, S:C times L to D, and seek T:B to C. Given parent components P
and N, require

  R;T is isomorphic to P,
  (T times identity_L);S is isomorphic to N,

compatibly with the actual supplied matching theta:(P times Q);S -> (R times Q);N. This
must preserve the very same witness of Q, not merely its endpoint values.

Apply the elementary theorem with boundaries R times Q, S, P times Q, and N. It
reconstructs an arbitrary U from B times L to C times L. But the required U must be T
times identity_L: one component independent of the other branch. The new theorem derives
exactly when that further factorization exists.

Assume L is nonempty and every R output fibre, Q output fibre, and S input fibre is
nonempty. After the elementary rectangle/permutation criterion passes, perform three
tests:

  Preserve Q. The whole matching must leave every actual Q witness q
  unchanged. This also forces U to preserve the parallel value ell.

  Preserve the factor across parallel witnesses. For u at level ell,
  read from alpha its function R_b -> P_c, taking each r to the
  resulting parent witness p. This actual function must be independent
  of which q ending at ell exposes u.

  Preserve the component across parallel values. The sets of these
  endpoint-typed functions must be identical at every ell. Equal
  cardinalities or endpoint supports are not enough.

These conditions are necessary and sufficient. The canonical T is the common set of
typed functions. Evaluating a function at r gives the first parent isomorphism; the
square's beta supplies the second. Every realization is uniquely isomorphic to this one.
A prescribed abstract support Tbar is realizable exactly when it equals the support of
that reconstructed T.

A fully active Boolean example isolates the distinction. R and Q negate bits, S computes
XOR, and the successful counterpart uses a bit negation for T. A twisted whole-event
matching still admits an elementary square filler. But its two R-labelled parent-event
blocks are

  at ell = 0:  {00,10} and {01,11};
  at ell = 1:  {00,11} and {01,10}.

The first bit labels the R event. No renaming of the two blocks makes these partitions
agree. A single T cannot explain both without seeing ell, which its prescribed interface
excludes. Untwisting only the whole-event matching restores a genuine grid, leaving
every support, count, and external behavior unchanged.

This adds a genuine crossing obstruction beyond elementary interpolation: a fitted
intermediate component may secretly change what its witnesses mean when the parallel
branch changes. The direct structural test assumes visibility.

For arbitrary empty-sector grid data, a separate complete presentation uses counts n_bc
satisfying the typed parent equations

  sum_b |R_ab| n_bc = |P_ac|,
  sum_c n_bc |S_(c,ell),d| = |N_(b,ell),d|.

Whenever R_b or some S_(c,ell) is nonempty, n_bc has an explicit finite bound from these
equations. Enumerate the bounded typed parent bijections satisfying the actual theta
equation, modulo relabelling T witnesses. The action is free, so this part is a finite
set of genuine alternatives. Pairs with R_b empty and every S_(c,ell) empty contribute
independent groupoids of arbitrary finite sets and their permutations. Prescribed
support imposes zero or positive counts.

This classifies every realization and gives a terminating existence procedure, including
empty Q or L. It is a potentially factorial enumeration fallback, not an efficient
extension of the visible permutation test.

For explicitly listed finite witness inputs, the fully visible problem is decidable in
polynomial time. The general problem allowing empty exposing fibres is NP-complete, even
with prescribed support. The reduction from three-dimensional matching [5] keeps Q and
every R output fibre nonempty, but sets S and N empty: the whole is empty in the
hardness instances. It does not show hardness of the visible test. Visibility therefore
changes both uniqueness and computational difficulty.

7 Why the abstraction and transport matter

The ambient abstraction is explicit:

  finite witnessed spans -> finite relations,
  E -> {(a,b) : some witness of E has endpoints (a,b)}.

It preserves identity interfaces, horizontal composition, and cartesian restriction
along endpoint maps. In equipment language, finite sets and functions supply objects and
vertical maps, spans supply horizontal processes, and witness maps supply cells. Taking
support respects this structure. The reconstruction problem therefore lives over a
genuine structure-preserving semantic translation, not an arbitrary forgetful picture.

It cannot be universally reversed while preserving composition and units. Compose the
total relation from a singleton to two points with its converse. The relation composite
is the singleton identity, but any spans realizing those relations have at least two
composite witnesses, whereas the identity span has one. A universal identity-on-objects
realization section is impossible.

Existing square fillers do transport through actual surrounding spans. Given H before A
and K after D, replace the boundaries by H;L, R;K, H;V, and W;K. Keep U and extend alpha
and beta by the unchanged surrounding witness coordinates. The coherence equation
remains true. Crossing-grid realizations likewise transport with the same T and
unchanged Q. Successive substitutions agree through the canonical tuple-regrouping
isomorphisms.

This is preservation, not an equivalence of solution spaces: surrounding context may
hide an obstruction or introduce invisible choices. Surjective outer-interface
restrictions also reflect existence; arbitrary restrictions need not.

Internal interface changes have a sharper two-sided condition. Start with a realized
visible grid and change B and C by functions f:Bnew -> B and g:Cnew -> C, keeping Q and
L fixed. Restricting the parent spans separately does not automatically provide a new
whole matching: every old whole event has g-fibre copies on one side and f-fibre copies
on the other.

A supplied matching over the old theta is exactly a family of bijections

  eta_(r,t,q,s) : g^(-1)(c_t) -> f^(-1)(b_t).

The retyped grid is realizable exactly when these bijections depend only on t. In
particular, they must be independent of the neighboring branch value ell, not merely
agree within each ell level. Equal fibre sizes alone do not suffice.

Writing the common bijection as lambda_t, construct new middle witnesses (t,cnew) with
endpoints lambda_t(cnew) and cnew. Their support is a union of these matching graphs,
not necessarily the full inverse image of the old support. A prescribed new abstract
support must match the actual reconstruction.

Compatible internal changes compose and commute with outer-context substitution through
explicit tuple bijections. Parallel transport paths with identical final interface
functions must still agree on their composite fibre matchings. This is a restricted
coherent transport calculus, not a claim that every independent cartesian restriction
preserves a realization.

8 What the supporting system models establish

At the support layer, one unknown relation U occurring once in each union-preserving
typed context C_k has an exact synthesis procedure. For required parent behaviors P_k,
set

  Umax = {u : C_k({u}) is contained in P_k for every k}.

A solution exists exactly when every C_k(Umax)=P_k. All solutions are subsets of Umax
still covering every required behavior. This applies to a crossing grid, but does not
preserve witness multiplicities or theta; repeated occurrences of U can invalidate the
construction.

The physical-model work gives another, independent view of the difficulty. Consider four
Boolean state coordinates c,u,v,z with update

  c' = u XOR v XOR z,

and the other coordinates unchanged. The apparatus supplies only a=v XOR z and b=u XOR
z. The crossing divisions {c,u}|{v,z} and {c,v}|{u,z} each work: the first computes c'=u
XOR a and the second c'=v XOR b.

Their only common finer partition is the singleton partition. Its c component receives
c,a,b, yet cannot distinguish states (0,0,0,0) and (0,1,1,1). These observations agree
while c' differs. Neither more careful bookkeeping nor combining the old sensors repairs
the missing decoding information. Adding any one raw sensor u, v, or z does repair it,
giving three genuinely different implementation options.

The supplementary models also establish two limits. A family on 2^r Boolean coordinates,
each updated by the AND of the others, has r crossing cuts whose every proper subfamily
admits a common refinement under a 2^r-2 raw-sensor budget, while the whole family does
not. Pairwise tests therefore cannot be uniformly complete over that class. For linear
updates, the observation row-space rank defect is the exact minimum number of
unrestricted scalar measurements needed, but can underestimate the cost of a fixed
sensor catalog.

These sensor-policy models have exact local updates, but are not yet connected to the
complete witnessed grid theorem by a structure-preserving implementation translation.
That remains a substantive integration problem.

9 Evidence and interpretation

The principal claims have mathematical proofs and independent AI proof review.
Independently written checks compared the rectangle diagnostic with direct alpha/beta
fillers, including all 40,320 matchings in the two-label, two-sheet case and 530 typed
squares, 459 with empty side fibres. The explicit two-sheet twist's nonidentity loop was
checked separately.

For the genuine crossing, 332,952 Q-preserving whole matchings across four small
profiles were compared with direct grid realizations. Further checks covered 235 small
all-fibre grid cases, including empty Q and nonunique bounded realizations. Among 256
retyping-matching families in another fixture, 16 admitted square fillers but only four
admitted cylindrical grid fillers.

The companion also includes executable checks of relational synthesis and finite-state
examples. These are regression evidence for stated finite domains, not substitutes for
the proofs or exhaustive verification of arbitrary systems. No Lean formalization has
been run.

The result is an exact one-missing-component grid theorem, not a new general theory of
holonomy. Rectangle reconstruction has a standard difunctional-relation core [1], and
permutation transport is classical covering mathematics [2]. Generic span factorization
[3] is an essential adjacent baseline. The useful result is the fixed-theta realization
fibre and its cylinder constraint, including prescribed support, empty sectors,
symmetries, and exact transport limits. A priority claim would require a sharper
theorem-to-theorem comparison than the present audit establishes.

10 The next substantive theorem

The next target is reconstruction over larger families of overlapping crossing
decompositions, not another isolated counterexample. It should derive compatibility of
local solution groupoids from primitive implementation data, including partially visible
overlaps and noninvertible interface changes. It must retain physical admissibility
rather than infer it from span realizability.

Three obligations make that target concrete. First, specify an implementation category
whose permitted sensor operations and composition laws are explicit. Second, prove how
its realization fibres map to and lift the span-level fibres. Third, identify enough
higher compatibility to assemble local reconstructions, proving sufficiency rather than
assuming global coherence under another name. Existing square-based unique-factorization
results make the higher-coherence comparison unavoidable [4].

The finite models supply useful tests: the positive theorem must explain the
raw-boundary successes, reject the encoded-sensor failure for the right reason, and
respect the high-order budget family. The invisible sectors supply a further test
because their choices and symmetries cannot be handled by a uniqueness shortcut.

The milestone is therefore substantial but bounded: an exact witnessed reconstruction
criterion for one genuine four-corner crossing schema, supported by a complete
elementary interpolation classification and explicit transport laws. The larger goal is
to assemble such calculations into a theorem about physically admissible decompositions
of systems.
