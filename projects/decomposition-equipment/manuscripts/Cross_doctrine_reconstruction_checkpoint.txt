Cross doctrine reconstruction and finite qualitative execution

30 October 2026

Main result

There is a coherent span-valued comparison between native reconstructions in
different semantic doctrines, under adequate cylinder factorisation hypotheses
and preservation of the underlying arrow classes. Exact free reconstruction
already commutes with every functor through Garner's Isbell construction [1].
A concrete operational result complements this structural law: finite
substochastic execution has an exact qualitative quotient recording support
and per-input missing mass. This quotient preserves composition and first-exit
feedback and both preserves and reflects normalization. It nevertheless has
no functorial section choosing probabilistic implementations. These are
forward comparison and abstraction results, not a general reconstruction of
quantitative behavior from qualitative data.

Native reconstruction and its coherence

A cylinder consists of labelled preparations, labelled tests, and their actual
pairing. Its chosen reconstruction factors that pairing through a middle
object R_C(r), using an E preparation cocone and an M test cone. For a functor
F:C->D, the two objects to compare are F R_C(r) and R_D(F r).

Suppose the chosen cylinder factorisation systems satisfy the adequate mixed
pullback and compatible-span rigidity hypotheses. Factor the translated
preparation cocone once more in D. Orthogonality against the target canonical
test cone then constructs the canonical comparison

    beta_F(r): F R_C(r) <- G_F(r) -> R_D(F r),

with left leg in the underlying class M_0 and right leg in E_0. This individual
span exists without class preservation by F. Preservation of E_0 and M_0 is
needed to transport and compose such spans. Under adequacy, translated mixed
pullbacks remain the required mixed pullbacks. The comparison for GF is
uniquely isomorphic, compatibly with the cylinder, to the composite of the
translated comparison for F and the comparison for G. Units and associativity
follow from this same uniqueness, rather than being assumed as extra axioms.

The law also respects signed context changes: preparations enlarge while
tests restrict, with their actual labels and maps retained. It is not ordinary
covariance under simultaneous enlargement of both axes. For declared finite
fixed-port wiring, strong symmetric monoidality transports the duals, cups,
caps, and raw assembly maps. If the honest assembly operations preserve the
required arrow classes, rigidity identifies translating a wired reconstruction
with wiring the translated local comparisons and then normalizing. This uses
the actual joint preparation and test maps, including correlations. Arbitrary
assembly languages are not covered automatically; an additional trace must be
preserved or explicitly compared, not inferred from strong monoidality alone.

Exactness has a local criterion. Both legs of beta_F(r) are invertible if and
only if the actual translated preparation and test factorisation already lies
in the target E and M classes. Global preservation of every E cocone and M
cone is sufficient for uniform exactness, but is unnecessary for a selected
cylinder. One-sided preservation gives the familiar direct comparisons:
preserving E gives F R_C(r)->R_D(F r), while preserving M gives the reverse
arrow [1]. Neither should be confused with the weaker span-valued law.

The weakening is genuine. In I(1)=Set x Set^op, the square functor
H(P,Q)=(P x P,Q x Q) preserves the underlying arrow classes but not the full
cylinder classes. Two unit preparations and two unit tests reconstruct to
(2,2); applying H gives (4,4), while reconstructing after H still gives (2,2).
The comparison apex is (2,4), and neither leg is invertible. This demonstrates
formal strictness only: all scalar observations in this example are trivial,
so it is not a physical information-loss example.

The free result and the support control case

For small C and D and every functor F, the free Isbell extension satisfies

    I(F) R_free,C(r) ~= R_free,D(F r).

The two left Kan extensions preserve the colimits defining the canonical middle
and carry representables to the corresponding representables. The induced
pairings agree on generators. This is an application of Garner's free CFS
construction [1], not a new general free-naturality theorem. It transports the
actual canonical normalization spans as well as the reconstructed endpoints.

Entrywise support from finite nonnegative matrices to finite relations is an
important control case [4]. Nonnegative sums have no cancellation, so support
preserves composition, Kronecker tensor, deterministic copying, and finite
compact trace exactly. Finite stochastic circuits therefore retain exactly
their positive-probability outcomes when their full joint sources are
translated. Replacing a correlated source by independent marginal supports
changes the model; it is not a failure of the support functor. Numerical risk
thresholds are lost, but a raw finite-wiring failure cannot be manufactured
from that fact. Compact matrix trace is not the first-exit execution operation
below, and normalized stochastic maps are not themselves a compact category.

An exact finite qualitative repair

Use finite substochastic kernels K:A->B, whose column sums are at most one.
The relevant tensor is disjoint union. For feedback f:A+U->B+U, write its
blocks as R:A->B, S:A->U, T:U->B, and Q:U->U. First-exit execution is

    Tr_U(f) = R + sum_(n>=0) T Q^n S.

It sums disjoint events that first reach an external output after finitely
many internal steps. The series converges entrywise to a substochastic
kernel, even when I-Q is singular. This is the established total execution
trace on finite substochastic kernels; its stochastic restriction admits
exactly those feedbacks whose result remains normalized [2]. The support part
is relational finite-path feedback [5].

Define J(K)=(R_K,D_K), where R_K(a,b) means K(b|a)>0 and
D_K={a:sum_b K(b|a)<1}. D records positive missing mass, not its size. The
exact composition law is

    R_(gf) = R_g compose R_f,
    D_(gf) = D_f union {a: some b has R_f(a,b) and b in D_g}.

Writing delta_f(a)=1-sum_b f(b|a), the law follows from
delta_(gf)(a)=delta_f(a)+sum_b f(b|a)delta_g(b), whose terms are nonnegative.
Disjoint union acts componentwise. Identities have diagonal
support and empty deficit. Thus J is an exact quotient, not merely a sound
overapproximation, and a kernel is normalized exactly when D is empty.

Feedback support is finite-path reachability. Its deficit is determined by a
finite graph calculation: an input is deficient exactly when, before exiting,
it can reach a locally deficient vertex or a closed internal strongly connected
class with no outgoing support edge. A positive path to either obstruction
gives positive loss or nontermination. Conversely, if neither is reachable,
every reachable internal state has a positive finite path to an exit. Finitely
many chosen paths supply a uniform positive progress probability, forcing
eventual exit with probability one. Consequently J preserves first-exit
feedback exactly. The quotient inherits the trace equations, and its external
(R,D) summary can be substituted into further finite feedback contexts.

For example, a->(1/2)b+(1/2)u with u->u has exit mass 1/2, although its
external may-relation is total. J retains the resulting deficit. By contrast,
a->u with u->(1/2)b+(1/2)u exits almost surely, despite a supported infinite
path. The criterion is not demonic termination along every graph path.

With named inputs and deterministic input selectors available, equality of
(R,D) is the coarsest support-refining compositional equivalence retaining
normalization observations. Any differing column deficit can be observed by
selecting that input; any support difference is already an allowed observation.
Conversely, equal pairs remain equal through composition, disjoint union, and
finite first-exit contexts. This is contextual completeness for these stated
observations only. J abstracts a supplied exact kernel; it does not estimate
whether a deficit is exactly zero from finitely many samples.

Realizability and the sharp boundaries

Every feasible pair has a substochastic realization. An empty support fibre
must be deficient; otherwise distribute total mass one uniformly over a
nondeficient fibre, or total mass one half over a deficient nonempty fibre.
This setwise construction is not a functorial choice.

Indeed, suppose an identity-on-objects section chose a lift of the total map
R:1->{1,2} as p=(r,1-r)^T, with 0<r<1. Let g have first column R and second
column the deterministic point 1, with neither column deficient. Deterministic
input probes force its lifted columns to be (r,1-r) and (1,0). Qualitatively
gR=R. Its lifted matrix must therefore fix p; the first component says
r^2+1-r=r, or (r-1)^2=0. This contradicts strict positivity of both outputs.
No tensor or feedback assumption is needed for this obstruction. Individual
implementations and qualitative program composition remain possible; a global
composition-preserving selection of representatives does not.

Finiteness is essential. On hidden states 1,2,..., enter state 1, move down
with probability p and up with probability q=1-p, and exit on moving down from
1. All 0<p<1 give the same open support and no local deficit. Yet eventual
exit mass is p/q for p<q and one for p>q. Thus p=1/3 gives 1/2, while p=2/3
gives one. Identical open J-images acquire different feedback deficits.
Countably infinite execution therefore needs additional progress information.

J is also not omega-continuous: scalar kernels 1-1/n are all deficient, but
their limit one is not. Likewise every finite unfolding of the almost-sure
geometric loop is deficient. The feedback deficit cannot be computed by
unioning finite-unfolding summaries; the finite-state graph argument is
essential. Numerical risk budgets, probability estimates, and uniform
expected-time bounds remain outside J's observations.

Connection back to reconstruction

Choose finite-set skeletons and write C=FinSubStoch and D=QualDiv for the
operational category and its quotient. The traced strong symmetric monoidal
functor J induces H=Int(J) between their compact completions [3]. Applying the
free Isbell construction gives the exact comparison

    I(H) R_free,Int(C)(r) ~= R_free,Int(D)(H r),

together with canonical span transport and the declared fixed-port wiring
coherence. This connects the operational quotient to the structural theorem
without asserting native CFSs on the operational categories. Int and I are
formal completions: their canonical objects and span apices are not thereby
finite physical implementations. The finite admission theorem remains exact
on original kernels and their finite contexts, not on arbitrary completion
objects. The no-section and countable-state examples block stronger automatic
lifting claims. No priority claim or completion of the broader research
programme follows from this checkpoint. The separate assumption-challenging
and discovery line remains deferred, as agreed.
