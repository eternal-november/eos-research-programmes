Discovering spatial decompositions from recorded readouts
27 October 2026

Abstract

This checkpoint supplies calibration examples and conditional realization theorems for decomposition discovery. In the permutation family, a special reset and fully observed label movements make the unknown partition recoverable by graph connectivity; this does not exercise the central architecture-inference difficulty. The family nevertheless proves exact crossing-witness and interface memory/communication results, including genuine cross-scope interactions. The positive-operator results give realization conditions under strong calibration, coverage and positivity hypotheses. Discovering interacting components and compatible positive mechanisms without architecture-revealing operations, while characterizing observationally equivalent alternatives, remains open.

What the calibration family establishes

The system has a conservation constraint: n distinct labels occupy n positions without replacement. A recorded reset uniformly reshuffles labels inside blocks of an unknown partition. From full before/after readouts, connect positions between which labels moved; connected components recover the blocks. Under independent uniform resets, N >= n log(n^2/delta) records suffice for success probability at least 1-delta. These records must actually exist. The reset class and noiseless readouts are assumptions, not conclusions from missing cross-block moves. Each record costs O(n log n) bits; discovery never enumerates n! configurations. The reset exposes the sought partition; the harder cross-block dynamics below are not the source of the identification theorem.

For recovered block sizes b_i, the exact minimal positive reset interface has Q = n! / product_i b_i! values: each records the label set assigned to every block. This bounds reset/preparation factorizations, not arbitrary memory with free correlated initialization. For multiple crossing scopes, joint realizability requires matching position and label counts in every membership-pattern cell. Pairwise agreement is insufficient; matching within cells constructs a common witness.

A nonempty proper k-position boundary needs binomial(n,k) states when static. Under all position permutations generated by adjacent swaps, an interface that must update itself and reproduce boundary readouts without local messages or a free boundary sensor needs n! positive states. That is Theta(n log n) bits, not exponential bit storage. Retaining local assignments repairs the obstruction: a crossing swap uses outgoing conditional alphabets k and n-k, plus return alphabets n-k and k. Broadcast alternatives must also be charged. The message lower bounds concern separate one-round zero-error encoders, not arbitrary interaction. Distinguishing command schedules establish the lower bound; inference cannot assume their records are available.

The permutation proof passed independent review with clarifications applied. The mathematical ingredients here and below are classical; no novelty priority is claimed.

Further exact sufficient classes

Under exact calibration and rank promises, recorded preparations and fully distinguishing probes determine positive operational coordinates. Whole-family reset identities can then discover component coordinates and a shared boundary rather than receive the partition as input. Pairwise reset tests alone do not suffice.

Given a learned frame and covered full-product inputs, outside-response profiles give necessary deterministic outgoing-message partitions for a specified one-round protocol. A simplicial channel-cone test constructs positive local kernels and correlated instructions. A linear search over all extremal local channels also handles redundant generators. With a checkable normalized positive retraction (a positivity-preserving projection), it is complete for that fixed frame and protocol: infeasibility excludes even generators outside the learned local spans. Successful tests attain this message bound. Without the retraction, infeasibility is inconclusive.

These are exact subclasses, not general hidden-state discovery. Full trajectories require separately admissible, charged joint initialization. Incoming instructions, broadcasts and shared-core descriptions count; inference and the learned global representation can be exponential.

What remains open

The finite observable-basis theorem and finite-horizon total-variation bridge passed independent review. They are classical certification foundations, requiring justified rank, conditioning, causal identification and coverage; a finite low-rank fit alone is insufficient. Numerical checks are regressions, not proofs.

The main target remains positive spatial/port factorization discovered from raw readouts within a fixed mathematical type universe: unknown components, interfaces and correlated couplings, with persistent joint witnesses across crossing cuts. A compact learned predictive intermediate is allowed, but signed predictive coordinates alone are not stochastic parts. General local-to-global discovery, compact positive couplings and robust finite-data guarantees remain open. Suitability checking is downstream once information and control demands are exposed. No revised contract has been adopted, and no full-target completion or Lean verification is claimed.
