Typed calculus and controller environment integration results
Research milestone for Sean
27 October 2026

Abstract

A gripper that cannot rotate an object alone can recruit a corner as its pivot.
The controller seats the object, learns which way to push, remembers the answer,
pushes, and releases the quarterturned object. This works only if sensing and
mechanics concern the same contact and that contact survives the push.
Separately plausible descriptions of each part are not enough.

The earlier work could analyze finite machines, but had not shown that it
respected the original decomposition language's rules. We now supply that
link. Replacing a particular piece by its own decomposition has the same
meaning in the original language and in the machine model. Only some
decompositions allow the whole to be reconstructed from compatible pieces;
that stronger property is tested separately and survives the specified
refinements and passage through time.

The gripper, fixture, object and sensor are specified locally before their
combined behavior is calculated. The controller is also decomposed, with one
memory owner and a clear distinction between choosing an action and receiving
its reply. Together these pieces support safe completion in three actions.
Counterexamples isolate missing retention, missing memory and incorrectly
duplicated contacts. This closes the integration gap under stated finite
modeling assumptions. It neither certifies a physical robot nor
completes the original equipment and reconstruction program.


1  The concrete gap now closed

The previous milestone supplied finite dynamic decomposition, discovery and
control results, but ended with a specific missing link: those results had not
been interpreted through the actual original presented calculus. A convincing
example also needed to start with local capabilities, rather than assume a
correct whole and then project out its parts.

Both requirements are addressed here. The original ordered types, tree
substitution, declared equations and occurrence-indexed refinement arrows are
retained. The fixture example supplies local primitive tables, derives the open
plant, checks its decomposition, and only then plugs in a controller. The
resulting chain is:

    original typed presentation
      -> interpretation in staged finite systems
      -> exact local execution comparisons
      -> a controller and environment guarantee.

The example makes the last link tangible. The gripper has translation commands
and release, but no free-space rotation primitive. A left- or right-handed
corner can convert an appropriate lateral drive into a positive turning pulse.
The hand is initially hidden. Seating returns it after the approach action;
later sensing reports only whether contact is engaged. The controller must
therefore retain the earlier answer, and the mechanical pivot must still be
the contact that supplied it.

The fixture has either HOLD or TOUCH grade. HOLD promises retention through the
next admitted drive. TOUCH permits a slip during that drive. These are different
capabilities even when both report contact now. Under HOLD, every possible
seating choice completes safely. Under TOUCH, the controller refuses the task;
refusal is not counted as successful completion.


2  What the interpretation theorem says

A component is modeled by a finite directed graph: states and actual event
records, with each record's source and target. Parallel records remain distinct
even when their endpoints and visible responses coincide. An interface is
another such graph, and a component's two boundary maps describe how its states
and records appear at its endpoints.

For an original ordered profile of components, form the graph of compatible
tuples. Adjacent components must agree on the shared boundary state and event.
A decomposition witness is a graph map in the backward direction:

    whole -> compatible tuples of pieces.

It says how each actual whole state and execution determines its pieces. It
does not say that every compatible tuple occurs, or that a tuple uniquely
recovers the whole. Different maps remain different witnesses even if their
images, counts or visible traces agree.

These operations form a target satisfying the original Calc laws. Substitution
applies a replacement witness at each particular component occurrence, then
flattens the resulting ordered tuple. Repeated component types retain separate
positions. Both unit laws and associativity hold as equalities of operations,
not merely as equalities of profiles.

Empty profiles require care. The compatible-tuple object for an empty path at
an interface is that interface itself, not an indiscriminate one-point object.
A nullary witness is possible only for a loop component whose two boundary maps
agree. When substitution removes every component, it retains the common
interface value. This prevents a hidden change to the original nullary syntax.

The resulting interpretation uses the existing source APIs directly:

* Tree.extend gives the unique strict interpretation of free typed trees once
  their primitive generators have been assigned valid witness maps.
* Presentation.extend passes to the original presented quotient when every
  declared parallel equation has equal interpreted maps.
* SemanticRefinement.functor carries the original refinement arrows to the
  target, retaining the entire family of occurrence-wise replacements.

The second condition is substantive. Equal outcomes or event counts do not
validate an equation. In the finite instance, both sides are evaluated on every
state and actual event. The worked presentation declares one regrouping equation:
first group gripper with fixture and then expand, or first group fixture with
object and then expand. Both trees return exactly the ordered triple of local
records. This is a genuine small presented instance, not a quotient by every
coincidence observed in the model.

A negative control exchanges the fixture's two seating pads. This is an exact
map preserving the structural interface labels, but inserting it in only one
grouping branch changes 288 whole event records. The proposed equation is
correctly rejected. The general theory also rejects swapping distinct records
with identical visible responses and endpoints: equality must concern the
actual maps, not merely their observed behavior.

Mark a witness exact when it is bijective on states and events. This is precisely
a graph isomorphism. Exact identities and exact replacements compose to an
exact witness, including nullary replacements. Nonexact witnesses remain valid
operations. Conversely, an exact composite need not make its constituent maps
exact: a nonbijective inclusion followed by a nonbijective collapse can compose
to an identity.

Interpretation also need not recover the syntax. Distinct generators may have
the same semantic map, and different replacement families can have the same
aggregate composite. Faithfulness requires additional injectivity; it does not
follow from the existence of this interpretation. Keeping the refinement forest
is essential even when only the composite happens to be observable.


3  Deriving the plant and its guarantee

The example has three independently specified local mechanisms. The gripper
owns held or released state and converts an external action into a command.
The fixture owns hand, grade, contact mode and seating pad. It consumes the
command, updates contact and emits a wrench class plus a returned reply. The
object owns its orientation and reacts only to that wrench. It cannot inspect
hand, controller memory or the external command.

There are two possible seating pads. They give the same macroscopic pivot but
are different fixture states and different actual seating executions. Zero
wrench preserves orientation; a positive pulse turns a flat object through one
quarterturn. A wrong-direction pulse, lost support, unsupported drive or another
positive pulse on an already turned object produces a conservative damaged
state. This response table specifies a bounded operating envelope; it is not a
rigid-body calculation.

The whole is generated by enumerating the local records: a gripper command, a
matching fixture response, and an object response to that response's wrench.
Their next local states determine the whole next state. This produces 72 open
states and 396 action records. All five actions remain available in the open
specification before control: approach, push left, push right, withdraw and wait.
Unsupported actions and damaged states are not removed to make matching easier.

The structural interface records contact before and after, action, command,
wrench and reply. Filtering the local component tables against its independently
declared full alphabet reproduces exactly the generated whole records. Every
matching triple has one whole execution, and every whole execution yields one
triple. The comparison is therefore exact, rather than an agreement only on
successful runs.

That structural transcript is not a bundle of sensors available to every
component. The physical dependency order is explicit: acquire current contact,
choose the action, issue the gripper command, obtain the fixture response, update
the object, and commit state and controller memory. Command goes to the fixture;
wrench goes to the object; the reply returns to the controller after the action.
No primitive reads the entire transcript. Pre-action acquisition has its own
exact comparison and is not inferred from the later event factorization.

The controller uses six named memory values: start, left, right, withdraw, done
and reject. A request initializer sets start. Approach is chosen without knowing
hand. A returned HOLD contact reply stores left or right; another reply causes
rejection. The stored hand selects the next push, then memory advances to
withdraw and finally done. This is a concrete finite implementation, not a claim
that six memory states are globally minimal.

The controller itself also has an explicit ordered decomposition, rather than
being supplied only as a correct policy. Its profile is Select, Decode, Commit.
Select determines the action from old memory and the permitted pre-observation.
Decode uses the actual post-action reply to compute a write instruction. Commit
performs that write. There is one memory owner, not three independent copies.
The matching local records reconstruct exactly the controller's complete tick
records, and grouping Decode with Commit then refining that occurrence gives
the same witness. A separate check confirms that changing the future reply or
future memory cannot change Select's action.

A common protocol carries controller memory, contact, the action and reply,
and the write instruction. Explicit passive extensions put controller and plant
at the same boundary type in the original calculus. The coupled whole first
decomposes into those two pieces; refining them yields the ordered profile
Select, Decode, Commit, gripper, fixture, object. Expanding either side first
gives the same six actual records. The independently checked comparison retains
all 468 coupled records, including unreachable and adverse states, not just
the successful protocol.

In this model, hiding memory from boundary matching separately for each
mechanism introduces extra combinations. The actual local record identities
are retained: three valid records can appear compatible although their old
memories disagree, so that particular tuple has no one-owner execution. Their
public values alone can still have a different, consistent lift. The failure
concerns exact reconstruction of retained records, not automatically safety.
The driver lesson is to assemble its mechanisms consistently before hiding
their shared memory.

For the two initial HOLD states, differing only in hidden hand, all executions
obey the following argument. Approach seats on either pad and leaves the object
flat. The returned hand is the actual hand of that contact. The next push uses
the retained answer. HOLD excludes slipping, so every matching fixture response
has positive wrench and the object turns. Withdraw releases the gripper, clears
contact and preserves the turned orientation.

Thus the controller is nonblocking, never reaches damaged state, and reaches
the released quarterturned goal in at most three actions. The rank 3, 2, 1, 0
decreases along every reachable nonterminal step. All environment choices are
included; no fairness assumption is needed. Exhaustive checking finds 12
reachable state-memory pairs, 12 closed-loop edges and two terminal states.

The assumptions remain explicit: sufficient geometry and clearance, an admitted
force class, the stated object response, a truthful configuration grade, contact
retention, and no unmodeled between-tick contact change. Numerical friction,
load and torque bounds are not supplied. Those are validation obligations for
an eventual apparatus, not consequences of the categorical interpretation.


4  Four failures that the integration distinguishes

Without the fixture, the specified gripper and object have no positive-wrench
execution. Every action sequence leaves a flat object flat or damaged; none
reaches the turned state. The controller genuinely recruits an environmental
capability absent from the fixture-free candidate.

Without retained handedness, later contact sensing cannot select the push.
Both hands give the same engaged observation but require opposite directions.
Waiting cannot guarantee completion, approaching again faults, and releasing
early loses the ability to finish. All 25 deterministic memoryless action maps
on the two current contact observations fail. Even granting protocol phase for
free does not supply the missing hand bit. Sequencing and remembering the
contact's hand are separate resources.

Without retention, correct direction is insufficient. TOUCH permits a slip
during the push, leading to damage. A wrapper that reports only contact and
hand can conceal the difference between TOUCH and HOLD. The error is promoting
an earlier observation to a future mechanical guarantee. This defeats that
retained-contact contract; it does not prove that every interface forgetting
grade is structurally invalid.

Without one shared contact witness, sensing and mechanics can each be locally
correct yet disagree. If the sensed contact has one hand and the mechanical
pivot has the other, the selected drive damages the object. Two of the four
independent hand pairs fail. Even equal hands do not justify duplicating a
physical invocation: one two-pad seating record gives only aligned pairs
(0,0) and (1,1), whereas two independent invocations give four pairs. The extra
off-diagonal pairs violate exact record matching. That multiplicity defect is
not, by itself, claimed to cause damage.


5  What persists through time and interface changes

For every execution length, a path of compatible component tuples is the same
data as compatible component paths. Transposing the time and component indices
gives a canonical bijection. Interpreting an original tree before forming paths
or afterward therefore gives the same result, as does interpreting its
occurrence-wise refinement arrows. The comparisons respect truncation and
concatenation. Exactness on states and one-step event records supplies exactness
at every finite length; separate horizon-by-horizon choices are unnecessary.

The staged version retains acquisition and action phases. It can transport a
fixed controller's guarantee through an exact refinement when observations,
actions, initialization and task predicates are also preserved. An arbitrary
refinement, or an isomorphism only of collapsed transitions, does not meet those
conditions automatically.

Interface changes have different answers. Forgetting boundary distinctions
always preserves a valid witness map, but may destroy exactness by allowing new
combinations of existing component records. With two labels, matching pairs
(0,0) and (1,1) become four pairs when the labels are forgotten. The old whole
still has only two records. For a nonempty profile, exactness survives precisely
when no newly compatible state or event tuple appears. For an empty profile,
the comparison is the interface map itself and must be an isomorphism.

Independently lifting both endpoints of every component is more demanding.
Internal cuts require compatible lifted boundary values. Missing values can
prevent transport; several values can prevent exact transport; independently
chosen state and event lifts can fail to preserve incidence. The theorem gives
the comparison map whose incidence-preserving lift must be supplied. When the
old witness is exact, exact transport exists precisely when that comparison is an
isomorphism, in which case it is unique. Arbitrary endpoint changes are not
silently treated as universally available restrictions.

By contrast, changing one common interface and giving all components the same
lift preserves exactness. Reflecting the result back requires enough coverage
of original states and events. Removing troublesome records can make a
restricted system pass without proving the unrestricted one passes.

The model and comodel language remains interface-relative. A finite
request-response runner can implement an upper operation using lower requests
and internal state; that operational role can be represented by the staged
model. Calling it a comodel of a specified algebraic theory additionally
requires checking that theory's equations. Not every graph span is thereby a
comodel. Assertions about possible or necessary behavior can be read from the
executions afterward; they are a derived modal view, not a new language or a
replacement for the retained witnesses.


6  Evidence and the original contract

The source bridge checks include 578 grouping and occurrence instances, 2,184
nontrivial path comparisons, 225 nonempty-interface coarsening cases and 31
nullary diagonal cases. Negative controls cover false event equations,
nonexact cancellation, loss of refinement information, and a surjective graph
map with no graph section. The fixture checker separately verifies the full
open record set, acquisition matching, regrouping, all reachable controller
executions, the decreasing rank and the four failure constructions.

These are reproducible finite checks supporting mathematical proofs, not
exhaustive verification of all finite systems. The new dynamic interpretation
has not been formalized in Lean. The existing Lean declarations supplying free
extension, presentation descent and refinement functoriality are identified
directly; using their API in a mathematical argument is not a new kernel check.
Independent AI reviewers inspected the arguments and reran finite checks
against the final sources, accepting this bounded integration result. That
review retains the limitations of AI-based review; no external human review
or empirical robot trial is claimed.

The underlying mechanisms are standard: free and presented typed calculi,
compatible products of graphs, finite-state control, and decreasing-rank
reachability arguments. The useful result here is their explicit integration
with actual occurrence and execution records, including the conditions that
block invalid inferences. No priority claim is made for those mechanisms.

Relative to the original contract, this instantiates the accepted algebraic
core rather than replacing it. It adds a concrete presentation and refinement
comparison, proved boundaries for specified interface changes, exact assembly
under tree substitution, and a synthetic controller-environment application.
These progress the corresponding B, D, E and G obligations. They do not discharge
their stronger statements merely by using similar terminology.

In particular, the new fixture presentation does not dynamically interpret all
generators or equations of the historical graph-cut family. The target calculus
does not construct the universal virtual equipment required by E2. Finite path
compatibility is not the original all-tree cut interchange or 2-Segal recovery
theory. The bounded application is not full discharge of G3 or of the general
recovery, amalgamation and observational-repair program.

The active integration gap is nevertheless closed at its stated scale: the
original source calculus has a precise dynamic interpretation; a separately
marked exact fragment supports refinement and time comparisons; and one plant
derived from local primitives supports a causal, adversary-safe controller
guarantee. The larger residuals remain identified obligations, not additional
branches opened by this milestone.
