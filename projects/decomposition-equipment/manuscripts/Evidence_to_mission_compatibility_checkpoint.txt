Evidence to mission compatibility checkpoint
30 October 2026

We can now tell exactly when a particular set of linear measurements is enough to
guarantee a chosen mission through one fixed feedback loop. The guarantee covers every
linear completion in the declared class consistent with those measurements. It does not
require identifying the system or forcing its internal state to be unique.

The result gives both a certificate of success and a concrete counterexample when the
guarantee fails. A second construction handles finite networks when their unknown
quantities and connections are explicitly represented. It retains their correlations and
handles exceptional cases that ordinary calculations can miss.

These are scoped mathematical results, not a solution to the whole research problem.
They do not yet tell us whether the model includes the real physical couplings, how to
collect evidence safely and cheaply, or whether a compact realization can be obtained.
Within this declared setting, the connection to the earlier structural theorem has also
been checked. Novelty is unresolved.

One fixed loop from two sided evidence

Work in finite-dimensional real vector spaces. An unknown linear box F maps V=A
direct-sum U to Y direct-sum U. Write p:V->A and t:V->U for projections. The exact
evidence specifies F on a known subspace S and specifies HF for a known output
measurement H. First check consistency; contradictory evidence is not certified by
vacuity.

Choose any consistent F0. Let K=ker H, i_K its inclusion, and q:V->V/S the quotient. The
complete supported family is exactly

    F_Z=F0+i_K Zq,                 Z:V/S->K arbitrary.

A difference between completions vanishes on S and lands in K, proving this
parametrization. The same Z controls internal and observed uncertainty; splitting it
into independent uncertainties would change the evidence.

Fix a linear mission readout h:Y direct-sum U->O, a fixed linear target J:A->O, and the
wiring that identifies internal output with internal input. Define

    L0=pr_U F0-t,     B=pr_U i_K,
    O0=hF0,          C=hi_K,          E0=O0-Jp,
    L_Z=L0+BZq,      E_Z=E0+CZq.

The mission is p(ker L_Z)=A and E_Z|ker L_Z=0 for every Z: every input has a fixed
point, and every fixed point gives the prescribed readout. This is mission-relative
kernel-image admission, not hidden-state uniqueness. One policy is fixed throughout.

Put W=S intersect ker p, R0=L0(W), and T=L0^{-1}(im B). The exact
necessary-and-sufficient theorem is the conjunction of feasibility and output below.

    Feasibility: F1 OR F2
    F1: p(S intersect ker L0)=A.
    F2: im B is contained in R0 AND p(ker L0)=A.

    Output: use the applicable branch
    O1, if T is contained in S: E0|ker L0=0.
    O2, if T is not contained in S:
        there exists D:U->O with DL0=E0 and DB=C.

These are finite subspace, rank, and linear-equation tests. Their truth is independent
of the chosen F0, because another choice merely translates Z in the same completion
family.

Witnesses and the reason the theorem is exact

Under F1, choose a section Kobs:A->S intersect ker L0 of p. It works for every
completion. Under F2, choose K0:A->ker L0 with pK0=id and R:im B->W with L0R=id. Then

    K_Z=K0-RBZqK0

satisfies pK_Z=id and L_ZK_Z=0, since pR=qR=0. Under O2, E_Z=DL_Z; under O1, every
possible fixed vector lies in T contained in S and is already nominal. Both branches
therefore control all fixed points, not just the selected section.

Conversely, if im B is not contained in R0, a functional annihilating R0 but not B can
be used to make a residual row equal a nonzero external-input functional unless F1
holds. For output, any x in T outside S can be made fixed by prescribing Zqx=k with
Bk=-L0x. Requiring E0x+Ck=0 for every such choice forces C to vanish on ker B, and then
yields exactly the multiplier D in O2. This also explains the separate O1 branch.

If the conjunction fails, some completion with rank Z at most one witnesses failure; Z=0
covers nominal failures. Otherwise choose Z=k ell to create the missing input or a bad
fixed point. Thus rejection has an explicit adverse model within the declared evidence
class, not merely a failed search.

Independent proof checking found the algebra sound. Direct exhaustive checks over the
two-element field agreed in 69,952 small instances: 320, 65,536, and 4,096 for the three
tested dimension pairs. The tested (dim A, dim U) pairs were (1,1), (1,2), and (2,1),
with dim K=dim O=1. These checks support the proof; they are not general empirical
evidence about physical systems.

A useful correlated example

Take A=U=Y=R, readout y, and target y=a. Observe the diagonal experiment F(1,1)=(1,1),
and measure y-v on an input basis, obtaining the row identity y-v=a-u by linearity.
These finite experiments give the exact completion family

    F_z(a,u)=(a,u)+z(a-u)(1,1),       z in R.

The diagonal supplies u=a as a fixed point. The conservation law plus actual feedback
v=u forces y=a at every fixed point. At z=0, every u is fixed; at nonzero z, u=a is
unique. The mission succeeds without identifying z or requiring a unique hidden fixed
point. Independent internal/output perturbations would destroy the conservation
correlation and invalidate this conclusion.

Optional stricter original admission

If the original operational specification demands inverse-domain feedback, retain that
requirement separately. With vertical inclusion i_U, Q=qi_U and D0=L0i_U, every residual
internal block D0+BZQ is invertible exactly when

    D0 is invertible AND QD0^{-1}B=0.

Under this guard let R=i_U D0^{-1} and K0 be the unique nominal fixed-point section. The
exact prescribed-output test is

    O0K0=J AND [qK0=0 OR C=O0RB].

The inverse follows from a square-zero update; output equals O0K0+(C-O0RB)ZqK0. Failure
again admits a rank-at-most-one countermodel. This optional contract intentionally
rejects the z=0 example, although its kernel-image mission is valid. Other original
safety or scheduling guards are not supplied by algebraic fixed-point existence.

Known contexts and the boundary of the one loop rule

For known linear pre/post maps P and G, the family GFP is exactly another two-sided
family with nominal GF0P, sampled subspace P^{-1}(S), and uncertainty image G(K). The
induced map V'/P^{-1}(S)->V/S is injective and K->G(K) is surjective; splitting these
maps lifts every transformed uncertainty back to an original completion. Successive
forward context maps compose exactly. If F remains correlated with other unknown
components, retain the shared parameter and lift relation; the projected family must not
be independently resampled. A transformed mission and its guards must still be checked.

Stronger consistent evidence restricts the completion family and its successful witness
family. Recomputed sections in the same world differ by an invertible shear id+(K'-K)p
on the fixed-point carrier, preserving both mission legs and satisfying the cocycle
identity. Different worlds need not have isomorphic raw carriers.

Unknown composition is different. Reusing an unknown scalar a twice yields a^2,
whose supported values [0,infinity) are not affine. Even independent unknown row/column
factors produce a rank-at-most-one matrix family, also non-affine. The one-loop rule is
therefore not recursively closed under arbitrary unknown composition.

Finite networks with shared parameters

A broader checked algebraic construction uses a nonempty semialgebraic locus Theta of
shared parameters theta, specified by evidence and a declared structural theory T. Fix
finite ports and a finite declared collection of networks, contexts, and substitution
routes. Each network has homogeneous wire equations

    A_N(theta)w=0,            external e=L_N(theta)w,

with polynomial coefficients in theta. The same parameters and all coupling equations
remain shared. Affine equations require a distinguished homogenizing coordinate fixed to
one; a free extra coordinate would change the problem.

Branch Gaussian elimination on every zero/nonzero pivot possibility, then take a common
refinement for the finite collection. All rank drops get their own strata. On each chart
one computes rational matrices N,H,Tc,Sr,Dk with explicitly nonzero denominators such
that

    ker A_N=im N,       L_N N=H Tc,
    Tc Sr=I,           ker Tc=im Dk,

where N,H,Dk have independent columns. The exact observable interface and all its
witnesses are

    e=Hc,              w=N(Sr c+Dk z).

This extracts an actual external relation and its invisible freedoms, not just a mission
truth value. Empty matrices cover zero-dimensional cases. No generic chart is
specialized through a vanishing denominator. Intersecting charts with stronger evidence
restricts the same formulas, retaining rank-drop strata; independently recomputed charts
use their overlap transitions.

Relational composition is exact when private witnesses are disjoint, shared cut
variables remain visible until gluing, and the same theta is retained. Hidden
cross-couplings must stay at the cut or appear as joint constraints. Composing unions
after independently forgetting theta would incorrectly mix worlds. Direct and staged
extraction then represent the same external subspace. Their basis-coordinate transitions
are uniquely determined by that subspace and obey the cocycle law. Chosen internal
sections differ by invisible-kernel shears; they need not be unique or executable.
Completion-dependent and chart-dependent internal witnesses certify existence. They are
not hidden-world policies; an observable implementation is a further obligation.

For required input projection p with m-dimensional codomain and readout residual
r=O_m-Jp, where O_m is the stated linear readout, the pointwise necessary-and-sufficient
mission tests are

    rank([A_N;r])=rank A_N,
    rank([A_N;p])=rank A_N+m.

The first controls every solution; the second supplies some solution for every input.
Conjoin the original admission guards for each required schedule. With real algebraic
coefficients and semialgebraic guards, quantifier elimination decides universal success
over Theta relative to T. A failure gives a supported parameter countermodel and a
failed guard, bad solution, or infeasible input. It is not necessarily a physically
realizable world if T only overapproximates reality.

Finite rational matrices explicitly present the raw interfaces, sections, and chart

Prior art and remaining work

Two-sided matrix completion is classical, including the Cecioni consistency result
presented by Penrose in A generalized inverse for matrices:
https://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/math/jaafarm/lec-notes/Moore-Pinrose.pdf

Tian and Cheng give extremal rank and range-invariance results for A-BXC, closely
matching full-block uncertainty: https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2003/9-18.pdf

Eising and Trentelman establish uniform rank/informativity tests for affine system
families: https://arxiv.org/html/2010.15564

Malherbe, Scott, and Selinger define the kernel-image partial trace and distinguish it
from inverse-domain feedback: https://arxiv.org/html/1107.3608#S3.SS3.SSS3

Van Waarde and coauthors already establish data informativity without plant
identification: https://arxiv.org/html/1908.00468

Ballarin and Kauers give constraint-maintaining zero/nonzero-pivot branching and uniform
solution regimes in Solving Parametric Linear Systems, Sections 3-4, the direct baseline
for the network extraction: https://wwwbroy.in.tum.de/publ/papers/rwca02.pdf

The bounded source audit did not locate the exact F1/F2 plus O1/O2 branching criterion;
it did not establish priority or rule out a classical reduction. Parametric elimination,
relational composition, rank tests, and splitting shears are established ingredients.

The coherent bridge is therefore proved within the selected linear doctrine and
