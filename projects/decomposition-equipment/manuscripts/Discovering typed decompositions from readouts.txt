Discovering typed decompositions from readouts

Start with recorded data and a chosen mathematical universe of allowed mechanisms, interfaces and composition rules. No actual subsystem boundaries, component models, interfaces or decomposition are supplied.

Discover components and their coupling that jointly account for the environment’s behavior, with stated guarantees beyond the recorded samples, while retaining uncertainty the data cannot resolve. These need not be unique physical parts: a useful decomposition of the environment’s operational behavior is enough. Internal randomness is allowed, not treated as a defect.

The hard theorem target is a constructive criterion for when the learned behavior admits such a decomposition, and a precise obstruction when a proposed assembly cannot work. It must handle shared interfaces, correlated uncertainty and crossing decompositions, not merely recover one global hidden-state model or arrange components in a single cascade.

A compact predictive representation learned from the data is a legitimate intermediate. The mathematical work is to turn it into compatible local stochastic mechanisms with independently meaningful types and certificates that the pieces coexist in one process. Fitting each component separately is not enough; their interface choices must work together and remain consistent through time.

The decomposition should expose what the environment demands. Matching these demands against a controller’s certified capabilities should then be a comparatively simple type/interface check, not another global analysis. The types must not hide the original problem inside themselves.

The next mathematical step is simultaneous positive interface realization, meaning mechanisms with genuine probabilities rather than merely signed algebraic weights: derive checkable conditions on the learned predictive operators that construct a coherent spatial port decomposition, or expose why compatible-looking local models cannot be assembled. A growing family must distinguish this from ordinary global-model realization.

This is an open theorem target. Finite predictive-basis and error-propagation results provide a foundation, not its completion. Statistical confidence, exact support and physical intervention claims need their respective assumptions; action labels in a log alone do not establish causality. The target does not promise efficient inference for every possible environment.
