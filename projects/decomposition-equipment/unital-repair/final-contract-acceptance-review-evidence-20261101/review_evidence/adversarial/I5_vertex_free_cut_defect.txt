I5 vertex free cut boundary and its dependent claims
Independent nonauthor adversarial acceptance review
1 November 2026

Finding

The delivered I5 source does not explicitly settle whether its permitted generated cut trees include the vertex free one edge identity shape. Under the standard all finite tree interpretation, its universal old cut lifting assertion is false. The source proof handles trees with at least one operation vertex. This is an unresolved scope and repair decision, not permission to silently narrow the selected language.

The tested statement is model_and_core_comparison.md Lemma 4, line 53, in logical source package i5_repair_delivery_20261031/source. It states that every old cut T of alpha lifts to an extended cut of (alpha,l) for every external tuple l. The principal i5_joint_repair_theorem.md section 2.2, line 37 repeats this claim. I1 cut_transport_draft.md section 3 and conservative_amalgamation_theorem.md section 6.2 repeat the same old label blind existence assertion.

The counterexample

Take any nonempty original graph component H and the old identity operation id_H. The vertex free one edge tree is a valid substitution tree for id_H: it has one input, no operation vertices, and evaluation equal to identity. Give the enriched identity old record a nonidentity branch label l in the declared monoid. In I5 one can take l=(b,e) in M_k times M_k for any k at least 4. The enriched operation (id_H,l) is not identity because identity has label (e,e). There are no operation input edges on which to place l in the vertex free tree. Its enriched evaluation is necessarily (id_H,(e,e)). Thus its cut fibre at (id_H,l) is empty. The old cut fibre is inhabited.

This is not a confusion with a nullary operation. The counterexample has a unary external profile and zero operation vertices. A nullary operation has a vertex and no external leaves. Nor is it the one vertex identity corolla, which does admit the requested enriched label and is the base case the submitted proof discusses.

Exact corrected lemma without weakening the domain

Let T be any actual finite old planar substitution tree of alpha, and let l be a label tuple over alpha's active external occurrences.

1. If T has at least one operation vertex, its enriched cut fibre over (alpha,l) is nonempty. Put monoid units on the input edges ending at internal operation vertices and put l_j at the actual external active leaf edges. Every root to leaf product is l_j. Empty old branches retain their old records and carry no added label.
2. If T is vertex free, then it has one external edge and alpha=id_H for its component H. The enriched cut fibre is inhabited if and only if (alpha,l) is the actual enriched identity. If H is nonempty this means its sole label is e; if H is empty there are no added labels and the condition is automatic. The fibre is then a singleton.

This retains all finite tree shapes and gives the missing exact boundary; it does not assert the currently claimed unconditional existence. It is a mathematical review correction, not an edit to any author artifact or an approved amendment of the admissible cut doctrine.

Why the source grammar is presently unresolved

concrete_model_gate.md line 21 selects all finite planar rooted ordered cuts generated by consecutive grouping and ungrouping, explicitly retaining empty runs, unary vertices and legal nullary nodes. It does not mention zero vertex shapes. profile_schemata_theorem.md sections 1 and 2 and i5_joint_repair_theorem.md section 4 characterize cuts by iterating nodewise equations x_j=a_g(j)b_j. Such generation naturally proves the positive vertex grammar. No explicit initial object or rule excluding the vertex free unit shape is given.

The inherited original Semantics_Transport_Factorization.md section 6.3 expressly treats q at least 1 and separately says that a vertex free shape realizes only an actual identity. That section is not among I5's explicitly imported section 2 through 5 dependency claims, so it does not by itself define I5's permitted language. It does show that the wider original calculus does not identify this edge case with a positive vertex corolla.

Consequently two readings must be kept separate. A precisely specified positive vertex generated doctrine can support the existing binary factorization proofs. The ordinary all tree reading refutes the unqualified lifting assertion. The acceptance review must not select the first reading without an explicit scope decision and consistent wording throughout the dependent packages.

Impact on I5 if the zero vertex test is included

A Boolean cut signature observes empty versus inhabited fibres. At the old identity record, the zero vertex signature is the identity test l=e. It distinguishes e from b although the submitted index h has h(e)=h(b)=0. Therefore C(P0)=ker h is not the cut repair for this enlarged language. The elementary round and block count formulas in monoid_repair_theorem.md sections 3 and 5 remain valid for the explicitly defined binary-only operator c; their application to every admitted cut fails without the missing grammar boundary.

The same test refutes C(top)=top. It also removes the hypothesis that every channel cut fibre is nonempty in repair_calculus.md section 5.3 and modular_repair_theorem.md sections 3 through 5. A fixed global source with a nonidentity omitted channel does not lift a zero vertex cut of its identity observed channel. Thus the existing channel product and optimality reflection proofs cannot be applied to an enlarged unital language merely by reusing their old nonemptiness sentence.

An independent calculation constructs the actual transformation monoids rather than importing the author code. At k=4 the binary-only counts from P0 are 2,3,4. Including the identity feasibility test gives 2,4,6,8. Starting indiscrete gives 1,2,3,6,8, not a fixed indiscrete partition. The supporting JSON records k=4 through 10. These finite numbers are diagnostics of the changed operator, not a repaired all-profile theorem.

The proper group quotient control fails as well. If a compositional equivalence on a group preserves the identity feasibility test and x is equivalent to y, multiplying by x inverse implies e is equivalent to x inverse y. Identity feasibility forces x inverse y=e. Thus the group equivalence is discrete. In particular C4 squared to C2 squared cannot remain a proper compositional exact-cut quotient under that added test. Merely adding a unit bit does not repair its compression claim.

I1 dependency impact

cut_transport_draft.md section 4 and conservative_amalgamation_theorem.md section 6.3 say a surjective group homomorphism has all finite tree fixed-source lifting. Under zero vertex inclusion, take a nonidentity kernel element x. Its image is identity and admits a zero vertex target cut; x admits no zero vertex source cut. The all-tree group criterion is therefore isomorphism, not surjectivity: binary cuts of target identity force surjectivity, and the zero vertex case forces kernel triviality. For a general monoid, the displayed positive vertex shared-parent lifting criterion must additionally be paired with h inverse of 1 equal to {1} for this extra shape.

The I1 operation pushout and equation-conservativity theorems, mixed occurrence refinement comparison, twisted literal fixed-tree coefficient bijection, and filtered local-factor reflection are not refuted. For the twisted bijection, the zero vertex fibre is empty on both sides unless both source coordinates are units, when both sides are singletons. Injective module maps reflect identity already. The asserted noninvertible group all-tree positive transfer and unrestricted label-blind cut-existence claims require the explicit boundary decision or a reviewed correction.

Disposition

OPEN exact-domain defect. Under ordinary all-tree interpretation the identified statements are FAILED as written. Under an explicitly positive vertex generated interpretation their displayed constructive proofs remain available, but that interpretation has not been silently adopted. No original source has been changed. Any amendment or correction requires an explicit decision in the final acceptance review.
