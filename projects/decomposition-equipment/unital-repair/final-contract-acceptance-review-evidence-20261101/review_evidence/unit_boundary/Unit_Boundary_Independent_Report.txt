Independent verification of the unit cut boundary
Final acceptance review 1 November 2026

Finding

The reported obstruction is mathematically correct. A vertex-free cut tree
is not a unary vertex labelled by an arbitrary operation. Its evaluation is
the actual identity. For a nonempty color H in P[L], that identity is
(id_H,e), whereas (id_H,l) for l != e is a different unary operation.
The bare unit wire has no operation-input edge on which to place l.

Consequently the unqualified old-cut lifting statement in I5 Lemma 4 is
false if its cut grammar includes the vertex-free tree. The corresponding
I1 old-skeleton statement and proper group-quotient controls in I5 and I2
have the same boundary. This does not invalidate the enriched multicategory
or its faithful all-e inclusion. Nor does this review choose a smaller
grammar: the selected observable cut domain remains an explicit decision.

The independent checker confirms C4 -> C2 as the exact counterexample,
reproduces the M4 distinction, and checks the corrected group-map criterion
on all 112 homomorphisms between cyclic groups of orders 1 through 8.

Corrected mathematical statements

1. Old-skeleton lifting

Fix an actual old connected planar cut tree T of alpha.

If T has at least one operation vertex, it lifts to (alpha,l) for every
external label tuple l: put e on every active internal operation-input edge
and l_j on the operation-input edge incident with external active leaf j.
Every active root-to-leaf product is l_j. This includes the one-vertex
corolla whose underlying old operation is id_H.

If T is vertex-free, its old composite is id_H. Its enriched lift at the
specified source exists precisely when that source is the actual enriched
identity. For nonempty H this means l=e. For empty H there is no added
coordinate, so the boundary holds automatically. Nullary operation vertices
and label-free empty graph occurrences are different cases; neither is the
vertex-free unary unit tree.

Forgetting labels always preserves existing cut records. The all-e section
preserves and reflects old existence, including this exceptional tree.
What fails is existence reflection at every arbitrary enriched source over
the same old identity. One cannot repair the claim by adding a D_H(l)
vertex without changing the specified zero-vertex skeleton.

2. Fixed-source lifting along group maps

Assume old geometry is unchanged, or independently has the required exact
old-source lift. For a surjective group map h:G -> K, every prescribed
positive-vertex target factorization lifts at every concrete source. At a
shared parent q and source tuple x_j with h(x_j)=q r_j, choose one
p in h^{-1}(q) and set v_j=p^{-1}x_j. Then h(v_j)=r_j and pv_j=x_j
simultaneously. Apply this to the positive tree, retaining every factor.

For a vertex-free target tree at h(x)=1, the sole possible source
vertex-free composite is 1. Thus the extra all-source condition is

    h^{-1}(1) = {1}.

Preserving identity and reflecting identity are different conditions.
Every monoid homomorphism here preserves identity, h(1)=1; that equation
does not exclude h(x)=1 for a nonidentity x. Vertex-free fixed-source
lifting needs the converse implication h(x)=1 implies x=1, in addition
to positive-tree factorization lifting. This obstruction does not refute
E2's preservation of universal units or the I3/F4 structural maps whose
unit squares were checked: unit preservation and unit-square commutation
are not the fixed-source reflection property being tested here.

For groups, positive binary lifting at every source forces surjectivity:
at source 1, lift 1=q q^{-1} for each q in K. Therefore all-source lifting
for all positive trees AND the vertex-free unit tree holds exactly when h
is both surjective and identity-reflecting, equivalently an isomorphism.
This is not a uniqueness requirement smuggled into existential lifting;
the empty factor tuple supplies no adjustable factor at all.

For a monoid map, the I1 shared-parent intersection tests must likewise be
supplemented by identity reflection if vertex-free units are admitted.
Over the stated fixed old geometry, those tests plus identity reflection
are sufficient by the same tree induction, with the exceptional base case
checked separately. Use only geometrically realizable stars for necessity.

3. Explicit quotient failure

Use additive C4 -> C2, x |-> x mod 2, J={0,2}. At the old nonempty
unary identity record take concrete label 2. Its image is target identity
label 0, so the target vertex-free cut has the empty factor record ().
The concrete vertex-free fibre at 2 is empty. The two concrete sources
0 and 2 therefore have different sourcewise cut signatures, {()} and
empty, despite having the same quotient class.

For I2's two-channel example use source label (2,0), mapping to (0,0).
No choice of a quotient cut relation on this same parity encoder can
satisfy equation (E) at both (0,0) and (2,0). This refutes exactness even
without prescribing concrete factor endpoints. The counterexample lies
in one fixed full profile and retains all old data.

If all group translations and literal unit feasibility are observed,
the fixed unary identity fibre is already discrete: for g != g', left
translation by g^{-1} sends only g to identity. In C4 squared this gives
16 distinct theories rather than the claimed four parity states. This is
a local obstruction, not a newly asserted all-profile repaired theorem.

Grammar status and required decision

The pinned old Semantics_Transport_Factorization.md, Section 6.3 lines
510-527, explicitly distinguishes q >= 1 from the vertex-free identity.
Its SHA-256 is dbbf64f3c2cb49301d03df01ed5cddca2b8dcbad3efe8a701d2f5f852f462c84,
matching the I5 model's dependency pin. This establishes the old semantic
boundary, not automatic inclusion in the new selected observable grammar.

The selected gate says all generated finite planar rooted ordered cuts and
includes empty runs, unary vertices and legal nullaries, but no inspected
passage explicitly settles the exceptional shape as an observable cut.
Thus the standard all-tree reading yields a verified counterexample;
the exact selected-domain ambiguity remains OPEN for acceptance purposes.

An explicit positive-vertex observational grammar would avoid this specific
obstruction while retaining ordinary algebraic identities, but would be a
scope/target choice to record and approve, not an inference made here.
An explicit unit-inclusive grammar requires corrected statements and new
evidence for the affected claims. This report supplies neither a silent
restriction nor a replacement research programme.
