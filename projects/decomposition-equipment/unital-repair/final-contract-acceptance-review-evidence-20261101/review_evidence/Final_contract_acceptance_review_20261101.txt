The unit cut defect and its exact boundary

For a nonempty old color H, the vertex-free tree evaluates to the actual enriched
identity (id_H,e). It does not evaluate to (id_H,l) for l different from e. A bare wire
has no operation-input edge on which to place that label. A unary identity-labelled
operation vertex, a nullary vertex and an empty graph occurrence are different cases.

Every positive-vertex old skeleton still lifts at arbitrary external labels: internal
active inputs can receive the identity and external active inputs the prescribed labels.
Forgetting labels preserves existing records, and the all-identity-label section
reflects old existence, including the bare unit. What fails is old-cut existence
reflection at every arbitrary enriched source over the old identity. Inserting a new
unary vertex changes the specified skeleton and is not a repair of its zero-vertex lift.
Identity preservation is intact; identity reflection and sourcewise zero-vertex
feasibility are the missing conditions. This does not refute E2 preservation or the
independently checked I3/F4 unit squares.

The exact counterexample is additive C4 -> C2, reduction modulo two. Source label 2 maps
to target identity 0. The target vertex-free cut has the empty factor record (), but no
vertex-free cut exists at source 2. Sources 0 and 2 therefore have different cut
signatures despite the same quotient class. In I2's two-channel example the concrete
source is (2,0), mapping to (0,0). No quotient cut relation on that same parity encoder
can make both sourcewise exact.

With old geometry fixed or independently lifted, all-source positive-tree lifting for a
group map requires surjectivity. Including the vertex-free unit additionally requires
identity reflection, or a trivial kernel. Thus all-source lifting for positive trees
plus the unit holds exactly for an isomorphism, not merely a surjection. The independent
review checked the boundary on all 112 homomorphisms between cyclic groups of orders one
through eight; its general conclusion rests on the symbolic argument, not enumeration.

The frozen older semantics explicitly distinguishes positive-vertex trees from the
vertex-free identity. The selected observable grammar is not explicit enough to resolve
whether the exceptional shape was included. Under the standard all-tree reading, the
counterexample is decisive. A positive-vertex-only observational grammar would be an
explicit scope choice requiring approval, not a convention silently supplied by this
review. It changes the admitted cut/observation guarantee, not the multicategory
identity or unit laws. A unit-inclusive reading requires corrected claims and evidence
at that scope.

Unaffected results include the faithful enrichment, positive-tree constructions, named
pushout classification and discrete simultaneous endpoint. The latter remains sufficient
when tests are added, while its positive-tree lower bound remains valid. Exact
intermediate cut-only classes and iteration counts cannot simply be transferred to the
larger unit-inclusive language.
