Original rule interpretation

Original section 3.2 defines program investigation closure as every prescribed positive or allowed negative outcome plus governance. It separately defines intended scientific scope as that closure plus generality/comparison/sharpness/anti-shortcut judgment. Section 3.4 explicitly says a known-result finding may close an investigation without silently satisfying desired significance. Therefore a negative or NOT ESTABLISHED significance verdict does not turn a correct theorem into a false theorem. Nor does an erroneous premise become a permitted B-class obstruction simply because an honest review found it.

H1 requires traceability, an acyclic graph, source/contribution/limitation audit and visible withdrawals. Finding an unresolved question does not make performing this audit impossible. H2 requires exact clean evidence/trusted base, not a positive novelty verdict. H3 requires actual independent review and a final matrix, but its positive acceptance discharge also requires every M/B/G outcome and no unresolved headline-undermining defect. The review can be completed with OPEN or FAILED outcomes; 'review performed' is not 'all obligations accepted'. A negative significance verdict alone need not invalidate completion of an honest H1/H3 review process. Current withholding is driven separately by the concrete unit-cut issue.

No zero-vertex exclusion is adopted here. If the original frozen generated-cut grammar already excludes it, record exact prior evidence. If it includes it, repair the results at unchanged scope or seek an explicit amendment before narrowing. Superseded author assertions remain visible.
