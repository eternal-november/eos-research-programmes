Physical stopping assurance from a discovered predictive model
29 October 2026

ABSTRACT

The previous result discovered a reusable predictor from action and readout
records. This result connects that predictor to a timed stopping controller
and explains precisely when its certificate implies a physical guarantee.
The controller must decide before it knows the future sensing delay, command
delay, or braking strength. Its proof covers acceleration, waiting, brake
engagement, and the complete stopping tail, rather than treating a stop
command as instantaneous safety.

The strongest implemented result uses learned memory to identify which
physical outcomes remain possible. In a supplied favorable history, a
controller can request 1,100 millimetres per second while stopping safely for
all eight supported outcomes. A controller that forgets the relevant history
can guarantee only 800 on the same speed grid and fixed motion protocol.
This gain comes from contextual response and memory, not from assuming rare
failures away. A separate example uses learned joint probabilities to admit
1,000 instead of 900, but its approximately 9.17 percent risk bound is only an
illustration, not a practical collision-safety target.

The general theorem bounds collision by the first loss of a proved assurance
condition, plus failure of the physical assumptions. It preserves joint
uncertainty, charges unsafe coverage exits, and permits later controller
selection without restarting the evidence budget. Integer C code reproduces
the reference model. The physical interpretation of the old readouts,
calibration, and hardware timing remain stipulated premises: no robot or
actuator was validated. This is a constructive discovery-to-execution bridge,
not completion of the broader research programme or a claim of new standard
stopping mathematics.

1 What has been established

There are two positive results: a general conditional theorem and a concrete
executable instance. The theorem specifies the additional obligations needed
to turn the previously discovered controlled predictor into a physical
assurance statement. The instance implements a finite-memory admission rule,
a local timed executor, conservative stopping calculations, and negative
tests that expose failures of shortcuts.

The main implication is simple enough to state without predictive notation:

  collision implies first assurance loss or physical-envelope failure.

An assurance loss is a declared failure of a sufficient certificate, not
necessarily a collision. This distinction allows the statistical model to
predict an event that conservatively contains physical danger. A separate
engineering argument must prove that successful certificates really imply
collision-free motion. Fitting readout records cannot supply that argument.

The most useful numerical finding is stronger than a small fitted failure
frequency. Given the correctly identified response and the promised noise
support, every possible outcome in the favorable context permits the
1,100 mm/s maneuver. No empirical probability estimate is required for this
claim. The physical risk bound then reduces to the externally justified
probability that the physical premises fail. The executable example does not
supply a measured value for that probability.

The comparison is deliberately specific. It holds for a stationary protected
boundary, the specified ramp and stopping schedule, the supplied observed
history, and a seven-element speed grid. It is not an optimality theorem over
all controllers. Braking earlier changes the protocol and can change the
answer. Requested speed is not itself throughput, successful travel distance,
or completion of a useful navigation task.

The work reuses the released twelve-channel learned model and its evidence.
There is no new training dataset and no new physical experiment. What is new
in this milestone is the explicit physical contract, its mission-wide
first-loss accounting, the compiled timed controller, and the check that the
retained history changes a sound admission decision.

2 What is learned and what is supplied

The inherited predictor discovers response scopes, recursively maintained
observable memory, and groups of shared stochastic innovations within its
declared finite-memory model family. It supports feedback controllers chosen
after learning, provided their histories and actions satisfy its coverage
and interface requirements. It is one coherent joint predictor, not a
collection of separately convenient local fits.

The executable bridge uses the previously released joint innovation group
with readout indices 0, 5, and 10. Their learned responses depend on current
action ports 7, 3, and 5, and capped run memories on ports 1, 4, 6, 8, and 10.
Those dependencies come from the response catalogue. The decision program
updates memory from observed actions; it is not given the simulator's hidden
physical mode. Twelve one-byte counters are retained for compatibility,
although this task uses only five of them.

The physical interpretation is supplied separately. The three readouts are
mapped to sensing delay, command-network delay, and braking deceleration.
That map is a synthetic calibration contract, not a discovery that the old
records came from a real sensor or brake. It must apply to the actual system
and to the simulator in the same way.

The added speed command also needs an explicit compatibility premise. The
old binary action/readout law must remain valid under the motion protocol;
speed must not alter its response except through the declared input map.
This is true by construction in the synthetic embedding. On a real vehicle,
speed, load, surface, temperature, and battery condition could change braking
or communication behavior. Their effects would need calibration, conditioning,
or new acquisition. A guarantee for all feedback policies inside the old
interface does not automatically cover a new physical intervention.

A causal adapter formalizes this boundary. Physical commands map to permitted
source actions using currently available information. Source outcomes map to
physical timing/status records through the same justified transducer. Failure,
missing-data, and termination labels must be certified outputs or sound
adapter results. Merely inventing a label for a crashed execution does not
inherit a probability certificate for crashes.

The adapter preserves trajectory guarantees, but not every conditional bound
after information is discarded. Two models can have different posterior
mixtures over hidden source histories even when they produce the same coarse
physical history. The proof therefore couples the augmented source history
and adapter state. A controller may still use only its permitted projection;
proof access to richer state never grants the deployed program that access.

3 The physical witness and the first loss theorem

For each delivered history h, the physical monitor maintains a conservative
set U(h) of possible current states. A separate event G says that the stated
sensor, geometry, dynamics, clock, and actuator premises hold throughout the
mission. The external physical contract supplies a bound eta_phys on the
probability of G failing, uniformly over the controllers covered by that
contract. An exploration-only calibration rate cannot silently be reused
under a different deployment policy.

Every admitted segment has a post-outcome condition F. If F reports success,
then, on G, the whole segment is collision-free and reaches the next certified
state set or a safe terminal state. The controller selects its action before
seeing the outcome. A segment may therefore be risk-qualified even though
its certificate is evaluated retrospectively. If a collision occurs midway
through a failed segment, it is charged to that segment; the record protocol
must not discard the failed execution.

For a stationary boundary, one familiar sufficient calculation starts at
speed v, allows acceleration at most a until braking engages within delay d,
and guarantees deceleration at least b afterwards. Maximum forward travel is

  R(v,d,a,b) = v*d + a*d*d/2 + (v+a*d)*(v+a*d)/(2*b).

The terms are travel before engagement and travel while braking. A range
sample must be corrected for error and any travel since capture. The full
delay must include the action lease, sensing and communication, computation,
scheduling, clock allowance, and mechanical engagement. This standard bound
is a sufficient physical witness, not a learned physical law.

A backup region V has an independently justified local program that reaches
a safe terminal state. It is stronger than merely having a current U(h).
The theorem does not require every risk-qualified segment to remain in V;
that would exclude the behavior the probabilistic branch is meant to assess.
But an uncovered continuation is free of statistical risk only if the
current U(h) is contained in V and that backup still applies.

The monitor has three states: live, safe terminal, and assurance loss.
The latter two are absorbing. A valid physical backup on coverage exit
reaches safe terminal. An
uncovered state without the required reserve or authority is assurance loss,
even if the software issues a best-effort stop. Initial-state violations and
an uncertified final braking tail are also losses.

Induction over successful segments now proves the implication in Section 1.
On G, a correctly initialized execution that never loses assurance remains
collision-free until its safe terminal state. Therefore

  P(collision) <= P(first assurance loss) + eta_phys.

For this example, terminal safety means stopped at positive separation with
no further motion authorized. It does not protect against an obstacle that
continues moving into the stopped vehicle, and it is not an infinite-horizon
collision-avoidance statement.

4 Transferring the predictive certificate through the mission

Let Q be the learned joint law and P the true law on the certified interface.
On the inherited training-confidence event, the conditional joint-output
error at each covered source history and action is at most epsilon(h,a) in
total variation. Run the actual controller and the same first-loss monitor
under Q. Define

  alpha_Q = Q(first assurance loss),

  Delta_Q = E_Q[sum of epsilon(h,a) over live covered decisions].

The sum stops at first loss, safe termination, or coverage exit. It does not
charge later outputs of a backup whose safety is independently proved. With
a coarsening adapter, the exposure sum is evaluated on augmented source
histories, even if the monitor uses a smaller observation.

The resulting deployment bound is

  P(collision) <= min(1, alpha_Q + min(1, Delta_Q) + eta_phys).

The proof couples P and Q while their complete live prefixes agree. The
chance of the next disagreement is bounded by the local joint error. The
mass reaching an equal prefix is no greater than its Q marginal mass, giving
the Q-weighted exposure sum. Without a disagreement, the two monitors reach
the same loss or safe outcome. The physical implication then supplies the
collision bound.

This improves on charging the largest possible horizon when executions
terminate early or visit low-error contexts. A uniform epsilon still gives
the simpler Delta_Q <= H*epsilon. An independently justified stopped-trajectory
TV or KL bound can be tighter; using it requires the same termination
semantics and honest accounting of its confidence event. Taking the smaller
of two bounds does not erase the confidence cost of establishing both.

The inherited pointwise certificate is simultaneous over admissible feedback
policies. Consequently selecting a controller after seeing the learning data
does not require a fresh union bound over policies. This does not license
changing structural parameters at every decision. Unresolved environmental
alternatives remain persistent choices, and the controller must face the
appropriate fixed-model supremum or a declared mixture.

Training confidence and deployment risk are different quantities. Delta_Q is
predictive discrepancy along execution. Alpha_Q is modeled first-loss risk.
Eta_phys is physical-envelope failure. The training failure probability delta
says how often the statistical certificate may be wrong across learning
experiments. It is not automatically a per-maneuver collision probability.
A Monte Carlo estimate of alpha_Q would add its own estimation requirement;
the finite example instead computes the relevant probabilities exactly.

The theorem also supplies finite certificate obligations for synthesis.
A risk-to-go potential must dominate its expected successor value, with
values one at loss and zero at safe termination. An exposure potential must
cover the local epsilon plus expected future exposure. Such inequalities can
be checked over finite states. They may be proved on a richer offline state,
but the selected action must be identical whenever the deployed controller's
actual observation and memory are identical.

5 Exact support gives the stronger assurance branch

The inherited additive specialization has a separate exact-response
identification event. On that event the deterministic response m(h,a) is
correct throughout the permitted domain, and its observer is correctly
initialized and recursively updated. The implemented identification result
relies on its declared integer-valued, bounded-degree response family and
symmetric binary-noise universe. The model class promises residual support:
each relevant residual coordinate belongs to {-1,+1}.

If the physical witness succeeds for every supported residual outcome, no
probability-estimation error is needed. The controller is safe against any
distribution on that support. With safe coverage handling, the theorem gives

  P(collision) <= eta_phys.

This is not the invalid argument that no failures were observed, therefore
their probability is zero. It is a finite universal check over all eight
possible residual triples, conditional on exact response identification and
a promised support alphabet. Outcomes absent from a finite sample cannot
otherwise be declared impossible.

This branch explains the strongest demonstrated benefit of discovery. A
history-aware observer can establish a narrower valid physical envelope than
a guardian that only knows global bounds. Learning which history matters can
therefore permit more motion without introducing modeled first-loss risk.
Correlations between the residuals do not create this advantage: independent
residuals with the same support would give the same hard result.

There remains a real distinction between model-side zero risk and real-world
zero failure. The source class, physical adapter, timing bounds, and actuator
authority must be correct. A statistical response-identification confidence
statement does not by itself certify a physical reliability target such as
one failure in ten thousand maneuvers. That target also needs an appropriate
bound on eta_phys and a justified model applicability claim.

6 The implemented motion and timing contract

The test starts stationary facing a fixed protected boundary. A clearance
packet reports 1,280 mm. Subtracting a 10 mm measurement-error allowance and
a 10 mm protected reserve leaves 1,260 mm for motion. The packet must have
the correct authority, valid timestamp, age no greater than 100 ms, and a
separate justified certificate that the vehicle has remained at rest since
capture. Being stationary only at the present instant would not suffice.
Under rest since capture and a stationary boundary, packet age consumes no
travel distance here. Moving-start cases need a different correction.

The supplied readout calibration is

  sensing delay = clip(150 + 50*Y0, 0, 1000) ms,
  network delay = clip(150 + 50*Y5, 0, 1000) ms,
  braking deceleration = clip(2500 + 500*Y10, 500, 5000) mm/s^2.

For requested speed v in mm/s, the plant ramps from rest at 1,000 mm/s^2.
The nominal ramp lasts v milliseconds. The nominal stop-sampling checkpoint
is 100 ms after that endpoint. The sensor response takes its realized delay,
computation receives a nominal 10 ms allowance, and the remote stop packet
then incurs its network delay.

Independently, the local watchdog becomes eligible to request braking at
310 ms after the scheduled ramp endpoint. All additional overhead shares
ONE 10 ms budget: sample-request dispatch lateness, acknowledgement dispatch
lateness, watchdog scheduling lateness, and physical engagement delay must
sum to at most 10 ms. These are not four independent allowances. Thus onset
after ramp completion is bounded by

  min(100 + sensing delay + 10 + network delay, 310) + 10 ms.

A request is latched at or after its scheduled time, not emitted only if a
sampled clock happens to equal one exact timestamp. Missing packets never
renew the watchdog deadline. Wrong-authority or stale-sequence stop messages
do not change the maneuver, and a valid stop or timeout latches braking. The
source identifier is a trusted interface tag, not a cryptographic
authentication implementation. Scheduler and actuator deadlines remain
contract premises.

The simulator uses 10 ms physical ticks and integer micrometres. Its final
fractional braking tick conservatively overestimates travel relative to
constant deceleration stopping within that tick. The C program uses a
separately expressed integer closed form. Its field named collision records
a conservative stopping-envelope violation; that can overapproximate actual
collision. The displayed adverse 1,000 mm/s case has no rounding gap and does
collide in the exact synthetic plant.

One predictive step describes one complete maneuver, including its stopping
tail. The source outcome is selected once and retained; it is not resampled
every 10 ms or changed when braking becomes inconvenient. This is why the
single-maneuver statistical certificate uses H=1. It is not permission to
call an arbitrary many-tick stochastic physical process a one-step model.

7 What the numerical comparisons show

The speed grid is 0, 700, 800, 900, 1,000, 1,100, and 1,200 mm/s. With current
actions all -1 and all retained runs zero, 900 mm/s is safe for every
supported outcome at the 1,260 mm conservative clearance. Speed 1,000 is not.

With the same current actions, a retained run length of two on action port 10
and zero on the other ports, 1,100 mm/s is safe for all eight outcomes. Its
worst supported stopping distance is 1,259.5 mm. This leaves only 0.5 mm of
slack after the explicit 10 mm protected reserve; the example should not be
read as a recommendation for such a tight hardware design. Speed 1,200 fails
the support check.

Across all 600 task-relevant memory contexts with those current actions,
a history-blind guardian must allow weak braking and the watchdog deadline.
Its worst stopping envelopes are 1,216 mm at 800 and 1,503 mm at 900, so
it admits at most 800 mm/s on the grid. The learned contextual result therefore
permits 1,100 rather than 800, a 37.5 percent requested-speed increase under
the same fixed protocol. A supplied full model retaining the same history
would obtain the same answer. The contribution is discovering and executing
the relevant history interface, not outperforming identical information.

The favorable history is a supplied observed context. The example does not
prove a robot can safely execute a preparatory sequence to create it, nor
that it can repeatedly enjoy this speed advantage over a mission.

The separate nonzero-risk comparison uses the zero-run context. At 1,000 mm/s,
one adverse residual triple, (+1,+1,-1), causes collision in the synthetic
plant. Its learned joint mass is 10,277/131,072, approximately 0.0784073.
Adding the outward-rounded inherited one-step TV allowance 0.013247 gives
0.0916543, below the deliberately illustrative budget 0.1. The complete
reused probabilistic certificate has training failure allowance at most
0.0065 + 12/2^64, under its original assumptions.

The physical-envelope allowance would still have to be added. Approximately
nine percent modeled collision risk is not practical collision assurance.
This branch demonstrates how an honestly priced joint-risk decision compiles
into motion; it is not the principal safety result.

Multiplying the learned marginal frequencies predicts adverse probability
approximately 0.124706 and rejects 1,000. Dependence is beneficial in this
particular example; it is not a case where independence underestimates the
risk. A direct full three-readout joint table exactly matches the learned
joint-risk decision. The decomposition supplies task scope, history, and a
reusable common-source interface, not a special advantage over that table.

Even a randomized worst-outcome guardian on the same grid has expected
requested speed at most 930 at budget 0.1: one supported adverse row defeats
every speed above 900, so at most one tenth of its probability can be assigned
to those speeds, whose maximum is 1,200. This remains a restricted
requested-speed comparison, not global controller optimality or throughput.

8 Negative tests that matter to the conclusion

History compression can change the answer incorrectly. Two histories of
port 10, [-1,+1,+1,+1] and [+1,+1,+1,+1], share their latest three actions
but have retained runs three and four. At 537 mm conservative clearance and
700 mm/s, their modeled first-loss probabilities are approximately 0.249641
and 0.078407. The illustrative 0.1-risk controller must reject the first and
may admit the second. Replacing both by the more favorable memory state
creates false assurance. The test reconstructs the states causally from
issued actions, rather than supplying hidden regime labels.

Ten milliseconds also matters. In the zero-run context, omitting the budgeted
computation interval makes the adverse 1,000 mm/s outcome appear to stop at
1,310 mm instead of 1,320 mm. At 1,315 mm clearance, the untimed calculation
therefore accepts a trajectory the timed model rejects. Mechanical engagement
lag is likewise charged after the brake request, including on the watchdog
path; a software flag is not a physical brake.

Packet loss illustrates why fallback is not a universal shield. The local
watchdog still commands braking, but some weak-brake realizations of the
risk-qualified 1,000 mm/s maneuver collide under timeout. If packet loss is
outside the certified source family, its probability must be separately
bounded or the admission must be safe for that possibility. Calling the
late command a safe fallback cannot restore distance already consumed.
In contrast, the favorable-history 1,100 mm/s branch remains safe under a
lost acknowledgement: all calibrated braking outcomes stop by 1,259.5 mm
even on the watchdog path. This requires loss not to invalidate braking
authority; an uncalibrated weaker brake remains outside that guarantee.

Unknown history and stale, invalid, or insufficiently justified initial
clearance cause no launch. That is a legitimate safe rejection because the
vehicle is initially stationary under the contract. The same rejection
label cannot make a moving vehicle safe after commitment.

Finally, two physical worlds could emit identical readouts while one has an
unobserved nearer obstacle or degraded brake. No transcript predictor can
distinguish those worlds from the records alone. They differ in the physical
premises. The theorem exposes this obligation through G and eta_phys rather
than hiding it inside a predictive confidence number.

9 Implementation evidence and remaining engineering work

The C file contains a bounded supervisor and timed executor plus a standard
input/output test harness, not board firmware or device drivers. Serialized
memory snapshots are harness inputs, not trusted physical observations; the
controller has a separate causal initialization from observed actions. No
actual device input/output was validated.

The C admission path uses bounded integer arithmetic and at most 48 outcome
evaluations: eight outcomes at each of six nonzero speeds. Probability tests
use integer cross-multiplication and an outward rational TV allowance, not
uncontrolled floating-point rounding. Inputs include explicit timestamp and
distance ranges before arithmetic. Memory updates are causal, and future
telemetry is never an admission input.

The check suite compares all 600 relevant memory states, eight relevant
current-action combinations, and three clearances: 14,400 decisions per
budget. It repeats at risk budgets 0.1 and 0.0001, for 28,800 C/Python decision
comparisons, and checks recursive updates. Executor tests cover nonzero
speeds, sensing/network combinations, stale sequence, wrong authority,
timeout, and latched braking. Host builds use warnings as errors and the
undefined-behavior sanitizer.

Independent rational checks exercise the transfer inequality on 104,976
stopped depth-two configurations, alongside 15,625 kernel-pair checks from
the theorem author. They include safe and unsafe exits. These are useful
consistency tests, not substitutes for the general coupling proof. Independent
context enumeration checks the hard-support comparison and conservative
physical integration. A timing checker covers 24,024 integer allocations
of the shared overhead, including 5,040 with positive lateness at every
stage. The underlying inequality also holds for nonnegative real delays;
the enumeration is a supplement, not a restriction to integer hardware.

This establishes reproducible reference-code behavior, not a target
processor's worst-case execution time or actuator performance. Before a
physical deployment claim, the open obligations are concrete: validate the
range and footprint interpretation; establish rest since capture; measure
braking and tracking envelopes across the intended operating regime; verify
clock, scheduler, communication, and brake-engagement bounds; and justify
source-law invariance under actual speed commands. A hardware test plan would
also need fault injection and a safe experimental setup. None was run here.

For repeated operation, the next mathematical task is an end-to-end mission
with safely reachable initialization, successive admissible states, complete
coverage accounting, and an actual progress objective. The general theorem
provides a framework for that task; the one-maneuver example does not already
solve it. No new Lean proof, external human review, hardware-in-the-loop test,
or field validation is claimed.

10 Relationship to existing work and final assessment

Stopping-distance calculations with response time and braking bounds are
established, including the longitudinal RSS formulation. Runtime-assurance
and Simplex architectures already emphasize timely recovery, while
sampled-data control-barrier-function work explicitly handles intersample
behavior, uncertainty, and input delay. Predictive safety filters already
combine learned uncertain models with certified continuations. These are
substantive baselines, not missing categories. Sources include

  [On a Formal Model of Safe and Scalable Self-driving Cars]
  (https://arxiv.org/html/1708.06374v6)

  [Control Barrier Functions for Sampled-Data Systems with Input Delays]
  (https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06418v1)

  [A predictive safety filter for learning-based control of constrained
  nonlinear dynamical systems](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.05506v3)

The earlier internal work also already combined a physical hazard envelope,
stopping distance, and predictive-risk transfer. Those ingredients are not
new results of this milestone. The defensible additional deliverable is the
explicit chain from discovered scopes and memory, through a lawful physical
adapter and timed implementation, to a first-loss certificate with correct
coverage and uncertainty accounting. No literature-wide novelty clearance
has been established.

The strongest conclusion is therefore positive and bounded. The released
predictor now drives a checkable stopping controller. Its learned history
can support a strictly less conservative all-outcomes admission than a
history-blind guardian, and its joint probabilities can support a separately
priced soft-risk admission. The proof specifies exactly why either conclusion
survives prediction error and exactly where physical evidence is still needed.
The broader research contract, including discovery beyond the promised model
class and assurance on a validated physical system, remains open.
