## Physical and resource blockers

1. **Access and transfers are trusted caller protocol, not an enforced plant state.** The constructed plant has no location or access state. `coordinate` trusts its phase argument; `aggregate` has no location restriction; `step` takes caller-supplied cap. Six `safe_transfer_*` ticks update counters while stationary and do not implement transport geometry, distance or locomotion cost. They can represent a supplied abstract certified action only if that action's endpoint/access/timing contract is stated; they do not independently verify travel to remote sensors.
2. **Only a partial resource model exists.** Every command tick is charged and active-port ticks are correctly counted, but sensor bits are not sensor time/energy, and no locomotion, sensing, communication or computation energy cost or energy budget is checked. Zero-time sensing may be an explicit abstract premise, not a claim that every physical probe cost is included. The first mission gets an allowance equal to the actually measured commissioning time; acquisition affects totals but cannot cause a first-mission timeout under that definition. This is not a pre-fixed hard total-budget acquisition challenge.
3. **Whole passage is substantially better than a guard lock.** Fast motion genuinely advances 10 m with exact continuous piecewise constant acceleration integration and stopping tail. The independent counterfactual check confirms it for both residual worlds. This remains a highly supplied straight-line physical abstraction: exact dynamics and high-response/braking map are premises, not discovered or calibrated properties.
4. **Safe terminal and consistency failures.** The simulation ends with zero velocity after each movement, but exceptions/assertions are not certified fallback paths. Missing acquisition outcomes cannot be silently regarded as safely successful.

## Baseline and task-significance blockers

- Rectangular comparison is a legitimate same-data marginal-relaxation ablation, not an optimal conservative controller. It does not explore extra paid diagnostics, mixed slow/fast routes, or alternative acquisition policies. `rectangular_full_mission_certified=false` only says not all jobs have a certified fast option under that restricted policy, not that no deadline-feasible conservative policy exists.
- Coarse baseline has an exact analytic 82 ticks per passage, but no full executed preparation/access/sensing policy or energy comparison. It CAN meet the smallest first mission with the equal total allowance; this is correctly reported and must not be suppressed.
- Supplied-scope reference replays exactly the learner's observations and reaches equal capability. It does not optimize oracle acquisition and establishes no oracle exploration-cost advantage.
- Probe-all comparison counts coordinate bits only. Since coordinate readouts cost no time in this implementation, reduced bits do not automatically mean fewer elapsed acquisition ticks or improved mission completion.
- Small full-world reference enumerates the already reduced two-function residual product, not the entire initial global class or optimal knowledge-state planner. That is a useful correctness reference, not a full baseline comparison.
- A fair compiled versus uncompiled same-knowledge planner comparison is absent. No measured decomposition-specific planning speedup is established.
- Held-out missions reverse/permutate the same independently solvable 10 m jobs on already identified target outputs. Counter histories persist and order can change preparation time, which the checker verifies. There is nevertheless no explicit map, destination feasibility, coupled delivery state or nontrivial routing tradeoff. **The research gate expressly rejects a chain of independently solved identical stops. This family remains an integration/unit-test family, not the motivating result.**
- The broader partial-acquisition diagnostic meaningfully shows relation benefit while scope/threshold/table ambiguity persists, but is neither a resource-constrained sequential mission nor evidence that safely selecting useful tests solves a nontrivial task.

## Theorem 5S alignment review

The newly added §6A / Theorem 5S is logically sound. It requires safe bounded acquisition for every retained initial mechanism, exact evidence retention, a safe rejection path, and acceptance only of a continuation certificate from the actual reached state with residual budgets. On the physical-contract event G, acceptance implies successful safe completion and rejection implies safe abstention. Consequently `P(success) >= P(acceptance) - eta_phys` follows from `acceptance ∩ G ⊆ success` and `P(G^c) <= eta_phys`; no independence assumption is needed. No positive acceptance probability follows, and neither the proof nor the experiments may invent one without a mechanism distribution.

The added explicit abstentions align the examples with this selective aim. They do not alone implement every premise of a fully total runtime wrapper: generic inference failure, search timeout and exceptions do not yet have an independently checked physical exception-handling path. In the scripts all abstract preparation/sensing is stopped in supplied protected bays, but this is a contract premise, not a physical fail-safe implementation test. Reported synthesis wall-clock work is not charged to simulated mission ticks or energy. Thus these are certificates under supplied timing/physical contracts, not a certified online real-time controller including computation runtime. The theorem explicitly calls out this distinction, and the final report must retain it.
