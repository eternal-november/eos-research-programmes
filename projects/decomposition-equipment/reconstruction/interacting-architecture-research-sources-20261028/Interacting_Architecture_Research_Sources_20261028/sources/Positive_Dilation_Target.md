# Positive dilation target: a verified stress test, not an architecture-discovery theorem

## Result first

There is an explicit stationary two-binary-channel family with:

- observable Hankel rank exactly 3, **nonnegative rank of the entire infinite temporal Hankel exactly 3**, and invertible unconditional observable evolution;
- strictly positive probabilities for every finite observable word;
- each channel separately realizable by a time-homogeneous positive 3-state edge-emitting HMM, hence by a 6-state state-emitting HMM;
- each one-time joint table realizable by a two-mode conditional-product mixture;
- nevertheless, for parameter theta = 2*pi/q, every common positive finite-state realization of the joint trajectories needs at least q states;
- a constructive q-state edge-emitting joint realization, hence a conditional-product state-emitting realization with at most 4q states;
- no finite positive joint realization if theta/(2*pi) is irrational.

All four observable symbol maps are invertible (for theta not a multiple of 2*pi), so no observed word is an exact reset. This is a concrete persistent-local-versus-global calibration. **It does not solve discovery of an unknown physical decomposition, and the underlying positive-realization obstruction is classical.** A simple XOR-mask baseline already produces a general marginal/joint gap, so the gap itself is not a novelty claim.

Here “positive realization” means nonnegative stochastic kernels. The state-emitting lift uses deterministic local outputs and can have zero transition entries. It is not a theorem for entrywise strictly positive state-emitting transitions.

## Exact construction

Use column-state convention

    p(y_1 ... y_n) = beta T_{y_n} ... T_{y_1} alpha.

Set

    alpha = (1, 1/8, 0)^T,
    beta  = (64/65, 8/65, 0),
    d = 1/10, c = 1/20,
    P = d I + (1-d) alpha beta.

Then beta alpha = 1, P alpha = alpha, beta P = beta, and P has eigenvalues 1,d,d. Explicitly

    P = [[641/650, 36/325, 0],
         [36/325, 37/325, 0],
         [0,       0,    1/10]].

Define planar reflections

    F1 = [[1,0],[0,-1]],
    F2 = [[cos(theta),sin(theta)],[sin(theta),-cos(theta)]],
    R_i = diag(1,F_i).

Thus F2 F1 is rotation by theta. Put

    T_00 = c R1,
    T_11 = c R2,
    T_01 = T_10 = Q/2,
    Q = P - c R1 - c R2.

The sum is P, giving both left and right consistency and stationarity.

### Positivity and finite rational realization

Let L = {(x0,z): x0 >= ||z||_2}. The reflections preserve L. For x=(1,z) in L, write t=beta x. Then t >= 56/65, and

    (Qx)_0 = (9/10)t,
    ||(Qx)_spatial|| <= 1/5 + (9/80)t.

The ratio is at most 193/504 < 1/2. Consequently Q sends every nonzero Lorentz-cone input into its strict interior. Alpha is interior and beta is strictly positive on the cone, proving strictly positive observable word probabilities for every theta.

For theta=2*pi/q, q>=3, let C_q be the cone generated by

    r_j = (1, cos(2*pi*j/q), sin(2*pi*j/q)),  j=0,...,q-1.

Both reflections permute its rays. Its cross-section contains the disk of radius cos(pi/q)>=1/2, so the displayed Q bound proves Q C_q subset C_q. Alpha belongs to its interior and beta is positive on its nonzero rays.

This gives an explicit construction, not an unknown-factorization oracle:

1. Normalize each ray g_j=r_j/(beta r_j), and form G with these columns.
2. For each y,j, decompose T_y g_j nonnegatively into the g_k, obtaining columns of H_y with G H_y = T_y G.
3. For Q outputs, use angular bracketing between adjacent polygon vertices: express the normalized spatial point in the triangle formed by the origin and the bracketing vertices, then replace the origin by the uniform mean of all vertices. The strict inner-disk bound supplies a positive origin coefficient.
4. H=sum_y H_y is column stochastic since beta P=beta. Moreover H_01 and H_10 can be entrywise positive under this decomposition, so H is primitive.
5. Compute its stationary distribution pi. Since P G pi=G pi and beta G pi=1, uniqueness of P's normalized fixed vector gives G pi=alpha. This is a stationary q-state edge-emitting realization.
6. Convert to a state-emitting driver on states (j,y), at most 4q total, with transition

       M[(j,z),(i,y)] = H_z[j,i].

   The emitted pair is z deterministically, so its two local emission laws are a conditional product. The transition is reused at every time. A stationary initial law is nu(j,z)=(H_z pi)_j.

The q lower bound below is exact for edge-emitting models. Only q <= K_product <= 4q is established for the required conditional-product state-emitting type.

### Bounded marginal realizations

Both channels have the same two marginal symbol maps

    U_0 = (P+c(R1-R2))/2,
    U_1 = (P-c(R1-R2))/2.

Use the fixed regular-triangle cone with circumradius 2/5 and inradius 1/5, independent of q. For x=(1,z) in its cross-section, ||z||<=2/5 and t=beta x>=304/325. Before dividing by two, either marginal output has time coordinate 1/10+(9/10)t and spatial norm at most 2/25+(9/80)t. Their ratio is at most 602/3061 < 1/5. Hence both U_0 and U_1 preserve this SAME triangle, strictly. The polygon construction gives a stationary 3-state edge-emitting marginal model; output augmentation gives 6 state-emitting states. No q-dependent resources are needed for either marginal. This is also the very triangle used below to factor the entire temporal Hankel.

A single-time two-by-two probability table always admits a two-mode product-mixture realization, for example by conditioning on its first binary coordinate. That local statement is not a temporal reconstruction.

### Stronger local-cut certificate: the whole temporal Hankel has nonnegative rank 3

This is stronger than bounded ordinary rank or bounded one-step spatial flattenings. Define

    L_R = {(x0,z): x0 >= 0, ||z|| <= R x0},  R=1/5.

Alpha belongs to L_R and reflections preserve L_R. For x=(1,z) in its cross-section, t=beta x >= 24/25. The sharper remainder bound is

    (Qx)_0 = (9/10)t,
    ||(Qx)_spatial|| <= (1/5)R + (9/80)t.

Thus its spatial/time ratio is at most 37/216 < 1/5. Every reachable vector T_w alpha therefore belongs to L_R.

Take the FIXED triangle with vertices

    g_j = (1, (2/5)cos(2*pi*j/3), (2/5)sin(2*pi*j/3)), j=0,1,2.

Its inradius is 1/5, so its cone contains L_R. It is also contained in the large Lorentz cone L, on which every future evaluation beta T_v is nonnegative. For each word w, choose nonnegative barycentric coefficients h_w with T_w alpha=G h_w. Define F[v,j]=beta T_v g_j. Then

    H[v,w] = beta T_v T_w alpha = sum_j F[v,j] h_w[j]

is a nonnegative factorization of the ENTIRE infinite temporal Hankel through three generators. Ordinary rank is 3, so nonnegative rank is exactly 3 as well. Every finite past/future cut inherits this factorization.

The failure is specifically joint temporal compatibility: this enclosing triangle is invariant for both marginal symbol families, but not under both joint reflections for q>3. The same three factor states support all static temporal cuts and either channel dynamics separately, yet cannot be updated by joint nonnegative transition-symbol maps. Thus even simultaneous low-resource nonnegative factorization of all past/future cuts does not provide a persistent finite-state realization of that size.

### Exact observable rank, unbounded global cost, and absence of resets

Let z denote the two-symbol word (00)(11). Then

    p(z^n) = c^(2n) (64+cos(n theta))/65.

For theta neither 0 nor pi modulo 2*pi, this scalar sequence has three distinct nonzero characteristic roots

    c^2, c^2 exp(i theta), c^2 exp(-i theta).

Its Hankel rank is 3. It is a sub-Hankel of the full process, whose displayed representation has dimension 3, so the full observable rank is exactly 3.

Suppose a K-state nonnegative realization exists. Its nonnegative word matrix L for z gives the same sequence. Trim states not accessible from the initial vector under powers of L and not coaccessible to the terminal functional. The trimmed matrix has Perron radius c^2: a larger accessible/coaccessible Perron block would force larger exponential growth, with no cancellation possible in a nonnegative realization. The scalar generating function has poles corresponding to c^2 exp(plus/minus i theta), so these are eigenvalues of the trimmed matrix. Their irreducible block therefore has Perron radius c^2.

For theta=2*pi/q, the Perron-Frobenius period of that block must be divisible by q; its number of states, and hence K, is at least q. For irrational theta/(2*pi), no finite irreducible nonnegative matrix has the required peripheral phase, so no finite positive realization exists.

A direct symbolic calculation gives

    det(Q) = 36(1-cos(theta))/8125.

R1,R2 are invertible and det(T_01)=det(T_10)=det(Q)/8. Thus all symbol matrices, and every finite product of them, are invertible away from theta=0 modulo 2*pi. No finite observation resets the rank-3 predictor to a single ray.

## Finite-evidence qualification

Exact peripheral angles are not identifiable as rational or irrational from noisy finite data. For a fixed candidate budget K, however, a rank-3 target and a K-dimensional candidate that agree on every word of length at most K+2 agree on all words: apply the standard reachable-span argument to their direct-sum difference representation of dimension K+3.

The stationary K-state conditional-product HMM parameter set is compact (including zero probabilities), and its finite-word probability map is continuous. For q>K, the target prefix vector through length K+2 therefore has a strictly positive distance from this compact candidate set. This proves an in-principle finite-prefix rejection margin for each fixed K, but provides neither an effective numerical lower bound on the margin nor an efficient fitting or certification method. It is not a finite-data test for irrationality.

## What this does and does not advance

The entire infinite temporal Hankel has nonnegative rank 3, and the binary readout alphabets keep every one-step observable block-flattening bound bounded, while the necessary joint positive-state budget tends to infinity. Separate marginal positivity and positive single-time assembly do not furnish the same positive realization across histories. The explicit cone construction also provides a checkable sufficient certificate for this family.

But this remains standard global positive realization with two readouts, not a theorem inferring physical components or admissible interfaces. Indeed, if Z is any hard binary process, X is an independent fair iid mask, and Y=X XOR Z, then X and Y separately are fair iid while the joint trajectory reveals Z. This baseline already shows that easy marginals can hide arbitrary joint temporal complexity.

A genuine remaining target must fix a non-vacuous interface/resource class and infer it from observable dependencies: reconstruct common positive local mechanisms and shared latent/port variables, classify observationally equivalent assemblies, and distinguish infeasible assembly from an overrestrictive chosen frame. A generic invariant-polyhedral-cone existence statement, generic polynomial feasibility, flat moment extension with unobserved moments supplied by an oracle, or a disguised one-step tensor flattening does not meet that target.

In particular, temporal words do not directly reveal higher powers of same-time emission probabilities. A latent microstate may be resampled conditionally on a persistent macrostate; inequivalent product-mixture refinements can then agree on all temporal words while disagreeing on unobserved same-time higher moments. Flat-extension reconstruction needs an additional identifiable observation/intervention scheme or structural assumption, not merely more trajectory length.
