Audit checkpoint on interacting architecture discovery
28 October 2026

Plain abstract

The main discovery theorem remains OPEN. We want to infer interacting parts and their
communication rules from recordings, without a special operation that reveals which
part anything belongs to. The audit has ruled out two tempting shortcuts and verified a
useful counterexample. Simple descriptions of separate channels, or even of every
past-and-future probability, need not fit together into one small mechanism that works
consistently through time. A short supplied proof can still make checking cheap even
when a particular tensor format becomes huge. This is not a discovery theorem.

What was ruled out

The noisy-feedback result is mathematically valid within its restricted model: linear
binary dynamics, an invertibly mixed view of the whole state, and independent noise
sources. It can recover hidden coordinates without resets. But independent noise makes
valid local probability rules and their joint assembly automatic. It therefore misses
the central validation target: discovering unknown positive mechanisms whose
compatibility is genuinely in question. Here "positive" means probabilities are
nonnegative, not necessarily strictly greater than zero.

The proposed defect bound adds no new numerical obstruction. It is exactly an ordinary
matrix-flattening rank bound, expressed differently. It remains a useful necessary test
under the specified shared-state emission assumptions, not a sufficient test for a
positive realization or architecture discovery.

What was verified

A stationary reflection family separates a static positive description from a reusable
positive dynamical mechanism. Its entire infinite table of past-and-future
probabilities has nonnegative rank exactly 3. Nevertheless, when its parameter has
rotation order q, for any integer q at least 3, the smallest joint model with outputs
attached to transitions has exactly q hidden states. Each channel separately needs at
most 3 such states. These exact process results are not finite-data guarantees.

Counting states differently matters: if both outputs must be conditionally independent
given the same hidden state, the verified joint count is only between q and 4q, not
exactly q. Every finite observed word has positive probability, and no observed word is
an exact reset.

This is a rigorous stress test, not recovery of a physical decomposition. Classical
positive-realization results, together with a simple masking construction, already
explain the broad small-marginal versus large-joint gap. The gap alone cannot support a
novelty claim.

What checking supplied witnesses buys

Checking a proposal with a supplied witness differs from finding a decomposition or
deciding whether one exists. One clocked interacting example needs exponentially large
tensor-train and CP representations of its all-state quotient, but has a short
circuit-and-cancellation proof. This does
not rule out all tensor-network formats or cheap checking in every proof format.

A separate restricted theorem derives compact signed witnesses under strong local-control
calibration. Actual resets are unnecessary, but reset maps lie in the controls' linear
span. An explicit family has predictive dimension 2n for 2^n hidden configurations, with
every bit relevant and shared communication counted. Efficient exact checking still
requires suitable contraction resources and encoded coefficient sizes. The signed
witness is not a positive state decomposition; a sparse substitute initial law preserves
observations, not necessarily hidden stationarity. These are supporting results, not
discovery from uncalibrated recordings.

What remains useful and unfinished

The remaining ambition is to infer unknown components and interfaces from readouts,
require the same valid mechanisms to work across time, and describe alternative
architectures that observations cannot distinguish.

The useful outcomes are reusable rejection tests, an exact benchmark audit, and
restricted certificate work. None is completed architecture discovery.

Independent AI proof reviews and scoped executable checks support these claims; no
Lean or external human verification is included.
