1. Free and native structural comparisons
-----------------------------------------

This is an application of established free cylinder-factorisation machinery. The compatible normalizer spans are transported as well; the conclusion is not only an isomorphism of normalized endpoints.

The native theorem uses adequate CFS hypotheses, not an arbitrary CFS. Required mixed pullbacks exist, and every commuting square with the stated horizontal M0 and vertical E0 classes is a pullback. These hypotheses make class-preserving functors preserve the relevant mixed pullbacks. Factoring the translated preparation cocone and lifting against the target canonical test cone constructs an M0-left/E0-right comparison span. Compatible normalizer rigidity supplies the compositor and its unit/associativity coherence.

This rigidity is restricted to the same actual cylinder, fixed endpoints, and compatible realization data. It is not a claim that arbitrary bare span maps are unique. 2. Finite support-plus-deficit execution
---------------------------------------
For a finite substochastic kernel f, let R_f be its positive support and D_f the inputs with positive missing mass. Feasible pairs are all realized by normalized or half-mass uniform columns on their nonempty supports, with zero columns for empty supports. This proves surjectivity on each hom-set, not functoriality of that choice.

The composition identity

  delta_(g f)(x) = delta_f(x) + sum_y f(y|x) delta_g(y)

has only nonnegative terms. It therefore proves the exact support/deficit composition law. Identity and direct-sum laws are immediate from their actual matrices.

The first-exit trace sums nonnegative finite-exit path masses. Its support is finite-path reachability. Its deficit is characterized by reaching a local deficit or a closed internal communicating class. In the converse direction, finiteness yields a common positive lower bound on exiting within a bounded number of steps, so surviving forever has probability zero. This finite progress argument is essential. Consequently equality of (R,D) is a congruence for the trace; the trace axioms descend from finite substochastic execution rather than being additional guessed axioms.

The contextual/coarsest-refinement statement has a precise observation class: support and per-input normalization, with deterministic input probes available. Such probes distinguish unequal deficit sets. It is not a minimality theorem for arbitrary encodings, state numbers, or quantitative observers.

3. Lifting, continuity, and infinitary boundaries
------------------------------------------------
The no-section proof is algebraic. Deterministic point probes force the columns of a putative lift. The qualitative relation g R = R would then require r^2 + 1 - r = r, or (1-r)^2 = 0, contrary to 0 < r < 1. No tensor or feedback preservation assumption is needed.

The deficit bit is not omega-continuous: every finite partial geometric exit sum can be deficient while its limit is normalized. Feedback preservation for the deficit bit therefore follows from the finite reachability/progress theorem, not preservation of infinite sums by the quotient.

For the countable random walk, the finite-barrier recurrence gives the displayed hitting probability. Increasing hitting events have union equal to eventual finite-time exit. Their limit yields exit mass 1/2 for downward probability 1/3 and mass 1 for downward probability 2/3, despite identical unhidden support and no local deficit. Thus the finite quotient is not a trace congruence on the countable-state extension.

5. Exact finite verification and its limits
------------------------------------------
The included verifier uses Python fractions.Fraction, with outputs as rows and inputs as columns. A rational linear solve is applied only to internal states that can reach an output. States with no output path contribute zero first-exit mass; the full internal matrix is not assumed invertible. An independent graph procedure predicts support and deficit.

All 66,729 enumerated feedback kernels pass. There are 2,993 qualitative input groups with genuinely distinct numerical trace results but identical qualitative trace results. The denominator-2 four-state family has no repeated qualitative input groups; its role is graph/linear-solve coverage, not varying-weight evidence. The denominator-3 families supply the varying-weight checks.

Additional checks pass for 10,000 ordered composition pairs, 16 direct sums, and 8,000 simultaneous-versus-successive eliminations of two internal states. Boundary regressions include 435 rational section-obstruction samples, 30 geometric partial sums, and 57 finite-barrier systems with 570 recurrence entries. Formal square-functor apex cardinalities are checked as a finite set-model sanity check.

Finite enumeration does not prove the all-finite-state theorem, CFS axioms or categorical coherence, the no-section statement for every real r, or the countable-state limit. Those conclusions rely on the mathematical source arguments. The executable checks concrete formulas and boundary examples without a counterexample.
