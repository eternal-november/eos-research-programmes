2 Exact class and primitive criterion

For each finite vertex bound N, take an induced-restriction-closed family F of
original ordered simple graphs, including the empty graph, and a finite nonempty
interface category K. Use the original C1/C5 operations with scalar monoid M=1.
An operation retains its whole graph, its entire composable K-arrow path, each
standardized input graph, and every ordered input occurrence. Empty blocks are
allowed. The theorem ranges over the parameterized family: arity and tree depth
are unbounded, and N is not a universal fixed cutoff. Nontrivial scalar monoids
are not silently treated as positive cases.

For each graph G on [r], choose a subgroup H_G of the vertex permutation group.
It moves vertex placements, never input slots. If e_U increasingly enumerates a
subset U, define rho(g,U)=e_(gU)^(-1) g e_U. The two primitive tests are:

R  For g in H_G and U subset [r], the standardized ordered graphs G|U and G|gU
   are literally equal, and rho(g,U) belongs to H_(G|U).

E  Every h in H_(G|U), extended on U through e_U and made identity outside U,
   belongs to H_G.

R/E are necessary and sufficient for the full fibrewise orbit doctrine to inherit
original substitution and representative-independent canonical interval cuts
with bijective cut/flatten maps. Necessity is for these actual orbit actions,
original substitution on representatives, and induced-union cuts; it does not
classify arbitrary congruences or unrelated possible cut doctrines.

E makes replacing an inner representative harmless. R transports an outer
representative and identifies its standardized inner restrictions. The inverse
cut of columns i+1 through j uses their actual vertex union U and intermediate
component (G|U, composite of the selected arrows). This constructs an inverse to
the full disjoint-union-over-components F1 map, not to a weakened map after an
intermediate component has been chosen.

Faithful singleton-occurrence probes prove necessity. They detect rho(g,U)
exactly, and force a substituted local permutation to extend while fixing every
outside vertex. There is no unobserved permutation kernel. R and E can fail
separately on complete graphs through size three: H_3=S_3,H_2=1 satisfies E but
not R; H_3=1,H_2=S_2 satisfies R but not E, with lower groups trivial.

All interval boundaries are included. Empty intervals insert only the empty
identity-arrow component. Unary cuts and whole intervals use the actual unary
identity. Nullary units are designated empty/id operations; an empty graph with
a nonidentity interface arrow is not a nullary unit. Repeated restriction and
uniqueness prove coherent iteration for all trees and triangulations.

3 Four symmetry regimes and the sharp erasure boundary

The edge and nonedge two-vertex swap flags determine the groups. Where both
two-vertex types occur, the exhaustive classification is:

Flags 00  Any induced-closed ordered graph family; every H_G is trivial.
Flags 10  Contiguous disjoint unions of complete cliques; H_G is the product
          of full symmetric groups on those cliques.
Flags 01  Complements of those graphs, equivalently complete multipartite graphs
          with contiguous independent parts; H_G is the corresponding product.
Flags 11  Only complete or edgeless graphs; H_G is the full symmetric group.

An induced-closed subfamily of an allowed class is permitted; it need not contain
every graph in that class. Missing two-vertex types are interpreted vacuously.
Degenerate descriptions do not represent distinct actual group assignments.

The proof does not extrapolate from small graphs. Every permutation is generated
by transpositions along its inversion pairs. R detects an active two-vertex type
at each inversion, and E supplies its ambient transposition. Thus H_G is exactly
the group generated by all pairs of active type. Graph preservation and the
ordering of induced restrictions force the clique or complementary contiguous
block constraints. This also explains why higher-size symmetry choices are not
free once two-vertex behavior is fixed.

Full parallel placement erasure identifies all operations with the same retained
whole and full input profile. It always respects substitution, but preserves the
F1 bijections if and only if every allowed graph is complete or edgeless. A
nonhomogeneous graph contains both an edge pair and a nonedge pair. Put either
pair in the first two singleton occurrences of the same erased operation.
The two cuts pass through different retained intermediate graph components;
they are distinct elements of the F1 coproduct with the same flattened image.
This is a direct noninjectivity witness. Homogeneous graphs instead force the
intermediate graph by its size and force its grade by the selected arrow path.

4 Nonhomogeneous positive repair and its information limit

For a contiguous cluster graph with clique intervals C_b, retain the occupancy
matrix a_(b,k)=|C_b intersect U_k| of an ordered partition. Row sums give the
original clique sizes; columns retain the occurrences. Two actual partitions
have the same matrix exactly when within-clique vertex permutations relate them.
No new vertex colors or changed whole/profile equalities are introduced.

Substitution replaces each column by its inner columns, matching surviving rows
and reinserting zeros in the rows absent from that input. A cut sums its selected
columns into one outer column and deletes zero rows from the inner matrix. Its
inverse reinserts those rows in positions fixed by the outer column. Distinct
clique rows never merge merely because an intervening clique disappears.

K_2 disjoint-union K_1 is a nonhomogeneous positive graph. Full erasure fails
there, while this quotient retains which clique supplies each occurrence.
The two placements of the singleton split in K_2 become equal, but whole K_2
and the two-isolated-vertex whole remain different with the same singleton input
profile. Thus the positive class is not determined by ordinary single-valued
composition. K_3 disjoint-union K_2 disjoint-union K_1 supplies a six-vertex
fixture with repeated occurrences and composable mixed-interface arrows.

Intrinsic target cut uniqueness is different from unique original lifts. Fix an
actual coarse labelled partition and a compatible fine matrix over an active
map. Within each coarse clique/block intersection of size s, splitting into
required fine sizes a_k gives s! / product_k a_k! lifts. Multiply these factors
over all clique/block intersections. This requires full typed compatibility:
interfaces and all composites agree, including identity for an empty run.
Incompatible data have zero lifts, even if numerical matrix sums match.

The K_2 split has two original lifts. Consequently the quotient map is generally
not CULF, although its target has unique intrinsic cuts. On the complete all-cuts
cluster family it is CULF exactly when all cliques are singletons, when it is an
isomorphism. The theorem does not confuse this with fixed-endpoint witness
recovery or the older scalar fixed-endpoint lifting classification.

5 All-degree recognition and actual morphisms

In degree n, the positive matrix model records a whole graph, an n-column matrix,
and a composable n-arrow K path. Degree zero is precisely an interface object.
Inner faces add adjacent columns and compose their arrows; outer faces delete an
end column and restrict the whole, deleting zero rows. Degeneracies insert a zero
column with an identity arrow. The formulas satisfy all simplicial identities.
The cut inverse gives each triangulation bijection, and its iteration proves
unital discrete 2-Segal structure in every degree. General R/E orbit records use
the same induced-restriction and grouping formulas without matrix notation.

Conversely, operations are simplices with prescribed principal edges and long
edge. Inverse triangulation gluing gives original quotient substitution. Both
constructions read the same full partition orbit and grade path, giving explicit
inverse/comparison laws. The empty-graph subsystem recovers K; retained
identity-grade graph edges recover F; equality of fully labelled singleton tuples
recovers each actual H_G.

The comparison is on independently specified marked categories, not all finite
discrete 2-Segal objects or all simplicial maps. Source maps preserve whole
graphs, designated nullary units and the images of actual original binary
generators, with a graph-independent grade action. Target maps are simplicial
maps preserving whole graphs, that grade action, and the corresponding marked
binary triangles; higher values are not prescribed separately. Units and empty-
graph triangles force the grade action to be a functor K to K'. Binary generation
and triangulation uniqueness force all higher values. Singleton probes force
H_G subset H'_G. Conversely these inclusions and an interface functor construct
both maps. The resulting hom-set bijections respect identities and composition.

6 Original local presentation and independent B3 arrows

The presentation keeps the original C,U,D signature and seven equations. Add
parallel binary equations L which replace a partition label by its canonical
representative in the same orbit. Original input graphs, grades and ordered
variables are unchanged. For a clique this means taking the first required
vertices in each clique, not introducing a group-action arrow or stabilizer loop.

Appending the number of noncanonical labels to the original shape/decoration
termination measure proves termination. Each orbit has one canonical comb, so
normal forms prove object-level completeness and confluence. The arrow proof is
separate. At fixed finite parameters, enumerate all typed nonvariable critical
overlaps, normalize both branches, and record their actual joining words.
Together with disjoint and variable-residual squares these form O_L. The
well-founded coherence argument then gives canonical equation paths.

MOut checks how equations survive outer grafting; MIn checks insertion of legal
forests, including nullary replacements. These relations are independently
specified, not defined as all arrows with equal semantic images. The refinement
comparison and its Hom inverse use actual forests entry by entry. They preserve
every original occurrence index, full child profile and length, including zero,
rather than retaining only the final composite operation.

On morphisms, a source canonicalization need not be a literal target L step.
It is sent to an explicit target normalization path. Ordinary coherence and the
derived mixed laws make this an actual functor, with a commuting square on full
refinement tuples. Sections have a natural normalization comparison and cocycle
law, not a falsely strict equality of chosen normal forms. A sound alternative
relation family yields the comparison equivalence exactly when it entails the
O_L/MOut/MIn basis; no general entailment decision procedure is claimed.

### F1 Cut criterion and its distinction from substitution

**Class and claim:** M. Specify the additional structure that allows an evaluated operation to be cut coherently along intervals, and prove that ordinary substitution laws alone do not supply it.

**Inputs:** A typed witness calculus $M$ and a finite or locally finite set-valued setting. The starting candidate is bijectivity of a factorization map for each proper contiguous interval:

$$\coprod_K M(p/K;W)\times M(p_{[i,j]};K)\longrightarrow M(p;W).$$

Here $p/K$ replaces the selected subpath by the single component $K$, and the map is the corresponding substitution. All intermediate boundaries and the exact interval range must be specified.

**Artifact:** An exact criterion with normalized unary and nullary/unit conditions where needed; a theorem relating unique interval factorizations to coherent cuts; and a counterexample showing why generic free or presented calculi do not automatically satisfy the criterion.

**Discharge:** P. The candidate must be corrected if it is too strong, too weak, or missing unit hypotheses; corrections are recorded through the amendment process before acceptance. It is not enough to name an axiom 2-Segal and then restate it as the theorem.

**Tests:** Multiple factorizations, no factorization, two incompatible cuts, and missing unit data. Check all boundary cases separately.

**Dependencies:** A3, B2, B3, C1, C5. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove a structural recognition criterion tied to C5 and B3, including units and coherent iteration. Naming the desired property as an axiom, or observing that a nontrivial monoid label has two factorizations, does not pass the research package.

### F3 Recognition theorem and necessary hypotheses

**Class and claim:** B. Prove an interface-typed recognition or comparison theorem between a clearly specified class of coherently cut calculi and finite discrete 2-Segal objects, and identify its exact limits.

**Inputs:** F1–F2 and the existing correspondence with invertible nonsymmetric operads or multivalued categories.

**Artifact:** Explicit forward and reverse constructions on the stated class, morphism-level comparison, and inverse/comparison laws; or a sharp obstruction to the proposed equivalence together with a correct fully stated replacement. Provide a hypothesis-independence analysis using finite examples whenever possible.

**Discharge:** A substantive recognition theorem or the B-class alternative, plus a literature comparison explaining which part is established and which typed/refinement/quotient compatibility is new. The recognition result must include a criterion for compatibility with the C5 presentation quotient and B3 refinement comparison; merely inserting interface labels into a known equivalence does not pass. General infinity-categorical machinery is neither required nor assumed. An unsupported analogy to higher categories does not count.

**Tests:** Remove each essential hypothesis in turn, including factorization uniqueness, coherence, and units. Include at least one example in the positive class not determined by an ordinary single-valued composition law.

**Dependencies:** B3, C5, D3, F1, F2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The theorem must characterize presentation-quotient and refinement compatibility on a declared unbounded class, with a real positive model and sharp missing-hypothesis boundary. A rewording of the corrected invertible-operad equivalence or a single obstructed nerve does not pass.
