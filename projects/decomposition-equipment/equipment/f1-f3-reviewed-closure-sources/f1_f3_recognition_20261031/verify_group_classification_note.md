# Independent R/E group-classification verification

31 October 2026. Independent finite check and proof challenge of the proposed uncoloured ordered-simple-graph classification. This note does not accept or discharge F1/F3 beyond the stated group claim.

## Exact computational check

`verify_group_classification.py` enumerates every subgroup of Sym(n), n=0,...,4, by starting from the trivial subgroup and adjoining every permutation, computing closure, until no new subgroup appears. Counts are 1,1,2,6,30. Graphs are all bit masks on canonical pairs; induced ordered types are obtained by actual increasing standardization.

For each of the four choices of H on the edge and nonedge two-vertex types, the program recursively tests every candidate subgroup for every graph on three and four vertices. Its compatibility predicate uses only literal R/E: for every proper subset U, every g in H must preserve its standardized induced graph type and have standardized restriction in the actual child group; every child permutation extended by identity must belong to H. A graph with an impossible induced child is impossible. Empty and singleton groups are checked too. Testing the full subset adds only tautologies, so it is omitted. The predicted classification is used only after collecting all compatible candidates.

All 7,872 candidate checks passed exact equality against the prediction (including uniqueness, not merely existence). Among the 64 four-vertex graphs, the compatible counts are respectively 64,8,8,2 for neither flag, edge only, nonedge only, both. On three vertices they are 8,4,4,2. Results are in `verify_group_classification_results.json`. Execution is deterministic, assertion guarded, without an elapsed-time cutoff.

## Independent inversion-generation proof

Let H=H_G satisfy R/E. For g in H and i<j with g(i)>g(j), R on U={i,j} says that the swap lies in H_(G|U). E then gives the ambient transposition (i j) in H.

For any permutation g, form its inversion graph I(g), whose edges are exactly those pairs. If (i,j) is an inversion and i<k<j, then either g(i)>g(k), giving edge (i,k), or g(k)>g(j), giving edge (k,j). Consequently every component of I(g) is an interval: an inversion edge's entire span belongs to its component, and spans along a connected path have connected union. Between distinct components there are no inversions. Since the components are ordered intervals, the values of g on every earlier component are all less than its values on a later component. Cardinalities then force g to carry every component onto itself. Edge transpositions of any connected graph generate the full symmetric group on its vertex set. Thus g is generated by its inversion transpositions.

Take the union of I(g) over g in H. Every component of that union is again an interval (a union of intersecting interval components). All its edge transpositions lie in H, so its componentwise Young group is contained in H. The preceding paragraph puts every g in H inside this Young group, proving equality. No general assertion about arbitrary subgroups having this property is used.

## Graph constraints and four cases

R on all two-element subsets implies every g in H is a graph automorphism. Hence a full symmetric factor on a block forces the block internally to be a clique or independent set, and all vertices in that block have the same adjacency to each outside vertex.

E forces the ambient transposition of every pair whose two-vertex type has its swap flag enabled. Conversely the inversion argument shows H is generated by transpositions of enabled pair types. In particular H is exactly the symmetric group product on connected components of the graph of enabled pairs, and those components must be intervals.

* Neither flag: no g can have an inversion, so H is trivial and every graph is permitted.
* Edge only: every graph edge is an enabled transposition; graph automorphism invariance then forces each connected component to be a clique. More explicitly, the symmetric group on a connected component acts transitively on its unordered pairs, so one edge forces all pairs to be edges. Interval necessity gives exactly contiguous clique components; H is their symmetric group product.
* Nonedge only: apply the preceding argument to the complement, obtaining complements of contiguous clique-component graphs and the corresponding block group.
* Both: all transpositions lie in H, so H=Sym(n), and its automorphism property forces all unordered pairs to have the same adjacency. Thus only complete or edgeless graphs occur.

Sufficiency follows directly: the trivial groups satisfy R/E on every graph; interval clique blocks (or independent blocks in the complement case) restrict to the same ordered surviving blocks under blockwise permutations, and child block permutations extend inside their parent block; full symmetry works on complete/edgeless graphs. This includes the empty and singleton cases.

## Scope qualification

The four descriptions are maximal allowed graph classes with their forced groups, **not** a claim that an induced-closed family must contain every permitted graph. Any induced-closed subfamily inherits the result. If a two-vertex type is absent from the family, its flag is unobserved and may be filled arbitrarily: no graph in that family has such a pair. Thus the four flag labels need not uniquely label a family with missing types. The finite recursion checks the maximal choice containing both two-vertex graph types; it also proves the same local necessity for a subfamily because a graph's entire induced downward closure is necessarily present.
