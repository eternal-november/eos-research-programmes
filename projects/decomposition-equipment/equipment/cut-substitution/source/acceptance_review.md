The following distinctions are essential to the reviewed result:

- **Evaluated transformation versus forest inverse.** Invertibility concerns the evaluated coefficient natural transformation over retained provenance. A forest-bearing refinement arrow can remain noninvertible even when its coefficient transformation is invertible.
- **Faithful cut/substitution encoding.** Cut-then-substitute is represented by the old exact cut skeleton together with the full external graft forest. That record reconstructs the literal grafted tree and its coefficients. A nonexact graft is not incorrectly treated as an exact-proper object. Substitute-then-cut has the recut exact skeleton and refined leaf bundles.
- **Fixed-interface boundary factorization.** Boundary transport factors through the comparison from composites of reindexed spans to the reindexed composite, followed by the reindexed coefficient map. These are separate retained arrows with separate tests. A raw apex map merely covering a boundary change must first factor into the actual pullback.
- **Separate labelled and orbit branches.** Arbitrary finite-span coefficient tables remain labelled actual data in Theorem A. Theorem B uses only the original M=1 faithful-category graph-span decoration and the established F3 orbit quotient. Arbitrary placement-dependent coefficients are not silently descended through that quotient.
- **Typed occurrences and units.** Full supplied grade paths, occurrence names, segmentation and empty-slot positions are tested. A genuine nullary diagonal unit is not confused with an arbitrary empty-graph component.
