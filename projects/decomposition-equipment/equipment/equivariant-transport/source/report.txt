### D1 Isomorphism transport and equivariant presentations

**Class and claim:** M. Add transport along a specified groupoid of interface changes and prove its compatibility with witnesses, substitution, equations, and refinement.

**Inputs:** A groupoid of allowed interface isomorphisms, actions on component types and primitive witnesses, and an invariant equation family.

**Artifact:** The transported calculus, its identity and composite transport laws, an equivariant free/presented universal property, and coherent comparison functors on refinement categories.

**Discharge:** P, with L for the finite typed action used in an example. The transport data must be supplied or constructed; they do not follow merely from the existence of interface names. Specify the strict or coherent convention and prove the required coherence at that level.

**Tests:** Nontrivial permutations of finite interface sets; composite relabelings; repeated occurrences; a non-invariant relation that does not descend; and the effect of taking isomorphism classes versus retaining witnesses.

**Dependencies:** A2, A3, B2, C2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Transport must descend through equations and induce the claimed coherent refinement comparisons for arbitrary groupoid actions in the declared class. Renaming endpoints in one example or assuming relation invariance without checking it does not pass.

3  Input data and inherited results

The general global input is any small groupoid H, a quiver J_x of interfaces and actual components at every object x, typed primitive fibres S_x(c;p) retaining each whole component c and full ordered input path p, and parallel free-tree equation pairs E_x. Every arrow g:x->y supplies a quiver isomorphism B_g and primitive-fibre bijections s_g. These satisfy strict identity and composition laws. No tree, quotient-operation or refinement action is assumed.

A2 supplies the ordinary free tree calculus and its genuine hom-set universal property among all strict maps. A3 supplies the independently generated least parallel substitution congruence and actual quotient, not a semantic-kernel substitute. B2 supplies refinement arrows as full original-slot tuples, with their category and functor laws. C2 supplies the actual relation-interface model. The concrete C5 illustration additionally reuses the existing graph-cut presentation and coherent completion comparisons. These are inherited results, not new claims or a recertification of their entire workstreams.

The calculus is planar and nonsymmetric: no interface change permutes occurrence positions. Finite profiles and finite trees may be built from infinite interface and primitive sets. Equalities are mathematical equalities of data/functions, not claims of definitional equality in Lean. Proofs of typing equality are not additional witnesses.

The signature action is strict by hypothesis. No weak action is silently strictified. The only natural-isomorphism coherence used for chosen normal sections comes from the independently established C5 completion result, subject to a separate path-relation-basis check.

4  Constructed free action and exact quotient descent

Write F_x=F(S_x), let ~_x be the least parallel substitution congruence generated by E_x, and put P_x=F_x/~_x. Composition hg means first g, then h.

On free trees, define

  T_g(1_c) = 1_(B_g c),
  T_g(a(t_1,...,t_k)) = s_g(a)(T_g t_1,...,T_g t_k).

Structural induction proves well-typing and preservation of the full ordered frontier. At a node, the replacement forest splits into the same consecutive child blocks on both routes, including empty blocks. Thus

  T_g(t[u_i]) = (T_g t)[T_g u_i],
  T_1=id,  T_hg=T_h T_g,  T_(g^-1)T_g=id.

The exact descent criterion is

  [t] |-> [T_g t] is well defined
    iff every (l,r) in E_x satisfies T_g l ~_y T_g r.

Necessity uses quotient exactness. Sufficiency is induction over the actual generated-congruence derivation: equation, reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and simultaneous substitution. The substitution case uses the already proved free transport law. Quotient lifting then yields strict calculus isomorphisms P_g with the same action laws. The quotient action is a conclusion, not supplied input.

Literal invariance of the declared equation list is a stronger sufficient certificate. Failure of literal invariance alone does not prove non-descent, since an image equation could still be derivable. The negative control in section 8 proves non-derivability, not merely absence from a list.

5  Equivariant universal properties

For a strict H-calculus diagram M over the same changing bases, a primitive assignment a_x is equivariant when

  M_g a_x(v) = a_y(s_g v).

A2 extends a_x uniquely. The two candidate base-changing free maps agree on all primitives, so uniqueness, or explicit tree induction, proves equivariance of the extension. Restriction and extension are inverse. If the assignment satisfies E_x, A3 factors it uniquely through P_x. Precomposition with the surjective free-to-presented projection proves equivariance of the factors. Hence

  Hom_H-Calc(F(S), M) ~= Hom_H-Sig(S, U M),

  Hom_H-Calc(P(S,E), M)
    ~= equivariant primitive assignments satisfying every E_x.

These are natural hom-set bijections for strict equivariant maps. They are not asserted 2-categorical biuniversal properties. They require neither a normal-form theorem nor decidable equations.

6  Individual interface occurrences and charted refinement

The local construction uses an arbitrary small interface groupoid I and a component bifunctor C:I x I -> Set. Components have actions c^(f,g) at their two boundaries; the covariant notation incorporates the inverse on the conventional source boundary. A full n-slot profile has n+1 boundary OCCURRENCES. A local arrow is a list f=(f_0,...,f_n), and primitive witnesses form a strict functor from the resulting profile groupoid to Set.

Repeated equal interface labels at different positions may receive different arrows. A nullary profile has ONE boundary occurrence, not two independently changeable endpoints. Shared incidences must receive the same arrow, including identifications created by empty child profiles.

Every subtree occupies a consecutive boundary interval. Transport recursively applies the primitive action at the interval boundaries and the induced sublists to the children. If child lengths are n_i and s_i=n_1+...+n_i, the substitution law is

  tau_f(a[u_i]) = tau_(f_(s_0),...,f_(s_k))(a)
                    [tau_(f restricted to [s_(i-1),s_i])(u_i)].

Tree induction proves strict identity, composition and all finite-depth substitution coherence. Local quotient descent is equivalent to preservation of every declared equation under every allowed profile arrow. At a substitution-congruence step, cumulative offsets supply the compatible outer and child charts. The same free extension and quotient-factorization argument gives the local equivariant universal properties.

For fixed endpoint arrows f and g, ChartRef_(f,g)(M,W) retains an operation a and a full boundary chart lambda. A morphism is the entire original refinement tuple (u_i):a->b with target chart mu, subject to

  lambda_i = mu_(s_i) for every original boundary i.

Composition retains the complete tuples; its offsets compose, so the incidence equations persist. Forgetting charts is faithful but need not be full. No essential-surjectivity assertion is made for arbitrary fixed endpoint arrows.

Local transport sends (a,lambda) to (tau_lambda(a),lambda^-1), and each child u_i through the corresponding subinterval of mu. Substitution proves the endpoint law and preservation of composition. Inverse arrows return the exact operation, chart and every tuple entry. Thus the charted categories are strictly isomorphic, giving an honest span

  Ref(M,W) <- ChartRef_(f,g)(M,W) -> Ref(M,W^(f,g)).

For successive changes, retain both composable charts and both incidence conditions on a common twice-charted domain. Two-stage transport and composite-chart transport have the same final operation and full tuple. This is equality of comparison functors from that common domain, not equality of differently indexed chart categories.

A supplied coherent whole-signature system, such as a groupoid bisection, gives compatible charts for all operations and refinement arrows and recovers an ordinary uncharted functor. The local theorem does not invent such a system from endpoint names.

7  Full occurrence refinement and normal-section coherence

Ref(M,W) has operations of fixed whole W as objects. An arrow a->b consists of an operation u_i at EACH original occurrence of a, retaining its own entire input profile, with a[u_i]=b. Equality is entrywise equality of tuples, not equality of the total substituted result. Repeated component types are not deduplicated. Zero-length blocks remain recorded.

For a global P_g, transport objects and all tuple entries. Preservation of substitution establishes endpoints. The composite entry is u_i substituted by its consecutive block of later entries; transport preserves exactly this expression and segmentation. Therefore

  Ref(P_1)=Id,
  Ref(P_hg)=Ref(P_h) Ref(P_g),
  Ref(P_(g^-1)) Ref(P_g)=Id.

Raw refinements retain complete leaf-indexed replacement forests. Transport maps the tree and every forest entry; the same substitution law proves endpoints and composition. The inherited raw/free refinement comparison uses the canonical component-preserving ordered leaf/slot bijection, which transport preserves, so the raw/free/presented comparison squares commute entrywise. The same construction applies with compatible local charts.

The free-to-presented projection and any equivariant semantic factor commute with transport on every tuple entry. A semantic interpretation is not automatically an isomorphism; when a separate soundness/completeness and generation result proves it is one, the refinement comparisons are isomorphisms too.

For the already studied C5 graph-cut presentation ONLY, completion-category transport additionally requires preservation of its path-relation basis, not just equality of operations. The concrete action preserves the seven rule names, all 18 labelled critical joins, disjoint/variable addresses and original-slot MOut/MIn relations. Existing coherent normalization then gives the comparison

  alpha_(g,w): T_g N(w) -> N(D_g w)

by the normalization path of T_g N(w). Tuple-level naturality and pure-path coherence prove

  alpha_(hg,w)=alpha_(h,D_g w) o Comp_h(alpha_(g,w)),
  alpha_(1,w)=id.

No arbitrary equation family is assigned a normalization theorem. Noncanonical chosen normal forms are compared by these coherent isomorphisms rather than declared literally invariant.

8  Controls that prevent false strengthening

Non-invariant equations. With one fixed component and unary primitives a,b,z, let C2 swap a and b and fix z. Declare a=z only. An additive natural-number interpretation gives a,z weight 0 and b weight 1. Every generated congruence step preserves weight; the image equation b=z does not. Thus the induced formula on quotient classes is not a function. Adding all translates would repair invariance by changing the presentation, not by descending the old one.

Illegal endpoint-only transport. In the exact relation model the diagonal whole has a nullary operation. Unequal endpoint permutations f,g send the diagonal to the graph of g f^-1, which is not diagonal and has no exact nullary operation. The original nullary profile has just one boundary occurrence, so this is not an admissible local chart. It refutes the stronger endpoint-only assertion, not the stated theorem.

Erasing transport arrows. In the action/refinement category an arrow is (g,u), and composition is (h,v)(g,u)=(hg,v o Ref(h)(u)). Both the transport arrow and the full refinement tuple remain data. A nonidentity g fixing an operation still gives a nontrivial retained automorphism. Object isoclasses erase such stabilizers.

Orbit substitution can fail. In the one-component scalar calculus with values Z/3, inversion acts compatibly with simultaneous substitution. Yet the nonzero orbit has representatives 1 and 2: substituting 1 with 1 gives 2, whereas 1 with 2 gives 0. Independent choices of orbit representatives therefore need not define substitution. A diagonal action is not automatically a congruence under independent replacements.

Repeated and parallel witnesses. For an empty-graph diagonal whole with two equal input components and Z/2 decorations, child tuples (0,0) and (1,1) have the same total result but are distinct parallel arrows. Transport retains both slots and both decoration values. Under independently chosen local f_0,f_1,f_2, two equal diagonal inputs may become different graphs of f_1 f_0^-1 and f_2 f_1^-1 while their composite remains the graph of f_2 f_0^-1.

9  Concrete relation action and all seven equation families

A component from A to B is an actual relation R subset A x B. For bijections f,g, transport is R^(f,g)={(f(a),g(b)):(a,b) in R}. Relational composition satisfies

  R^(f,g) ; S^(g,h) = (R;S)^(f,h),
  Delta_A^(f,f)=Delta_A'.

The middle-witness maps b->g(b) and b'->g^-1(b') are inverse; full realizing tuples transform coordinatewise. This works for empty carriers as well as nonempty ones. It supplies the component action, exact/containment semantics and validity of transported primitives.

The selected finite action has three interface labels A,B,C, each with three carrier points. Its group is (S3)^3 with 216 actions. Each ordered hom set contains all 512 relations. The action moves actual relation entries while retaining interface labels. A nontrivial example sends {(0,0),(1,2)} to {(1,1),(2,2)} using the 3-cycle at A and transposition (0 1) at B.

For the unchanged C5 signature S(N,K,M), graph data, ordered cuts and canonical graph restrictions are retained, while every grade relation is transported. Decorations remain fixed or are carried by a separately specified monoid automorphism chi. Binary primitives use relational-composition equivariance; nullaries use diagonal preservation.

The P proof checks literal invariance of the actual typed instances of every equation family:

  R   Reassociation preserves ordered graph blocks and the grade string;
      intermediate relation composites transform by the same formula.
  UL  The left nullary diagonal and its shared boundary are preserved.
  UR  The corresponding right unit calculation preserves its occurrence.
  D0  chi(1)=1 preserves the identity decoration equation.
  DA  chi(mn)=chi(m)chi(n) preserves multiplication of decorations.
  DL  The same chi(m) occurs at the transported left child and whole.
  DR  The same calculation holds at the distinct right occurrence.

The existing C5 quotient-to-flat-witness isomorphism is equivariant because both routes agree on every primitive; free uniqueness and quotient factorization extend the equality. This witness-sensitive comparison is stronger than merely interpreting the source in thin Rel.

12  Relation to established work

The free and presented transport mechanism is standard equivariant algebra. This result supplies the chosen interface action and proves its compatibility with the particular calculus's retained witnesses and full occurrence-refinement structure. It is not a novelty claim for free operads, invariant quotients or groupoid colors.

Jonathan Rubin, Combinatorial N-infinity operads, arXiv:1705.03585v3, sections 5.2, 5.4 and 5.5, supplies equivariant congruence/quotient and free-adjunction baselines in its stated finite-group symmetric setting. The external group action and input-permutation action are distinct.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.03585v3

Clemens Berger and Ieke Moerdijk, Resolution of coloured operads and rectification of homotopy algebras, arXiv:math/0512576v2, section 1.6 and Theorem 3.2, supplies change-of-colors and typed free-tree baselines. Fixed colors alone do not supply primitive witness actions or this refinement transport.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512576v2

Dominik Trnka, Category-colored Operads, Internal Operads, and Markl O-operads, arXiv:2304.02446v2, Definition 1.1, Proposition 5.6 and Example 5.7, treats genuinely multiobject category/groupoid coloring. Its internal-edge balancing and linear ideal setting must not be silently identified with an external whole-signature diagram or set-valued congruence.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02446v2

Alexander S. Corner and Nick Gurski, Operads with general groups of equivariance, and some 2-categorical aspects of operads in Cat, arXiv:1312.5910v2, Definitions 1.10 and 1.14, concerns arity-indexed action operads and input symmetries, not automatically arbitrary external interface transport.
https://arxiv.org/html/1312.5910v2

The bundled literature comparison records exact verified locators and qualifications. The comparison is bounded, not an exhaustive novelty search.
