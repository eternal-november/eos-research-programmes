# Research program for witnessed decompositions

## Proposed completion contract

Version 0.1, final review candidate proposed on 24 October 2026. This document proposes a research baseline for approval. It does not claim that the user has accepted its individual theorem statements or that the obligations below have already been discharged.

The objective is a mathematically substantial calculus of witnessed decomposition: a theory in which components have interfaces, a decomposition is supplied by a witness rather than inferred from its pieces, repeated component occurrences remain distinct, and refinement records how a presentation is extended. The program must explain exactly when additional structure supports interface transport, exact assembly, coherent cuts, and observational comparison. These are separate questions, with separate hypotheses.

The intended scale is approximately 4,100–7,100 expert person-hours of mathematical work before AI assistance, with a working midpoint near 5,600 hours. This is a bottom-up scope forecast, not a time guarantee, a measurable AI speedup claim, or a requirement to spend hours after the mathematical questions are settled. Software setup, downloads, document production, and routine engineering are excluded. The draft contains 36 individually identified obligations grouped into integrated result packages; that number is a tracking device, not a measure of significance. The current Lean seed is a small initial contribution, not evidence that this research program is nearly complete.

Successful completion means a coherent body of theorems, explicit limits, substantive models, a checked foundational implementation, and independently reviewed evidence. A useful negative result is acceptable only where an obligation explicitly permits it. A short counterexample, a collection of definitions, or a successful build cannot substitute for the program.

## 1 Research questions and scientific product

The research will settle five connected questions.

1. What is the precise free and presented algebraic structure generated by typed decomposition witnesses, and what distinctions survive its universal property?
2. How do occurrence-wise refinements compose, and what is lost when presentations are evaluated or quotiented?
3. Which additional hypotheses permit coherent interface changes or represent witnesses as exact assemblies, and which ordinary decomposition examples violate them?
4. When does a witness calculus admit coherent cuts and a finite discrete 2-Segal interpretation, rather than merely associative substitution?
5. Which aspects of components, wholes, witnesses, and presentations can a specified semantics or observation recover?

The final product is a coherent result dossier with a theorem ledger; a small, auditable Lean library for the foundational claims; executable finite models and counterexamples; a reproducibility package; and an independent acceptance report. The report must distinguish established background, a new proof or implementation of background, a genuinely new result, a conditional result, and an unresolved question. The user’s benchmark is the breadth and significance of a substantial collection of mathematical results, not a request for papers, manuscripts, publication counts, or publication work. Completion is not a guarantee of novelty. If comparison with existing mathematics removes the claimed novelty, that finding must be stated and the research direction reconsidered.

No empirical claim about brains, physiology, intelligence, or a computome follows from this program. Any such application would require a separately approved observational model, data, identification assumptions, and validation protocol. This contract does not restart paused automations or authorize indefinite execution of every phase. The presently authorized code milestone remains a bounded task.

### 1.1 One connected theory rather than a structure catalogue

The principal mathematical spine is a semantic presentation and comparison theorem for a parameterized family of typed graph cuts with retained witnesses. Local tree equations must describe exactly the chosen semantic equality; refinements must descend in the claimed sense; interface transport and exact assembly must have proved compatibility boundaries; independent cuts must support the claimed simplicial construction; and observations must have a measured information loss. The modularity, minimization, incidence, interchange, and repair results below all act on this same spine and its relational comparison model.

The standard free multicategory, the raw refinement poset, an ordinary nerve, and a generic semantics fold are shared infrastructure. They do not each count as a major contribution. The intended depth lies in sound-and-complete witness presentations; quotient/refinement and cancellation boundaries; normalization tied to those semantics; interface descent; exact assembly under transport; coherent-cut recognition with quotient compatibility; objective versus numerical incidence descent; compositional observation minimization; minimal recovery information; conservative amalgamation; cut–substitution interchange; and compatibility of the resulting completion operations. These are connected result targets, not predetermined novel discoveries or a paper count.

A result-level contribution ledger must show what each target adds beyond the strongest applicable prior theory, which other target uses it, and which parameterized example would fail without it. A target found to be a direct standard corollary must be merged into its baseline rather than counted again. Conversely, a well-delimited impossibility theorem can be a substantial contribution when its accepted branch also contains the promised sharp boundary and positive repair.

## 2 Frozen mathematical core

### 2.1 Interfaces and profiles

Fix a set or type of interfaces $I$ and a directed family of component types $J(A,B)$ for $A,B\in I$. An ordered profile is a composable path

$$p=(J_1:A_0\to A_1,\ldots,J_n:A_{n-1}\to A_n).$$

A whole $W$ for this profile belongs to $J(A_0,A_n)$. A signature supplies a set of primitive witnesses $S(p;W)$. A witness is not required to reconstruct $W$ from the component types alone. Two different wholes may admit witnesses with the same input profile; two witnesses may have the same profile and whole.

The default foundational development permits nonempty profiles. A separately tested nullary extension permits the empty path at $A$ only with output $W\in J(A,A)$. Such a witness is additional supplied data. It does not assert that every loop component is a unit, that every interface has a chosen unit component, or that a component with unequal endpoints can be the output of an empty profile.

### 2.2 Presentations and evaluation

A presentation is a finite planar rooted tree. Its edges carry component types; a vertex carries a primitive witness with the matching ordered incoming edges and outgoing edge. The identity presentation on $W$ is a single edge with no primitive vertex. It is not a new primitive unary witness. Every external leaf has its own occurrence position, even when multiple leaves carry exactly the same component type.

Substitution replaces each leaf occurrence by a presentation whose output is that occurrence's component. The new frontier is the ordered concatenation of the substituted frontiers, with the required interface identifications. The main laws are left unit, right unit, and associativity for simultaneous occurrence-wise substitution. There is no exchange, symmetry, contraction, copying, or permutation rule unless introduced in a separately named extension.

Let $F(S)(p;W)$ denote the operation fibre of free presentations with frontier $p$ and whole $W$. Semantic evaluation maps these presentations into another typed witness calculus. Evaluation may identify different trees. Neither equality of frontiers nor equality after evaluation implies equality of presentation trees.

### 2.3 Equations

A relation may equate only parallel operations: the same ordered frontier, the same intermediate interface data, and the same whole. The presented calculus $F(S)/E$ is the quotient by the least substitution-stable congruence containing the declared equations. It is not the quotient by all equalities observed in one selected model.

Arbitrary equational theories are not promised normal forms or decidable equality. A named decidable fragment and a separate limitation result are mandatory. A proof may be unexpectedly short and still fully succeed: the acceptance floor constrains mathematical strength and scope, not labor expended.

### 2.4 Refinement

A raw refinement $T\to T'$ is a leaf-indexed forest $F$ with $T'=T[F]$. Composition is forest substitution. Its data retain the internal tree structure added at each original leaf, not the chronology in which independent grafts were performed. Two event sequences that merely interleave independent grafts are not automatically different refinements.

For an evaluated calculus $M$, the analogous semantic refinement category has objects $a\in M(p;W)$ and arrows given by an occurrence-indexed tuple $b_i$ satisfying $a'=a[b_i]$. The tuple, including its individual frontier lengths and types, is arrow data. Semantic equality of the composite does not erase which original occurrence was refined.

The program will prove the precise relation between these categories. It will not assume that identifying presentation objects automatically produces the correct quotient category.

### 2.5 Independent additional layers

The following structures are additional layers, not assumptions of the minimal core. The full virtual-equipment layer is nevertheless a central mandatory outcome of this program:

- transport of interfaces along isomorphisms or general maps;
- a full virtual equipment with restrictions and universal units, plus a distinct ordinary-equipment representability/completion analysis;
- exact assembly of a whole from independent pieces;
- chosen coherent cuts, factorization uniqueness, and a 2-Segal nerve;
- observations, measurement protocols, or identifiability assumptions.

An extension must state its added data, prove its laws, and give the exact comparison with the core. An equipment completion may add horizontal components and cells, so its full identity-boundary part need not literally equal the original core. No extension may silently redefine a legitimate nonreconstructive primitive cut as invalid.

## 3 Completion rules

### 3.1 Obligation classes

Each obligation below has an identifier, claim or question, inputs, required artifact, discharge rule, tests, dependencies, and current status.

**M means must pass.** A constructive proof, model, or specified verification is required. An obstruction to an M obligation exposes a defective baseline and triggers an amendment decision; it does not count as completion.

**B means bounded theorem or obstruction.** A positive theorem may discharge the obligation. A negative branch must instead identify a natural, explicitly bounded class; prove the failure and isolate the failed hypothesis; and provide either a useful repaired positive theorem with a nontrivial model or a sharp impossibility boundary for that class. It must compare with the relevant existing construction. Finding one tiny counterexample or writing that a problem is hard does not discharge a B obligation.

**G means governance or integration gate.** It requires the stated evidence, not a mathematical theorem.

All obligations start OPEN. An artifact may be recorded as submitted or verified without being accepted. ACCEPTED requires the evidence and reviewer requirements below. Observed seed artifacts do not automatically discharge broader claims.

### 3.2 Distinct completion predicates

**Core achieved:** A1–A3, B1–B2, the core-facing model certificates, and every mandatory Lean claim in Section 13 are accepted. This does not mean all research obligations are complete.

**Full virtual equipment achieved:** E1–E3 and their dependencies are accepted, including the universal presentation, coherent change of choices, and positive recovery on original profiles. A Rel/Span interpretation alone does not qualify.

**Ordinary equipment specialization achieved:** The positive representability/completion theorem in E4 is accepted for its named class, including actual universal composites and coherence. This does not assert that every virtual model is ordinary or that every legitimate witness is exact.

**Program investigated to closure:** Every obligation has its prescribed positive or explicitly allowed negative outcome, and all governance gates pass. A failed attempt, an open mandatory theorem, or an unapproved weakening prevents this status.

**Intended scientific scope achieved:** In addition to investigation closure, non-author review confirms that the integrated result packages meet their generality, comparison, sharpness, and anti-shortcut floors. If the ambitious claims all reduce to known results, trivial enrichments, or elementary counterexamples, the report must say the intended scope was not achieved, even if definitions and code are correct. It must propose a material amendment or report the shortfall, never fabricate novelty.

Creating this proposed contract satisfies none of these mathematical predicates. The final verdict must also distinguish a wholly positive program from closure containing certified obstruction branches.

### 3.3 Evidence levels

- **L:** Lean-checked theorem, with the exact statement, source location, dependency closure, and axiom report.
- **P:** complete mathematical proof that a competent independent reviewer can verify without filling a central gap.
- **C:** executable computation with stated finite domain and independent or redundant checking. Computation alone does not establish an unrestricted theorem.
- **X:** explicit counterexample with all types and laws checked, together with the required scope analysis.

Every theorem must list its assumptions and quantifiers. A conditional theorem is accepted only as conditional. A missing assumption is not cured by silently narrowing a theorem after testing it. In particular, loss of cross edges from isolated pieces is not a failure of 2-Segality when the simplicial data retain the whole and its cut. A negative bridge must identify either failure to define coherent simplicial maps or a specified nonbijective comparison in an actually defined simplicial object.

### 3.4 Minimum passing work and managed-task handoffs

For every obligation, the minimum passing deliverable must already reach its intended depth. An author may not substitute a narrower toy case, a weaker theorem, a familiar corollary under renamed hypotheses, or a code-only result and rely on broad wording to obtain acceptance. The explicit floors and nonpassing shortcuts below are part of the contract, not optional advice.

Before dispatching an attempt, a non-author reviewer must identify the cheapest technically compliant solution and ask whether it would satisfy the intended mathematical goal. If not, tighten the target class, integration requirement, or proof obligation before the work starts. The reviewer records the proposed cheap solution and the clause that excludes it. This check is mandatory even when the individual task looks easy.

Each task or agent handoff must carry the contract version, obligation ID, complete acceptance clause, frozen inputs and hypotheses, nonpassing shortcuts, dependencies, required evidence, and the permitted theorem/obstruction branch. A handoff that says only “prove the theorem” or “implement the structure” is insufficient. Subdelegation preserves these clauses. A successful build may pass implementation review while failing scientific-scope review.

Acceptance has two distinct dimensions. **Investigation closure** records a complete positive or allowed negative account of the stated question. **Scientific acceptance** additionally requires the promised generality, substantial integration, prior-art distinction, and nondegenerate model coverage. A proof attempt that shows a proposed contribution was already known may close that investigation, but does not silently satisfy the program’s desired significance. It prompts a scope decision or an honest shortfall report. Hours, lines of code, numbers of definitions, tests, and new terminology are never substitutes.

### 3.5 Minimum nonvacuity

The mandatory model suite must jointly include at least three distinct interfaces; genuinely composable mixed-interface profiles; repeated occurrences of one identical component type receiving different substitutions; primitive witnesses of arity at least two; nested trees with at least four ordered leaves; competing binary groupings; parallel distinct witnesses where the selected model permits them; a nonempty, nontrivial equational theory; and different wholes with identical ordered restricted pieces because cross-piece information has been lost.

An empty signature, a single terminal model, a one-object example, or a table containing only identities may be a sanity check but cannot be the principal certificate. Numeric thresholds are coverage minima, not a substitute for mathematical substance. Raising their size without adding a phenomenon does not increase research credit.

### 3.6 Integrated result acceptance

The following bundles prevent a technically correct but shallow collection of isolated deliverables from passing:

- Witness presentation and refinement: A4, B3–B4, C5. Require sound-and-complete semantics, effective reasoning in the selected fragment, and the comparison of refinement arrows, not only ordinary tree associativity.
- Full decomposition equipment: D1–D3, E1–E5. Require the complete virtual theory, universal presentation and original-profile recovery, with ordinary representability and exactness treated separately.
- Coherent cuts and incidence: F1–F4, I3–I4. Require quotient/witness-sensitive recognition, a positive all-degree typed model, structural lifting limits, and the incidence/interchange consequences.
- Observation and recovery: G1–G3, I2, I5. Require explicit recoverable information and lower bounds, mixed context/cut minimization, and structural compatibility or noncommutation of repairs.
- Modular reasoning: I1 together with B3, D3, and E2. Require conservative combination across genuinely shared interfaces and transport of the accepted certificates.

Every bundle must connect to the common parameterized models, identify its strongest established baseline, and be used by at least one other bundle or a concrete reasoning task. Definitions, standard corollaries, and elementary negative controls remain valuable infrastructure but do not separately discharge a bundle's scientific floor. These are conjunctive acceptance requirements, not a menu from which an implementer may pick the easiest result.

## 4 Workstream A Foundations and presentations

### A1 Typed algebraic specification and representation theorem

**Class and claim:** M. Specify the category of path-profile nonsymmetric multicategories over fixed $(I,J)$ and prove that the chosen indexed tree representation implements the intended occurrence-linear syntax, rather than a weaker recursive fold language.

**Inputs:** The frozen core; an explicit definition of ordered composable profiles and equality of profiles; a finite signature used as a running example.

**Artifact:** A mathematical specification, a representation comparison between abstract typed planar trees and the implementation, and named fibre-level operation and substitution declarations.

**Discharge:** L for typing, occurrence transport, frontier substitution, units, and associativity in the implemented operation fibres. P, supplemented by L where practical, for the representation comparison and the correspondence with the standard nonsymmetric colored multicategory formulation restricted to admissible path profiles. The comparison must be two-sided on the selected representation, not merely a semantics-preserving map.

**Tests:** Wrong endpoints, reversed order, a nullary nonloop, and substitution at the wrong repeated occurrence must be rejected or distinguished. Test both nonempty and explicitly enabled nullary cases.

**Dependencies:** None. **Status:** OPEN; the existing seed is relevant but does not yet meet this statement.

**Minimum passing floor:** The fibre-level structure and two-sided representation comparison must cover arbitrary signatures over the fixed base. Output-indexed recursion, one-interface syntax, or passing example tests alone does not pass. This is infrastructure, not an independently major research contribution.

### A2 Free universal property

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the free functor on typed signatures and establish its universal property against every target calculus over the fixed base.

**Inputs:** A1 and the category of signature maps and strict calculus morphisms.

**Artifact:** A natural bijection

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Calc}}(F(S),M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sig}}(S,U(M)),$$

with explicit mutually inverse maps, preservation of all operations, and uniqueness of the extending morphism.

**Discharge:** L for the extension, preservation, uniqueness, and inverse laws on the core implementation; P for any categorical packaging not formalized. Naturality in both arguments and the free-forgetful adjunction must be stated and proved. A structural fold theorem with arbitrary values assigned to component leaves does not by itself discharge this obligation.

**Tests:** Two target calculi, including a nontrivial relational or graph-cut model; two distinct assignments of a primitive parallel witness; and a failed assignment that does not preserve declared types.

**Dependencies:** A1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The universal quantifier over target calculi, morphism preservation, inverse correspondence, and naturality are required. A recursive fold with arbitrary leaf values, evaluation in one model, or uniqueness only among chosen recursive functions does not pass.

### A3 Presented universal property and semantic soundness

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the least parallel substitution congruence and prove the presented universal property. Explain the relation between derivability, equality in the presented calculus, and equality in all models satisfying the presentation.

**Inputs:** A2; an explicit relation schema whose instances are all parallel.

**Artifact:** Congruence rules, the quotient calculus, the factorization theorem for interpretations satisfying $E$, and a model-theoretic separation statement using the presented calculus itself where applicable.

**Discharge:** L for the core congruence closure and quotient substitution well-definedness; P or L for the complete universal property and the precise all-models statement. The quotient must preserve the ordered boundary typing. The report must not substitute equality in one model for equational derivability.

**Tests:** A genuine associativity-style presentation with nontrivial intermediate interfaces; a model satisfying it; a separating model for an undeclared equation; and rejection of a nonparallel equation.

**Dependencies:** A1, A2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The least parallel congruence and universal quotient must be constructed for arbitrary declared equations. Soundness in one model, a quotient type without substitution descent, or silently identifying all observed equalities does not pass.

### A4 Decidable fragment and limits

**Class and claim:** M. Establish one useful, nontrivial decidable equational fragment and an explicit boundary beyond which decidability is not asserted.

**Inputs:** A3; a finite, typed, occurrence-linear rewrite specification with decidable matching and a stated reduction order. The mandatory concrete fragment contains C5’s binary cut rotations, unit elimination, finite-monoid decoration aggregation, and movement of decorations through cut constructors, with genuinely interacting schemas. The abstract theorem may state wider checkable hypotheses, but this mixed fragment cannot be replaced by associativity alone.

**Artifact:** A termination proof, a complete critical-overlap or equivalent confluence proof, a normalization procedure, and a theorem identifying normal-form equality with the presented congruence for the selected fragment. Include a typed composition-associativity example over several interfaces, not solely an untyped binary tree toy.

**Discharge:** P for the general fragment and its soundness/completeness; L for a sufficiently substantial worked instance and executable normalizer soundness. Supply a rigorous reduction showing how unrestricted unary witness presentations encompass monoid word problems, with a primary source for the undecidability theorem used. No claim of general decidability is permitted.

**Tests:** All critical overlap shapes of the selected rewrite system; equalities requiring several rewrites; a deliberately nonconfluent presentation; and a nonterminating orientation whose rejection is explained.

**Dependencies:** A3, C1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The declared class must have a complete proof of matching, termination, confluence, and agreement with the semantic congruence; its normalization must support C5 or B3. A standard associativity normalizer by itself, a bounded exhaustive search, or an assertion that critical pairs can be checked does not pass.

## 5 Workstream B Refinement and semantic comparison

### B1 Raw refinement category and occurrence coherence

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the raw leaf-graft refinement category, prove its category laws, and characterize its thinness and antisymmetry for the selected planar syntax.

**Inputs:** A1; leaf-indexed forests and typed grafting.

**Artifact:** Category construction; unique recovery of the graft forest when valid; a proof of the tree-prefix characterization; and a theorem identifying finite graft-event partial orders and showing that complete histories for the same forest differ precisely by interchange of independent events. Specify how event addresses change under grafting and prove the correspondence, not just two sample swaps.

**Discharge:** L for the category laws, typed forest composition, and core occurrence correspondence. P or L for the full prefix and chronology comparison. If raw refinement is a poset, the result must say so; artificial parallel arrows must not be added to make it look richer.

**Tests:** Repeated labels at different positions; independent grafts in both orders; a dependent graft that cannot be interchanged; nullary grafts when enabled; and a failed attempt to refine an existing internal vertex rather than a leaf.

**Dependencies:** A1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove raw thinness and antisymmetry as well as the category and chronology comparison. A free category of execution traces, a reachability relation with lost forest data, or category laws proved only after erasing occurrence positions does not pass.

### B2 Evaluated refinements and comparison functors

**Class and claim:** M. Construct the semantic refinement category for a general target calculus and prove that interpretation induces a functor from raw refinements.

**Inputs:** A2, B1, and an interpretation $F(S)\to M$.

**Artifact:** Explicit objects and arrow tuples; the evaluation functor; its identity and composition laws; and sufficient hypotheses for reflecting or preserving the distinctions the report claims.

**Discharge:** L for the basic construction and functor laws where representation permits; P for the general comparison. Provide a genuine example in which two different presentation trees have the same evaluated witness. Provide a justified nonthin semantic refinement example, such as two different original occurrences refined to give the same quotient operation, and identify the arrow data that distinguish it.

**Tests:** The associative binary-tree quotient gives parallel semantic refinements corresponding to different leaf expansions while raw tree-prefix refinements remain thin. A semantics that discards all witnesses is a negative control, not a certificate of faithful comparison.

**Dependencies:** A2, A3, B1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Construct actual arrow tuples and a functor for arbitrary target calculi, then distinguish the nonthin semantic example from raw prefix refinement. Showing equal evaluations of two trees or naming a category without arrow composition does not pass.

### B3 Quotients and comparison coherence

**Class and claim:** B. Determine when forming the presented witness calculus and then its refinement category agrees with an explicitly specified categorical quotient or completion of the raw refinement category.

**Inputs:** A3–A4 and B1–B2; the interacting C5 presentation family and an independently specified categorical completion. The candidate completion is generated by elementary raw refinements and invertible equation steps, with raw category relations and explicit ordinary/mixed critical-coherence relations. The relations must have a finite or effectively enumerable basis under the selected hypotheses, rather than being defined as every equality true in the intended semantic target.

**Artifact:** A precise comparison construction, a sufficient criterion for agreement, and a necessity analysis within the named class. All comparison squares involving interpretation, quotient, and refinement must have explicit commuting equalities or specified natural isomorphisms with coherence proofs.

**Discharge:** Either a comparison theorem covering a nontrivial presentation class, or an obstruction showing exactly why the proposed quotient fails together with a repaired construction and positive theorem, or a sharp impossibility boundary for the stipulated class. A statement that quotients are subtle is not a result.

**Tests:** Associativity equations; an equation that identifies different internal structures; a witness-collapsing semantics; and a case where quotienting objects alone fails to account for arrow witnesses.

**Dependencies:** A3, A4, B1, B2, C5. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The comparison must cover an unbounded presentation family with genuine witness identification and characterize which information its arrows retain. A quotient of objects alone, one associative-tree example, or assuming the comparison is full/faithful as a hypothesis does not pass.

### B4 Refinement invariants and recoverability

**Class and claim:** M. Establish useful invariants of refinement and characterize what a refinement record can recover about its source and added structure.

**Inputs:** B1–B3 and the principal models in Workstream C.

**Artifact:** Theorems for frontier order, vertex growth or an appropriate well-founded measure, retained source structure, and a precise distinction between graft forest, final tree, evaluated witness, and chronological history. Include a classification within a stated finite or finitely presented class of when the chosen forgetful maps are injective or have nontrivial fibres.

**Discharge:** P plus L for the elementary invariants. The classification must go beyond displaying one pair of equal images; it must identify a reusable structural criterion or an exact finite-family description with proof.

**Tests:** Unary primitive vertices, nullary witnesses, repeated occurrences, and two distinct event schedules yielding the same forest.

**Dependencies:** B1, B2, B3, C1, C2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** A structural criterion or exact family classification must identify recoverable source/forest information and support G2. Node counting alone, the tautology that injective maps are recoverable, or one equal-image pair does not pass.

## 6 Workstream C Substantive models and separation examples

### C1 A finite typed graph-cut family

**Class and claim:** M. Build a model family that simultaneously exhibits nontrivial interfaces, repeated occurrences, nonreconstructive cuts, and competing refinement trees.

**Inputs:** A1–A3. Use finite simple graphs with explicitly ordered vertices, a bound $N\geq6$, and a finite category with at least three objects and nonidentity composable arrows. A three-object chain is an admissible starting case.

**Artifact:** Component types pair a graph with an arrow $A\to B$. An $n$-ary witness consists of a whole graph, an ordered partition of its vertices into $n$ possibly empty blocks, and a composable interface-arrow string whose composite is the whole arrow; the input components are the induced subgraphs with the relevant arrows. Restrictions use a declared canonical relabeling convention. Substitution refines the partition inside each block. Explain explicitly why the bound is closed under this operation.

**Discharge:** P for the family and all calculus laws; L for a typed nontrivial fragment; C for the finite test instance. The report must identify exactly which data are stored in a witness, in a whole, and in a leaf. It must not erase the whole graph while relying on it in the proof.

**Tests:** A mixed-interface four-leaf path containing two identical loop-arrow component types at the middle interface, refined differently; two binary groupings evaluating to the same flat cut; and two different whole graphs with the same ordered induced leaf graphs because a cross-block edge differs. Empty blocks test units, not the principal nontriviality condition.

**Dependencies:** A1, A2, A3. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Uniform laws for arbitrary finite sizes, a declared closed bounded instance, the six-vertex repeated-nonempty-component certificate, and its use in C5/F2 are required. A one-off graph, empty middle blocks, or a tree-size truncation not closed under composition does not pass.

### C2 A relation-based interface model

**Class and claim:** M. Develop a second model with genuinely different semantics and explicit boundary changes.

**Inputs:** Finite interface sets; binary relations as components; compositional containment or equality as separately declared witness conditions.

**Artifact:** The model definition, substitution laws, exact versus nonexact witnesses, and interface-restriction maps. Include witnesses whose whole relation strictly contains the composite relation so that exactness is not built into the definition.

**Discharge:** P for the family and L or independently checked C for the finite certificates used elsewhere. Clarify that a posetal relational semantics may identify different primitive or presentation witnesses; that loss is a property to analyze, not a reason to equate the syntax.

**Tests:** At least three interface sets; unequal cardinalities; a nonempty composite; a strict containment witness; and a non-surjective intermediate boundary map that removes intermediate relational witnesses and makes the comparison strict. Containment cells may survive even when exactness fails.

**Dependencies:** A1, A2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove the parameterized relational model and its actual interface comparison maps, then use it in D2/E. A singleton relation example or a containment/equality conflation does not pass. This familiar model is a baseline, not novelty by renaming.

### C3 Separation and nonreconstruction theorems

**Class and claim:** M. Prove which distinctions the models separate and which reconstruction claims they refute.

**Inputs:** C1–C2 and the semantic comparison definitions.

**Artifact:** A separation matrix with proof or explicit counterexample for: same frontier versus same tree; same tree shape versus same primitive labels; same leaf types versus same whole; same whole versus same witness; same evaluated witness versus same presentation; and same observation versus same semantic object.

**Discharge:** P and X. Each entry must name the domain and map in question. The nonreconstruction result must quantify over a meaningful family, not merely state that one graph edge was deleted. For graph cuts, characterize the fibre of the map that retains only the induced block graphs: cross-block edges give freely varying missing data in the unrestricted simple-graph family.

**Tests:** Exhaustive checks on all ordered simple graphs up to four vertices and their relevant ordered partitions, plus independently verified six-vertex repeated-component examples and cross-block families of arbitrary size in the proof. Check that any claimed injectivity disappears in the intended negative control.

**Dependencies:** B2, C1, C2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Determine the full missing-cross-edge fibre and distinguish whole, witness, and presentation maps. A two-vertex counterexample alone or a matrix of unsupported yes/no claims does not pass.

### C4 Baseline and ablation comparison

**Class and claim:** M. Establish what the new presentation buys relative to mature existing structures, and which ingredients are essential.

**Inputs:** A1–A4, B1–B4, C1–C3 and the primary literature.

**Artifact:** Explicit translations to the relevant ordinary nonsymmetric multicategory, restriction-species, and relation or double-category baselines. Include ablations that remove interfaces, occurrence positions, retained whole data, primitive witness multiplicity, or parallelism of equations, with a theorem or counterexample showing the effect of each removal.

**Discharge:** P with an independent literature check. Terminological novelty is insufficient. Each proposed new contribution must survive translation into the strongest applicable baseline, or be relabeled as exposition, synthesis, or implementation.

**Tests:** Reproduce at least one established construction without changing its hypotheses; then identify precisely what additional result or comparison the program supplies.

**Dependencies:** A1, A2, A3, A4, B1, B2, B3, B4, C1, C2, C3. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Compare equally informative structured baselines, with explicit translations and exact hypotheses. An informal bibliography, withholding whole/witness data from the comparator, or listing new names does not pass.

### C5 Sound and complete witness presentations

**Class and claim:** M. Give a semantic presentation theorem for the parameterized typed graph-cut family, then identify exactly how finite witness decorations change it. This is the central link between the free syntax, the models, and the independent-cut theory.

**Inputs:** C1 for arbitrary finite ordered graphs and finite interface categories, with each bounded instance explicitly closed under the operations; binary cut generators, the designated nullary units, and explicit local rotation/unit equations. The enriched family adds witness values in an arbitrary finite commutative monoid, with occurrence-wise composition aggregating values; unary decoration operations and their equations must be stated. This enrichment retains genuine parallel witnesses and need not satisfy the reduced/unit hypotheses of a 2-Segal recognition theorem.

**Artifact:** Surjective generation by the chosen generators; soundness and completeness of the local equations for equality of the full flat cut witness, not just its leaves; an explicit normal form or comparably effective decision procedure; and a theorem comparing presented refinements with semantic refinements. For every finite commutative witness monoid in the declared family, prove the complete extended presentation with an explicit decoration normal form. The separate F-workstream may prove that such an enrichment fails a stronger unique-cut condition; that failure does not excuse the required semantic presentation theorem. An elementary observation that monoid labels admit multiple splittings is a baseline counterexample, not the enriched presentation/refinement compatibility result.

**Discharge:** P for arbitrary finite sizes and monoid parameters under the stated hypotheses; L for a representative nontrivial instance; C using independently implemented tree evaluation and flat-cut evaluation. The semantic comparison must commute with interface relabeling and specify what happens to arrow witnesses. Tie the factorization/uniqueness analysis directly to F1–F3, rather than assuming decorations are harmless.

### E1 Axioms morphisms and the core comparison

## 9 Workstream F Coherent cuts and the finite discrete 2 Segal bridge

$$\coprod_K M(p/K;W)\times M(p_{[i,j]};K)\longrightarrow M(p;W).$$

**Tests:** Multiple factorizations, no factorization, two incompatible cuts, and missing unit data. Check all boundary cases separately.

**Dependencies:** A3, B2, B3, C1, C5. **Status:** OPEN.

**Discharge:** P and C. If an optimal domain fails to exist in the selected representation class, prove that failure and the exact enlargement needed for a useful positive theorem. At least one application must show how the extra retained information changes a refinement, cut, or transport judgment.

**Tests:** A one-pass positive case; a decomposition-specific noncommuting family; an abstraction complete for one operation but not the others; and a modular local-repair case that does and does not give the global optimum.

**Dependencies:** I1, I2, I4, D3, G2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Existence of lattice fixed points, a generic complete-shell theorem, or two unrelated closure operators on a tiny lattice does not pass. Compare with complete abstract-interpretation shells/cores and identify the new criterion in witnessed-decomposition terms.

## 13 Formalization boundary and current seed

It covers the typed path/profile and tree representation; occurrence-indexed substitution and frontier laws; the fibre-level nonsymmetric multicategory laws; the genuine free extension theorem; the core parallel congruence and quotient-substitution construction; raw refinement composition; basic semantic comparison; selected invariants; and a nontrivial typed model or rewrite fragment. Full Lean formalization of double categories, groupoids, simplicial objects, or infinity-categories is not part of this baseline. The specifically verified seed currently contains output-indexed typed trees, occurrence-wise substitutions, grafting, both unit laws, an associativity statement through composed substitutions, a correspondence with structural leaf positions, and ordered frontier/grafting results. The final seed verification passed for these specific claims. The seed's operation fibre does not yet by itself provide the complete fibre-level multicategory composition/coherence package. ### 13.1 Explicit deferred scope

“Full” means complete at the defined classical virtual-equipment level, including its morphisms, transformations, universal presentation, coherence, and the stated ordinary-equipment branch. It does not mean every conceivable enrichment.

The following remain a visible backlog, not hidden omissions or mandatory outcomes: arbitrary symmetric or parallel wiring; causal feedback, traced, or cyclic extensions beyond what a chosen model actually supplies; continuous or stochastic dynamics; physiological identification or claims about real systems; full infinity-categorical generality; and complete Lean mechanization of all equipment, descent, simplicial, and observational theorems. The estimates assume one expert already comfortable with category theory, operadic presentations, and basic 2-Segal/decomposition-space literature; access to primary sources; and no requirement for a novel general infinity-categorical formalization. They include conjecture refinement, counterexample search, proof development, checking hidden assumptions, and mathematical revisions after criticism. They are not estimates of elapsed calendar time or a person's historical labor.

The working midpoint is approximately 5,600 hours. The unrounded package sum is 4,090–7,100 hours; the total is rounded to avoid implying precision the estimates do not have. The range is wider than the initial narrow-core forecast because the clarified goal requires several additional reasoning mechanisms, a full virtual-equipment theory, and their compatibility theorems; it would be misleading to preserve a smaller estimate by relabeling existing lemmas. Workstreams overlap conceptually, but the ledger assigns each proof and mathematical revision to one stream to avoid double-counting. D owns the concrete transport/descent criteria; E owns their extension to arbitrary multicell contexts and universal equipment presentations, not another proof of D. The full-equipment workstream contains approximately 160–260 hours for axioms, morphisms, 2-cells, and core comparison; 320–520 for universal presentation/reconstruction and choice coherence; 180–280 for substantive witness models and interoperability; 170–300 for ordinary-equipment representability/completion; and 270–440 for exact markings, transport, and descent. This totals 1,100–1,800 hours. It includes, rather than adds again, the earlier interpretation/exactness work. The full target adds roughly 790–1,300 nonoverlapping mathematical hours relative to an interpretation-only equipment stream.

If a reduction to an established theorem settles a large branch cheaply, that is a successful finding, not a reason to invent unnecessary labor. ## 15 Stage gates and stopping decisions

### Gate 0 Baseline approval

Freeze the core definitions, obligation versions, candidate model conventions, formalization boundary, and effort assumptions. Before an affected proof attempt begins, append its exact quantified target, model/morphism class, comparison maps, and any allowed obstruction alternative. In particular, resolve the strict/coherent E2 universal-property convention, the I1 common-interface embedding class, the I2 allowed observation/probe family, the I3 target quotient span, and the I4 output structure. These are explicit pre-proof specification gates, not permission to choose an easy class after seeing results. No choice may reduce the minimum floors without a material amendment. This document is the proposed baseline. Approval of it is distinct from authorizing every later phase to run unattended or incur external costs.

### Gate 1 Foundational viability

Require A1–A3 and the basic parts of B1–B2, with verified nontrivial examples from C1. Decide whether the representation supports the intended multicategory and quotient universal properties. If it does not, stop the dependent claims and repair or amend the core. A compiling recursive tree datatype is not enough.

### Gate 2 Model and novelty viability

Require the principal models, nonreconstruction theorem, decidable-fragment result, and the mature baseline comparison. State what remains mathematically substantive beyond established multicategory and restriction-species machinery. If only a renaming remains, report that outcome and seek a direction decision rather than certifying the original research ambition.

### Gate 3 Extension viability

Evaluate D–F against their bounded classes. Require the positive typed finite 2-Segal model regardless of whether the broad recognition claim succeeds. Require the full E1–E3 virtual-equipment construction and universal presentation, not merely a positive specialization. E4 ordinary representability/completion and E5 faithful exact assembly may use only their specified bounded obstruction branches. Unsupported infinity-categorical generalization cannot rescue an unsuccessful finite claim.

### Gate 4 Synthesis and identification

Require semantic and observational distinctions and all I-workstream mechanisms to agree with the proved comparisons and counterexamples. Check that no reconstruction, CULF, exactness, or empirical claim has been strengthened in the exposition beyond the theorem.

### Gate 5 Independent acceptance

Run H1–H3. Completion requires the full accepted ledger, not a selectively summarized list of successes. The final report must state the actual assurance level, including whether review was agent-managed and whether optional external human review occurred. It may not imply an external certification that was not obtained.

Each gate produces a short decision record: continue under the frozen plan; repair within scope; propose an amendment; or stop with a precise negative outcome. No gate permits silently deleting a hard obligation.

## 16 Amendment and failure protocol

Every amendment receives a new version, rationale, affected obligation IDs, old and new claims, dependency impact, and a revised effort estimate. Obtain approval before materially weakening acceptance conditions, changing a target class, dropping a required comparison, or expanding authorized scope. Routine stronger proofs, equivalent formulations with unchanged scope, and in-scope implementation repairs do not require repeated user decisions. Whenever possible, fix criteria before observing the outcomes they will judge.

An unexpected counterexample may motivate an amendment after discovery, but it may not be used to relabel the original claim as passed. Preserve the original version and its FAILED or SUPERSEDED status, the counterexample, and the reason for the replacement. A replacement theorem is a new obligation with its own review. Changing from M to B, shrinking a model class, dropping a unit law, weakening faithfulness, or excluding a difficult boundary case is a material amendment.

Maintain a failure register containing false conjectures, failed proof strategies that reveal a genuine issue, counterexamples, rejected encodings, and failed negative controls. Keep a separate backlog for optional extensions and unresolved questions. Backlog items do not count as discharged obligations and do not become implicit promises of future execution.

Progress reporting must distinguish definition complete, proof drafted, formalized, tested, independently verified, and accepted. A tool log or author assertion cannot promote its own result to accepted. Preserve all negative results needed to understand why the final theory has its stated hypotheses.

## 17 Primary sources and attribution plan

The core algebraic comparison will be checked against standard nonsymmetric colored multicategory and operad theory, with exact definitions and theorem locations recorded in the final source ledger. Background results must be cited rather than claimed as discoveries of this program.

Gálvez-Carrillo, Kock, and Tonks, *Decomposition spaces and restriction species*, establishes the restriction-species route to decomposition spaces, including graph examples. The graph-cut model here is an adaptation and typed test bed, not a claim to invent that construction. Its retained whole and ordered layers are essential to the comparison. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02570.

Dyckerhoff and Kapranov, *Higher Segal spaces I*, develops 2-Segal structures and their connections with multivalued categorical composition. The finite recognition work must compare directly with its discrete theory and operadic formulations rather than treating associative substitution as sufficient. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3563.

Walde, *2-Segal spaces as invertible infinity-operads*, supplies a direct comparison with invertible nonsymmetric operads. This is a prior-art constraint on the proposed recognition theorem; the program's contribution must lie in the precise typed, presented, refinement, or model-specific analysis beyond the applicable existing result. Use the corrected version 5 or the published version, and check every imported hypothesis. Version 5 records a missing assumption added to a key lemma, so an older unqualified statement is not acceptable provenance. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.09935v5.

Shulman, *Framed bicategories and monoidal fibrations*, provides the relevant double-categorical framework for base change and framed structure. The program must choose its exact convention and prove the specialized compatibility it needs. Merely citing framed structure does not show that a particular witness is an opcartesian or exact assembly. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1286.

These sources establish the mathematical neighborhood and constrain novelty. They do not discharge the obligations above. Additional exact theorem citations, including the word-problem undecidability source and a standard free-operad reference, must be verified before the corresponding acceptance gates.
