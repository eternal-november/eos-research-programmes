I3 Objective and Numerical Incidence Descent

Author proof for the original completion contract
1 November 2026

Scope and conclusion

This document addresses original I3, not the earlier deferral. The primary
objects are the original ordered graph cut sets with the cut compatible
scalar subclass M = 1, arbitrary finite graph size, and an actual finite
interface category K. The named F3 placement equations L are used after
F4 edge erasure, and separately on F3 contiguous clique graphs. No arbitrary
commutative monoid decoration is assumed to have a cut nerve.

There are three different claims, with different answers.

1. The prescribed cut map descends the actual decomposition span when its
   canonical fixed source whole comparison is bijective, including units.
2. A whole observation admits a numerical quotient coalgebra when the
   aggregate cut counts and counit are independent of its representative.
3. That numerical quotient is the intrinsic unweighted incidence coalgebra
   of a prescribed cut target only when its coefficients also agree with
   the actual target cut counts.

For the map which forgets graph edges and vertex placements but retains
vertex count, block sizes and all actual K arrows, claim 2 holds for every
finite graph size. Claims 1 and 3 fail at every size at least two. The
numerical coefficients are binomial, while the prescribed F3 size cut
object has coefficients one. Thus the separator is not a hidden assertion
that these are the same coalgebra. A sourcewise surjective finite set cut
map cannot have equal intrinsic coefficients yet fail its prescribed
binary objective comparison; a precise no compensation theorem proves
this boundary. The nearest numerical correction is a coherent multinomial
weighting of that same target. A factorial change of basis relates this
weighted coalgebra to the intrinsic one over the rationals. The nearest
actual witness repair restores the labelled subsets, precisely the F4
intermediate target, not an unspecified set of equally many witnesses.

The proof also gives the general F3 orbit weight formula, its concrete
cluster specialization, sharp effective length bounds, a standard Möbius
comparison with an explicit zeta warning, and an unbounded edge count
observation that fails coefficient independence. All primary counts are
counts of SETS. There are no graph automorphism denominators. Finite
verification and independent review are separate artifacts, not inferred
from the author's assertions.

2 Actual carriers and the two prescribed targets

Write [v] = {0,...,v-1}. A graph G is a simple graph on this ordered vertex
set. Let K be a finite nonempty category; a grade h means an actual arrow,
not just its endpoints. A size bound N may be fixed arbitrarily, or all
finite v may be allowed. Local finiteness below holds in either case.

The original n simplices X_n are triples

  (G, (U_1,...,U_n), (h_1,...,h_n)),

where the U_i are disjoint subsets with union [v], empty blocks are allowed,
and h_i:A_(i-1) -> A_i are composable actual K arrows. The whole is
(G,h_n ... h_1). The i-th principal edge is (G|U_i,h_i), where restriction
uses increasing enumeration. X_0 is Ob(K), since a zero block partition
forces the empty graph. Its image in X_1 is (empty,id_A).

For a monotone alpha:[m] -> [n], restrict to the union V of the blocks with
alpha(0) < i <= alpha(m). Replace G by G|V, group blocks over the successive
alpha intervals, and compose the corresponding grades. An empty interval
contributes an empty block and an identity. These are the actual original
operators. Nested increasing enumerations, unions and category composition
prove all simplicial identities. The root triangle retains the entire
whole G and the actual two subsets, so recursively lifting the two
subtriangulations reconstructs every partition uniquely. This proves the
all degree unital 2 Segal property; cross edges are retained at the root,
not reconstructed from isolated factors. This is the F2 construction.

The intermediate E_n has the same records except graph adjacency is
omitted. Thus an E_n record is ([v], actual blocks, actual K path). It may
be identified with the edgeless graph subfamily. Define

  F:X -> E             by forgetting edges only.

The final prescribed target Z_n is

  (v, (s_1,...,s_n), (h_1,...,h_n)),  s_i >= 0, sum s_i = v.

Every operator groups the selected sizes and composes the same actual
arrows. Z_0 = Ob(K). Define

  L:E -> Z             by s_i = |U_i|,
  q = L F:X -> Z.

The notation L denotes the map induced by the named F3 presentation
quotient, not an informal loss of ordering. Occurrence order is retained.
In the edgeless family, the local equation identifies each actual binary
cut with the contiguous binary cut having the same left and right sizes.
Both sides have the same whole, ordered input graph types and actual
arrows. F3 proves these equations together with the original C5 equations
present exactly the size vector quotient, by canonical right combs and
coherent original occurrence substitution. The family is induced closed
and homogeneous, so F3's full erasure boundary admits it. For each finite
N and K the actual labelled signature and L rule set are finite; arity,
unit insertion and tree depth remain unbounded.

Edge erasure commutes with induced restrictions and maps each original
binary generator and each original equation to its edgeless counterpart.
Consequently q is the specified original calculus map followed by that
specific presentation quotient. F changes graph component types; it is a
type changing interpretation, NOT a parallel congruence on the fixed
original sorts. Only its second factor L is the named parallel presentation
quotient. This is an allowed coarsening of whole and witness data in I3,
not an assertion that the two different whole graphs were related by an
additional parallel C5 equation. It is also the displayed all degree
simplicial map. No quotient of a nonhomogeneous graph by full placement
erasure is asserted to be cut compatible. We first erase graph edges,
then apply full placement erasure where F3 permits it.

Z is unital 2 Segal directly: a coarse size vector and an inner vector
with the required sum glue by replacing the selected entry by its ordered
inner entries. This is inverse to grouping. Iterate at every node of a
finite tree. The full arrow path glues through the named intermediate
interfaces. Empty zero size identity entries give exactly the unit maps.
Thus both incidence objects are justified before any counts are taken.
In fact Z is the ordinary nerve of the category whose objects are Ob(K),
whose arrows are pairs (v,h), and whose composition adds sizes and composes
K arrows. Its native incidence is ordinary additive monoid/category
incidence, not a new intrinsically multivalued target. The raw graph source
and the nonhomogeneous cluster quotient below retain the distinct graph
whole phenomenon; this standard Z is deliberately the fixed coarsening
target against which lifting is tested.

3 Finite set incidence and numerical descent

For a locally finite unital discrete decomposition object X, write

  D = X_2,  t=d_1:D -> X_1,
  l=d_2:D -> X_1,  r=d_0:D -> X_1.

A cut u has whole t(u), left factor l(u), and right factor r(u). Its
incidence coalgebra over Z is the free module on whole basis elements
[x], with

  Delta_X[x] = sum_(u in t^-1(x)) [l(u)] tensor [r(u)],
  epsilon_X[x] = 1 if x=(empty,id_A), and 0 otherwise

for the present graph models. All fibres t^-1(x) are finite: there are
2^v actual binary partitions and finitely many pairs a,b with ba=h.
Unbounded v causes no infinite sum at a fixed whole. K may have loops;
these do not spoil degreewise local finiteness, though they can spoil
finite effective length and Möbius inversion later.

Coassociativity follows either from the two triangle decompositions of
X_3 or directly: both iterated coproducts enumerate actual ordered triples
of disjoint vertex blocks and composable three arrow paths with fixed
composite. The two parenthesizations use the same retained whole and
increasing restriction identities. The counit selects the unique empty
identity block on the selected side. These arguments apply to E and Z
as well. They do not count raw syntax representatives.

Let p:X_1 -> B be a surjective whole observation. Set

  c_x(b,c) = number of u with t(u)=x, p(l(u))=b, p(r(u))=c.

The numerical criterion is that c_x(b,c) and epsilon_X[x] are constant
as x ranges over each p fibre, for every b,c. This is an integer criterion,
not equality after an accidental reduction modulo a prime.

Theorem 1 Numerical quotient criterion

The criterion is necessary and sufficient for a unique coalgebra on the
free module Z[B] for which P[x]=[p(x)] is a coalgebra map. Its coefficients
are those common c values, and its counit is the common epsilon value.

Proof. If P is a coalgebra map, compare the coefficients of [b] tensor[c]
in (P tensor P)Delta_X[x] for any two representatives. The counit equality
is also necessary. Conversely define Delta_B[b] using any representative.
The criterion makes it well defined and each sum finite. The equality
Delta_B P=(P tensor P)Delta_X holds by construction. Apply both iterated
coproducts to P[x] and use coassociativity of X. Since P is onto, equality
on all such elements gives coassociativity on B. The same argument gives
both counit identities. Surjectivity also proves uniqueness. Equivalently
the subgroup spanned by differences [x]-[x'] with p(x)=p(x') is a counital
coideal. This is the familiar compatible equivalence criterion. QED.

Theorem 1 alone is not I3's substantive result. It does not identify a
prescribed target witness set or show that its actual cut map descends.
The remaining sections do so, and compute a nonvacuous whole quotient.

4 Prescribed objective comparison and its exact numerical boundary

Fix the target cut data BEFORE asking for descent: a finite fibre span

  Y_1 <-t_Y- Y_2 -(l_Y,r_Y)-> Y_1 x Y_1,

and specified maps p:X_1 -> Y_1, a:X_2 -> Y_2 commuting with t,l,r. In the
primary example these are Y=Z, p=q_1, a=q_2, literally as in section 2.
No replacement of Y_2 by an arbitrarily equicardinal set is permitted.

The canonical comparison is

  chi:X_2 -> X_1 x_(Y_1) Y_2,
  chi(u)=(t(u),a(u)).

Objective binary descent in this document means that THIS comparison is
a bijection, with its given endpoint compatibility. In other words the
source cut span with its two factor legs composed with p is the pullback
of the prescribed target cut span along the source whole map, with the
prescribed comparison, not merely some
unspecified isomorphism. Unit descent is the corresponding cartesian
square for s_0. This convention is the actual fixed source lifting notion
needed for the standard incidence comparison. It is not a denial that
other differently decorated targets could exist.

For a fixed x and y with t_Y(y)=p(x), let

  m_x(y) = |{u:t(u)=x and a(u)=y}|.

Theorem 2 Objective and intrinsic numerical comparison

(a) Objective binary descent is equivalent to m_x(y)=1 for every compatible
x,y. Unit descent must be checked separately. Objective binary and unit
descent imply that p induces the intrinsic target incidence coalgebra map.
They therefore imply numerical independence on p fibres when p is onto.

(b) Without assuming objective descent, equality with the target's
intrinsic unweighted coproduct is EXACTLY the identities

  sum_(y:t_Y(y)=p(x), l_Y(y)=b, r_Y(y)=c) m_x(y)
    = |{y:t_Y(y)=p(x), l_Y(y)=b, r_Y(y)=c}|

for every x,b,c, together with the separate counit equality.

(c) If chi is sourcewise surjective, meaning that EVERY such y has a lift
for EVERY compatible fixed x, then intrinsic numerical coproduct equality
implies chi is a bijection. The same implication holds without that
surjectivity assumption if, for each target whole, a target binary cut is
uniquely determined by its ordered pair of factors.

Proof. Part (a) is the actual fibre description, and summing one lift per
target cut gives the claimed coefficients. Part (b) partitions the finite
set of source cuts by their actual a image. For (c), sourcewise surjectivity
gives m_x(y)>=1, so equality of the finite sum to the number of summands
forces every summand to equal 1. Under factor pair uniqueness each relevant
sum has at most one term, so the same conclusion follows. Empty sums are
handled by endpoint compatibility. QED.

Global surjectivity of a does NOT replace sourcewise surjectivity. Without
these hypotheses different target cuts having the same factor pair could
have multiplicities 0 and 2 over one selected x, giving the same aggregate
2 as two singleton fibres. This abstract finite span observation does not
claim a new all degree graph example. It identifies exactly why an
unqualified converse would be false. Likewise, over positive
characteristic equal residues do not force integer counts to agree.

The boundary is explicitly about sets and unit nonnegative weights. For
finite groupoids, BC2 disjoint union BC2 has homotopy cardinality one but
is not equivalent to a point. Equality of homotopy cardinality therefore
cannot replace an equivalence proof even with essential surjectivity.
That is only a boundary control, not the principal I3 separator. Our orbit
targets are orbit SETS, not action groupoids; their stabilizers enter orbit
counts below, not homotopy cardinality denominators.

If X,Y are unital 2 Segal sets and the supplied map is simplicial, binary
and unit cartesianness also imply all active cartesianness. To see this,
first glue an n simplex along a root triangle. Its two lower degree
simplices lift uniquely by induction after the root binary lift fixes
both source factors. The 2 Segal inverse gives exactly one full lift.
Thus every long edge comparison X_n -> X_1 x_(Y_1)Y_n is bijective,
including degree zero by the unit square. For arbitrary active alpha,
a compatible coarse source a and fine target b have the same whole.
Lift b by that long edge comparison. Its alpha image and a have identical
whole and target coarse image, so injectivity of the coarse long edge
comparison makes them equal. This proves every active square, rather than
assuming binary counting alone supplies higher coherence.

5 The CULF positive map and its nonidentifiability

For F:X -> E, fix an active alpha:[m]->[n], a coarse source simplex A and
a compatible fine target simplex B. Activity retains the entire vertex
set. The unique lift is obtained by taking the whole graph from A and
the full actual blocks and arrow path from B. Compatibility says grouping
these blocks and arrows gives A; the graph is unchanged. Conversely each
of these fields is forced. This proves all active cartesian squares,
including degeneracies, zero size graphs and empty alpha intervals.

Thus F is the required CULF positive case on the actual graph family,
not a two point map. At vertex size v its whole fibre over ([v],h) has
2^(v(v-1)/2) elements, independently selectable edges. Noninjectivity at
v>=2 is compatible with CULF, because lifting fixes the source whole first.
The induced coalgebra map is

  F_*[G,h]=[v,h]_E.

This is the restriction species baseline specialized to the exact original
typed carriers, with no graph reconstruction claim.

6 The unbounded stable numerical but nonobjective family

A binary target Z cut of (v,h) is uniquely determined by k in {0,...,v}
and a composable pair a:A->B, b:B->C with ba=h. Its two factors are
(k,a) and (v-k,b). Every such cut has a lift over every source (G,h) of
size v: choose any k element subset U of [v]. The actual lift fibre is

  m_(G,h)(k,a,b) = binomial(v,k).

Different subsets are different actual original witnesses, including
when their induced graphs or their observed sizes happen to agree. This
is a literal bijection from the lifting fibre to the set of k subsets.
The graph adjacency imposes no restriction because its induced input
graphs are observed only through sizes in Z.

Consequently the whole map q_1(G,h)=(v,h) satisfies Theorem 1, with

  Delta_bin[v,h]
    = sum_(ba=h) sum_(k=0)^v binomial(v,k)
        [k,a] tensor [v-k,b],
  epsilon_bin[v,h] = 1 if v=0 and h=id_A, else 0.              (6.1)

These are genuinely representative independent counts on nontrivial
whole fibres, not independence made vacuous by retaining every graph.
For each v>=2, edgeless G and any graph with an edge have the same whole
observation; the entire fibre has 2^(v(v-1)/2) graphs, and all coefficients
in (6.1) agree. This proves the assertion for arbitrary finite size, not
only a table of examples. The quotient coalgebra C_bin is exactly C_E.

In contrast the intrinsic target Z has

  Delta_Z[v,h]
    = sum_(ba=h) sum_(k=0)^v [k,a] tensor [v-k,b].             (6.2)

For v>=2 choose k=1 and the grade factorization h=h id_A. The prescribed
chi fibre has v distinct lifts. Thus q is not objective and not CULF, and
q_1 is not a map to C_Z with its intrinsic coefficients. The coefficient
v in (6.1) is not the coefficient 1 in (6.2). Even an arbitrary endpoint
preserving bijection of these source and target cut sets cannot repair
this particular mismatch. At all sizes<=1 the fibres are singleton;
q is CULF on that induced closed subfamily. The same size criterion holds
for any induced closed all cuts family containing a graph of size>=2.

For a target n cut with sizes s_1,...,s_n, fixed source G and full typed
path, the fibre is

  v! / (s_1! ... s_n!).                                      (6.3)

More generally fix an active alpha:[m]->[n], a coarse original partition
(B_1,...,B_m), and a compatible fine target size vector s. Its fine lifts
have cardinality

  product_(j=1)^m |B_j|! /
    product_(alpha(j-1)<k<=alpha(j)) s_k!.                    (6.4)

The denominator in each j factor is over that j interval. Empty intervals
force |B_j|=0 and contribute 1. Compatibility includes the full K path:
each fine grade composite must equal the prescribed coarse arrow, and an
empty run forces an actual identity. If it fails, the fibre is empty,
regardless of the factorial expression. Given compatibility, (6.4) follows
by independently splitting each KNOWN actual set B_j into labelled subsets
of the required sizes. These independent choices are all and only the
lifts. This is an all active map formula, not a degree two extrapolation.

An arbitrary finite refinement tree gives the same multinomial (6.3):
multiply its local splitting multinomials, and the factorial of every
intermediate block cancels between its parent denominator and its own
numerator. More strongly the actual bijection is recursive restriction
and union of the named subsets. It commutes with regrouping because every
vertex receives the same final occurrence label. This gives the coherent
all tree comparison behind the numerical correction, not just a numerical
identity assigned to unrelated arities.

7 Numerical correction and actual provenance repair

Put the weight binomial(v,k) on the prescribed binary size cut. For a
higher size cut use (6.3). The cancellation just proved gives exactly the
same total weight along every binary triangulation. For example

  binomial(v,i+j) binomial(i+j,i)
    = v!/(i! j! k!)
    = binomial(v,i) binomial(j+k,j),  i+j+k=v.

Empty identity cuts have weight one. The actual K composites agree under
both parenthesizations. Thus these weights define the coassociative,
counital coalgebra C_bin in (6.1); this also follows from Theorem 1.
Weights restore the correct aggregate multiplicity but do not recover
the identities of the original subsets.

Over Q define

  T:C_bin -> C_Z,     T[v,h]_bin = v! [v,h]_Z.                (7.1)

For each term of (6.1), binomial(v,k) k! (v-k)! = v!, so
(T tensor T)Delta_bin = Delta_Z T. The counit is unchanged because
0!=1. The inverse divides each size v basis element by v!, so T is a
coalgebra isomorphism. The corrected map from actual graphs to the
intrinsic target is therefore

  [G,h] |-> v! [v,h]_Z,                                    (7.2)

not the unweighted whole observation. Formula (7.2) is a numerical
coalgebra map; it is not presented as the cardinality of the original
map q_1 or as an objective CULF map.

For a finite size bound N the same statement works over any ring in which
1!,...,N! are units. The displayed T remains an integral coalgebra map
but is not an isomorphism of free abelian groups once a size>=2 occurs.
Over F_p it kills the displayed size p basis element, so the same map
cannot be inverted. This is a ground ring limit of the correction, not
a claim that the original integer counts were representative dependent.

There is a stronger integral obstruction in the terminal K case, independent
of choosing this diagonal map. The binomial and native coalgebras are not
isomorphic as coaugmented coalgebras over Z once size two is present. Let
Dbar be reduced coproduct on the coaugmentation ideal. In either coalgebra
F1=ker Dbar is the rank one subgroup on size one, and
F2=ker((Dbar tensor id)Dbar) is the subgroup on sizes one and two. Indeed,
for a highest nonzero size d>=3, the coefficient of the triple of sizes
(1,1,d-2) is d(d-1) in the binomial case and 1 in the native case; different
total sizes cannot cancel. The analogous binary coefficient proves the
F1 statement. A coaugmented isomorphism must send e1 to plus or minus z1
and e2 to a z2+b z1 with a=plus or minus 1, since it induces isomorphisms
on F1 and F2/F1. Reduced coproducts force a=2, a contradiction.
For unbounded sizes over F_p, each e_(p^r) is primitive by the freshman
power identity, so the binomial primitive space has infinite dimension.
In the native coalgebra the coefficient of z1 tensor z_(n-1) in the reduced
coproduct of a finite sum is exactly its size n coefficient for n>=2;
its primitive space is only the span of z1. Thus no coaugmented isomorphism
exists there either. These are a precisely stated terminal grade control,
not a universal nonisomorphism claim for arbitrary enriched K.

There is also an actual, stronger repair. Replace Z_n by the specified
E_n records which remember the subset assigned to each original vertex.
Then F:X->E is CULF in every degree, as proved in section 5, and its native
incidence coefficients are already (6.1). This is the nearest repair in
the sense that exactly the forgotten placement field is restored; graph
edges remain forgotten and all K grades remain unchanged. It is not a
minimality theorem among all conceivable encodings. Conditional on a
fixed source G and prescribed size cut, any exact decoder for the actual
witness must distinguish all v!/product s_i! possibilities; a finite
record alphabet therefore needs at least that many possible values, or
ceil(log_2(v!/product s_i!)) bits in a uniform fixed length binary code.
The actual ordered vertex assignment attains the required distinctions.
No coefficient alone distinguishes those choices.

Over a complete target n record, before fixing the source graph, the
number of source records is

  2^(v(v-1)/2) v!/product s_i!.

The first factor is lost graph adjacency, the second is lost placement.
After fixing the source whole only the second remains. This separates
F4 nonidentifiability from the new failure of objective cut lifting.
Neither count includes forgotten raw refinement forests or equation paths;
those are additional B4 provenance, not implied by an incidence simplex.

8 General F3 orbit quotients and the cluster correction

This section applies the same comparison to the full F3 R/E permutation
class, not just the final edgeless target. A graph G has a specified group
H_G acting on actual vertex placements. R says induced restrictions remain
literally the same ordered graph type and their rank permutations belong
to the corresponding H. E says every allowed local group action extends
by identity outside its selected subset. F3 proves R and E are equivalent
to the original quotient substitution and canonical unique interval cuts
in this declared orbit class. Its actual local L equations present it.

Let A be a target orbit of an actual ordered partition pi=(U_i), with
whole G and full grades retained. The binary fixed whole lift fibre is
exactly its orbit of actual partitions. R and E imply

  Stab_(H_G)(pi) is isomorphic to product_i H_(G|U_i).

Indeed a stabilizing global permutation restricts to one allowed group
element on each U_i by R; these restrictions determine it uniquely.
Conversely E extends each independently chosen local element by identity;
the extensions have disjoint supports, commute, and their product
stabilizes pi with exactly the chosen restrictions. Therefore

  |A| = |H_G| / product_i |H_(G|U_i)|.                       (8.1)

This is a count of an orbit SET. No quotient groupoid or division by an
automorphism group in homotopy cardinality has been introduced. The
ratio is an integer because it counts that orbit. It applies to arbitrary
finite arity, including empty blocks, since H_empty is the trivial group.
The earlier all active formula can equivalently be obtained by applying
(8.1) to every fixed coarse source block.

Objective descent of the raw partition map into the orbit cut target
holds for all cuts exactly when all H_G are trivial. If H_G is nontrivial,
the singleton partition has trivial stabilizer, so its orbit has more
than one point. The all active comparison fails at that simplex. Moreover
some binary subset is moved by any nonidentity permutation, so a binary
lift fibre already fails. Conversely trivial groups make the map an
isomorphism. Sourcewise lifting is always surjective: every orbit has
representatives, and the fixed whole is retained. Thus Theorem 2 also
shows exact equality with the intrinsic unweighted target coefficients
is possible for the raw whole basis map only in that trivial case.

More generally, for a map between two F3 orbit quotients with same graph
family and groups H_G <= J_G, every target J orbit is partitioned into
source H orbits. Its objective binary fibres count those H orbits, not
merely |J_G|/|H_G| in general because stabilizers can vary. Sourcewise
surjectivity still holds. Equality of intrinsic coefficients forces every
such binary fibre singleton. Units are unchanged, so section 4 then gives
all active comparisons. If all active comparisons hold, apply the singleton
partition, whose group action is free: it forces H_G=J_G. Conversely equal
groups give the identity quotient. This is a sharp lifting boundary within
the actual named F3 parameter map class, not a classification of all
possible witness congruences.

Set w(G)=|H_G|. The raw partition coalgebra and intrinsic orbit coalgebra
have the SAME whole basis but different decomposition coefficients. For
every target orbit A with inputs G_i, its contribution to the raw
coproduct is w(G)/product_i w(G_i) times its native contribution. Hence

  T_H[G,h]_raw = w(G) [G,h]_orbit                           (8.2)

is a coalgebra map; it is an isomorphism over Q. Its counit is preserved
because w(empty)=1. The proof is exactly the termwise cancellation in
(8.1), including when different target orbits share the same input graph
pair: sum the equal termwise identities, rather than assuming that pair
uniquely determines the orbit. All tree weights telescope, since an
intermediate graph contributes the same w as parent denominator and child
numerator. This is an explicit coefficient correction theorem on every
F3 R/E family, not merely on one homogeneous example.

For the substantive nonhomogeneous positive class

  G = K_(r_1) disjoint union ... disjoint union K_(r_b),

with contiguous clique blocks and H_G=product_b Sym(r_b), F3 target cuts
are occupancy matrices a_(b,i) with row sums r_b. In this case

  w(G)=product_b r_b!,
  |orbit A|=product_b r_b! / product_(b,i) a_(b,i)! .          (8.3)

The i-th induced input graph consists of its nonzero column entries as
ordered cliques. Deleting zero rows does not merge distinct cliques, so
w(G_i)=product_b a_(b,i)!, including 0!=1. Thus (8.2) is justified on the
actual original input graph types, not on artificial persistent colours.
For a fixed coarse original cut with occupancies c_(b,j), an active target
refinement has lift count

  product_(b,j) c_(b,j)! /
    product_(alpha(j-1)<i<=alpha(j)) a_(b,i)! .              (8.4)

This is F3's exact active formula, here used for incidence transfer and
provenance. It is CULF precisely when every clique has size at most one.
Unlike full placement erasure on a nonhomogeneous graph, this occupancy
quotient preserves enough row information to be a valid F3 cut object.
The weight corrects the multiplicity lost when that valid object is used
as a target of original cuts; intrinsic target cut uniqueness does not
supply unique original lifts.

9 Effective length and nullary or unary phenomena

A principal edge is degenerate exactly when its graph is empty AND its
actual grade is an identity. Call an n simplex effective when none of its
principal edges is degenerate. Let ell_K(h) be the supremum of the number
of nonidentity factors in a factorization of h; the empty factorization
of id_A has length zero. Identity factors may be deleted when computing
this number. It can be infinite even for a finite category.

Theorem 3 Sharp length

For every graph G on v vertices and grade h, the maximal effective cut
length, with infinity allowed, is

  length_X(G,h) = length_E(v,h) = length_Z(v,h)
                = v + ell_K(h).                           (9.1)

The same equality holds in each F3 orbit quotient on its retained G.

Proof. In an effective cut, at most v blocks are nonempty. Every remaining
block must carry a nonidentity grade. Deleting identity grades from the
full composable path gives a factorization of h, so at most ell_K(h)
blocks can be counted by nonidentity grade. Thus n<=v+ell_K(h); a block
having both properties only improves this bound. Conversely take a
factorization of h into r nonidentity arrows. Use v singleton graph
blocks with identity arrows at the initial interface, followed by r empty
graph blocks carrying those nonidentity arrows. Every block is effective,
the whole and composite grade are the required ones, and the length is
v+r. This works for arbitrary graphs, since a singleton partition imposes
no prohibition on retained cross edges. Taking maximal r, or arbitrarily
large r, proves equality. Passing to the allowed quotient does not change
principal edge sizes or grades, so it preserves effectivity and the same
construction works. QED.

For K=[d], h:i->j has ell_K(h)=j-i, so the exact bound is v+j-i. The
construction uses v singleton identity blocks followed by j-i empty
successive nonidentity grade blocks. Already K=[2] distinguishes an
empty nonidentity component from a unit; graph size alone is not a valid
bound. For terminal K the bound is exactly v.

A nullary original operation exists only at (empty,id_A). Inserting or
removing it at a specified boundary gives a zero size identity column,
which is degenerate; it does not increase effective length. Repeating
such insertions yields unbounded raw arity and simplicial degree but not
unbounded effective length. Similarly an arbitrary string of raw d_1
unary identity decorations or unit/rotation syntax in the M=1 presentation
is equated by the original equations. Counting those raw representatives
would not be the incidence set X and would destroy the finiteness premise.
No claim of bounded raw tree size follows from (9.1).

For the finite category with one object and group C2={1,g}, the empty
whole with grade 1 has effective cuts of length 2r for every r: all blocks
are empty and all grades g, whose even composite is 1. These are genuine
nonidentity grades, not scalar witness decorations reintroduced into M.
Thus degreewise finite levels and finite binary cut fibres do NOT imply
finite length or permit the usual finite alternating Möbius sum. The
obstruction is an actual admissible grade category, not hypothetical
infinite syntax.

10 A precise Möbius consequence and its normalization limit

The transfer requirement of I3 is already met by sections 7 and 8. For
completeness this section states what the familiar Möbius functions do
under that correction, under the necessary EXTRA finite length condition.
Assume ell_K(h)<infinity for every h. The incidence convolution of
integer valued functions is locally finite. Write zeta=1 on every whole,
eta=zeta-epsilon. On a fixed whole, eta^*r counts effective r cuts, by
coassociativity and the principal edge definition. By (9.1) it vanishes
above the stated bound. Hence the finite geometric sum

  mu = sum_(r>=0) (-1)^r eta^*r

is a two sided convolution inverse of zeta at each whole. This proves
existence directly; it is not inferred from mere degreewise finiteness.
Let mu_K be the analogous grade incidence inverse.

The source after edge erasure has binomial convolution in the size
coordinate and grade convolution in K. Separating these coordinates gives

  mu_bin(v,h) = (-1)^v mu_K(h),
  mu_X(G,h)   = (-1)^v mu_K(h).                             (10.1)

Indeed sum_k binomial(v,k)(-1)^k is delta_(v,0), and the grade convolution
is the defining inverse identity for mu_K. The X identity follows by the
actual CULF coalgebra map F and its zeta preserving pullback, or directly
by the same counts.

For the native size cut target, ordinary size convolution instead gives

  mu_Z(v,h) = (delta_(v,0)-delta_(v,1)) mu_K(h).              (10.2)

The finite sum of delta_(k,0)-delta_(k,1) for 0<=k<=v equals delta_(v,0),
proving the size inverse, and again grade convolution factors. These two
standard Möbius functions are NOT identified by factorial normalization.
Although T in (7.1) is a coalgebra isomorphism over Q,

  T^*(zeta_Z)(v,h)=v!,

not zeta_bin(v,h)=1. Conversely, transporting the original standard zeta
onto C_Z through T inverse gives the functional 1/v!; its inverse is
(-1)^v mu_K(h)/v!. The inverse of T^*(zeta_Z) is instead
v! (delta_(v,0)-delta_(v,1)) mu_K(h), the pullback of (10.2).
Thus it is correct to transfer convolution inverses together with their
functionals, but false to claim that T automatically preserves the
standard zeta or its standard Möbius inverse. For v=2 and identity grade,
(10.1) is 1 whereas (10.2) is 0, an immediate separating calculation.

11 An unbounded quotient that fails coefficient independence

Let p_bad(G,h)=(v,e(G),h), retaining vertex count, total edge count and the
actual grade. This is an explicitly specified whole observation. For
each v>=4 let S_v be the ordered graph consisting of a three leaf star
on the first four vertices plus v-4 isolated vertices, and let P_v be a
four vertex path on the first four vertices plus the same isolates.
Both have whole observation (v,3,h), for any fixed actual arrow h.

Choose the factorization h=h id_A. Consider the coefficient of the
ordered observed input pair

  (1,0,id_A) tensor (v-1,0,h).

For S_v exactly one singleton left block leaves an edgeless complement:
the central star vertex. For P_v none does, since no path vertex has
degree three and an isolated vertex removes no edge. Thus the coefficients
are 1 and 0 for two representatives of the same whole observation.
Theorem 1 proves that p_bad admits no representative independent numerical
coalgebra quotient on this whole basis, even before any objective target
is proposed. In particular retaining more apparent graph statistics than
vertex count need not preserve a valid numerical quotient.

If a candidate simplicial observation is desired, retain for every
interval in an n cut the vertex and edge counts of its induced graph,
together with the actual full K path. Its degreewise image is a defined
simplicial set: operators only select or repeat these interval records.
Its degree one observation is p_bad. No 2 Segal property is asserted for
that image, and none is needed for the obstruction: the displayed binary
representative dependence already rules out the claimed quotient
coalgebra with its prescribed whole map. No undefined coarse coproduct
has been treated as an incidence coalgebra.

12 What has and has not descended

The actual source whole graph is retained by the source incidence span.
F4 forgets it only in the target whole, but has a unique target cut lift
when the source whole is supplied. F3 placement L then forgets different
data: the original vertex assignment to each named occurrence. The target
still has coherent intrinsic cuts, but the specified map no longer has
unique original lifts. These are independent statements.

Aggregate counts forget the identities of those placements, but remain
constant on all graph whole fibres. They are useful for numerical
reasoning and give the exact weighted quotient. They are not enough to
transport a chosen occurrence forest or the individual exact coefficient
certificates used in I4/E5. Formula (6.3) counts the missing placement
alternatives and section 7 restores them with actual records. The scalar
M=1 assumption is the F3 proved cut compatible subclass; arbitrary old scalar
monoid factorizations are not claimed to be uniquely cut, and the F4
non 2 Segal duplicated triangle control is not used as an incidence source.

Finite graph size bounds are parameters, not a truncation of arity or
simplicial degree. Every theorem quantifies over arbitrary finite sizes
and all finite cut trees. Executable checks can corroborate selected
instances, test implementations and catch convention errors; they do
not prove the all parameter statements. Historical original documents
and acceptance ledgers remain unmodified.
