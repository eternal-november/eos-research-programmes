## 1. What “mark preservation” can mean

In E3 §4, a full globular cell of S(N,M) is `(π,φ,m)`. Its independently computed marks are:

* literal normalized coefficient exactness: `Ex_coeff^1 ⇔ φ is bijective and m=1`;
* convenient enlarged coefficient mark: `Ex_coeff^× ⇔ φ is bijective and m∈M×` (this name is notation here, not a claim E3 used it);
* intrinsic opcartesianness: `Ex_op ⇔ s(p)≤1, φ is bijective, m∈M×`, E3 §7, Corollary 7.2;
* normalized opcartesianness additionally requires `m=1`.

The graph-count condition is not optional: a bijective coefficient can accompany a nonuniversal cross-edge graph assembly.

For a quotient q, keep separate:

* **image/existential marking:** `[α]` is marked if *some* actual representative is marked;
* **representative-invariant descent/reflection:** `α~β ⇒ (mark(α)⇔mark(β))`;
* **intrinsic quotient universality:** q(α) satisfies its contextual universal property in the quotient;
* **certified marking:** retain an actual marked representative or actual witness data as extra structure;
* **faithfulness:** distinct prescribed actual certificates/morphism tuples are not identified.

The first is automatic as a definition and proves none of the others. A quotient can preserve opcartesianness while enlarging its intrinsic marked class. Fixed-endpoint lifting of quotient refinements also proves existence, not uniqueness or faithfulness of the supplied tuples.

## 2. General relation-level universal-cell criterion (already proved)

E2 §6.1 gives the exact criterion for a designated nonnullary contextual opcartesian cell `e:q⇒H`: for every surrounding path context `(r,-,s)` and correctly typed pair a,b,

    a[1_r,e,1_s] ~ b[1_r,e,1_s]  implies  a~b.

Equivalently, the inverse source-compression operation `Q_(r,e,s)` respects the congruence. Surjectivity is inherited from the prequotient factorization; this implication is exactly injectivity on quotient fibres.

The corresponding generator/relation test is not “check only bare generating equations.” For every correctly typed declared equation instance inside an ordinary one-hole substitution context, apply the relevant inverse factor and require a congruence derivation of the resulting equation. Every finite congruence derivation is a zigzag of such contextual steps, so this test is sufficient and necessary. No general finite termination/decidability claim follows.

For cartesian restrictions and contextual units, E2 §6.2, Theorem 6.1 proves each inverse factor is itself an ordinary one-hole substitution context in a **complete** VE; therefore every fixed-sort substitution congruence already passes that test. E2 §6.3 explains the different preequipment/raw-presentation saturation setting and its equality cost. These are different input levels, not competing theorems.

## 3. Scalar quotients: actual generators, intrinsic marks, exact limitation

### 3.1 Structural kernel test and positive H family

KERNEL §3, Lemma 2: a graph-indexed scalar relation retaining each full profile and partition is a substitution congruence iff every R_G is a monoid congruence and

    R_(G|U) ⊆ R_G

for each actual induced subgraph. The necessity is already witnessed by original binary cut generators; sufficiency follows from multiplication of vertex labels under substitution. The finite N, finite M case is independently checkable from finite graph-incidence and monoid tables.

KERNEL §3, Theorem 3: **within this stated scalar family**, all fixed-actual-endpoint refinement lifting holds iff

    R_empty is equality;
    m R_G n ⇔ n=mh for some h∈H≤M×,  G nonempty.

KERNEL §4 gives the original local equations `D(W,h)=id_W` and derives their exact kernel from the actual original seven equations. KERNEL §5 extends the semantic relation to all finite-span horizontals, retaining actual φ. The generator statement at this larger level uses D on **every concrete nonempty horizontal**, not merely old original graph-span horizontals.

BRIDGE §2, Theorem 1 proves the two presentation-order comparisons for free virtual/ordinary completion, including interpretation categories. BRIDGE §§3–4, Lemma 2 and Theorem 3 prove exact old-kernel reflection by the word/span separator, after all interface restrictions and universal factor constructors. It does not give a complete kernel on all new free-envelope cells. E2 §7.2 records both levels explicitly.

### 3.2 Why unit marks survive Q_H

For any surjective quotient μ of a commutative monoid,

    μ reflects units ⇔ μ⁻¹(1)⊆M×.

Necessity is immediate. Conversely, if μ(m) is invertible, represent its inverse by μ(n). Then mn lies in μ⁻¹(1), hence is a unit. In a commutative monoid this makes m a unit: if (mn)t=1, then m(nt)=1. Finiteness is not needed for this short argument.

For H-orbits, μ⁻¹(1)=H at nonempty graph and `{1}` at empty graph. Thus `[m]` is a unit iff m is. Actual φ, graph partition and source graph count are untouched. E3 §7's canonical-composite factorization therefore yields exactly the same intrinsic `Ex_op` classification after this quotient. The `Ex_coeff^×` predicate is also invariant. Neither failure of an actual coefficient comparison nor graph nonreconstruction is repaired.

For literal `Ex_coeff^1`, however, take a nonempty graph, any identity span coefficient, and `h≠1` in H. The scalar-1 and scalar-h cells are equal in Q_H, but exactly one has scalar 1. Thus the literal mark is not representative-invariant. Its existential image has condition `φ bijective and m∈H` at positive grade, not `m=1` on a supplied representative. Both actual unit scalars are invertible, so this example does not contradict intrinsic opcartesian preservation.

### 3.3 Faithful refinement data are not supplied by FL alone

KERNEL's FL allows adjustment of a child representative within its quotient class to hit the fixed target literally. It does not assert the quotient functor is faithful. With two nonempty child graphs and nontrivial h∈H, the two actual refinement endomorphism tuples `(1,1)` and `(h,h⁻¹)` of an outer scalar-1 cut are distinct, evaluate to the same actual root scalar, and have the same quotient tuple. Complete profiles and occurrence addresses stay fixed.

CUT §§1–3 fixes an actual flattened root w and normalized quotient cut tree T. If r is the number of positive nonroot internal nodes, its actual lifts are exactly H^r: choose h_v and set the root label to `m(∏h_v)⁻¹`. Actual original invertible D(g) comparisons are retained as gauges; the nodewise equations force a unique gauge between any two lifts. The groupoid is contractible, while the set of actual lifts has |H|^r elements when finite.

If the outermost factor's actual scalar is additionally frozen to m, the allowed choices satisfy `∏h_v∈Stab_H(m)`, **not** necessarily `∏h_v=1` (CUT §2). Collapsing lift objects while keeping the H^r gauge tuples produces isotropy, not the same contractible repair (CUT §3).

CUT §4 proves contractions, normalized expansions, rotations, and **admissible normalized-quotient grafting** coherently. Arbitrary factorizations are not admitted: in additive C4 with H={0,2}, the identity can factor as 1+3 outside H. CUT §6 explicitly does not claim this scalar repair makes coefficient transport exact.

### 3.4 Literal normalized marks versus universal FL

A scalar relation retaining φ makes the literal scalar-one marking invariant exactly when `[1]_(R_G)={1}` on every relevant graph; the canonical identity coefficient tests necessity. KERNEL's FL classification gives `[1]_(R_G)=H` for every nonempty graph. Hence, for N≥1, universal FL plus literal normalization invariance forces H={1} and therefore equality throughout this scalar family.

If only literal marked-cell preservation is needed, there is a genuinely nontrivial example. Set M={1,a,0}, with 1 the unit, 0 absorbing, and a²=0. At nonempty graphs use classes `{1}`, `{a,0}`; at the empty graph retain equality. Multiplication compatibility and induced inclusion are immediate finite table tests, so KERNEL Lemma 2 supplies a congruence. Keep actual coefficients. The scalar-one and unit predicates are identical and invariant; the quotient is injective on actual scalar-one coefficient-exact cells. Thus both normalized coefficient and intrinsic opcartesian marks survive and reflect.

But FL fails: let an actual unary outer have positive graph and scalar a, use its identity child, and specify an otherwise equal target of scalar 0. The quotient equation holds. Every child in the identity's quotient class has scalar 1, so the actual composite is still a, never 0. This isolates the exact strengthening which fails. This example should not be mislabeled universally faithful refinement recovery.

## 4. Activated coefficient equations: generation and what precisely breaks

ACT §2, Theorem 2.1 and E2 §7.1: choose graphs I satisfying

    G|U∈I ⇒ G∈I.

For every active graph G and finite span S with apex E, add the single schema

    s_0=s_1 : (G,S)→(G,S[2]),    s_i(e)=(e,i).

Then the generated congruence retains π and m, and forgets φ exactly at active output graphs. The finite graph test is necessary for this all-or-nothing relation, using two selectors at an active child read by a binary outer cut of an inactive whole. Sufficiency follows because any active child forces its retained parent whole active.

Generation is constructive: for parallel φ,ψ on common source tuple span P, substitute the canonical coefficient-id cell into the doubling equation at P and postcompose `c(z,0)=φ(z), c(z,1)=ψ(z)`. These are finite span-map calculations, not an enumeration of all semantic equalities. E2 §7.2 proves independence when combining this relation with the H scalar quotient.

ACT §3 preserves every original full-profile cell because its coefficient was already forced. This says nothing about full coefficient-faithfulness on new span profiles.

### 4.1 The smallest marking failure

At any active graph, singleton endpoints and E={0,1}, the coefficient maps `id_E` and `const_0:E→E`, both scalar 1, become equal. The former is bijective and the latter is not. This refutes representative-invariance and reflection of actual coefficient exactness, despite preserved restrictions, units, original profiles, and preserved scalar normalization.

The existential mark at an active full profile asks whether an endpoint-preserving bijection between P(p) and E exists, equivalently equality of the cardinalities of every endpoint fibre. This is stronger than support equality and weaker than retaining a supplied actual bijection.

### 4.2 Intrinsic quotient opcartesianness is different again

ACT §4, Proposition 4.1: a scalar-one unary cell at an active graph is quotient-invertible iff its source and target spans have the same endpoint support. At an inactive graph its actual coefficient must be bijective. With scalar quotient Q_H as well, additionally require m a unit, by §3.2 above.

For `s(p)≤1`, E3 §7's canonical composite cell remains contextual opcartesian in the concrete Q_(I,H): graph-block compression/expansion is bijective; tuple regrouping preserves actual coefficient equality at inactive output, while at active output it preserves existence of the unique coefficient class; scalar orbit classes are unchanged. This is an explicit check of E2's `Q_e` criterion, not an application of automatic `L/U` stability.

Thus compare an arbitrary α with this canonical composite and apply the unary criterion. At active graph the intrinsic condition is support equality between the flat source tuple span and output span, not actual φ bijectivity. At inactive graph the usual bijective condition remains. The graph obstruction `s(p)≥2` is unchanged: distinct cross-edge wholes still receive cells, but no fixed horizontal graph can admit unary cells to both, E3 Theorem 7.1. Quotients create no new cell in an empty fixed-profile fibre.

A map from a two-witness span to a one-witness span over singleton endpoints is therefore a quotient isomorphism at an active graph, even though its class has **no bijective representative**. An active endomap `const_0:2→2` has a bijective representative in its class but is not itself bijective. These two examples separate all three notions.

## 5. Actual refinement certificates and retained symmetry

ACT §1 explicitly retains PREQUOTIENT actual endpoint-preserving span bijections as morphisms of its cut doctrine. The intrinsic scalar-one automorphism groups at active graphs in Q_I itself are trivial. These must never be substituted for the retained automorphism groups in the theorem below.

ACT §5 fixes all actual profiles, graph blocks, occurrence positions, root quotient cell, and a planar cut tree T. Inner scalar labels are 1. C_T retains a literal bijection at inactive nodes but only an existentially bijective quotient class at active nodes. Its morphisms retain actual intermediate span bijections; the node equations are literal only where the coefficient is retained. D_T additionally retains an actual bijective β_v at every active nonroot node and imposes literal node equations everywhere below the root.

ACT §6, Theorem 6.1 proves:

    Aut_C_T(x) = ∏_(v active nonroot) ∏_(a,b) Sym(E_v(a,b)).

A contraction deletes exactly its active-node factor. Therefore that contraction is an equivalence iff the contracted node is inactive or its span is endpoint-thin. C_T is connected and nonempty, not generally contractible. D_T is nonempty and contractible, with the forced actual comparison

    h_v = β'_v P(h_children) β_v⁻¹.

Composition of actual β maps supplies contractions; canonical tuple spans supply coherent expansions, units and rebracketings. The root retains the intentionally forgotten quotient coefficient, not an unclaimed literal original root map.

ACT Corollary 6.2 gives the sharp **fixed-profile** unmarked domain: every realizable active nonroot run's flat span must be endpoint-thin, including singleton runs, occurrence-positioned empty runs, and the entire frontier if unary insertions are allowed. This is not automatic stability after arbitrary grafting; newly realizable runs must be checked. D_T does not need this thinness restriction.

ACT §7 gives the exact finite equivariant-marking bound, not an absolute storage bound: if the repair retains all those actual morphisms and only equips objects with equivariant finite marks, contractibility requires a free transitive action, hence exactly

    ∏_(v active nonroot) ∏_(a,b) |E_v(a,b)|!

marks over x. Actual bijection certificates attain it. Deleting/identifying morphisms, taking π₀, or selecting a representative while discarding its transports changes the preservation doctrine and is not ruled out by this bound.

ACT §8's unbounded fixed-graph family has naive isotropy `(S_n)^(d−1)` in a left comb but trivial isotropy when empty slots are combined first. Its cross-edge variant exposes one active proper node in one bracketing and none in another. These are actual all-tree failures rather than a one-map toy example.

## 6. Quotient does not repair internal-interface coefficient failure

ACT §4, condition (7) computes the active-root quotient comparison: it is invertible when every externally supported target endpoint pair supports at least one lifted assignment. This can hold while individual target assignments fail to lift, because a reverse map may choose a different assignment at the same endpoint pair. A root support-isomorphism also says nothing about an inactive proper node of the tree.

Actual β certificates require actual bijections. They cannot be transported by every ambient active quotient isomorphism: composing a retained identity bijection on a two-witness span with the active quotient-isomorphism `2→1` produces a nonbijective actual map. The repair's morphisms are actual span bijections, not arbitrary quotient isomorphisms. Both ACT §10 and CUT §6 explicitly maintain this boundary.

* If a nontrivial quotient literally preserving normalized marks is useful as a separate regression, use the finite square-zero scalar example above and explicitly report the FL failure.
* Do not infer new general free-envelope kernels, representability, or arbitrary interface-exact transport from these quotient statements. Retain the nonexact cells in the ambient equipment throughout.
