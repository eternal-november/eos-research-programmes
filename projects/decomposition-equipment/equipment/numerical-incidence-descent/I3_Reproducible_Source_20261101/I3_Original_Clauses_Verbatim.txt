### I3 Objective and numerical incidence quotients

**Class and claim:** B. Characterize when a presented/coarsened cut system descends its actual decomposition span, and distinguish this from representative-independent aggregated numerical decomposition coefficients.

**Inputs:** F2–F4, a specified quotient of whole and witness data, and precise finiteness hypotheses. Begin with the typed graph-cut family and a decoration subclass or explicit repair proved cut-compatible by F3, together with their named presentation quotients; use arbitrary finite size rather than one table. Specify the target cut-span data before asserting descent.

**Artifact:** Separate structural criteria for objective descent and numerical coalgebra descent; a theorem comparing them; an unbounded family showing a genuine distinction; and a transfer or correction result for decomposition coefficients, length bounds, or Möbius inversion. Any cardinality calculation must disclose whether sets or groupoids and automorphism weights are being used.

**Discharge:** P, worked formulas on the parameterized family, and C for finite instances. Prove the hypotheses that justify the incidence construction and the chosen transfer result. If a proposed distinction cannot occur in the selected class, prove that boundary and exhibit the nearest precisely specified extension where it does, rather than asserting a difference abstractly.

**Tests:** A CULF positive case; a quotient with stable aggregated coefficients but inadequate objective lifting in the prescribed target; nullary/unary phenomena affecting length; and a quotient that fails coefficient independence.

**Dependencies:** C5, F2, F3, F4, G1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The known fact that CULF maps induce coalgebra maps, a generic semiring evaluation, or cardinality arithmetic without descent/coherence does not pass. Compare with restriction-species incidence, operadic incidence, and tracelet quotient constructions. The quotient criterion must apply to the program's witness presentation and report lost provenance.
