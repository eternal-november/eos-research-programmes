### D2 Noninvertible transport and exactness criterion

**Class and claim:** B. Identify the additional hypotheses needed for transport along noninvertible interface maps to commute with composition or decomposition.

**Inputs:** C2 and a declared class of maps of finite interface sets. Begin with relational restrictions along maps $f:A'\to A$, $g:B'\to B$, and $h:C'\to C$.

**Artifact:** A comparison between composing the restricted relations and restricting their composite; a sharp equality criterion for a meaningful map class; and a generalization or limitation theorem in the chosen categorical setting. For relations, analyze the role of surjectivity of the intermediate map, including the degenerate empty-interface exceptions rather than hiding them.

**Discharge:** A proved criterion and nontrivial positive family, or a bounded obstruction plus a repaired transport construction or sharp impossibility theorem. The report must distinguish a comparison map, an inclusion, an isomorphism, and an equality.

**Tests:** The basic inclusion can be strict when a needed intermediate witness lies outside the image of $g$. Include a positive surjective case, a non-surjective failure, and boundary cases where emptiness makes a naïve converse false.

**Dependencies:** C2, D1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** A criterion must cover a parameterized map class, handle empty-interface exceptions, and prove arbitrary-tree compatibility where claimed. The elementary non-surjectivity counterexample alone, or assuming comparison invertibility as the only criterion, does not pass.

### D3 Descent and change of interface presentation

**Class and claim:** B. Determine when passing between equivalent presentations of interfaces preserves the presented witness calculus and its refinement theory.

**Inputs:** D1–D2, invariant equations, and a bounded class of finite interface groupoids or selected equivalences.

**Artifact:** A descent or invariance theorem with explicit comparison maps and coherence, or a classified failure identifying the information lost by quotienting interface symmetries. State separately the set-valued and groupoid-valued conclusions.

**Discharge:** A useful theorem applicable to at least one principal model, or a precise obstruction plus an adequate retained-symmetry repair. A groupoid-valued extension need not be formalized in Lean, but its proof must not be replaced by an appeal to unspecified higher coherence.

**Tests:** Nontrivial automorphisms, stabilizers of repeated components, and a case where naïvely taking isomorphism classes destroys a required lifting or uniqueness property.

**Dependencies:** B3, C1, C2, D1, D2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Produce a concrete descent/invariance theorem and retained-symmetry repair applied to the presented witness family. Declaring two structures equivalent because they preserve everything by definition, or citing generic descent without checking the witness data, does not pass.

4  D2 Relational exactness and every empty case

Relations compose left to right. Write

  R'=(f,g)*R,  S'=(g,h)*S,
  W(a',c')={b | R(f(a'),b) and S(b,h(c'))}.

Here (f,g)* means inverse image along the endpoint map, not a choice of
witness. There is always the inclusion

  R';S'  subset  (f,h)*(R;S).

A restricted realization b' maps to g(b'). For fixed relations, equality
holds exactly when every nonempty W(a',c') meets im(g). One available
realization can therefore establish equality even when another old
realization has no lift. Equality forgets which realization supplied it.

For every R,S simultaneously, the exact condition is

  A'=empty OR C'=empty OR g is surjective.

For necessity outside the empty-exterior cases, choose a missing b outside
im(g), one point at each primed exterior, and singleton relations supported
only through that b. The old composite has the chosen exterior pair but
the composite of restrictions does not. If B is empty, the existing map
g forces B' empty and is already surjective; no extra exception is needed.

For a positive path R_1;...;R_n with maps f_i:X'_i->X_i, retain the entire
old internal realization tuple. Exactness for that path means every
nonempty feasible tuple set has at least one member in the product of the
internal map images. At n=1 equality always holds. For n>=2, equality for
all relation choices holds exactly when

  X'_0 is empty, or X'_n is empty, or some OLD internal X_i is empty,
  or every internal f_i is surjective.

The old-empty-internal exception matters: a different empty old interface
can make all paths impossible even when another internal map is not onto.
All maps are existing total functions; impossible functions into an empty
codomain are not silently included as inputs.

5  D2 Nullaries global actions and tree compatibility

A nullary has one boundary occurrence, not two unrelated endpoint charts.
Its comparison is

  diagonal(X') subset (f,f)*diagonal(X)=kernel(f),

which is equality exactly when f is injective, including an empty domain.
A surjective two-to-one map preserves all binary relational composites but
fails this nullary comparison. A proper subset inclusion can pass the
nullary test despite not being surjective. Unary operation identities are
not nullary interface units.

On C2's complete relation quiver, an entire family of carrier maps preserves
all exact positive-arity operations iff every primed carrier is empty or
all maps are onto. It preserves all exact operations INCLUDING nullaries
iff every primed carrier is empty or all maps are bijective. If any primed
carrier is inhabited, binary sandwich paths through every other interface
force surjectivity, and the nullaries then force injectivity. This is a
sharp limitation of ordinary restriction in this specified complete model,
not a prohibition on every alternative transport doctrine.

Noninvertible positive families remain: arbitrary internal surjections
preserve positive relational composition; image-supported subset changes
can be exact on their declared support; arbitrary maps preserve the
containment calculus. The domain and permitted units must be specified.

For arbitrary finite incidence-compatible ordered trees, each child whole
must remain its parent's actual corresponding input, and shared incidences
use the same chart. Exact transport tests every named actual node; all
regroupings of a retained path require every relevant contiguous run and
every permitted nullary position. Root-only tests and adjacent primitive
pair tests are insufficient. A length-three control has both adjacent
comparisons vacuously exact but a full old path with no lifted realization.

Containment transport and the justified exact transport commute with
substitution, contraction and successive changes. The proof follows the
same named coordinates, cumulative occurrence offsets and empty blocks on
both routes. It is an all-finite-tree P proof, not extrapolation from the
five-leaf tests.

6  D2 Actual span witnesses are a stronger level

For actual finite spans S and T, the comparison forgets the lifted seam:

  (a',s,b',t,c') |-> (a',s,t,c').

Over a feasible old pair whose middle value is b, its fibre is g^(-1)(b).
For a positive path, the fibre is the product of its internal lift sets.
It is onto iff every such fibre is nonempty, injective iff every fibre has
at most one element, and bijective iff every fibre has exactly one element.
Empty target apexes satisfy these statements vacuously. For nullaries the
map is X'->X' x_X X', x'|->(x',x'), again bijective exactly for injective f.

Uniform binary span bijectivity for all spans requires empty primed
exterior or bijective g, not merely surjective g. At longer positive paths,
the old-empty-internal exception remains; outside all stated empty
exceptions, every internal map must be bijective. On a particular supported
slice a nonbijective global map
can still have exactly one lift of every relevant old witness.

For example, a constant map from two points to a chosen point in a
two-point middle set can give comparison fibre sizes [2,0]. Source and
target apexes then have equal cardinality and relational equality holds,
but the specified span comparison is neither injective nor onto.
A span isomorphism is a bijective specified comparison, not literal
equality of the chosen apex presentations.

CUT/I4 retain actual child spans and actual proper certificate bijections
at every named node. Transport first restricts an endpoint-preserving
coefficient map at its existing domain, then separately precomposes with
the comparison from individually restricted children. Those two maps must
not be conflated. Root support does not repair a failed retained inner
certificate. The package reuses this stronger theorem at its stated scope.

7  D3 Retained presentation change and explicit coherence

The declared general class is finite groupoids G,H, a fully faithful and
essentially surjective functor e:G->H, and strict primitive-level D1 data
with invariant equations. No action on the completed quotient or its
refinement category is assumed. Empty groupoids are included; the finite
input size does not bound arity, tree depth or refinement length.

D1 constructs the presented calculi P_x and their strict transports.
Let R_x be the disjoint union over actual wholes W of Ref(P_x,W). An arrow
in this refinement category is the full tuple (u_i), one actual operation
at each original slot, with a[u_i]=b. Equality is entrywise, not equality
of the substituted result or of the total scalar.

The retained transport/refinement category has arrows

  (h,u):(x,a)->(y,b),  with u:R_h(a)->b,
  (k,v) o (h,u) = (kh, v o R_k(u)).

It is generally a category, not a groupoid. The transport-only operation
groupoid is separately defined; its stabilizers and orbit set are not
confused with the core or isoclasses of the larger refinement category.

The comparison along e maps (x,a) to (e(x),a) and (g,u) to (e(g),u).
Every fixed-endpoint hom is a disjoint union over base arrows, so full
faithfulness follows from e's hom bijections and identity on each actual
tuple fibre. Essential surjectivity inverse-transports an entire operation
along a chosen epsilon_y:e(dy)->y, not merely its name or orbit.

This is sharp for a uniform claim across every primitive action in the
declared class. A constant signature with one component and no primitives
has only its identity operation, so its transport-only groupoid is the
indexing groupoid itself. Its comparison is e. Uniform equivalence thus
forces full faithfulness and essential surjectivity as well as using them
sufficiently; a particular special action can have a weaker comparison.

The quasi-inverse transports every tuple by epsilon_y inverse and recovers
base arrows through e(dh)=epsilon_z inverse h epsilon_y. The proof verifies
naturality and both unit/counit triangles on actual arrows. Different
representative choices are compared by unique lifted conjugation arrows;
the comparisons satisfy a cocycle. Forward comparisons compose strictly.
Thus coherence is given by explicit formulas, not unspecified higher data.

Effective descent is also constructed from arbitrary primitive data on G:
precompose it with the chosen d, then use the lifted comparison arrows to
identify its pullback with the original primitive diagram. Tree recursion,
generated-congruence induction and entrywise tuple transport extend this
to the original calculus. Equivalence is not built into the definition of
the desired completed structures.

8  D3 The actual C5 family and the B3 certificate comparison

The principal family uses three named interfaces, any fixed finite carrier
sizes, a finite nonempty list of carrier presentations, a finite commutative
monoid M, and ordered graphs of size <=N with N>=4, hence every original
N>=6 instance. Carrier sets may differ between presentations. Chosen
encodings into standard finite sets give an equivalence from the groupoid
of actual carrier-family bijections to the corresponding product of
symmetric groups.

At each presentation the native finite category consists of all relations
between its named carriers. A carrier bijection sends every relation entry
(a,b) to its image. Coordinatewise bijections of internal realizations
prove preservation of relation composition and diagonal units. This is the
bijective D2 specialization, not arbitrary noninvertible restriction.

The primitive maps keep the whole ordered graph, actual partition, scalar
and each occurrence position, and change every grade by the relation map.
They check all seven ORIGINAL equations: R keeps subpath composites and
induced blocks; UL/UR keep the designated empty unit data; D0/DA keep the
identity and monoid product; DL/DR keep the same scalar and indicated slot.
The induced map is therefore on the actual generated A3 quotient, not a
semantic-kernel replacement. Original decorator placement is not claimed
as a new invariant after those equations.

B3 supplies the independently presented mixed completion Comp, with O,
MOut and MIn. D3 checks transport on its actual path-relation basis,
including labelled critical joins, disjoint/variable squares and original
forest insertion slots. Operation-equation invariance alone would not
justify this stronger comparison.

For a full semantic transported arrow (h,(b_i)), its completion certificate
retains h, inserts the individual normal terms N(Phi(b_i)), then follows
the coherent pure equation path to the specified target. The B3 hom theorem
proves both inverse identities. Each entry retains its own partition,
profile length, empty blocks and scalar. The native carrier comparison
first recovers the base bijection, then every tuple entry separately.
Normal sections commute up to the specified coherent equation paths; they
are not falsely declared literally invariant.

9  Symmetry erasure and the adequate repair

For three two-point carriers, H=(S2)^3 has order 8. Take a whole graph of
two disjoint ordered edges and two repeated full components (K2,diagonal_A)
in separate original slots. All eight carrier symmetries stabilize the
selected binary operation, although the A swap moves other relation
components. This is real object isotropy, not permission to exchange slots.

With M=Z/2, the unary-decoration tuples (0,0) and (1,1) are distinct
refinement endomorphisms with the same total scalar and endpoint operation.
For each h in H they yield distinct retained arrows. The 16 selected
arrows form an H x C2 subgroup; the theorem does not claim they exhaust
all automorphisms of the full refinement category.

Two separate failures show why coarse classes are inadequate.

- Independent operation orbits need not support substitution. On a
  three-point carrier, let P=(01), Q=(02). Original empty-graph binary
  children with grade strings (P,P) and (Q,Q) are conjugate. Substituting
  them independently into a fixed binary outer cut gives (P,P,P,P) versus
  (P,P,Q,Q), which cannot be related by one simultaneous conjugation.
  The scalar action is trivial; this is an interface-only counterexample.

- Taking isoclasses before matching loses seam lifts. Two rigid charts
  into B(C2), at the repeated original diagonal component, have a weak
  pullback with two discrete seam objects, id and swap. Their coarse set
  pullback has one element. Its comparison has two lifts, so the class
  does not recover a unique chosen seam certificate.

The repair retains the action groupoid of the whole compatible substitution
diagram, the actual seam arrows, and the full transport/refinement
category. Compatible weak-pullback comparisons retain and conjugate seam
arrows through the stated square; tuple reassociation supplies coherence.
No new equation merges the two orbit-substitution outcomes or the two
scalar tuples.

The set-valued conclusions are correspondingly precise: bijections of
corresponding fixed-full-profile operation fibres, fixed-endpoint hom sets
and transport orbit sets. The groupoid/category conclusion is equivalence
with retained automorphisms. Aliases need not give equal total object sets,
and orbit sets do not inherit every matching or substitution operation.

10  The separate provenance groupoid doctrine

A map f:A'->A between discrete groupoids is an equivalence iff it is a
bijection. An equivalence of presentation groupoids may identify distinct
isomorphic objects while remaining bijective on every hom set; that is a
different assertion.

PROV replaces A'_discrete by K_f, with one arrow between names having the
same OLD value. K_f->A_discrete is fully faithful and is an equivalence iff
f is onto. Lift groupoids over a fixed old witness are empty or contractible;
nonempty multiple lifts cease to obstruct equivalence, but missing values
remain an obstruction. The old graph, partition, coefficient witness and
local semantic scalar records are fixed by these arrows.

This changes the unit and matching doctrine. The diagonal becomes a
groupoid equivalence, not a bijection of its object sets. Successive
changes must retain equality in the earliest old interface; independently
recomputing the later kernel pair can remove required arrows. These are
prior PROV results, not new D3 proofs or a claim that original C5 generators
freely generate every object of the extended groupoid semantics.

In short, the three levels are different:

  Rel             one usable witness per feasible exterior pair
  Span of sets    one and only one lift of every retained old tuple
  PROV groupoids  nonempty contractible lift groupoid fixing old data
