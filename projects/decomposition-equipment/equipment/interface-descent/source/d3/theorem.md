# D3 Descent between finite interface presentations with full refinement witnesses

31 October 2026. Author proof at level P, supplied for independent review.
The exact target is original completion contract D3, lines 398–412. No
historical source or acceptance record is changed.

**Main result.** Equivalent finite groupoid presentations of a specified
strict interface action give equivalent retained transport calculi and
equivalent full occurrence-refinement categories. The comparison retains
each actual transport arrow and each original-slot refinement entry; it is
fully faithful, not merely a bijection of operation orbits. Explicit choices
of presentation representatives give quasi-inverses, natural unit/counit
maps, and choice-independent coherence. The theorem applies to the actual
seven-equation C5 presentation over finite relation categories, including
nontrivial carrier permutations and repeated nonempty components. Its B3
comparison uses the independently presented mixed completion, not a new
category defined by semantic equality.

The set-valued conclusion is deliberately narrower. Fixed-profile operation
fibres and fixed-endpoint refinement hom sets compare bijectively; transport
isoclasses compare bijectively. Taking independent operation orbits need not
admit substitution, and taking isoclasses before matching can lose distinct
seam lifts. Retaining the action groupoid and matched transport certificates
repairs both losses without identifying old graph partitions, local scalar
entries, or occurrence positions. This is not a theorem that an arbitrary
noninjective map of finite carriers is an equivalence of discrete interfaces.

## 1 Scope and what is already proved

Fix finite groupoids G and H and a functor e:G→H which is **fully faithful
and essentially surjective**. Equivalently, each map

    G(x,x') → H(e x,e x')                                  (1.1)

is bijective and every y in H has a specified isomorphism from some e x.
Finiteness is a bounded declared input class, not a bound on tree depth,
profile length, or number of empty occurrences. Empty groupoids are allowed:
if H is empty then G is empty and all comparisons below are empty; otherwise
finite choice suffices for the displayed quasi-inverse data.

At x in H give exactly the primitive-level data of D1 §2: a quiver of actual
interfaces/components, a typed signature S_x, and parallel equations E_x.
An arrow h:x→y supplies a quiver isomorphism and a bijection on primitive
fibres. Identity and composite laws are strict; transported declared
equations must belong to the target's **generated** congruence. No action on
a quotient or refinement category is part of the input. Literal preservation
of the equation templates is a sufficient check and is verified in §6.

D1 constructs the free transport recursively at every primitive node,
proves transport commutes with arbitrary occurrence substitution, and proves
that the generated A3 congruence descends. Write P_x for the resulting actual
presented calculus and P_h for its strict transport isomorphism. D1 also
constructs the full refinement functors. We reuse those results; we do not
repeat them as a new general quotient theorem.

The following earlier sources have materially different scopes.

* **PROV** (`KernelPair_Provenance_Repair.md`, §§1–9) already proves that
  surjective finite carrier maps become equivalences after replacing discrete
  interfaces by their kernel-pair groupoids and using weak seam matching.
  Its fibres fix the entire old coefficient/graph/scalar record and vary
  only lift names. It gives nullary and all-tree coherence, provenance-aware
  successive changes, and soundness for all seven original equations. It
  explicitly does **not** prove that the enlarged groupoid doctrine is freely
  generated by the old C5 generators. We retain that boundary.
* **ACT** (`GraphDependent_Quotient_and_Cut_Memory.md`, §§5–6) already computes
  the nontrivial isotropy produced by forgetting actual inner bijections
  while retaining prequotient span-isomorphisms. Its certified cut groupoid
  restores the bijections and is contractible. Its quotient-intrinsic arrows
  are not the retained arrows in that calculation.
* **B3** (`Completion_Comparison.md`, §§5–6) already constructs an independent
  generators-and-relations completion and proves its hom bijection with
  actual full semantic refinement tuples. The equations O, MOut and MIn
  are essential. Object-only quotienting does not supply that theorem.

The gap filled here is equivalence of interface **presentations**, with
nontrivial isotropy and original presented/refinement compatibility. PROV's
thin contractible lift fibres alone do not test that gap. The elementary
Grothendieck-construction and equivalence arguments are standard; the
source-level witness, equation and B3 integration is checked explicitly.
For primary-source attribution, see the verified locators in
`../literature_comparison.md`: Kock, *Data types with symmetries*, §§2.1–2.5;
Baez–Hoffnung–Walker, *Groupoidification*, Proposition 43 and Lemma 65; and
Buckley, *Fibred 2-Categories and Bicategories*, §1.1 for the ordinary
Cat-valued construction (in the covariant dual convention). No result from
those sources replaces the explicit proof below.

## 2 Retained transport objects and full refinement arrows

Let Op_x be the set of all P_x operations, retaining their actual whole and
entire profile. The transport-only groupoid O_H has objects (x,a), a∈Op_x,
and arrows

    h:(x,a)→(y,b)   with h:x→y and P_h(a)=b.                (2.1)

Different h are different arrows even when their action on a is identical.
Its isoclasses are transport orbits. We do not call these the isoclasses of
the larger refinement category defined next.

For each x put

    R_x = disjoint union over actual wholes W of Ref(P_x,W).

This disjoint union is necessary: a transport can move W. An arrow u:a→b
in its W-summand is the **entire** ordered tuple (u_i), one operation at each
original slot of profile(a), with a[u_i]=b. Each u_i retains its actual
output component, full child profile and scalar/partition witness. In
particular, zero-length child blocks, repeated equal components, and equal
total products do not identify different tuple entries. Arrow equality is
entrywise. Composition substitutes each later consecutive block into its
own u_i, using the canonical cumulative offsets; this is the original B2/B3
definition, not a new endpoint relation.

D1 gives strict functors R_h:R_x→R_y which act on objects by P_h and on
arrows by (u_i)↦(P_h u_i). Form the retained transport/refinement category
T_H with objects (x,a) and arrows

    (h,u):(x,a)→(y,b),  h:x→y,  u:R_h(a)→b in R_y.         (2.2)

Writing v∘u for first u then v, its operations are

    (k,v)∘(h,u) = (kh, v∘R_k(u)),
    id_(x,a) = (1_x,id_a).                                (2.3)

The strict laws R_kh=R_k R_h and the category laws of R give associativity
and units. Thus this is an ordinary category, generally **not** a groupoid.
O_H embeds by h↦(h,id); the fixed-x refinement category embeds using h=1.
Neither embedding erases information. Its core can have further refinement
isomorphisms, so its isoclass set need not equal the orbit set of O_H.

Substitution is retained at the appropriate matching level. For any finite
typed substitution tree shape T, let D_x(T) be the set of compatible vertex
operations with every whole, occurrence and shared component retained.
Arrows in its action groupoid transport **the entire compatible diagram** by
one h. Evaluation, grafting, cutting to a subdiagram, and flattening have
their existing occurrence-wise formulas; they are equivariant by D1.
They consequently give functors between these diagram groupoids. We do not
claim that arbitrary independent arrows with unmatched interface data may
be substituted. D1's local-incidence version permits independent boundary
arrows exactly where incidences allow them, including the single shared
boundary at a nullary; the same construction applies to such supplied
finite chart diagrams. The selected global-action class suffices below.

## 3 Equivalence of presentations with explicit inverse data

Pull back the primitive diagram along e. Its calculus at x in G is P_(e x),
and its transport at g is P_(e g), by D1's explicit free/quotient formulas.
There are comparison functors

    E_O:O_G→O_H,                  E_T:T_G→T_H,
    (x,a)↦(e x,a),                (g,u)↦(e g,u).           (3.1)

The same formulas apply to the action groupoid of every D_x(T).

**Theorem 3.1.** All the comparisons in (3.1) are equivalences. E_T induces
a bijection on every actual fixed-endpoint hom set and therefore preserves
and reflects the equality of full original-slot refinement certificates.
It commutes strictly with substitution-diagram evaluation, grafting,
contraction and every existing equivariant source/target projection.

**Full faithfulness.** For two fixed objects (x,a),(x',b), the source hom set
is the disjoint union

    coproduct_(g∈G(x,x')) Hom_(R_(e x'))(R_(e g)a,b).        (3.2)

The target hom set is the identical union indexed instead by
h∈H(e x,e x'). Bijection (1.1) sends each summand to itself by the identity
on its full tuple. This proves a literal hom-set bijection, including the
segmentation of every tuple. For O the same proof replaces each tuple fibre
by the condition P_(e g)a=b. For D(T) it replaces a by the entire diagram.

**Essential surjectivity.** For (y,b), choose d y∈G and an arrow
ε_y:e(d y)→y. Then a=R_(ε_y^-1)b is an operation at e(d y), and

    (ε_y,id_b):E_T(d y,a)→(y,b)                            (3.3)

is an isomorphism; its inverse is (ε_y^-1,id_a). This inverse-transports
the full operation, not just its whole or isoclass. The identical argument
works on operation and substitution-diagram groupoids. The proof requires
no normal form or arbitrary lifting assertion. Strict commutation follows
because (3.1) leaves each vertex/tuple field unchanged and applies e only
to its supplied common transport arrow. QED.

The criterion is sharp as a uniform retained-presentation criterion. For a
constant signature with one component, no primitives and only its identity
tree, the transport-only operation groupoid is exactly the indexing
groupoid. The comparison is e itself. Thus a functor e inducing equivalences
for **every** primitive action in the declared class must be fully faithful
and essentially surjective. A nonfaithful e already merges distinct
transport certificates of that identity operation; a nonfull e misses
them; failure of essential surjectivity omits a presentation object.

### 3.1 The quasi-inverse and both triangles

These formulas give coherence rather than appealing to unspecified higher
descent. Choose the finite family (d y,ε_y). Define d on h:y→z as the unique
arrow with

    e(d h) = ε_z^-1 h ε_y.                                (3.4)

Full faithfulness gives existence and uniqueness; conjugation proves d is
a functor. A quasi-inverse D_T is

    D_T(y,b) = (d y,R_(ε_y^-1)b),
    D_T(h,u) = (d h,R_(ε_z^-1)u).                          (3.5)

The displayed tuple has the required source since
R_(e d h)R_(ε_y^-1)=R_(ε_z^-1)R_h. Applying (2.3) proves the functor laws
entry by entry: for h:y→z, k:z→w, the tuple of D_T((k,v)∘(h,u)) is

    R_(ε_w^-1)(v) ∘ R_(ε_w^-1 k)(u),

which is exactly the tuple obtained by composing the two images in (3.5).

The counit θ:E_T D_T⇒Id has components (ε_y,id_b). Naturality is the
equality of actual arrows

    (h,u)∘(ε_y,id) = (h ε_y,u)
                   = (ε_z,id)∘(d h,R_(ε_z^-1)u) mapped by E_T. (3.6)

For x∈G let q_x:x→d(e x) be the unique arrow with
e(q_x)=ε_(e x)^-1. The unit η:Id⇒D_T E_T has component (q_x,id).
Naturality follows by applying e to the base-arrow equation and using
faithfulness, while its tuple equation is the same strict transport law.
The first triangle has base ε_(e x)e(q_x)=1 and identity tuple. For the
second triangle, (3.4) gives e(d ε_y)=ε_(e d y), whereas
e(q_(d y))=ε_(e d y)^-1. Thus d(ε_y)q_(d y)=1 by faithfulness; its tuple
is again identity. Both triangles hold as equations in the actual category.
All formulas restrict to O and to compatible diagram groupoids.

### 3.2 Independence of choices and composite changes

For another choice (d' y,ε'_y), the unique arrow ρ_y:d y→d' y satisfying

    e(ρ_y) = (ε'_y)^-1 ε_y                                (3.7)

gives a natural comparison D_T⇒D'_T by (ρ_y,id). The tuple is identity
because R_(eρ_y)R_(ε_y^-1)=R_((ε'_y)^-1). Equations (3.4) and (3.7)
prove naturality on each actual (h,u). For a third choice, the two successive
ρ arrows equal the direct ρ by (1.1), and their tuples are identity. Thus
the choice comparisons satisfy the identity and cocycle laws exactly.

For equivalences G --e→H --f→J, the forward comparisons obey
E_f E_e=E_(fe) on objects, base arrows and tuples. Choosing composite
quasi-inverse data via the chosen d's gives the usual composites of the
displayed ε arrows; associating a longer composite differently multiplies
the same arrows in the same order. The corresponding tuple transports are
equal by strict D1 laws. Other choices compare by (3.7). This proves all
finite identity/composite coherence at the declared strict level, without
claiming that arbitrary weak actions were strictified.

## 4 Effective descent is not assumed by reindexing

The previous theorem starts with a diagram on H to make its comparison
maps transparent. It does not limit the claim to data already copied from
H. Suppose instead arbitrary primitive-level strict D1 data are given on G.
Choose d and ε as above and give y∈H the actual signature/equations at d y;
give h the primitive transport along d h. This is a strict H diagram by
(3.4), with the required equation invariance inherited from G. D1 builds its
presented calculus from these primitive data.

The arrows q_x:x→d(e x) above induce the comparison on original primitive
types and witnesses. Their naturality

    d(e g) q_x = q_(x') g                                 (4.1)

follows by applying e and (3.4). Tree induction extends this comparison to
free trees. A3 derivation induction makes it well defined on presented
classes. On refinements it maps every original tuple entry by P_(q_x).
Every q_x is invertible, so these are strict fibre isomorphisms with explicit
inverses. They identify the given G diagram with the pullback of the newly
constructed H diagram, naturally by (4.1). They preserve each substitution
diagram, not only its evaluated output.

Equivariant maps between two G diagrams descend by the same precomposition
with d, and the q comparison is natural in such maps by their equivariance.
Different choices compare by the same uniquely lifted conjugation arrows
(3.7). Hence this is effective presentation change for the stated strict
data, not the definition that two calculi are equivalent whenever they
already preserve everything. The concrete construction below independently
checks the primitive maps for two actual carrier presentations.

## 5 Set valued conclusions and the limits of isoclasses

**Precise set conclusions.** A primitive/type isomorphism, such as the
chosen carrier encodings in §6, induces bijections of the actual fixed-full-
profile presented operation sets. For every pair of chosen source/target
objects, Theorem 3.1 gives the hom-set bijection (3.2). It also induces a
bijection π₀(O_G)→π₀(O_H). The equivalence of T separately induces a
bijection of its object isomorphism classes. These statements are distinct.
There is generally no bijection of total object sets: adding an alias
presentation duplicates objects. No equality of the two categories is
asserted.

An equivalence preserves stabilizer groups through its hom bijections.
Replacing either side by a discrete set of isoclasses does not: at (x,a),
the automorphisms in O_H are

    Stab_H(a)={h∈H(x,x):P_h a=a}.                          (5.1)

The hom map (1.1) identifies these groups for matched objects. They are
not generally trivial. They must not be confused with ACT's prequotient
coefficient automorphisms or with any additional invertible refinement
tuples in the core of T.

### 5.1 Independent orbit representatives can destroy substitution

This control uses only original interface permutations, not an extra action
on scalar labels. Take one named interface A with carrier {0,1,2}, category
K of all relations on A, and M=1. Let Δ be the diagonal relation, P the graph
of transposition (01), and Q the graph of transposition (02). They satisfy
P;P=Δ=Q;Q, and conjugating by a carrier permutation sends P to Q.

Use original C5 empty-graph components. Let a be the binary flat cut with
whole (empty,Δ), input profile ((empty,Δ),(empty,Δ)), and its two retained
empty blocks. Let b be the binary cut with whole (empty,Δ), input grades
(P,P), and b' the corresponding one with grades (Q,Q). These are well-typed
original binary generators; b and b' lie in the same transport orbit, and
every carrier permutation fixes a. Now

    a[b,b]  has full grade profile (P,P,P,P),
    a[b,b'] has full grade profile (P,P,Q,Q).                (5.2)

No single conjugation sends the first full profile to the second: all four
entries on the first side are equal after any conjugation, but the first
and third entries on the second side differ. Thus the composites are in
different operation orbits although their outer and individual child
orbits coincide. The original seven-equation completeness also distinguishes
them, since full typed profiles are retained. Independent operation-orbit
representatives therefore do **not** define substitution on orbit sets.

The repair is the action groupoid of the *matched whole substitution
diagram* in §2, not an unsupported operation on products of separate orbit
sets. It retains the relative transport used for a child; (5.2) need not
become equal or even isomorphic. No new equation is imposed.

### 5.2 Isoclasses before matching lose seam lifts

Let L=C₂ be the actual carrier-swap group at an interface with two elements.
Fix two rigidly selected charts of the same original intermediate component
X=(K₂,Δ_A), where K₂ is the ordered two-vertex complete graph. Their boundary
presentation maps are 1→BL and 1→BL. A matched retained seam is an object
of their weak pullback: an actual arrow ℓ∈L between the selected boundary
charts. Because each selected chart is rigid, a morphism between seam
objects ℓ and ℓ' exists only when ℓ=ℓ'; the weak pullback is the **discrete
two-element groupoid L**. Both id and swap act on the fixed diagonal grade,
and both are legitimate D1 transport certificates. This matching can be
inserted at an intermediate X-edge of an original C5 cut tree. It does not
permute planar occurrence positions.

Taking isoclasses first gives instead

    π₀(1) ×_(π₀(BL)) π₀(1) = {one point}.                 (5.3)

The natural map from the two retained seam lifts to (5.3) is not injective.
Therefore the quotient matching datum does not specify a unique lift, nor
does it recover the two already specified lifts. This is failure of that
particular naive isoclass matching/uniqueness property, not a claim that no
set-valued encoding can retain seams.

For general chart maps A→C←B, the retained weak pullback has objects
(a,b,α:F(a)→G(b)), with morphisms (u,v) satisfying

    G(v) α = α' F(u).                                    (5.4)

It retains exactly the missing comparison arrow. For invariance of this
construction one needs **compatible cospan presentations**, not three naked
groupoid equivalences. Explicitly, let a:A'→A, b:B'→B and c:C'→C be
equivalences, with F':A'→C', G':B'→C', and supplied natural isomorphisms
σ:cF'⇒Fa and τ:cG'⇒Gb. The functor on weak pullbacks is

    (x,y,α) ↦ (a x,b y, τ_y c(α) σ_x^-1),
    (u,v)   ↦ (a u,b v).                                 (5.5)

Naturality of σ,τ proves its seam equation (5.4). Full faithfulness follows
by lifting u,v uniquely along a,b and using faithfulness of c to reflect
that equation. Given (a₀,b₀,β), choose λ:a x→a₀ and μ:b y→b₀. Fullness of c
uniquely lifts the arrow

    τ_y^-1 G(μ)^-1 β F(λ) σ_x : cF'x→cG'y

to α; then (λ,μ) is the required essential-surjectivity isomorphism. This
proves the comparison rather than inferring it from unrelated equivalences.
For successive compatible changes, the displayed seam conjugations
multiply and cancel in the same order; the identity change gives the
identity seam. Repeated matching reassociates the same tuple of charts and
seam arrows, with the same component projections and seam equations.
Thus associativity and unit comparisons have actual coordinate formulas,
not merely a cited coherence promise.

## 6 Principal original presented family with genuinely different carriers

Fix N≥4 (hence also every original N≥6), a finite commutative monoid M, and
a finite nonempty collection D of presentations. At each j∈D give three
named interfaces A,B,C with respective finite carriers X_i^j of fixed sizes
r_i, and choose encodings ψ_i^j:X_i^j→[r_i]. Allow r_i=0.

Let G have objects j and arrows the **actual families of bijections**
g_i:X_i^j→X_i^k. Let H have one object and group
L=Sym([r_A])×Sym([r_B])×Sym([r_C]). Define

    e(j)=*,
    e(g)_i = ψ_i^k g_i (ψ_i^j)^-1.                       (6.1)

This is a functor by direct cancellation. Its hom maps are bijective, with
inverse g_i=(ψ_i^k)^-1 h_i ψ_i^j; it is essentially surjective because D is
nonempty. Its object map is usually noninjective, but its arrows are genuine
groupoid equivalences, not noninjective carrier maps.

At j take the finite relation category K_j on the three named objects with
all relations X_i^j×X_k^j. Use the **actual** original signature
S(N,K_j,M): components (G₀,R), binary primitive cuts C with an ordered actual
graph bipartition and composable individual grades, designated nullaries U_i
at (empty,Δ_i), and decorators D(W,m). Its presentation is the original
seven equations R, UL, UR, D0, DA, DL, DR, not a new semantic-kernel quotient.

For g:j→k map relation R:X_i^j→X_l^j by

    R^g = {(g_i(a),g_l(b)):(a,b)∈R}.                      (6.2)

For a path of relations, the internal realizing coordinates map by the
corresponding g at **each occurrence**. Since every g_i is bijective,

    (R;S)^g = R^g;S^g,             Δ_i^g = Δ_i.            (6.3)

The first equality has inverse witness maps b↦g(b) and b'↦g^-1(b'); for an
arbitrary path use their coordinatewise products. The second uses both
injectivity and surjectivity and the same arrow on the nullary boundary.
These are precisely the C2 bijective transport lemmas and the invertible
special case of D2. They preserve and reflect containment and exactness,
but do not reinterpret existential realizing tuples as C2 thin-cell labels.

On every primitive, keep the whole ordered graph, actual partition, scalar,
and every occurrence position; apply (6.2) to all grades. This is typed by
(6.3). The independent native diagram j↦S(N,K_j,M) is therefore a strict
primitive signature diagram. Applying the encodings ψ to these same fields
gives a primitive/type isomorphism

    L_j:S(N,K_j,M) → S(N,K_*,M),                           (6.4)

natural with respect to e(g) by (6.1). It is not an unexplained replacement
of a whole calculus by an isomorphic copy; (6.2) explicitly constructs its
action on the actual relation entries and every generator label.

### 6.1 Check of all seven original equation families

R retains the actual ordered triple of graph blocks and its entire grade
string. The grade on each grouped block is its subpath composite; (6.3)
identifies its transported value with the composite of the image grades.
Graph induced-restriction embeddings are unchanged. Hence the image is the
literal typed R instance at the image labels, on both sides.

UL and UR keep their designated empty block, shared boundary occurrence and
diagonal identity grade. Equation (6.3) sends these to the same designated
unit data. These rules do not delete an arbitrary empty-graph component or
an external empty occurrence.

D0 has scalar 1 unchanged. DA preserves the actual multiplication m n and
the grades of the decorated whole. DL and DR preserve the same scalar m
at the indicated input and at the whole, respectively; the grade/cut
transports are those already checked for C. Thus each of D0, DA, DL, DR is
sent to its identically named typed instance. No decorator placement is
retained as an extra semantic invariant after the original equations.

Consequently (6.4) descends through the **actual** A3 quotient with its
explicit inverse induced by ψ^-1. Free tree recursion, quotient projection,
and the C5 isomorphism Φ to independent FlatCut commute with it. In FlatCut
coordinates, it preserves the actual whole graph, ordered partition, full
grade profile and scalar, changing only the grade entries by (6.2).
This proves bijection on every corresponding full-profile operation fibre;
no thin-Rel interpretation is used to identify distinct C5 witnesses.

Combining (6.4) with Theorem 3.1 yields equivalences between the native
retained transport operation/diagram groupoids and full refinement category
for the distinct carrier presentations and their skeletal-carrier versions.
Its arrow map is explicitly

    (g,(u_i)) ↦ (e(g),(L_k u_i))                           (6.5)

for an arrow ending at presentation k. Naturality of L gives its source;
(6.3) and original substitution give its endpoint. Inverse hom maps first
recover g by (6.1), then recover each entry using L_k^-1. These steps recover
every occurrence's profile, partition and scalar separately.

### 6.2 Repeated nonempty components and stabilizers

Take r_A=r_B=r_C=2 and M=Z/2, in additive notation. Let the retained whole
graph G₀ be two disjoint ordered edges on vertices 0,1,2,3. Its blocks
U={0,1}, V={2,3} both induce the same ordered K₂. Let
X=(K₂,Δ_A), W=(G₀,Δ_A), and let a be the binary flat operation with whole W,
profile (X,X), partition (U,V), and scalar 0.

Every element of L=(S₂)^3 fixes a: diagonal grades are fixed by conjugation,
and the graph/partition/scalar fields do not change. Thus the stabilizer of
a in the transport-only groupoid is exactly L, of order 8. The nontrivial
A transposition is not a globally ineffective action: it sends the relation
{(0,0)} at A to {(1,1)} and therefore moves other actual components and
primitive witnesses. Fixing a is genuine object-level isotropy.

There are two distinct original semantic refinement endomorphisms of a:

    u⁰=(D(X,0),D(X,0)),        u¹=(D(X,1),D(X,1)).         (6.6)

Here D denotes the semantic unary decoration. Their entries each have the
single unchanged component profile. Both total scalars are 0, so a[u⁰]=a
and a[u¹]=a; nevertheless the first entry of u⁰ differs from that of u¹.
C5 flat completeness and the B3 hom theorem retain this distinction. For
each h∈L the arrows (h,u⁰) and (h,u¹) remain distinct in T. In fact these
16 selected arrows form the subgroup L×C₂: action on these unary entries
is trivial, and entrywise scalar addition composes u^s with u^t to u^(s+t).
No assertion that these are **all** automorphisms in T is needed.

Under (6.5) the eight transport stabilizer arrows and all these full tuples
are recovered bijectively. Taking just the isoclass of a forgets them. There
is no exchange of its two slots: the entire discussion fixes positions 1
and 2, even though their actual component values coincide.

## 7 Original B3 completion and full refinement certificates

Let Comp_j be the existing independently presented mixed refinement
completion for S(N,K_j,M), and let Q_j be the actual seven-equation quotient.
B3 supplies a functor

    V_j:Comp_j→Ref(Q_j),                                  (7.1)

over all wholes, sending a raw graft forest (F_i) to the tuple ([F_i]) and
each equation edge to the appropriate identity tuple. Its full faithfulness
is proved using O, MOut and MIn, not assumed from object normalization.

To transport Comp_j one must check its **path-relation basis**, not merely
the seven operation equations. D1 §8 and its §9.2 concrete check apply to
(6.2): each of the 18 labelled critical-join diagrams is the same join with
changed grades; disjoint/variable squares keep their addresses; Rcat keeps
the original insertion slots and forest substitution; O is unchanged at the
new labels. MOut transports the same outer equation and inserted full
forest on both sides. MIn transports the same inner equation in its same
original slot; its outside entries remain identities. Category composition,
monoid multiplication and graph restriction identities are preserved by
(6.3) and unchanged graph/scalar coordinates. Thus all defining relations
are respected and the resulting Comp transports are actual functors.

The square of retained categories

    integral_G Comp  --E_Comp--> integral_H Comp
          | integral V                  | integral V
          v                             v
    integral_G Ref(Q) --E_T----> integral_H Ref(Q)          (7.2)

commutes strictly for a pulled-back diagram; for the native carrier diagram
insert the explicit L isomorphisms (6.4)–(6.5). On a general arrow it sends

## 8 Relation to D2 and the existing retained symmetry repairs

Three distinct assertions must not be conflated.

1. A finite **groupoid equivalence** e has bijections on every hom set and
   may identify distinct *isomorphic presentation objects*. Its stabilizer
   groups are preserved. The explicit native example (6.1) has this form.
2. A carrier function f:A'→A between **discrete** interface groupoids is
   fully faithful iff it is injective, and essentially surjective iff it is
   surjective. Thus it is an equivalence iff it is bijective. An arbitrary
   noninjective finite function is not an instance of item 1.
3. PROV replaces A'_discrete by K_f, with one arrow x→y when f(x)=f(y).
   Its projection K_f→A_discrete is fully faithful, and is essentially
   surjective iff f is onto. This is a changed interface doctrine. It fixes
   every old witness and permits isomorphisms of its duplicated lift names.

## 9 Result and stopping boundary

The declared finite-groupoid class has explicit comparison maps, a proved
full-faithful/essentially-surjective criterion, primitive-origin effective
descent, unit/counit triangles, choice cocycles and strict forward composite
laws. Its principal C5 instance checks actual carrier entries, all original

presented B3 full refinement certificates. Tests include nontrivial
stabilizers of repeated nonempty components, distinct equal-total scalar
forests, failure of independent orbit substitution, and failure of unique
seam lifting after taking isoclasses before matching.

The result is P-level. Executable finite checks are separate evidence and
do not replace the quantified proof. No Lean formalization of the whole
groupoid theorem is asserted or required by D3. No assertion is made about
arbitrary weak actions, arbitrary noninvertible carrier functions, symmetric
slot permutations, arbitrary coefficient-quotient descent, a new free
groupoid equipment, global equipment completion, or novelty of generic
descent/Grothendieck constructions. Original D3 acceptance remains an
independent review/governance decision, not an author declaration here.
