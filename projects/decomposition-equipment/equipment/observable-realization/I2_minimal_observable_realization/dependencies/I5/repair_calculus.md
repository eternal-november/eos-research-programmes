# I5 repair calculus: finite schematic theorem and mixed-witness gates

31 October 2026. **Repair theorems with explicit hypotheses.** Scope: the selected graph-C5_N enrichment by active-occurrence labels, palette M_k×M_k, initial first-channel H observation, and declared channel-frame swap. The family-specific constructions are in `model_and_core_comparison.md`, `profile_schemata_theorem.md`, and `modular_repair_theorem.md`. Generic shell existence is baseline infrastructure, not by itself a discharge of I5.

## 1. Exact finite-schema hypotheses

The theorem below takes a FINITE set A of schematic sorts, finite carriers X_a, and finitely many semantic operation schemes. Full concrete profiles p remain distinct sorts. A schema certificate consists of:

1. A computable assignment a(p) and bijections i_p between the full-profile witness carrier X_p and the corresponding schematic carrier (or explicitly declared finite subcarrier) X_a. Distinct profiles are never identified in the calculus; a partition at p is pulled back through i_p. If a profile has extra observable structural data, it needs its own schematic shape/state, not an ignored coordinate.
2. Every permitted elementary one-hole graft/context and interface transport, with all other arguments fixed, becomes under i_p one of a computably enumerated finite family of **typed partial functions**. Equal definedness is observable. Every generated context is a composite of this elementary family, by an actual syntax induction including repeated occurrences, empty/nullary occurrences and arbitrary unary chains.
3. Every permitted cut becomes one of a finite family of relations into an ordered **tuple** of schematic carriers, together with finitely valued joint witness tests. Recuts and projections actually required by the chosen judgment occur in the family. A proof identifies arbitrary admissible cuts/generated cut trees with this grammar and its actual shared-witness semantics. Crossing cuts require their own admissibility/comparison theorem.
4. The carrier/morphism/record identifications are compatible with composition and actual simultaneous choices, not merely with coordinate marginals. Every schematic witness used by the algorithm has a concrete realization at every claimed profile instance. Conversely every permitted concrete operation and joint witness is represented.
5. Initial observations are transported through these identifications; the admitted representation class consists of **all typed partitions on the schematic carriers**, pulled back to profiles. Its closure under the algorithm is automatic. If the admitted class instead permits independently varying partitions at infinitely many profiles, this finite theorem does NOT solve that larger optimization problem. To claim the global optimum there, prove uniformity: the all-profile least complete refinement is schema-uniform, normally by a two-way full-profile experiment equivalence argument.

For graph bound N, 'at most N nonempty input blocks' helps but is not alone this certificate. The certificate must account for finite graph/interface palettes, finite old scalar palette (or its effective treatment), active ordered partitions, unbounded empty gaps, graph automorphisms and fixed context constants. Empty blocks having no extra label prevents one particular unbounded-coordinate defect. A bounded-depth truncation does not satisfy item 2 or 3.

A concrete uniformity proof can proceed by full-profile splitter induction: for every p of core sort a, require that the set of semantic elementary maps and joint cut relations realized at p is EXACTLY the same finite typed template set under the identifications. Duplicates do not matter. The forward inclusion gives stability of the pulled-back finite answer; the reverse, per-profile realizability, prevents over-refinement and proves optimality even among independently varying full-profile partitions. Starting from uniform E0, every full-profile splitter is then uniform and agrees with the finite splitter. Grade/empty skeleton data must not change template availability.

The weaker finite-instance theorem requires only a genuinely closed finite typed system; it makes no unbounded-profile claim.

## 2. Concrete operations, observations, and information order

Let E=(E_a) be a family of equivalence relations on X_a. Write E <=info F iff F_a⊆E_a for all a: F has more information. A repair is extensive in this order. Every repair below is relative to its INPUT E; it retains all distinctions already made by earlier repairs.

S has partial functions g:X_a ⇀ X_b, generated by actual elementary occurrence-wise grafts with other actual arguments fixed. T has declared partial transport functions of the specified variance. No function is inferred merely because it exists set-theoretically. A partition is g-stable if equivalent inputs have identical definedness and, when defined, equivalent outputs.

For each cut c with source a, declare a finite record set W_c and maps source:W_c→X_a and joint output

    out_c:W_c → Z_c × X_b1 × ... × X_br.

Z_c records declared joint seed/readout tests, admissibility/provenance information that must be preserved, and any fixed witness labels. If further record distinctions evolve under repair, represent the relevant records as additional state sorts and include their required operations; do not pretend their initial color is sufficient. Multiple records with identical output are intentionally indistinguishable for the present Boolean existential judgment.

For a partition E the joint signature is

    sig_c^E(x) = { (z,[u1]_E,...,[ur]_E) :
                        w∈W_c, source(w)=x, out_c(w)=(z,u1,...,ur) }.

C-stability means equality of these SETS for equivalent sources. It means a single matching witness with all component classes and the joint seed simultaneously preserved, in both directions. It preserves existence, emptiness and Boolean combinations of declared cut tests, not multiplicities, automorphism groups, unique lifting, actual-arrow bijections, or CULF. Those are different target semantics.

For the selected active-label cut, each record must retain all a_i,b_ij satisfying x_ij=a_i b_ij with the SAME a_i for a group. Independent existential tests for each j are invalid. The signature acts on the whole outer operation and the whole ordered tuple of inner operations, including any shared record sort, rather than on independent label coordinates.

## 3. Repairs and algorithm

For A⊆{S,C,T}, define Split_A(E) by splitting each block according to its current block id together with:

* for g∈A deterministic families, ⊥ if undefined and otherwise the current block id of g(x);
* for c∈A cuts, sig_c^E(x).

Define R_A(E) by iterating Split_A to its first fixed point. In particular R_S,R_C,R_T are individually saturated repairs, not one-step splitters. A common repair can be computed either by R_{S,C,T} or by fair iteration of the three individually saturated repairs.

```
repair(E, families):
    P := E
    repeat:
        for each sort a and x in X_a:
            key[x] := (P_a(x),
                       [definedness and P(g(x)) for selected g],
                       [set((z,P(u1),...,P(ur)) for w over x)
                                                    for selected cuts])
        Q := canonical typed equivalence by equal key
        if Q == P: return P
        P := Q
```

All keys are typed. Nullary cut tuples and empty fibres are distinct (`{(z)}` versus empty set). Repeated equal components occupy distinct positions. A changing concrete interface abstraction that changes which operations participate is outside this fixed-carrier algorithm.

### Theorem 1 (finite exact least repair)

R_A(E) is the coarsest A-stable refinement of E. As a function of E it is extensive, monotone in information order, and idempotent. Fair joint iteration yields the least common S/C/T-stable refinement.

Proof. Each step refines its input. If F refines P and is stable for the chosen families, then F refines Split_A(P): deterministic outputs of F-equivalent inputs have equal P classes, and an F-matching joint tuple projects to a P-matching tuple. Induction shows every A-stable F refining E refines every iterate. Termination gives stability and therefore the universal property. Monotonicity follows by projecting finer output classes to coarser ones at each stage, or by the universal property. Idempotence follows from stability. In fair joint iteration any limiting partition is stable for every family, and the same invariant proves optimality. **No intersection-of-bisimulations claim is used.**

### Exact experiment-language characterization

At each sort allow atoms for INPUT E-blocks, Boolean operations, domain predicates and deterministic pullback modalities g*φ. For each cut c allow the polyadic modality

    <c,z>(φ1,...,φr)(x) iff exists ONE w over x
                                  with z(w)=z and φi(ui) for every i.

Joint seed predicates can equivalently replace z. Build all finite well-typed formulas, interleaving these constructors arbitrarily. The fixed partition is equality of truth values of all such formulas. A stable partition preserves formulas by induction. Conversely, in a finite system every class of a finite approximation is definable using a finite conjunction of discriminating formulas and negations. If two sources have different joint signatures, select a tuple of current classes and its seed; the displayed modality distinguishes them. Likewise a deterministic signature difference is witnessed by a domain test or pullback. This proves exactness and the coarsest property for the ACTUAL declared mixed grammar, not only for a finite collection of tested contexts.

Finite tuples of class predicates suffice even though they denote rectangular output tests: existential quantification is over the **joint relation**, so any desired tuple of classes is tested simultaneously. Replacing that relation by its coordinate projections changes the grammar's truth values.

## 4. Bounds and what they do not prove

Let n=Σ_a|X_a| and b=Σ_a number_of_blocks(E_a). Every strict split increases total block count, so there are at most n−b strict global splitter iterations, and at most n−b strict changes during any fair sequence of full repairs. A cycle of S,C,T with no change certifies completion. The n−b statement bounds changes, not an unfair schedule's idle calls or the total syntax depth represented.

A direct implementation costs, per splitter sweep, O(n + L + V) expected hash operations, where L is the sum of deterministic table entries and V is the total explicitly stored record-output length, including seed encoding; canonical sorting gives corresponding logarithmic factors. Memory is O(n+L+V). Arithmetic/bit complexity depends on actual witness and label encodings. At most n−b+1 sweeps therefore gives an elementary polynomial table-size bound. This is not a near-linear coalgebraic bound and not a succinct-input polynomial claim: V may be very large, and finite schematic reduction must supply it or a proved exact symbolic splitter.

The unary M_k family of `monoid_repair_theorem.md` supplies a different, meaningful necessity: every C-complete refinement of H-membership distinguishes k factor-length indices, hence any simultaneous repair needs ≥k blocks and ≥ceil(log2 k) encoding bits. Its witness ae=a versus ab=constant shows S-then-C fails after one pass. This does NOT show unbounded numbers of alternating full-repair rounds. Transferring the exact unary repair output to all graph/multiary sorts requires the singleton-spine separation/conservativity lemma in `model_and_core_comparison.md`; higher-arity cuts can potentially refine sooner. The lower bound alone transfers whenever the unary fragment and its generated tests genuinely embed.

## 5. Structural commutation: finite synchronized witnesses, not equivariance alone

### 5.1 Exact two-gate criterion at an input

For any two repair families A,B and input E, compute P=R_B(R_A(E)) and Q=R_A(R_B(E)). They commute at E iff P is A-stable AND Q is B-stable. Indeed these two gates make each a common stable refinement, and their universal properties then identify both with the joint optimum. Conversely equality makes the common result stable for both. This closure observation is standard; the following is its explicit witnessed version.

Let D be deterministic contexts (S or T, or their union). Include identity and all generated typed composites, completed by absorbing undefined states. Their semantic action on finite carriers has a finite enumerable closure. Put K=R_C(E). Then

    x R_D(K) y iff for EVERY typed h∈D*,
                    K(h(x))=K(h(y)), including undefinedness.

The **synchronized cut-lifting gate** is: for every cut c, every such pair x,y, and every actual record w over x, there exists ONE record v over y such that

    z(v)=z(w), and for EVERY output position i and EVERY h∈D*,
                    K(h(out_i(v)))=K(h(out_i(w))),

and conversely exchanging x,y. This is a finite, checkable necessary and sufficient condition for R_D(R_C(E)) to be C-stable. It is stronger than 'each h separately admits some matching record': the same v must satisfy all tests. Actual labels, shared multipliers and provenance restrictions are included in w and v. This is precisely where incompatible matchings defeat the naive equivariance argument.

For the other order, set L=R_C(R_D(E)). The **cut-theory context gate** says that every permitted generator g preserves equality of the finite cut-theory classes L, including domain. This can be checked directly by the finite signature table. A more structural sufficient certificate is a context/cut rewriting law: pullback of every seed cut modality through an elementary g can be expressed by a Boolean combination of cut modalities with elementary contextual pullbacks on their factor predicates, with all admissibility predicates retained. Require the rewriting identity to move contextual pullbacks strictly below each cut constructor, with right-hand-side predicates built from the original factor predicates and their contextual pullbacks (or supply another well-founded normalization measure). Induction on cut nesting then normalizes every g*φ with φ in the cut-only language over R_D(E) back into that language. Then L is D-stable. The identities must be proved on actual jointly compatible records; independent marginal distributivity is insufficient.

Together these two witnessed gates are necessary and sufficient at E. Requiring them for every admissible E is necessary and sufficient for global operator commutation. They are algorithmic witnesses, not a promise that the selected family satisfies them. A compact mixed-diamond law that implies the gates is an additional family-specific result. Equivariance of cut records under invertible transport establishes the cut-theory context gate but generally not synchronized lifting.

An explicit warning example: terminal states a,b,c,d have no transitions, while R(x)={a,d} and R(y)={b,c}. P has blocks {x,y}, {a,b}, {c,d}. This is C-stable. The involution swapping b,c and fixing all other states is an automorphism of the relation (it fixes each successor set). T(P) separates all four terminals but keeps x~y, and is not C-stable. Thus equivariance alone does not make T preserve C stability; the two source states require incompatible matching choices across the two observations. This is a diagnostic counterexample, not the I5 decomposition family.

### 5.2 A genuine deterministic positive certificate

If invertible transports conjugate the declared context family to itself, with domains and sorts preserved, every mixed deterministic word rewrites to transport-after-context and also to context-after-transport. Hence the two block-normal-form experiment languages agree and R_S R_T=R_T R_S for every E. This uses actual function equality and context generation, not just a square on one element. The named-channel swap satisfies the required action on frames and grafts as specified in `model_and_core_comparison.md`.

### 5.3 Special two-channel C/T certificate, only at the stated inputs

Suppose each carrier is a product X_a^1×X_a^2; corresponding channel carriers/operations are identical; T is swap; every cut fibre is literally a product of the two channel record fibres over the SAME structural cut; all those fibres are nonempty; and seeds are channel-separable. For channel-product partition P⊠Q, cut signatures are Cartesian products of the corresponding channel joint signatures. Nonemptiness makes equality of products equivalent to equality of both factors. Therefore Split_C and, by iteration, R_C act channelwise:

    R_C(P⊠Q)=R_C(P)⊠R_C(Q).

This is a theorem about channel-product partitions, not arbitrary correlated partitions. If ⊤ is cut-stable, E=P⊠⊤ satisfies

    R_T(E)=P⊠P,
    R_C R_T(E)=R_C(P)⊠R_C(P)=R_T R_C(E).

The proposed first-channel H observation has exactly this form, provided the full-profile cut records and fixed graph/scalar observations satisfy these product/nonempty hypotheses. If a joint seed couples the channels, a cut is absent in one channel, or a prior refinement correlates channels, the displayed argument must be rechecked. In general R_C need not preserve intersections of partitions, so this proof cannot be promoted to global C/T commutation.

### 5.4 Group-valued one-pass positive control

For actual multiplication cuts on a finite group, S(E) is a normal-coset congruence. Every tuple of quotient factors of a source coset has a simultaneous lift at every representative: choose all but one factor in their desired cosets and solve for the last. For a genuinely branching shared-parent cut in the active-input operad, choose the parent representative once and solve every child by a_i^{-1}x_ij; normality gives the requested child cosets. This proves C stability of the relevant product-coset partition under that cut doctrine. It does not show an arbitrary full-profile S(E) has that product form; the positive control must explicitly assume/prove that. With a transport-invariant normal subgroup (or S/T repair yielding it), this gives a real one-pass common repair. Classical group theory is imported; the profile-sensitive shared-parent lift is the application step.

## 6. I1 boundary and modular obligation

The algorithm can combine operation families on one already-fixed carrier, but this is NOT conservative amalgamation of presented theories. To conclude I1 local/global optimality one additionally needs actual conservative embeddings over the shared presentation, compatible initial observations, a gluing construction for compatible local partitions and actual witness records, and a mixed-test coverage/normalization theorem. The glued candidate must pass each global deterministic and synchronized joint-cut gate. Its minimality then follows by restriction of any global stable refinement to the local systems and the specified universal property of gluing. Restriction stability itself must be proved, not assumed if a local cut has more witnesses globally.

The M_k example gives a failure of independent observation-module repair: S(E)=E, C(E)=Q, but their information join Q fails substitution. It is NOT by itself an example of two conservative I1 presentation extensions. The selected conservative-presentation bridge and its limitations are treated separately in `modular_repair_theorem.md`.

## 8. Full-profile existence without a finite profile normalizer

The finite-schema theorem is not necessary just to DEFINE the separate global repairs. There is a standard image-finite modal theorem that applies to the actual full-profile family, while leaving finite effectivity and uniform convergence separate.

Let the full typed profiles form a set, with carriers X_p (not required finite). Keep every elementary deterministic operation typed and partial. For each FIXED typed cut skeleton c and source x assume its actual admitted record fibre W_c(x) is finite. The output arity r_c is finite. There may be infinitely many profiles, cut skeletons, contexts and transports; finiteness is required only of the matching candidates for a single cut test at a fixed source. Exact old graph/profile/scalar data stays fixed in the skeleton/readout as declared. In the active-label family the label records over a fixed old cut form a finite subset of a finite power of L, so this condition holds directly; aggregating infinitely many old cuts under one unnamed modality would require a new argument and is not done here.

For any input partition family E and chosen A⊆{S,C,T}, form the SET of all finite well-typed formulas of §3, using E-block atoms, Boolean connectives, chosen deterministic pullbacks/domain tests, and chosen fixed-skeleton polyadic cut modalities. Define R_A^∞(E) as equality of all formula truth values. Then R_A^∞(E) is the coarsest A-stable refinement of E on the FULL profile-indexed carriers. In particular the separate global repairs and their compositions are defined even without any finite profile normalizer.

Proof of the only nontrivial direction. Suppose x and y have equal complete formula theories, and w is a cut record over x. Write its outputs u_1,...,u_r and seed z. Suppose no record over y matches z and all output theories. Ignore records with different seed. For each remaining candidate v over y, choose an output position i(v) and a formula distinguishing u_i(v) from v_i(v); by negating if necessary take it true at u_i(v) and false at v_i(v). There are finitely many candidates. For each position i, conjoin the formulas assigned to i (empty conjunction is true), obtaining φ_i. The ONE modality <c,z>(φ_1,...,φ_r) is true at x, witnessed by w, and false at y, since every same-seed candidate fails at its assigned position. Contradiction. Hence a single matching record exists. Swap x,y for the reverse direction. The argument preserves a whole tuple and never selects incompatible coordinatewise witnesses. For nullary cuts, seed-presence alone separates unequal fibres. Deterministic stability follows because disagreement of definedness or of an output formula pulls back to a source formula. Any A-stable refinement of E preserves all formulas by structural induction, so it refines R_A^∞(E); this proves coarseness. Extensivity, monotonicity and idempotence follow as in Theorem 1.

This is the standard image-finite Hennessy–Milner/partition-modal argument, given here to expose joint-record semantics; it is not a new I5 contribution. It yields NO finite enumeration of all profiles/formulas, NO uniform finite stabilization bound for arbitrary E, and NO finite running-time claim. Equality of finite-formula theories may require unbounded formula depths. The finite theorem applies when its extra effective finite-schema assumptions are actually proved. An explicit formula/lower-bound argument proving the principal optimum discrete can bypass such a normalizer for that principal case, but does not magically produce a finite algorithm for arbitrary E.

The global commutation equivalence (two mixed stability gates) remains valid for these least repairs. For the synchronized witness diagnostic, replace the finite context-action vector by all generated typed contexts when there is no finite semantic action basis; it remains an exact criterion but not a finite decision procedure. A finite witnessed diagnostic is claimed only on the genuine finite closed systems or under a proved finite action/schema basis.

### Concrete mixed-witness equations for the finite diagnostic

For the active-input graph cut, fix its exact old cut skeleton and write the full factor record as (a_i; b_ij), with x_ij=a_i b_ij, including every declared seed/mask. To test D(C(E)) stability, compute K=C(E) and the finite semantic context-action vectors

    v_K(u)=(K(h(u)))_(all generated typed h),

including absorbing undefined entries. For x,y with equal source vectors, enumerate ALL actual solutions of y_ij=α_i β_ij. For each record (a_i;b_ij) over x retain only candidate solutions over y whose seeds/masks agree and whose whole outer factor and each whole inner factor have the same v_K as those of (a_i;b_ij). The gate passes iff this candidate set is nonempty for every record and both source directions. The SAME α_i is used for every j of a block, and the SAME candidate record must survive every context test. This is a finite intersection of actual solution sets, not intersections of separately matched marginal relations. A failed gate supplies x,y, one source cut record and the finite list of context probes eliminating every candidate, hence a concrete mixed counterexperiment.

The other order C(D(E)) is tested by enumerating each declared elementary context g and pairs in its cut-theory classes, checking equal definedness and target cut-theory class. A compact sufficient replacement is the well-founded joint cut/context normalization law of §5.1. We distinguish this constructive finite diagnostic from a compact structural classification of all monoids: neither it nor the group positive control proves that groups are exactly the commuting cases. For scalar group multiplication cuts, every S(E) is a normal-coset congruence and cancellation proves it C-stable, giving CS=SC=S; for full multiary cuts claim the corresponding result only for the proved coordinate-product coset class, solving each shared parent once and all its children jointly while preserving the exact old skeleton.

Equivalent non-effective construction: iterate the elementary splitter globally through finite stages P_n and put P_ω=∩_n P_n as equivalence relations. This particular descending intersection IS stable under image-finite cuts: for a fixed record w over x and y with x P_ω y, the sets of candidate y-records matching w modulo P_n are finite, nonempty and nested, so their intersection is nonempty. The matching record therefore works modulo P_ω in every coordinate. Deterministic stability also passes to this limit. The usual minimality invariant proves P_ω is the least repair. This argument does not assert that arbitrary intersections of unrelated bisimulations are stable; nested finite candidate sets are the decisive extra fact. It gives an ω-stage set-theoretic construction, not a finite or effective global algorithm.

Consequently the scoped channel-product C/T proof extends to the infinite full-profile family without a profile normalizer: at every finite elementary stage, literal nonempty fixed-skeleton product cut fibres keep the partition channel-product and perform the two local splitters independently; their ω intersection is the product of the two local ω intersections. Within each channel partitions may correlate ALL active leaves. The product is between complete channel records, never an unjustified product across leaf coordinates.
