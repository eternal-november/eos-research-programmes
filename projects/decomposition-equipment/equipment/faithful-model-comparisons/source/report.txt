1  Result and acceptance boundary

ORIGINAL E3 LOCAL FLOOR: PASS.

The two parameterized full virtual-equipment models and their faithful
comparisons have passed non-author mathematical review within the working
team against the exact original E3 clause. Source-bound finite checks and a
fresh checked typed core instance also pass. This is a local E3 assessment,
not external human certification, an exhaustive novelty claim, a rewrite of
the original historical contract, or acceptance of E4 or the whole
workstream.

The primary model retains whole ordered graphs, actual groupoid arrow
grades, ordered partition witnesses and scalar values. Its cartesian
restriction changes grade h to g^-1 h f and leaves the full witness
unchanged. The second model retains graphs together with actual finite spans
and arbitrary finite-set functions vertically; it therefore supplies
genuinely noninvertible interface changes and independent multiple apex
witnesses. Both models have all cartesian restrictions and universal units
even when a genuine finite path has no horizontal composite in the unchanged
horizontal universe.

The comparison is bijective on every complete cell fibre over the specified
image profiles and image boundary maps. This is not a vertically full
embedding into finite sets and does not cover all ambient spans. It
preserves restrictions through a concrete coherent isomorphism, not a false
equality between graph-span and pullback-triple apex sets. A separate
relation-valued comparison deliberately forgets information; its precise
failures of faithfulness and reflection are part of the result.

Substantial inherited work is credited. The earlier GraphSpanModel already
constructs the full witnessed noninvertible model, proves its universal
properties and original-profile recovery, and establishes its graph
nonrepresentability and scalar/base-change obstructions. Accepted E1
supplies the equipment and marking framework; accepted E2 supplies the
universal presentation and selected graph-span reconstruction. The new E3
package adds the explicit finite-groupoid full model, its witness-level
grade restriction and coherent intermodel comparison, integrates the
required original-core/refinement and application boundaries, and checks an
actual typed instance and finite regressions. Reuse is not presented as new
discovery.

P is uniform classical mathematical proof. C is bounded executable evidence.
The checked Lean fragment supplies the required typed core instance, not a
mechanization of every full-model axiom, morphism and comparison theorem.
The source ZIP retains the complete P proof, exact source maps, minimal
proof premises, the typed adapter and checked sources, clean acceptance,
final evidence and reproduction scripts.

### E3 Substantive models and faithful comparisons

**Class and claim:** M. Construct and compare at least two parameterized full virtual-equipment models, including genuinely witnessed nonreconstructive decomposition.

**Inputs:** For the primary graph-cut equipment, use a finite interface groupoid and prove cartesian restriction of an arrow grade $h$ along $f$ and $g$ by the formula $g^{-1}hf$, together with its witness-level universal property. An arbitrary C1 interface category is not assumed to have this restriction; extending it requires a specified restriction envelope. A separate relational, span, profunctor, or similarly established model supplies genuinely noninvertible vertical arrows.

**Artifact:** All cell sets, restrictions, universal units, morphisms, and exact markings; proofs of every required universal property; and comparison morphisms to the core and between the relevant models. At least one model must retain multiple witnesses and wholes, rather than mapping all cells into a posetal relation. Explain what each comparison preserves, reflects, or forgets.

**Discharge:** P uniformly over the stated parameter families, plus C and a checked typed core instance. Prove that the witness model is a virtual equipment even where chosen finite horizontal composites do not exist. Demonstrate nonrepresentability of a genuine path in the unchanged horizontal-object universe when applicable.

**Tests:** Three or more interfaces, repeated nonempty components with distinct refinements, nonreconstructive wholes, nontrivial interface change, an arbitrary witness that is not opcartesian, and a positive exact assembly. A groupoid-only transport model cannot by itself meet the separate noninvertible-interface requirement.

**Dependencies:** C1, C2, C3, C5, D2, E1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Verifying Rel or Span alone, or decorating a terminal equipment with irrelevant labels, does not pass. The witness-sensitive model must do work in E2, E4, and the cut/refinement comparisons.
[END ORIGINAL E3 VERBATIM]

3  Parameters and full primary cell sets

Fix a graph bound N >= 0, a finite groupoid K, and a finite commutative
monoid M with identity 1. Graphs are actual ordered simple graphs on [r] for
r <= N; equality retains the complete edge relation. The negative cross-edge
theorem uses the full graph family. The equipment construction itself works
for induced-closed graph families, but a restricted family needs its own
cross-edge hypothesis before the same negative conclusion can be used. There
is no bound on source arity, tree depth or number of empty occurrences.

In G(N,K,M), objects and vertical maps are exactly K. A horizontal arrow
from A to B is (G,h), where h:A -> B is an actual groupoid arrow. For a full
path p of horizontals with composite grade c(p), an output (G,h), and
outside boundaries f,g, a cell is an ordered partition U of every vertex of
G, together with m in M. Each input graph must equal its induced graph on
the corresponding block, and the boundary equation is g c(p) = h f. The full
source path, intermediate interfaces, output whole and boundary maps remain
part of the cell's type.

Repeated equal components are still different occurrences. Empty blocks,
empty graphs and empty source paths are not interchanged. At arity zero the
output graph is empty and the grade equation is g = h f; it does not require
f = g or an identity grade. These legitimate nonglobular nullaries are not
silently replaced by the narrower original-core nullary shape.

Substitution lifts each child block into its named parent block using
increasing enumeration, lists the resulting blocks in lexicographic
occurrence order, and multiplies the outer and every inner scalar. Shared
boundary arrows remain in the row, including the sole precomposition map for
an outer nullary. Multiplying the inner grade equations in path order gives
the composite boundary equation. The induced-subgraph restriction identity
identifies both associations of nested block lifting. Both scalar
expressions contain the same finite multiset of factors, including
nullaries; commutativity and associativity of M give equality. The full
singleton block and scalar 1 provide identity cells. These are actual all-
arity virtual-double-category laws, not equations in a posetal shadow.

4  Witness level restrictions and contextual universal units

For H=(G,h):B -> C, f:A -> B and g:D -> C, choose H[f,g]=(G,g^-1 h f):A ->
D. Its cartesian cell has the whole singleton graph block, scalar 1, and
boundaries f,g. For any finite source path p and further boundaries u,v,
composition with this cell is a bijection between the complete cell sets
with target H[f,g] and sides u,v, and those with target H and sides f u,g v.

The inverse uses the same actual partition U and scalar m. Its typing
follows by multiplying g v c(p)=h f u by g^-1 on the left. Composing back
leaves U unchanged and multiplies m by 1; any other factor must have those
same coordinates. This proves existence and uniqueness of full witness
lifts, not just a grade identity or uniqueness after forgetting decoration.
The same proof covers zero arity. It is natural under arbitrary typed
substitution rows.

    Cell(p; H[f,g] | u,v)  <->  Cell(p; H | f u,g v)

    (H[f,g])[u,v] = H[f u,g v]
    v^-1 g^-1 h f u = (g v)^-1 h (f u)

These chosen groupoid restrictions can be split. Other cartesian choices
compare by their unique invertible cells over H, and uniqueness proves
coherence. The formula is not asserted for an arbitrary finite category: g
need not have an inverse there. The span envelope below is the specified
broader model.

The chosen unit at A is the empty graph with grade id_A; its nullary witness
has empty partition and scalar 1. Inserting this unit in any named junction
of an arbitrary prefix and suffix inserts a specified empty block and
identity grade. Deletion is the inverse on complete cells, with arbitrary
outside boundaries and all scalar values retained. This proves contextual
opcartesianness, including the empty context and repeated unit insertions,
not merely a context-free composite or a nullary satisfying unit equations.
Other nullary witnesses of scalar m remain present and can be nonuniversal.

5  Full graph and span model with noninvertible maps

S(N,M) is the existing GraphSpanModel V(N,M), renamed only to distinguish it
from G. Its objects are actual finite tagged sets and its vertical arrows
all functions. A horizontal retains a graph G and a span A <- E -> B with
actual apex E and leg functions. Spans are not divided by apex isomorphism.
For a horizontal path, P(p) is the set of compatible tuples recording every
boundary value and apex witness. At arity zero, P(()) is the interface set
itself.

A cell is (U,phi,m): the full ordered induced-graph partition, a function
from P(p) to the output apex commuting with both boundary legs, and its
scalar. All three coordinates participate in equality. Composition assembles
the inner coefficient functions and shared boundary values into a compatible
outer tuple, applies the outer coefficient, lifts the blocks, and multiplies
scalars. The same fixed final tuples, actual graph embeddings and scalar
factors prove the laws, including empty subpaths and nullary coefficient
values.

Restriction along arbitrary f:A' -> A and g:B' -> B uses the apex of triples
(a',e,b') satisfying f(a')=l(e), g(b')=r(e). The cartesian cell projects to
e. A cell with coefficient phi and outside boundaries f u,g v has the unique
lift z |-> (u pi_0(z),phi(z),v pi_n(z)). The first and third coordinates are
forced by its boundaries, the middle by factoring to phi; U and m are
retained. No injectivity, surjectivity or invertibility hypothesis is
needed. This proves every-arity cartesianness.

Identity and iterated restrictions compare by inserting or deleting forced
coordinates. These unary maps have scalar 1 and inverse functions, and all
finite coherence diagrams agree because they delete the same coordinates.
Nested tuples are not falsely treated as literally equal. The unit is the
empty graph with identity span; inserting or deleting its forced junction
coordinate and empty block proves contextual universality in every
surrounding path.

The constant function {0,1} -> {*} is genuinely noninvertible and lies
inside this model's actual vertical category. Its cartesian restrictions
still exist. Multiple apex witnesses are independent of scalar labels: over
one-point interfaces a two-point apex has distinct nullary choices, identity
and transposition unary functions, and a noninvertible collapse. These are
different complete cells, not decorations on a terminal equipment.

6  What each comparison preserves reflects and forgets

For the finite groupoid K define F(A) as the tagged disjoint union of arrows
t:C -> A over every C, and F(h)(A,C,t)=(B,C,h t). Evaluation at id_A
distinguishes parallel arrows, so F is faithful. Tags distinguish objects.
F(g) is a bijection for groupoid arrows, but arbitrary functions between
F(A) and F(B) need not come from K. The realization is therefore not
vertically full.

The normal functor J sends (G,h) to (G,Gamma F(h)), where Gamma is the graph
span with left identity leg. It sends (U,m) with outside boundary f to
(U,F(f) pi_0,m). Following the graph-span functions identifies the full
source tuple set with F(A_0), even at arity zero. The output span's left
identity leg forces this coefficient; its right equation is equivalent, by
faithfulness of F, to the original grade equation. Reading U,m is the
inverse. Thus J is bijective on every full cell fibre over the named image
profiles and image boundaries, including nonglobular and nullary sorts. It
is not an equivalence onto all ambient spans or vertical functions.

The blocks and scalars prove substitution preservation, and the coefficient
is forced to be the same composite function on either route. Chosen units
are preserved literally. Chosen restrictions are preserved coherently by the
explicit map below, with inverse first projection; bijectivity of F(g)
forces the last coordinate. Both composites and all iterated comparison
diagrams commute. This preserves the universal property without asserting
equality of different apex encodings.

    k = g^-1 h f
    Gamma F(k)  ->  (Gamma F(h))[F(f),F(g)]
    a' |-> (a', F(f)(a'), F(k)(a'))

Parameter functors of finite groupoids and unital scalar-monoid maps give
actual model morphisms by retaining the graph/partition and applying the
respective maps. They preserve the chosen groupoid restrictions and units.
Faithfulness, scalar injectivity and unit reflection are stated separately;
no arbitrary parameter map is treated as information preserving. The theorem
also gives the analogous scalar morphisms on S.

The projection S -> Span(FinSet) forgets graphs and scalars but retains
actual apices and coefficient functions. The further comparison Q:S -> Rel
sends a span to its endpoint-support relation. Q preserves restrictions,
units and opcartesian cells, but is not faithful and does not reflect
opcartesianness. It forgets retained wholes, ordered partitions, scalar
values, apex multiplicity and actual coefficient maps. Distinct cross-edge
wholes, scalar-1 versus scalar-0 witnesses, and different maps between
nontrivial apex fibres can have the same relational image. These functors
are useful loss comparisons, not information-preserving ordinary completions
of the full witness model.

7  Original core and occurrence sensitive refinements

For a groupoid K, the independently defined original C1/C5 calculus D(N,K,M)
is isomorphic to the identity-boundary core of G: it has exactly the
original graphs, grades, full profiles, ordered partitions and scalar
values, and their same substitution. A globular nullary exists only at the
empty graph with identity grade, with one witness for every m. An existing
nonidentity loop is not relabelled a universal unit.

For any finite category K, not merely a groupoid, the same faithful finite-
set realization gives the original-profile core map D -> Core(S). The left
identity span leg forces phi=pi_0, and faithfulness forces the output grade
to equal the complete source composite. This gives a bijection on each
entire original operation fibre, including nullaries. It does not claim that
the arbitrary-category graph-grade calculus already has restrictions; S is
the supplied restriction envelope. The realization also reflects vertical
isomorphisms: a preimage of id_B under a bijective F(h) supplies a right
inverse t, and injectivity applied to t h and id_A makes it a left inverse.
Thus an original noninvertible arrow remains genuinely nonbijective in this
envelope. This fact does not turn the separate chain-to-groupoid inclusion
into an invertibility-reflecting functor.

On a fixed retained original whole, each comparison induces an isomorphism
of refinement categories onto the full subcategory whose entire profiles and
whole lie in the named original image. A refinement retains one cell for
every occurrence, its full child path, and the equation a[t_i]=b. Each entry
has a unique original preimage; substitution preserves and fibre injectivity
reflects the equation. Composition is entrywise substitution. Repeated
types, empty child runs, ordered graph embeddings and separate local scalars
all survive. This is not a classification of ambient refinements through
arbitrary new span horizontals and does not identify distinct tuples with
the same evaluated root.

8  Intrinsic universality and the separate coefficient marking

Let s(p) count the nonempty graph components of a path. In G a globular cell
is contextually opcartesian exactly when s(p) <= 1 and its scalar is a unit
of M. In S it additionally requires a bijective coefficient phi. The
normalized genuine exact submark requires scalar 1. The separate
coefficient-exact marking requires scalar 1 and, in S, coefficient
bijectivity, but does not impose s(p) <= 1. A multi-nonempty graph cut can
therefore be coefficient-exact and still fail to be an opcartesian assembly.

These are predicates on the complete cell sets, not a replacement of all
witnesses by exact ones. The units and arbitrary nonuniversal nullary
primitives coexist. J preserves and reflects all three markings on its
globular image because the relevant coefficient is bijective and it retains
the graph/scalar data. Forgetting graphs or scalars need not reflect the
same universal property.

For s(p) <= 1 the positive assembly in G has the unique nonempty graph,
composite grade, forced graph partition and scalar 1. In S its span apex is
P(p), with endpoint projections and identity coefficient. In arbitrary
surrounding contexts, unioning the substring's graph blocks is reversible
because at most one is nonempty. Compressing and expanding the span tuple
substring are inverse endpoint-preserving bijections. Arbitrary output
coefficients and scalars therefore factor uniquely. This proves genuine
contextual assemblies, not just the existence of a written composite symbol.

Comparing an arbitrary assembly with this canonical one gives the stated
classification: a unary comparison is invertible exactly when its scalar is
a unit and, in S, its coefficient is bijective. Conversely, two universal
factorizations are mutually inverse by uniqueness. A scalar-0 unary witness
in M={1,e,0}, with e^2=1 and 0 absorbing, is legitimate but nonuniversal:
distinct factors of scalar 1 and e both compose with it to scalar 0.

9  A genuine path without a representing whole

If a path has at least two nonempty graph inputs and their total size
exceeds N, no output can partition that many vertices. If the size is at
most N, place the inputs in consecutive blocks and choose two whole graphs
differing by one edge between two nonempty blocks. They have the same full
induced input path but are unequal retained wholes. Each receives a cell
from that path, using its composite grade in G, or an endpoint-product span
in S.

A purported weak universal target would need unary cells to both wholes.
Every unary graph partition is the whole vertex set, so those cells force
its graph to equal both distinct targets, a contradiction. No different
scalar, span apex or choice of cartesian restriction can repair it. For N >=
2 the path of two singleton graphs already gives this genuine cross-edge
obstruction. For N=1 the corresponding failure is only the size bound. For
nonempty K, the full G family, and likewise S, is representable on all
finite paths exactly when N=0.

This is the E4 model contribution, not E4 completion. It supplies a
parameterized genuinely nonrepresentable class and an information-
preservation obstruction: an ordinary equipment with exactly the same
objects, vertical arrows and horizontals cannot be related by a functor that
fixes those names and is bijective on every old full cell fibre, since its
universal composites would lift to forbidden old universal composites. A
merely equality-conservative inclusion may add new cells; the stronger full-
cell claim is the one ruled out. E4's positive ordinary completion and
universal property remain separate obligations. The span/relation
projections show the information-loss alternative, not its replacement by a
preservation theorem.

10  Required nonthin three interface witness instance

The principal instance is the original six-vertex C1 graph, not a substitute
empty model. Its edge set is {01,12,13,24,34,45}; the alternative whole adds
edge 05. The ordered blocks are ({0},{1,3},{2,4},{5}), and the middle two
induced graphs are the same nonempty two-vertex edge graph. The groupoid has
objects 0,1,2 and S3 labels in every hom-set, for 54 actual arrows. Let a:0
-> 1 have transposition (01) and b:1 -> 2 have transposition (12). The path
grades are (a,id_1,id_1,b), with composite b a. This uses noncommuting
permutations and genuinely distinct interfaces.

Both wholes have identical full input components and grade path, but retain
different cross-edge data. Each has its separate scalar witnesses in
{1,e,0}. Refining the second block in its increasing singleton order and the
third in the opposite order gives the actual flat ordered blocks
({0},{1},{3},{4},{2},{5}). Refining only one of the repeated middle slots or
the other gives distinct occurrence-indexed refinements.

There are also distinct parallel refinement endomorphisms with scalar tuples
(1,1,1,1) and (1,e,e,1). Both total products are 1, but the full tuples
differ at the repeated positions. J retains them; Q identifies them. The
scalar decoration therefore changes real refinement and factorization
information.

Nonidentity boundary changes test g^-1 h f with all endpoints correctly
typed and noncommutative label order retained. The principal four-nonempty
witness is not opcartesian even with scalar 1. A separate two-input path
with one nonempty graph and one empty graph has the positive exact assembly
described above. Changing only the positive binary assembly's scalar from 1
to 0 produces a nonuniversal companion on the very same full profile, with
distinct scalar-1 and scalar-e factors colliding. A scalar-0 unary witness
supplies the corresponding unary failure of universal uniqueness. The Span
instance also tests multiple actual apex functions and the noninvertible map
{0,1} -> {*}. These positive and negative cases coexist without discarding
the nonuniversal cells.

11  Actual cut fibres and interface change

For a fixed full original witness and a specified proper contiguous interval
of length at least two, any factorization through an intermediate original
component forces that component's graph to be the induced graph on the union
of its blocks and its grade to be their ordered composite. The outer and
inner graph cuts are forced. Reading the only free coordinates, their
scalars, gives the exact core-restricted factorization fibre {(a,b) in M^2 :
a b=m}. This is not an assertion that all ambient intermediate spans have
been classified.

For M={1,e,0}, the fibre over 1 contains (1,1) and (e,e), and the fibre over
0 has the five pairs with at least one zero. These factorizations remain
different after the faithful comparisons. For the idempotent monoid {1,z},
z^2=z, the three factorizations over z cannot be removed by an intermediate-
isomorphism gauge: its only unit is 1 and the original graph span's identity
left leg has no nonidentity endpoint-preserving apex automorphism. Thus
witness ambiguity is not always merely a change of span presentation.

For a pair of spans with a changed middle interface g:B' -> B, the canonical
comparison from the composite of restrictions to the restriction of the
composite forgets the lifted middle coordinate. Its fibre over a feasible
tuple is exactly {b' : g(b')=b}. Bijectivity requires one lift for every
feasible middle value; surjectivity requires at least one. The constant map
from two points to one gives a two-to-one comparison. Taking empty graphs,
or only one nonempty input graph, and scalar 1 ensures both composites
really are opcartesian; this failure is not caused by the graph obstruction.
Relations forget the multiplicity.

Longer runs give the product of the internal lift fibres. The nullary unit
has a different comparison, the diagonal A' -> A' x_A A', invertible exactly
when the boundary map is injective; it cannot be replaced by an empty
internal product. Groupoid image maps pass these tests by bijectivity, while
the second model exposes the genuinely noninvertible failures.

Retained labelled cut/refinement diagrams use actual coefficient functions,
proper cut certificates, occurrence addresses and elementary stages. Their
comparison is obtained by composing the cut certificate, the product of
child comparisons and the inverse target certificate. Compatible planar
cut/substitution squares commute by flattening the same named atom tuples,
composing the same actual functions, applying the graph restriction identity
and multiplying the same scalar factors. Invertibility here concerns the
evaluated coefficient over retained provenance, not an inverse to the arity-
changing refinement forest; a binary split can have a bijective coefficient
without an inverse forest contraction. Stagewise hypotheses and the
interface lift-fibre condition are retained. A root map alone can hide a
failed stage, and no comparison is invented for reversed nonparallel
occurrence orders or arbitrary orbit descent.

12  Substantive use in the accepted E2 constructions

G is an eligible target for the accepted E2 chosen-envelope interpretation.
Original binary cuts map to their actual partitions, scalar generators to M,
nullaries to the chosen units, restrictions to g^-1 h f, and cartesian/unit
factors to the actual unchanged witness or specified empty-block operations.
The local equations hold by the complete model laws. The resulting
evaluation, composed with the original-core map, is the original D-to-
Core(G) isomorphism. Applying it reflects equalities of parallel original
cells and separates the actual scalar and graph refinements. This is
equality conservativity, not a claim that the C5-only free envelope is full.

For arbitrary finite interface categories, the actual map D -> Core(S) gives
the corresponding separating interpretation without inserting fictitious
inverses. Separately, accepted E2's enlarged span presentation reconstructs
S by its local normal form with an actual span square, scalar and flattened
graph cut. Evaluation recovers exactly (U,phi,m). Omitting any of those
coordinates would destroy equality reflection, as the explicit E3 collisions
show. The graph-span comparison J commutes with this evaluation and its
restriction comparison is the concrete coherent map already displayed.

This is real use of the witness model in E2 and in cut/refinement
comparisons. The representability criterion and counterexample above are its
E4 input. None of these statements accepts E4's positive completion theorem
or claims that the smaller C5-only presentation generates every ambient
span.

13  Checked typed core and exact formalization boundary

The formal package rebuilds the unchanged original calculus and graph-cut
dependencies, the inherited E1 relation fragment and examples, and the new
E3GraphGroupoidCore and E3FiniteExamples modules from source. The
P_CORE_ADAPTER document gives the precise map between the finite Lean data
and the classical E3 model. It must be read with the theorem rather than
treating a typed record or an API regression as a proof of every semantic
comparison.

The new graph-groupoid fragment uses three distinct interface tags, S3

identifies the latter with the multiplicative two-element scalar group. Its
complete cells retain the partition and scalar, with the actual boundary
equation. The typed restriction equivalence proves full-witness
factorization and uniqueness at every path length, including zero, and
proves identity/composite restriction coherence. restrictionCompose is the
implemented inverse equivalence; its identification with actual composition
through the singleton-block cartesian cell is proved in the P adapter, not
by a separate general Lean multicell-substitution implementation.

The typed generator/tree construction uses the original Signature,
Tree.freeCalc, occurrence and refinement APIs. It checks the retained
evaluation profile, scalar substitution, identity and associativity.
primitiveCell constructs an actual semantic Cell for every binary or nullary
generator; primitive_bridge_blocks and primitive_bridge_scalar retain both
coordinates. The principal example supplies an actual full Cell and
actual_core_semantic_bridge identifies its complete profile, whole, ordered
blocks and scalar with a concrete tree evaluation. A genuine original
Calc.Hom, eraseScalar, extends scalar erasure on generators; its induced
original refinement functor is retained, and concrete distinct witnesses
prove eraseScalar noninjective on operations. This is not a claim about
faithfulness on the induced refinement functor's local hom-sets. These are
substantive checked typed bridges, not merely an identity-map regression.
Universal validity of arbitrary evaluated tree coordinates and the full
original-core/intermodel fibre theorems remain in the P adapter and
classical proof. The separately retained E1 relation instance supplies its
inherited unbounded core/restriction/unit theorems, not a new Lean proof of
the graph-span family.

The checked finite Lean examples witness the named interface, graph, grade,
repeated-slot and scalar distinctions at the exact typed core instance. Full
all-row virtual-double-category assembly, every parameterized full-model
universal property, the arbitrary-category span envelope and the complete
intermodel/refinement theorems are not silently promoted to Lean results.
