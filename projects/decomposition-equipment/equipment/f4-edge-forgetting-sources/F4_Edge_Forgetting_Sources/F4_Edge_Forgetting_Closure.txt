### F4 CULF and nonidentifiability

**Class and claim:** M. State and test the appropriate unique-lifting condition for a simplicial map, and prove that it does not imply recovery of the underlying whole or witness from its image.

**Inputs:** F2 and the map forgetting graph edges while retaining the underlying ordered vertex set, partition, and interface data.

**Artifact:** An all-degree proof that the designated forgetful map is CULF, formulated by the relevant cartesian naturality squares, together with an explicit noninjective map on whole objects. State the direction of every map. Explain that the lift is unique after fixing the source whole; it does not identify that whole from its image.

**Discharge:** P and C; X for the nonidentifiability example. Separately test a map that fails unique lifting. The report may not use CULF as a synonym for faithfulness, injectivity, reconstruction, or observational identifiability.

**Tests:** Two graphs with the same ordered vertices and different edges; a fixed graph and a prescribed partition having a unique lifted cut; and a deliberately duplicated cut-witness model that violates the lifting condition.

**Dependencies:** C3, F2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove the actual all-degree lifting condition, nonidentifiability, and a failing-lift control on the selected witness maps. A discrete two-point-to-point example is only a regression check, not the CULF/quotient analysis required by F3/I3.

3  The actual source target and direction

The primary object is the original M=1 discrete typed graph-cut
simplicial set, not a refinement nerve or the full span groupoid.
C1 and CUT supply its all-degree simplicial and unital 2-Segal structure.

The original finite family uses a three-object category and size bound
N>=6. The direct discrete proof permits any small K and bounded or
unbounded graph size, without thinness or unique arrow factorization.
CUT's faithful finite-span embedding is invoked only for the original
finite K with its faithful tagged finite-set functor, not arbitrary K.

An n-simplex of X is

  (G, (U_1,...,U_n), (A_0 --h_1--> A_1 --...--h_n--> A_n)).

G is a simple ordered graph on [v]; disjoint U_i cover [v]; h_i are actual
composable K-arrows. Empty blocks and their positions remain data. Blocks
need NOT be contiguous in vertex order: a simplicial interval selects a
union of whole blocks. Restrictions use increasing vertex enumeration.

Y has the same records except for the edge set. The map F:X -> Y erases
only graph edges. In degree one its direction is

  F_1:(G,h) -> ([v],h).

It retains the entire actual arrow path in higher degrees, not merely
interface endpoints. For a nonthin K, two arrows with the same endpoints
can differ, so endpoint-only forgetting is a different map. That different
map is not covered by the positive theorem.

Wholes are X_1, not X_0. Degree zero forces v=0 and X_0=Ob(K).
An empty graph with a nonidentity K-arrow is not a degenerate unit.

For a monotone alpha:[m] -> [n], the simplicial direction is X_n -> X_m.
Select the union of U_r for alpha(0)<r<=alpha(m), restrict the graph to
that subset, merge blocks over each interval
alpha(j-1)<r<=alpha(j), and compose the arrows over the same interval.
An empty interval gives an identity arrow. Y uses exactly the same rule
without adjacency. Edge erasure commutes with restriction, so

  F_m X(alpha) = Y(alpha) F_n.

C1's increasing-enumeration identities and category associativity give
all simplicial identities, including outer faces and degeneracies.
CULF is derived, not assumed as an equivalent defining hypothesis.

4  All active lifting squares and the explicit inverse

A monotone alpha:[m] -> [n] is active when alpha(0)=0 and alpha(m)=n.
The theorem is that every naturality square

       X_n  --F_n-->  Y_n
        |             |
     X(alpha)      Y(alpha)
        |             |
        v             v
       X_m  --F_m-->  Y_m

is a pullback of sets. Equivalently, the comparison

  c_alpha:X_n -> X_m x_(Y_m) Y_n,
  x |-> (X(alpha)x,F_n x)

is bijective. This specifies the direction of every map and the precise
meaning of unique lifting.

Proof sketch. A pullback element is a pair (a,b), where a is a source
m-simplex, b is a target n-simplex, and

  F_m(a) = Y(alpha)(b).

Because alpha is active, its interval selects all the vertices of b.
Consequently b's ambient ordered vertex set is exactly that of the whole
graph G_a retained in a. Define

  L_alpha(a,b) = (G_a, blocks(b), arrow_string(b)).

This is a valid fine source simplex. Its edge-erased image is b. Its
alpha-restriction has the entire graph G_a, and the equality defining the
pullback makes its merged blocks and composite arrows exactly those of a.
It therefore restricts to a. Conversely any lift must have b's blocks and
actual arrows; activity forces its whole graph to be G_a. Every field is
forced, so this is the unique lift. Both inverse identities follow by
literal field equality.

The proof includes empty graphs, empty and noncontiguous blocks, parallel
arrows, nontrivial composites, and degree zero. Every constant [m] -> [0]
is active; no [0] -> [n] with n>0 is active. These cases are not omitted.

CULF means cartesian on active maps. This includes degeneracy squares
and conservativity: a target-inserted empty block and identity arrow
can be deleted uniquely. Inner faces have unique factorization lifting.
The fine target supplies its arrow string; no unique factorization of
a K-composite on its own is assumed.

General outer-face squares need not be cartesian. An outer restriction
can discard vertices and their incident edges, so the missing graph data
need not be recovered. CULF is not a claim about those squares.

5  What is lost and what remains uniquely liftable

For noninjectivity assume K has at least one object and the allowed size
range includes v=2. The original three-object, N>=6 family satisfies both
conditions. Fix an actual arrow h, which may be an identity. On the same
two ordered vertices take G_0 with no edge and G_1 with the single edge.
Then (G_0,h) and (G_1,h) are distinct elements of X_1 with equal F_1 image.
This is a noninjective map on whole components in the actual model.

More generally, above a fixed ordered [v] and actual arrow h, every
unordered pair of vertices can independently be an edge or not. Hence

  |F_1^(-1)([v],h)| = 2^(v(v-1)/2).

This formula includes v=0 and v=1, when the fibre is a singleton.
Noninjectivity requires v>=2; it is not asserted for an empty category
or a family restricted to at most one vertex.

Choose the partition ({0},{1}) and the same composable two-arrow path for
the two graphs. Their individual input components are identical edgeless
singletons, and their target cuts are equal, but their wholes and retained
cut witnesses differ. Each separately fixed whole has exactly one lift of
that prescribed target cut. The two examples are not two lifts of a
single pullback datum: their source-whole coordinates differ.

The distinction is therefore exact. A large observation fibre may
contain many possible wholes, while the active-lifting fibre is a
singleton after one such whole is supplied. CULF does not mean injective,
faithful, reconstructive, or observationally identifiable. It does not
recover the whole or all witness provenance from an observation alone.
The proof also describes the original six-vertex mixed-interface graph
and its cross-edge variation as a larger mathematical example.

6  A duplicated cut witness that genuinely fails lifting

The negative control is defined in every degree on the same cuts. Let
C2 be the additive two-element group. Form D_n by adjoining to each
X_n-record a bit a_ijk for each 0<=i<=j<=k<=n, subject to

  a_iik = a_ikk = 0,
  a_ijk + a_ikl = a_jkl + a_ijl  in C2
  for every i<=j<=k<=l.

The bits are retained discrete witness data, not labels identified by a
gauge equivalence. For any monotone alpha, pull back the indices. The
normalization and four-index equation are preserved, including repeated
indices, and successive index restriction composes literally. Thus D is
a fully defined all-degree simplicial set, and the forgetful map
P:D -> X is simplicial. This is not an arbitrary doubling of degree two
with no higher faces or degeneracies.

There is no extra label in degree one, so D_1=X_1. In degree two the single
free label a_012 is either 0 or 1. Consequently the very same whole,
input components, ordered partition, and actual two-arrow path admit two
distinct cut witnesses in D_2 with the same image in X_2. Both restrict to
the same fixed whole. For the active long-edge map [1] -> [2], 0 |-> 0 and
1 |-> 2, the lifting comparison has a two-element fibre. P is not ULF and
not CULF; F composed with P fails at the same square.

This is a duplicated-cut-witness control with actual simplicial structure,
not a bare two-point-to-point map. It separates erased witness decorations
from erased whole edges.

D is NOT asserted to be 2-Segal. Its scalar labels in degree three have
8 possibilities, whereas a triangulation sees two triangle bits and only
4 possibilities. The scalar comparison is therefore not bijective.
F4 requires a separately tested failing-lift map; it does not require this
negative source to remain 2-Segal. No F3 or I3 conclusion is drawn from it.

A distinct supplementary control keeps a 2-Segal source: take
B=X x N(C2), with n bits decorating its n factors. Faces add adjacent
bits or discard an outer bit, and degeneracies insert zero. The ordinary
nerve N(C2) is Segal and unital 2-Segal; the product retains those
comparisons. Projection Q:B -> X nevertheless has two lifts of a binary
cut over a fixed whole bit g, with factor bits (0,g) and (1,g+1).
This control duplicates factor decorations, not two witnesses with
identical decorated inputs. It supplements, and does not replace, D.

7  Optional full CUT homotopy extension

The optional full CUT extension erases edges but retains all interval
spans, comparison bijections, and morphisms. Its active squares are
homotopy cartesian, not merely set pullbacks of representatives.
For homotopy data (a,b,theta), put a's graph on b's fine coefficient
record. The coarse isomorphism theta then lifts, giving essential
surjectivity; compatible morphisms determine a unique fine span family,
giving full faithfulness. Thus the comparison is an equivalence.
Lifts form a contractible groupoid for specified homotopy data; counting
representatives while ignoring automorphisms is insufficient. This
separate argument does not replace the primary discrete set theorem.

The checker and domain explanation are evidence/verify_f4.py and
evidence/verification_readme.md. Final evidence/results.json and

The domain includes every ordered graph on 0 to 4 vertices, every
ordered partition in degrees 0 to 4, and every monotone [m] -> [n] with
m,n<=4. Empty/noncontiguous blocks are included; degree zero forces v=0.
Paths use the nonthin C2 x [1] category with two objects and six actual
arrows, retaining bits. The original three-object 0->1->1->1->2 path is
also tested on its mandatory six-vertex mixed-interface fixture. Adding its cross-edge 05 changes the whole but
not its four component graphs. For each of those two separately fixed
wholes, all 32,768 six-vertex edge sets are searched and exactly one lifts
the prescribed cut. This checks two specific fibres, not all six-vertex
partitions.

Coverage is documented and factorized. The complete graph-times-path
Cartesian product is NOT enumerated. The all-parameter proof shows the
simplicial action is coordinatewise, F retains the full path coordinate,
and the active inverse has no graph/path interaction. Geometry
composition is checked on every per-vertex block-label lineage for all
bounded composable monotone-map pairs. Increasing-order preservation and
induced-edge restriction extend that equality to every graph. Each full
bounded graph/partition action is also compared with an independent
literal block-set and edge-lookup implementation. Actual category paths
and their composition laws are checked separately.

For active geometry fibres, candidates are independently enumerated and
grouped by their computed coarse record. Every possible coarse whole
edge set is tested for exactly the explicitly proposed inverse. This is
an existence-and-uniqueness test, not merely re-evaluation of the proposed
lift. The cocycle control is enumerated through degree four, including
pullback closure and composition, and its two-cut fibre must have size
two. Mutation-sensitive checks distinguish loss of coarse edges and
loss of actual arrow bits; endpoint-only data produce a two-lift failure.

The four-vertex, degree-four bounds and six-vertex fixtures are dependency
regressions associated with F2, not numerical text inserted into F4's
original clause. Finite support is not an all-degree proof. This checker
does not by itself verify every triangulation or all other F2 claims,

It is standard-library Python evidence with assertions, not a kernel

10  Baseline and closing assessment

The edge-forgetting restriction-species mechanism is standard reused
infrastructure, not a new recognition theorem. The proof cites
Galvez-Carrillo, Kock, and Tonks, Decomposition spaces and restriction
species, arXiv:1708.02570, for that baseline and strict versus homotopy
pullbacks. This submission explicitly instantiates the designated typed
map, inverse, limits, and coherent failing-witness control. It claims no
novelty for the general mechanism.

Forgetting edges has unique active lifts but loses the graph and retained
witness provenance. Arbitrary witness erasure need not have those lifts.
The original F4-local P+C+X deliverable and its original minimum passing
floor now pass. Formal dependency and program acceptance remain separate.
