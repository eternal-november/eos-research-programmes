C4 Baselines and five information ablations

Prepared 1 November 2026. Author proof and evidence map for original C4.

Conclusion

The full typed witnessed calculus is an ordinary colored nonsymmetric
multicategory with a specified path-admissibility condition. Its refinement
category is an opposite slice of its standard monoidal envelope. Ordered
graph cuts reproduce the restriction-species construction, with the order
retained; relational containment cells reproduce the thin Rel baseline.
None of these identifications establishes novelty. The additional local
presentation, compatibility checks and executable examples are attributed
specialization, synthesis and implementation. Original C4 expressly allows
this disposition. The program's independent scientific-significance finding
is not changed to a pass by completing this comparison.

The five required ablations have precise effects: removing interface tags
admits spurious paths; indexing substitutions by component instead of
occurrence removes legal independent choices; discarding the whole loses
exactly the cross-edge bits on an anchored graph family; collapsing primitive
witnesses loses semantically distinct cuts even at the same whole and profile;
and nonparallel equations cannot descend the original profile/output map.
These failures are equally failures of the corresponding impoverished
standard baseline. No information is withheld from the full comparator.

2 Exact full information algebraic baseline

2.1 Multicategory comparison

Let A be a path-typed calculus over a component quiver J on interfaces I.
A complete color is c=(i,j,x in J(i,j)), not merely x and not just an
interface name. Let P be the ordinary colored nonsymmetric multicategory
whose operation set P(c1,...,cn;c) is A(c;p) if the list is a composable
typed path p with endpoints matching c, and empty otherwise. At n=0 it is
admissible only for a loop output; existence is still governed by A's actual
nullary fiber, not inferred from that admissibility condition alone.

Identities are A's identities. Substitution uses A's actual independently
chosen operation at each ordered occurrence. Matching output colors ensure
the flattened input sequence is a path with the same outside endpoints.
Thus substitution is well typed; both units and associativity are exactly
the accepted A laws. Conversely a multicategory with these prescribed empty
inadmissible fibers restricts to A. These constructions are inverse on
operations, units, composition and fixed-base strict morphisms. The interface
incidence assignment is retained external structure on the color set. An
arbitrary bare multicategory without that assignment does not reconstruct
the interface system. This is equivalence with the equally informative
structured baseline, not a claim that colors alone canonically reveal I.

The full source proof is OPERATOR section 1. The construction is standard
multicategory algebra. A1 occurrence positions are standard ordered inputs;
A2 free trees and A3 parallel presented quotients are standard free/presented
multicategory infrastructure. Their typed implementation is useful, not a new
categorical algebra merely because it has application-specific names.

2.2 Envelope and refinement

For such P, Env(P) has objects finite ordered color lists. An arrow q to
p=(c1,...,ck) is a division of q into k consecutive sublists qi, including
empty sublists, and an actual tuple bi in P(qi;ci). The division is retained
arrow data. Composition substitutes the corresponding subtuples into bi;
identities use singleton sublists and identities. Associativity and units
follow from P, and concatenation gives its strict monoidal product.
This reproduces Leinster's free strict monoidal category on a plain
multicategory, with its same colored and zero-arity conventions.

Fix output W. An object of Env(P)/(W) is exactly a:P(p;W). A slice arrow
a':P(q;W) to a:P(p;W) is a forest b:q to p such that a[b]=a'. Reversing
arrows gives the actual refinement arrow a to a'. Hence

    Ref(P;W) is isomorphic to (Env(P)/(W)) opposite.

The functors in both directions read precisely the same operation/tuple/
division data. Their composites are identity; identity and composition are
the same forest operations. This proves the comparison, not just an analogy.
Zero input entries, repeated equal colors and nonthin parallel forest arrows
are present on both sides. Erasing a division or remembering only endpoints
would not be this baseline. This is OPERATOR section 2, independently checked
against the primary construction in the literature audit.

The local equation-path completion has a further proved comparison
Comp isomorphic to Int(G;W), where an arrow is a forest F followed by an
operation isomorphism T[F] to U, modulo pointwise entry isomorphisms. The
seven bare mixed input relations and outer whiskering derive the general
entry compatibility; this is not assumed. OPERATOR sections 3 and 4 and COMP
prove the result. Their specialized finite local relation basis and its
effective normal forms are the additional supplied presentation theorem.
They present the standard forest construction rather than invent it. Trnka's
constant-free uncolored statement is not silently imported for colored
nullaries; the direct proof supplies those cases. No claim about arbitrary
quotients commuting with slices is made.

3 Restriction species and relation baselines

3.1 Ordered graph restriction species

For a finite set S, let R(S) be the SET of pairs (a total order on S, a
simple undirected graph on S). For any injection f:T to S, pull back the
order and graph along f. This is a valid total order and simple graph on T;
identity and composite pullbacks agree literally. Regard these sets as
discrete groupoids. Thus R is a restriction species on all finite sets and
injections. It is not restricted to contiguous subsets.

The standard layered construction has degree n objects (S,<,E,l:S to [n])
and bijections respecting every field. The map l allows empty fibers.
The unique increasing enumeration e:[m] to (S,<), where m=|S|, sends this
object to the literal ordered graph (m,e inverse E) and ordered blocks
B_i=e inverse(l inverse(i)). A bijection respecting the total order is
forced; consequently every object has trivial automorphism group and each
component has exactly one such standard representative. Conversely an
ordered graph on [m] and its block list is an object of this groupoid. These
give an equivalence to the discrete set of C1 untyped flat cuts in degree n.
This is NOT an equivalence from the species of unordered graphs: that
unframed groupoid has nontrivial automorphisms. The added total order is the
same information already retained by the original ordered graph, not a
benefit denied to a comparator.

Inner faces merge neighboring layers. Outer faces discard an end layer and
take the induced graph on the remaining vertices. Degeneracies insert an
empty layer. Standardization after restriction uses the increasing
enumeration of the retained subset. The identity
e_(e_U(V))=e_U composed with e_V proves that standardization commutes with
every iterated restriction. It follows that the degreewise equivalences
respect all simplicial maps (with the unique natural identifications in the
groupoid model). This is C1 section 2 and F2 sections 2 and 3 expressed in
restriction-species notation. No cross-edge is deleted from the retained
whole of a cut; only specified outer-face restriction removes vertices.

For any small category K, a typed degree n cut adds a full composable string
A0 --h1--> A1 ... --hn--> An, with output grade hn...h1. This is exactly
the degree n nerve N(K). Therefore the full typed degreewise identification
is X_n = OrderedGraphLayers_n times N(K)_n, with componentwise simplicial
maps. It includes n=0, the empty graph and an actual object of K. This
separate factor checks interfaces rather than attributing arbitrary grading
to the graph theorem. Products preserve the relevant pullback diagrams,
and the category nerve is unital 2-Segal (indeed Segal); alternatively F2
gives the explicit all-triangulation root reconstruction on exactly these
data. An optional graph size bound m<=N is preserved by every restriction
and gluing of triangle records whose root already retains the whole. It is
not a truncation in arity or degree.

The operation fiber at prescribed principal edges and long edge is exactly
C1 FlatCut.Operation. A tree of triangle cuts grafts by lifting every child
block through its parent's increasing enumeration, in ordered pair (i,j)
position. That is C1 substitution and the standard layered-tree construction.
For example, a retained whole G split into B1,B2, then splitting B1 into
C11,C12 gives (e_B1(C11),e_B1(C12),B2), including empty pieces. Associativity
is the same enumeration identity above. This explicitly reproduces the
established construction without a changed hypothesis.

The scalar-enriched C5 family is not automatically the same simplicial
construction: scalar multiplication may have nonunique factorization.
SEM identifies its algebra as the full-profile product with the scalar
operad, and refinement as the corresponding typed pullback. The additional
presentation/refinement/transport and factor-fiber formulas are supplied
specialized results; general 2-Segal or CULF conclusions require their own
proved hypotheses. C4 does not infer them from the word operad.

3.2 Relations and witness-aware spans

Fix interface labels i and arbitrary finite carrier sets Xi. Complete
horizontal components are (i,j,R subset Xi times Xj). The equally informative
baseline is Rel WITH this named carrier family retained. Precisely, take
objects to be labels i and vertical arrows (i,j,f:Xi to Xj), and use the
corresponding Rel fibers. This is the labeled base change of Rel along
i maps to Xi, with all names retained even when carriers coincide or are
empty. The comparison is a bijection on each fixed fully labeled cell fiber;
it is not an object-injective equivalence with bare Rel when distinct labels
have identical carriers. In this indexed baseline, horizontal arrows are
relations and a cell from R1,...,Rn to W with outer maps f,g exists exactly when

    R1(x0,x1) and ... and Rn(xn-1,xn) imply W(f(x0),g(xn))

for every actual chain; its fiber is empty or singleton. For identity
boundaries this is exactly C2's C(p) subset W containment operation. Its
marked exact subcalculus uses C(p)=W. Both constructions give the same
fibers, units and substitution, not just the same endpoint outputs.
Substitution is valid by existential composition and implication; equality
of the parallel thin cells then proves the laws. The empty path composite
is the diagonal at its ONE actual interface.

Restriction of R along f:X' to X and g:Y' to Y is R(f(-),g(-)). This is the
same relation and comparison as C2 R4. The comparison from the composite
of pulled-back pieces to the pullback of the composite is always inclusion.
For positive arity it is equality if all internal maps are surjective; for
arity zero equality is equivalent to injectivity of the single boundary
map. These are proved C2 results with those exact hypotheses, not a generic
claim that restrictions preserve exact composition. The nullary exception
and surjective noninjective example are retained in the finite checks.

For a span A <- E -> B the map to the relation of pairs with at least one
realizing e is support. Rel records existence, not the number or identity
of witnesses. The standard Span baseline retains E, its legs, actual
compatible tuples and tuple maps; it is the equally informative comparison
when those data matter. GraphSpanModel sections 1 through 6 add full graph
partitions and scalar values to those standard span multicells and check
restrictions/units explicitly. C4 does not contrast a witness-aware model
only against thin relations and call the lost witnesses an exclusive new
capability. Full graph/span baseline data are allowed and the same.

4 Interface ablation

Define an explicit erasure on the C2 family where all interface carriers
are the same two-element set X. Erase ONLY labels i,j from each component
(i,j,R), leaving the relation R. On a listed input/output record erase every
interface label and then test composability as ordinary relations on X.
The result is a well-defined record map, not a faithful typed calculus
isomorphism. Every valid typed cell gives a valid untyped relation cell;
the converse is false.

Proposition IF. Let labels be distinct 0,1,2 and D=diagonal_X. The typed
record ((0,1,D),(2,0,D);(0,0,D)) has no composable input path, since 1 is
not 2. Its erased record (D,D;D) is an exact relation cell, because D;D=D.
Thus erasure followed by untyped completion introduces a spurious admissible
operation. Also (0,0,D) and (1,1,D) erase to the same component, so no map
from erased relations alone recovers the original interface tags uniformly.
These are separate composability and recoverability failures. Proof is the
displayed endpoint mismatch and equality of the erased data. More generally
every nontrivial family of tags with identical carriers has the latter
collision, while a one-tag family need not lose tag information.

Positive boundary: retain complete component colors and the interface
incidence assignment as in section 2. Then the path admissibility predicate
and every typed fiber are recovered exactly. No standard colored
multicategory is deprived of these fields. Label erasure may be safe for a
specified label-invariant query, but not for reconstruction of all typed
composition. Distinct equal carrier sets do not license interface identity.

5 Occurrence position ablation

Let p have finite slot set S and component-at map c:S to C. Fix for each
component a set V_c of allowed substitutions with that exact output (in
the example below the replacements even have the same full input profile).
The original admissible family is product over s in S of V_(c(s)). The
ablated component-indexed choice is product over c in image(c) of V_c,
mapped by delta(v)_s=v_(c(s)).

Proposition OCC. Delta is injective and its image consists exactly of
families constant on equal-component slots. Injection follows by choosing
one occurrence for each present component. The displayed formula proves
constancy; conversely any constant family defines its unique value at each
present component, yielding the inverse on the image. If all V_c are
nonempty and a repeated component has two distinct choices, delta is not
onto: place different choices at its two slots and arbitrary allowed choices
elsewhere. If all repeated components have singleton choices, delta is onto.
This characterizes the lost independent substitution data on this family.

The C3 six-vertex witness has edges 01,12,13,24,34,45; blocks
({0},{1,3},{2,4},{5}); and interfaces (0,1,1,1,2) in [2]. Its two middle
components are the identical nonempty two-vertex edge at id_1. Let u cut
that edge into ({0},{1}) and v into ({1},{0}). Their outputs and ordered
singleton input components agree, but their actual blocks differ.
Substitute u at the first middle slot, v at the second, identities outside.
The actual final block order is ({0},{1},{3},{4},{2},{5}). Reusing u gives
({0},{1},{3},{2},{4},{5}); reusing v gives
({0},{3},{1},{4},{2},{5}). Both differ from the legal independent result.
This is C3 section 5, checked below independently. No permutation symmetry
of the planar calculus was granted. Ordered standard multicategories have
exactly the same independent slots; they pass this control unchanged.

6 Retained whole ablation

Fix a finite ordered partition B=(B_i) of [m]. Let X_B be the pairs with
endpoints in different blocks. Erase a whole graph to its ordered induced
block graphs, retaining B, all interfaces and every leaf graph. For any
prescribed block graphs H_i, the full fiber of this erasure is in bijection
with the powerset of X_B. The inverse adds a selected set C of cross edges
to the fixed lifted internal edges of H_i. Restriction recovers each H_i
because no cross edge has both ends in a block; intersecting the rebuilt
edge set with X_B recovers C. Every pair is internal or cross, so these are
both inverse laws. This is C3 Theorem 2.1, not only one ambiguous pair.

Thus every fiber has 2^q elements, q=sum_(i<j)|B_i||B_j|. Erasure is injective
exactly when at most one block is nonempty. Whenever q>0, no uniform decoder
from the retained pieces alone can recover the whole; no standard baseline
given only those pieces can do so either. Retaining the q cross-edge bits
is sufficient. Any injective retained record must have at least 2^q values,
so a fixed-length binary record requires at least q bits. Omitting a
specified cross-edge bit while retaining only the others is noninjective;
other equally informative recodings remain allowed. A standard
restriction-species object retains the same whole and therefore has no such
loss. We do not withhold it to manufacture an advantage.

For the six-vertex B in section 5, q=15-2=13, so the full anchored fiber has
8192 graphs. G and G plus 05 are explicit different wholes with the same
retained leaves and blocks. If B is also erased, C3 Theorem 3.1 gives a
disjoint union over placements, with 6!/(1!2!2!1!)*8192=1474560 total
witnesses. The latter counts witnesses, not distinct graphs. The current
ablation isolates whole loss by retaining B; placement erasure is additional.

7 Primitive witness multiplicity ablation

Let S be the actual binary graph-cut signature. Its primitive symbols
include g_u and g_v for the two cuts in section 5. Both have output the
two-vertex edge at id_1 and ordered input the same two singleton components.
They are distinct supplied partition witnesses, not merely two names for
the same semantic cut. Their one-node trees have the same shape, whole and
frontier, but evaluate to u and v and are therefore semantically distinct.

Define the ablated signature Sbar by replacing each nonempty set of
primitive symbols at each complete input/output fiber by a singleton.
The projection S to Sbar induces the ordinary free multicategory map
F(S) to F(Sbar). It identifies g_u and g_v, so is not injective on that
fiber. No decoder of the ablated primitive tree can distinguish them.
This is a lawful parallel quotient of the primitive alphabet, not an
ill-typed equation and not the same ablation as forgetting the whole.
All color, interface, arity and occurrence fields are retained here.

Positive boundary: retain the primitive value, in this case its actual
ordered block assignment. C3 Theorem 4.1 reconstructs every raw typed tree
from output, planar shape and its primitive label at every internal address.
The structural induction treats a leaf separately from a nullary node.
Multiplicity is not universally indispensable: labels already declared
equal, or invisible to the selected query, may legitimately be collapsed.
The proposition refutes uniform faithful recovery for the stated signature.
Standard multicategories and standard spans both admit multiple operations
or witnesses at the same boundary, so the full baseline retains g_u and g_v.

8 Parallel equation ablation

Let O be the disjoint union of all operation fibers and let index:O to D
record the complete output AND ordered input profile, including interfaces.
For any equivalence relation E on O, write q:O to O/E for its set quotient.

Proposition PAR. An index map indexbar:O/E to D with indexbar q=index
exists if and only if E is contained in the kernel of index. Necessity:
if t E u, q(t)=q(u), and applying indexbar gives index(t)=index(u).
Sufficiency: define indexbar([t])=index(t); the condition makes the value
independent of representative. Surjectivity of q gives uniqueness. This
is a complete elementary criterion; no choice of a clever quotient carrier
can evade it while preserving the same index and equating the same pair.

For a concrete same-output failure take the one-color free nonsymmetric
multicategory with binary generator b. Let t be its identity leaf and
u=b(t,t). Their output is c, but profiles are [c] and [c,c]. Declaring t=u
and dropping parallelism makes the profile map impossible by PAR. In
particular substitution no longer has a fixed common slot family. Another
failure equates identity operations at two distinct colors; even the output
map cannot descend. These are separate full-profile and output controls.

Positive boundary: a parallel equivalence relation also stable under all
occurrence-indexed substitutions admits the quotient multicategory. A3
constructs the least such congruence generated by arbitrary declared
parallel equations, proves representative independence, both units and
associativity, and the universal factorization. Parallelism alone is not
sufficient for substitution descent; stability is a separate requirement.
The criterion PAR concerns only the index map and does not replace A3.
One may change colors, allow heterogeneous identifications or replace the
typing discipline, but that is a different target, not a quotient preserving
the original full profiles. No claim that all such reindexed theories are
impossible is made.

9 Source to conclusion and contribution disposition

Multicategory and refinement: OPERATOR sections 1 through 4, COMP and
Leinster section 2.3. Exact comparison maps in section 2 above. Disposition:
standard infrastructure plus specialized finite local presentation.
Restriction species: C1 sections 1 through 5, F2 sections 2 through 5,
GKT restriction-species theorem. Exact ordered graph and category-nerve
identifications in section 3.1. Disposition: attributed adaptation with
checked typed/nullary/occurrence conventions, not a new graph construction.
Relations and spans: C2 sections 1 through 7, SPAN sections 1 through 6,
Cruttwell and Shulman examples 2.6/2.7 and restriction/unit definitions.
Exact existence versus witness translation in section 3.2. Disposition:
standard models plus retained-graph/scalar specialization and compatibility.
Interfaces: new elementary IF proof, C2 nonloop-nullary and wrong-interface
source controls. Effect: unsound composability completion or unrecoverable
tags if erased. Positive repair: full color and incidence data.
Occurrence positions: OCC plus C3 section 5. Effect: only diagonal choices
survive. Positive repair: ordered original slots.
Retained whole: C3 Theorems 2.1 through 3.2. Effect: exactly q independent
cross-edge bits lost. Positive repair: the cross-edge record, not relabeling.
Primitive multiplicity: C3 section 4 and its evaluated distinct cuts.
Effect: noninjective parallel primitive-alphabet quotient. Positive repair:
actual primitive/partition data, with full syntax reconstruction theorem.
Parallel equations: PAR and actual arity/output counterexamples, A3 sections
1 through 4. Effect: original index cannot descend. Positive repair: the
proved least parallel substitution congruence and universal quotient.

Every proposed mathematical contribution relevant to these C4 comparisons
is either identified with an established construction or explicitly labeled
specialization, synthesis, exposition or implementation. The new elementary
IF/OCC/PAR arguments sharpen the evidence ledger; they are not presented as
novel theorems. Later D/E/F/I results are not given blanket originality credit
by this comparison. Their exact theorem-level assessments remain in their
own artifacts. The later parity mission comparison already permits standard
binary elimination, normal realizations and common-response methods all
the same evidence and costs; it is additional synthesis evidence, not a
replacement for any of the five original ablations.
