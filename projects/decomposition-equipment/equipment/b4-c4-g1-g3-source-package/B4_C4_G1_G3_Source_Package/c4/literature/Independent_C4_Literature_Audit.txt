Scope and conclusion

The standard colored, nullary, occurrence-sensitive monoidal-envelope baseline applies. Order-framed simple graphs give a verified restriction-species application. Further decorations and equipment comparisons require additional hypotheses. The local equation-groupoid and presentation comparisons remain separate proof obligations. This is a bounded C4 applicability audit, not an independent reproof of the program, a comprehensive priority search, or a Gate decision.

1 The strongest plain multicategory baseline

Leinster, Higher Operads, Higher Categories, arXiv math/0305049v1, Definition 2.1.1, pp. 35-36, treats many-object plain multicategories; Examples 2.1.11 and 2.1.15 explicitly include empty inputs. Section 2.3, pp. 56-58, constructs the free strict monoidal adjunction F left adjoint to U using lists of objects and operations, with concatenation as tensor. Equation (2:10) decomposes homs over consecutive ordered subdivisions, including empty parts. Proposition 2.3.1 is an equivalence with the specified category PRO, whose factorization bijection is essential, not with arbitrary monoidal categories.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305049v1

Consequently, the comparator must retain whole colors, nullaries and every occurrence. Translation section 1 identifies the specified path-compatible subclass, not all multicategories; closure of admissible operation sets under substitution is required.

The formula Ref(A;W) isomorphic to (Env(A)/(W))^op follows directly from the translation's definitions: a slice arrow a' to a is a forest b with a composed with b equal to a'. Reversing it gives exactly the refinement direction. This is a direct derivation, not a quoted Leinster theorem, and implies no arbitrary quotient/slice commutation.

2 Restriction species and decorated graph data

Galvez-Carrillo, Kock and Tonks, arXiv 1708.02570v1, sections 2.1, 4.1, 5.4 and 5.7, use restriction species R:I^op to Grpd on finite sets and injections. The construction allows pseudofunctors. R_n consists of R-structures on a finite set A together with a function A to {1,...,n}: ordered layers can be empty. Inner faces merge layers, outer faces restrict, and degeneracies insert empty layers. Theorem 5.8 yields a decomposition space and makes natural transformations CULF. Section 5.3 imposes locally finite values for numerical finiteness.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.02570v1

The graph example in section 2.1 restricts to induced graphs and permits loops and parallel edges. Cross-layer edges are lost on restriction, explaining failure of the Segal condition. These results do not automatically provide interfaces, decorations or cut-crossing witnesses. Their restrictions and functoriality must be defined. Fixed-label sets or discrete fibers must be distinguished from groupoids with relabelings and automorphisms; taking connected components generally loses symmetry. Operadic substitution needs separate justification. Translation section 5's blanket inheritance claim therefore needs qualification.

3 A verified order framed graph application

Our direct check takes R(S) to be simple graphs on S equipped with a total order, as a discrete groupoid. Every injection pulls back the graph and order: a precedes b exactly when i(a) precedes i(b). Identity and composite restrictions hold strictly, and each value is finite. Thus Theorem 5.8 applies to this ordinary restriction species over injections, not the directed variant using convex injections.

Layer objects are (S,G,<,ell:S to {1,...,n}), with arbitrary ell, not necessarily monotone in the vertex order. Unique increasing enumeration identifies these with discrete representatives on [m]; order eliminates all automorphisms. The levelwise equivalence respects layer merges, empty-layer insertions and outer restrictions followed by increasing reindexing. This is an equivalence for framed graphs, not for the unframed graph groupoid.

Independently, a product with the ordinary nerve N(K) of a category remains a decomposition space: nerves are Segal and products preserve the defining pullbacks. This applies only when the intended object really is that product, not a constrained coupling. Scalars are not automatically simplicial data.

4 Virtual equipments require restrictions and units

Cruttwell and Shulman, arXiv 0907.2460v3, Example 2.6, define V-Mat using sets, vertical functions, horizontal V-matrices and coordinatewise multicells; the nullary tensor is the monoidal unit. Rel is the Boolean instance with tensor conjunction. Example 2.7 gives Span(C) for C with pullbacks, using maps from iterated pullbacks to the target apex, with identity spans for nullaries. Definition 7.1 specifies cartesian restrictions by unique factorization; Example 7.3 realizes matrix restrictions by reindexing and span restrictions by pullback. Definition 7.6 requires all units and all restrictions for a virtual equipment. Example 7.7 includes Span(C), V-Prof and Prof(C); V-Mat is covered when V has an initial object preserved by tensor.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2460v3

Calling the audited system an equipment thus requires specified vertical maps, closure and the restriction universal property, beyond its fixed-base multicategory. Matching cell shapes or renaming structures is insufficient. Boolean relational multicells are posetal; replacing witness sets by existence loses multiplicity.

5 A witness preserving span comparator

Shulman, arXiv 0706.1286v2, Example 2.4, constructs Span(C) from a category with pullbacks: vertical maps are maps of C, horizontal arrows are spans, squares commute and horizontal composition uses pullback. Theorem 4.1 gives equivalent fibration, opfibration and base-change-object conditions; Definition 4.8 names framed bicategories. Example 4.15 identifies span restrictions with pullback and extensions with composition.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0706.1286v2

Finite sets satisfy the pullback hypothesis and span apices retain multiplicity. As an independent elementary check, the spans 1 <- {u} -> 1 and 1 <- {u,v} -> 1 have the same support relation but are nonisomorphic: their apices have different cardinalities. A full witness comparator is therefore spans or set-valued matrices, not posetal Rel.

6 Local presentation dependencies and bounded disposition

None of these four sources establishes the translation's thin equation groupoids or Comp/Int comparison in sections 3-4. Those claims require the cited local O coherence, substitution stability, derived general MIn and arbitrary-forest MOut, and representative independence. This audit identifies these dependencies but has not independently re-proved those source-local results. Entrywise compatibility cannot be replaced by equality merely of total composites.

C4 disposition: the exact envelope and order-framed graph baselines apply; further decorated restriction-species and equipment applications are conditional on their explicit hypotheses; the local presentation theorem needs its own verification. Renaming does not establish novelty, nor does failure of a deliberately weakened comparator. Conversely, this bounded audit does not establish a whole-program novelty verdict or authorize a Gate decision.
