## 10 Workstream G Semantics and observations

### G1 A typed semantics and observation hierarchy

**Class and claim:** M. Formalize the distinction between presentation equality, equality in a presented calculus, equality under a selected semantics, and equality of observations.

**Inputs:** A3, B2 and at least two interpretations of the principal models. Observations are explicitly supplied maps or families of maps, not unnamed physical measurements.

**Artifact:** A diagram of typed maps and kernel relations; the valid implications between the equality notions; and theorems giving conditions for factorization through a presentation. State whether an observation concerns a component, whole, witness, refinement arrow, or entire presentation.

**Discharge:** P, with L for the kernel and factorization facts used by the core. At least two implication converses must be refuted in substantive models, and at least one nontrivial positive separation theorem must be proved.

**Tests:** A graph observation retaining only blockwise induced graphs; a richer observation recovering cross edges; a semantics identifying two binary trees; and an observation that is not substitution-compatible and therefore cannot silently be treated as a calculus morphism.

**Dependencies:** A3, B2, C3. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Use typed maps to prove all claimed implications and the positive separation criterion, then supply the proper converses and failures in C5 models. A generic chain of kernels with no witness/observation interpretation does not pass.

### G2 Identifiability and minimal sufficient retained information

**Class and claim:** B. Determine, for specified model families and observation classes, which retained data are sufficient and necessary to distinguish the target objects.

**Inputs:** C1–C3 and G1. Targets and allowed observations must be frozen before a recovery claim is tested.

**Artifact:** A model-specific identifiability theorem and a matching impossibility or lower-bound result. For graph cuts, analyze the exact missing cross-block edge data; for refinements, distinguish access to a final operation from access to the occurrence-indexed forest.

**Discharge:** A classification on a stated nontrivial family, or a bounded obstruction plus a minimal or explicitly sufficient augmentation and a positive recovery theorem. Merely adding the complete original object as an observation does not count as a useful recovery result unless accompanied by a sharp necessity statement showing why no smaller allowed data suffice.

**Tests:** Families with multiple independently variable missing edges; occurrence ambiguity under quotient semantics; and a positive decoder verified independently of its encoder.

**Dependencies:** B4, C3, F4, G1. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** Prove necessary and sufficient retained information, a meaningful structural lower bound or exact fibre classification, and a nontrivial recovery method for the chosen observation class. Adding the entire original object, or giving one indistinguishable pair, does not pass.

### G3 Scientific use and nonclaims

**Class and claim:** M. Derive a disciplined template for applying the calculus without confusing formal structure with empirical validation.

**Inputs:** G1–G2 and all established model assumptions.

**Artifact:** One fully synthetic application case, with specified objects, witnesses, observations, recoverable quantities, and falsifiable failure tests. State what an empirical application would still need: a measurement map, calibration, noise and intervention assumptions where relevant, identifiability, and independent validation.

**Discharge:** A mathematically complete worked example and a reviewer-confirmed separation between formal consequences and empirical claims. This does not require or authorize a physiological study or collection of new personal data.

**Tests:** A synthetic ambiguity that survives all selected observations, and a deliberately overclaimed inference rejected by the stated identification assumptions.

**Dependencies:** G1, G2. **Status:** OPEN.

**Minimum passing floor:** The synthetic case must use the accepted refinement, cut, and identification results to answer an explicit question and reject an invalid inference. A hypothetical application paragraph or physiological analogy does not pass.
