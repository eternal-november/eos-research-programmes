### E5 Exact assemblies and local to global transport

**Class and claim:** B. Develop a sharp theory of marked exact assemblies inside the full equipment, including preservation and failure under substitution, internal interface reindexing, and presentation quotients.

**Inputs:** E1–E4 and D2; a precise universal-cell or exactness marking and a bounded class of allowed models and maps.

**Artifact:** A local-to-global criterion connecting the relevant comparison squares to compatibility with arbitrary finite tree substitution; a faithful exact-assembly criterion; stability or failure theorems for restriction and quotient; and a nonreconstruction obstruction that does not exclude nonexact witnesses from the equipment itself.

**Discharge:** The positive criterion must apply to a nontrivial parameterized family and be independently checkable from its structural data. 3  Full model and three distinct notions

S(N,M) uses all ordered simple graphs on at most N vertices, a finite
commutative monoid M, all finite tagged interface sets including the empty
set, and all functions. A horizontal consists of a retained graph G and an
actual span A <- E -> B. An n-source cell records an actual ordered
partition of its output graph inducing the source graphs, an endpoint-
compatible map phi from the complete compatible source-tuple span to the
output apex, and one scalar m. Repeated and empty occurrences are retained.
A nullary tuple carrier is its specified interface A, not an automatic
singleton.

Substitution lifts the actual graph blocks, substitutes tuple maps, and
multiplies every local scalar. The inherited SPAN and E3 construction
supplies the complete virtual equipment, all cartesian restrictions and
contextual universal units. Nonbijective coefficients, nonunit scalars and
cross-edge-dependent assemblies remain cells.

The three predicates on globular cells are:

    Ex_c^1    coefficient phi bijective and scalar m = 1
    Ex_c^unit coefficient phi bijective and scalar m a unit
    Ex_op     contextual opcartesian universal composition

For this full graph family, E3 proves that Ex_op holds exactly when phi is
bijective, m is a unit, and the source path contains at most one nonempty
graph entry. Thus normalized coefficient exactness is not universal
composition. A coefficient-exact cut with two nonempty graph pieces need not
be opcartesian.

A cartesian restriction cell is different again. Its apex projects from
E[f,g] = {(a',e,b'): f(a') = l(e), g(b') = r(e)} to E, and this projection
need not be bijective. Pulling back an existing endpoint-preserving
coefficient bijection preserves bijectivity. This does not make the
comparison from separately restricted pieces invertible. For nonglobular
comparisons first factor into the stated exterior pullback and test the
resulting map over the same new exterior objects, not a raw map covering
changed endpoints.

4  Faithful exact assembly from primitive tables

A valid retained planar tree specifies every local graph, span, partition,
coefficient table t_v, scalar m_v and typed child occurrence. Its flattened
map d_v from the leaves below v to its output apex is computed by
substituting its child maps and regrouping the actual tuples. Coefficient-
faithfulness means every named d_v is bijective; normalization or unit
exactness additionally tests each named local scalar.

Theorem 2.1 proves the exact finite-table criterion:

    for every named vertex v and every target witness y,
    |{z in P(children_v): t_v(z) = y}| = 1.

The proof is uniform. Compatible-tuple formation preserves endpoint-
preserving bijections by applying their actual inverses while retaining
every shared boundary equality. Local bijectivity therefore implies every
d_v is bijective by induction. Conversely, when all retained d_v and child
maps are bijective, solve the substitution equation for t_v using the child
inverses. This gives necessity at every actual retained node. The
opcartesian version further requires at most one nonempty graph child at
each node.

Empty-to-empty functions pass vacuously; empty-to-nonempty functions fail; a
nonempty-to-empty total function is invalid. Empty support does not erase
graphs, scalars or occurrence names. The criterion reconstructs the actual
witness tuple, with the whole graph and partition retained; it does not
reconstruct missing cross-edges.

Root-only exactness is weaker. Over singleton endpoints, singleton -> two
points -> singleton has bijective flattened root map while both local maps
fail. Likewise inverse nonidentity unit scalars can have product 1 without
either local label being normalized. If an original semantic refinement
retains only its evaluated occurrence operation, the correct criterion is
the recursively computed fibre count of that evaluated operation, not every
discarded primitive stage. E5 preserves I4's distinction rather than
changing that original doctrine.

5  Arbitrary finite tree transport

Theorem 3.1 gives the explicit inherited CUT construction. Normalized
coefficient-exact cells and unit-coefficient-exact cells are closed under
finite typed substitution, identities and genuine unit insertions. Actual
opcartesian cells are closed when every local graph condition also holds.
This is an induction for arbitrary finite depth, not an extrapolation from
bounded tests.

For a fixed outer cell, possibly nonexact, and a cut tree with a root
vertex, a proper factorization retains an actual coefficient bijection beta
with scalar 1 at every proper vertex. Its root coefficient and scalar may
remain arbitrary. The canonical factorization stores the flat compatible
tuple span for every retained leaf run. Every other marked factorization has
a unique endpoint-preserving comparison to it obtained by inverting its beta
tables. The fixed-root groupoid is therefore nonempty and contractible. A
vertex-free tree is available only for an identity operation, not an
arbitrary outer cell.

Contractions compose the actual beta tables; splits insert a flat run and
its flattening; unary insertions use actual tuple identities; nullary
insertions use the specified empty run and the chosen unit. Exact grafts
apply the supplied child bijections at their named occurrences. These maps
are constructed before uniqueness proves coherence.

For supplied old and new proper diagrams, the comparison at a proper vertex
satisfies

    k_v beta'_v = beta_v P(k_children)
    k_v = beta_v P(k_children) (beta'_v)^(-1).

At the root the equation uses its actual outer map gamma and does not cancel
or invert it. A supplied root must satisfy that equation; it does not follow
from exact proper nodes. For genuine scalar-labelled comparisons, the scalar
at a proper node is the product of its child-comparison scalars, and the new
root scalar is the old root scalar times its child-comparison product. An
independently supplied root must pass that equation too. Coefficient
invertibility alone makes no scalar claim; genuine unary-cell invertibility
additionally requires unit scalars, and the normalized certified doctrine
requires scalar 1.

Nonexact grafts still give directed comparisons, but not automatically
inverses or a literal new exact-proper tree. Their actual occurrence tuple
remains external graft data over the old exact skeleton.

Every route through a finite contraction, split, regrouping, rotation or
graft applies the same local function to the same finest named subtuple,
with the same graph inclusions and scalar list. Hence the comparisons
commute after the specified tuple regroupings; pentagons and all finite
rotation diagrams follow. After the boundary tests below, successive
interface changes and graft/interface squares commute too. Mixed comparisons
require the same named planar atomic frontier; an unrelated crossing cut
does not acquire a slot permutation by fiat.

The construction is a rooted cut/module statement allowing an arbitrary
root, not a fabricated simplicial object with undefined faces. Coherence
never licenses dropping an intermediate failed certificate.

6  Exact internal boundary criterion

For a nonempty path S_1,...,S_n and incidence-compatible boundary maps
f_i:A'_i -> A_i, separately restrict each S_i at its two ends. The canonical
comparison c_f maps their composite tuple span to the exterior pullback of
the old composite, retaining each old apex witness and forgetting the chosen
internal lifts.

For a fixed target tuple z with internal values a_i, Theorem 4.1 identifies
its actual fibre:

    c_f^(-1)(z) is the product of f_i^(-1)(a_i), 0 < i < n.

The comparison is bijective exactly when this product has one element for
every feasible target tuple. Unary paths have no internal factors and always
pass. Empty target support passes vacuously. Surjective internal maps ensure
existence, not uniqueness. If the old coefficient t is bijective, its
transported coefficient is the exterior pullback of t composed with c_f, so
it is exact exactly when c_f passes.

A genuine nullary unit instead has comparison

    A' -> A' x_A A',    x |-> (x,x).

This is bijective exactly when f is injective, including empty domain. It is
not the empty product from a positive path.

For one retained tree, apply the test at every named local comparison using
its actual child-composite spans. For all regroupings of a path, test every
realizable contiguous composite run, including singleton runs, the whole
frontier when unary expansion is allowed, and each permitted unit position.
These conditions are necessary and sufficient for componentwise transport of
every actual certificate through all finite cut trees, contractions,
rotations and permitted unit insertions. Necessity exposes each run as a
retained proper node; it is not inferred from the root.

The adjacent-pair shortcut fails on a three-span path with singleton
endpoints, two internal {0,1} interfaces, and its unique old tuple using 1
at both seams. Both internal changes select 0. The two primitive adjacent-
pair targets become empty after their exterior restrictions and pass
vacuously, but the full old tuple has no lift. The next actual composite-
node or all-run test detects the failure. This is an inherited CUT/D2/I4
control, not a new example.

7  Noninvertible positive families and relational controls

Choose actual subsets U_i of finite interfaces A_i and the inclusions U_i ->
A_i. Let every participating span leg land in the corresponding supported
subset. Each feasible old internal value then has exactly one lift; the
inclusions are injective, so genuine unit insertions pass too. Composite
spans retain the support condition. Thus all finite exact trees and
permitted units transport coherently. A proper supported inclusion with
nonempty participating support is genuinely noninvertible.

Maps may also duplicate values outside every feasible support while each
supported value has exactly one preimage. This is a positive-arity repair,
but a noninjective map still forbids the nullary unit insertion there. That
restriction is essential.

For relations R and S, restriction after relational composition contains
composition after restriction. At an exterior pair let W be the set of old
middle witnesses. Equality holds exactly when nonempty W has at least one
witness in the image of the middle map. Uniformly for a binary comparison,
this is equivalent to an empty primed exterior or a surjective middle map.
Relation equality therefore needs some liftable witness per supported
exterior pair; actual-span exactness needs one lift of every retained old
tuple.

A missing-value inclusion makes containment strict. A surjective
noninjective map 2 -> 1 gives relational equality while duplicating the
actual span witness. With all graphs empty, the old coefficient-exact
assembly is truly opcartesian but its internal transport is not. All
cartesian restrictions still exist. These examples demonstrate the required
failure inside the full equipment, not failure to construct the equipment.

D2's complete relation-quiver criterion remains intact: uniform positive-
operation transport requires all primed carriers empty or every map
surjective; with all nullaries, all primed carriers empty or every map
bijective. The supported family is a declared restricted repair, not a
contradiction of that global obstruction.

8  Generated quotient equations and the complete equipment guardrail

The quotient theorem uses the complete concrete all-finite-span signature,
not merely original C5 names. It fixes objects, vertical maps, horizontal
spans, full profiles, graph partitions and occurrence positions. Let H be a
subgroup of the units of M, and let I be an induced-upward graph family:
whenever an induced subgraph is active, its retained whole is active. This
is an independently checkable finite incidence condition.

The actual unary generating templates are:

    D((G,S),h) = identity, for every span S, nonempty G, h in H;
    s_0 = s_1 : (G,S) -> (G,S[2]), for every active G.

Here S[2] has doubled apex E x {0,1}, both sections have scalar 1, and the
legs ignore the bit. No scalar template is imposed at the empty graph. These
are equation schemes over finite spans, not all desired semantic equalities
declared as axioms.

Theorem 6.1 proves that the generated substitution congruence identifies
precisely cells with the same partition, the same scalar for empty output
graph or the same H-orbit otherwise, and the same coefficient unless the
output graph is active. Upward activity makes this relation stable under
every typed substitution. Conversely scalar changes are generated by
postcomposition with D_h; any two active coefficient maps are obtained from
the doubled-apex section equation followed by the map selecting the two
desired outputs. No other equality follows by the soundness direction.

Upwardness is necessary in the stipulated all-or-nothing graph rule: an
active induced child can carry two identified selectors while an inactive
whole reads their different bits. Their substitutions would be distinct at
the whole. This is ACT's exact family boundary, not a classifier of all
possible congruences.

The quotient remains a full virtual equipment. Restriction and contextual-
unit factorization preserve the same graph and scalar data and give mutually
inverse coordinate rearrangements on coefficient functions, so both
directions descend. More generally, accepted E2 Theorem 6.1 proves this for
every fixed-sort substitution congruence of an already complete virtual
equipment. E5 does not claim that a quotient of this kind breaks cartesian
uniqueness.

9  Sharp marking descent and intrinsic quotient composition

Theorem 6.2 separates representative invariance from the quotient's own
universal cells. In the stated family, unit-coefficient exactness and the
prequotient opcartesian predicate are representative-invariant exactly when
I is empty. Literal scalar-one coefficient marking is invariant exactly when
I is empty AND (H is trivial OR N = 0). At N = 0 the nonempty-graph scalar
equations have no instances, but activation of the empty graph must still be
excluded.

Scalar-only quotients preserve and reflect coefficient bijectivity and
scalar unitness. Their intrinsic opcartesian criterion is the original one:
at most one nonempty graph source, bijective coefficient and unit scalar.
Multiplication by a unit cannot turn a nonunit into a unit. A nontrivial
scalar equation 1 = h nevertheless identifies normalized and nonnormalized
representatives.

At an active graph, the doubled-apex equations identify identity and
constant endomaps of a two-point apex. One is an actual bijection and
opcartesian; the other is neither before quotient. Existence of some marked
representative is a different predicate and does not reflect every
representative or recover a chosen bijection.

Intrinsic quotient opcartesianness still requires at most one nonempty
source graph and a unit scalar. At an inactive output the coefficient must
be bijective; at an active output it need only connect spans with equal
endpoint support. A reverse endpoint map can then be chosen fibrewise, and
the active equation identifies its composites with identities. This support-
level inverse is not an actual retained witness bijection.

A separately selected nonnullary universal cell survives a congruence
precisely when insertion remains cancellable in every surrounding context. A
generator-level proof must apply the inverse source-compression operation to
every contextual instance of every generating equation and derive the
resulting pair from those equations. The scalar-only family verifies this
concretely. Testing only bare uncontexted equations is insufficient, and no
general congruence-decision algorithm is claimed.

Section 6.4 also gives a nontrivial quotient preserving the literal
normalized mark when N >= 1. For each d >= 2 take the truncated-power monoid
{1,a,...,a^(d-1),0}, with a^d = 0. At every nonempty graph and concrete span
impose D_a = D_0. The generated scalar classes are exactly {1} and the
nonunit ideal {a,...,a^(d-1),0}; the empty-graph scalar relation stays
literal. Coefficients and partitions do not change, so all three marks
descend and reflect, and actual normalized exact cells are not identified
with each other. At N = 0 there are no such equation instances and the
specialization is equality. This is a generated structural positive family,
not an invariance assumption.

That family does not lift every quotient refinement at fixed actual
endpoints: outer scalar a, identity child and target scalar 0 satisfy the
quotient equation, but the child's identity class cannot change the actual
composite a to 0. In the inherited graph-indexed scalar-kernel
classification, universal fixed-endpoint refinement lifting instead forces a
unit-subgroup orbit family. For N >= 1 its literal scalar-one marking
descends only for the trivial subgroup. Thus nontrivial normalized-mark
preservation is available after dropping that stronger lifting demand, not
by claiming both incompatible conclusions.

The all-span equation family here is explicitly larger than original C5-only
scalar equations. KERNEL and BRIDGE prove the latter's original-profile
kernel and conservative passage through chosen virtual and ordinary
envelopes. They do not prove a complete kernel for every new all-span
horizontal or every added envelope cell. E2's corrected full-equipment
stability supersedes any older overly broad warning that substitution
closure alone never preserves complete-equipment restrictions or units; the
valid original-core bridge results remain credited.

10  Certified repair and the exact unmarked boundary

At H = {1}, fix an outer operation and its entire retained occurrence
profile. ACT's naive quotient cut object keeps an actual beta at an inactive
proper node, but only a class admitting a bijective representative at an
active proper node. Its morphisms still retain actual prequotient endpoint-
preserving span bijections. This is not the intrinsic isomorphism groupoid
of the quotient.

The automorphism group at such an object is the product, over active proper
vertices and endpoint pairs, of the symmetric groups of the corresponding
apex fibres. Contraction deletes the contracted factor and is an equivalence
exactly when that factor is trivial. Hence every naive contraction over
every realizable tree of that fixed profile is an equivalence exactly when
every active realizable run composite is endpoint-thin: each endpoint fibre
has size at most one. Singleton, empty, repeated and whole-frontier runs are
included where allowed. An arbitrary new graft can create new runs; their
endpoint-thinness must be rechecked rather than inferred from the old fixed-
profile criterion.

Retaining the actual intermediate beta tables repairs the missing
information. Requiring the literal equation h_v beta_v = beta'_v
P(h_children) recursively forces the unique comparison, giving a nonempty
contractible certified groupoid and the coherent contractions, splits and
grafts of CUT. In the stated finite equivariant marking model, retaining the
original morphisms requires exactly the product of the endpoint-fibre
factorials many marks for a contractible repair. This is not a universal
bit-complexity lower bound for every possible doctrine.

The scalar-only inherited repair separately retains the H-valued local unit
labels at positive proper nodes and adjusts the actual root label to keep
the flattened witness fixed. If the root scalar is fixed independently, its
H-stabilizer determines the product constraint. Fixed-endpoint lifting is
existence, not faithfulness: the distinct actual two-child tuples (1,1) and
(h,h^(-1)) can have the same composite and quotient tuple. Retained scalar
labels and gauge tuples distinguish them; quotient labels alone do not.
Scalar memory neither repairs a failed lift fibre nor makes a noninjective
nullary diagonal invertible.

An active root can report support-isomorphism while an inactive proper
composite has a two-to-one comparison. Thus root quotient exactness cannot
replace actual runwise certificate tests. Nonexact roots and witnesses
remain available throughout the repaired marked action.

11  Nonreconstruction and the ordinary completion boundary

Two singleton graph pieces over singleton spans admit normalized
coefficient-exact cells into both an edgeless two-vertex whole and a whole
with one cross-edge. Their pieces, coefficient tables and scalars agree. The
whole cannot be reconstructed from those data. Neither assembly is
opcartesian in the full single-graph equipment, because a common
representing whole would need unary factors to two distinct graphs, while a
unary partition forces graph equality. This is genuine for N >= 2, not a
size-bound artifact.

Coefficient-faithfulness therefore retains the whole graph and partition as
data. The quotient also retains them. Constant coefficient maps, nonunit
scalar cells and arbitrary rooted outer cells are not discarded merely
because they are nonexact.

Through the faithful tagged realization of an original finite interface
category, original C5 components are graph spans. On each original full
profile the left endpoint forces the coefficient, and the right endpoint
equation recovers the grade-composite equation. Graphs, partitions, grade
paths, scalars, repeated and empty occurrences, and actual refinement tuples
are recovered. Coefficient activation adds no new equality where that
coefficient was already forced; scalar equations add precisely the declared
original H-orbit identifications.

Accepted E4 completes the free chosen C5 virtual envelope by adding genuine
horizontal composites, while conserving original profiles and refinement
tuples. Its ordinary separator has graph words and actual tuple spans; the
empty word is not the one-entry empty graph. An old cut factors through a
new universal path composite, but its unary factor need not be invertible
and does not erase cross-edge data. This is not a normal completion of the
entire concrete single-graph model by singleton words, and E5 does not
reinterpret E4 as one.

The finite checks do not prove every tree, every contextual universal
