### 2.2 Companions do not assert the additional comparison invertibility

Sections 1.1–1.2, p. 5, give pullback/dependent-sum/dependent-product functors and Beck–Chevalley isomorphisms **for a cartesian square**. ## 5. The three comparisons E5 must not collapse

The following is analysis of the retained concrete data, not attribution of these model-specific statements to the primary papers.

For `R⊆A×B`, `S⊆B×C`, and maps `f:A'→A`, `g:B'→B`, `h:C'→C`, fix `(a',c')` and let

    W = {b : R(f(a'),b) and S(b,h(c'))}.

Composition after restriction requires `W∩im(g)` nonempty; restriction after composition requires `W` nonempty. Thus existence equality needs one usable middle witness, not lifting of every old witness. A surjective g suffices for every positive relational composite; it need not preserve actual witnesses or nullary units. A nonsurjection can still work on a supported family.

For actual spans and a fixed old feasible tuple with specified exterior lifts, the comparison fibre is

    product over internal occurrences i of f_i^{-1}(a_i).

Each **retained old tuple**, not merely each endpoint pair, must have exactly one lift for a bijection. An empty target passes vacuously; unary arity has a singleton internal product. Nullary arity instead compares `A' → A'×_A A'`, so requires injectivity. Restriction of an existing endpoint-preserving bijection is a bijection, but it does not identify the domain with the composite of individually restricted source spans. That second identification is exactly the extra comparison under test.

In ACT's active coefficient quotient, a unary comparison may be invertible merely because its endpoint supports agree. An actual β marking is stronger, and need not descend as a predicate on those quotient classes. A repair retaining actual β data restores that information; an intrinsic support theory deliberately forgets it. Neither should be described as the other.

This also proves why mere companions/conjoints cannot discharge E5: even the full finite-span equipment already has them and all cartesian restrictions, while a binary internal `2→1` map duplicates a seam witness and a missing-value inclusion deletes one. Its ordinary ambient universal structure exists in both cases; the desired internal comparison is not invertible. The counterexamples occur inside the declared equipment, not by failure to construct it.

## 6. Exact residual proof obligations, not novelty claims

### 6.1 Specify what “faithful exact assembly” retains

The theorem must name the comparison and the information it preserves: fixed whole, ordered partition, complete profile, old apex tuple, actual β map, local/global scalar, and/or complete occurrence-refinement tuple. A root equality cannot recover arbitrary nonexact graft tuples. Likewise contractibility of abstract `D_T` fibres cannot by itself recover a discarded β or prove that a supplied comparison map is invertible. CUT 174–178 already states both restrictions.

For genuine opcartesian marks, contextual source insertion gives faithful factorization in each declared context; in the concrete positive class the graph condition, actual coefficient bijection and scalar normalization are separately testable. For the wider coefficient-exact mark, the theorem must explicitly refrain from inferring reconstruction of cross edges or horizontal universality. Any stronger faithfulness assertion needs a new argument beyond the supplied coefficient criterion.

### 6.2 Quotient universal structure, quotient marking and witness reflection differ

For an already opcartesian `e`, insertion in a fixed prefix/suffix is a bijection before quotient. It remains surjective after a substitution congruence. Its quotient injectivity is exactly the cancellation/inverse-compression condition of E2 §6.1. That is a general criterion, but a positive family must verify it from its structural equations rather than merely assume that the desired map is still a bijection.

Separately, an old predicate can descend **with reflection on every representative** only if it is constant on congruence classes. Taking classes which possess an exact representative is a different existential marking. ACT's active identity/constant example violates representative invariance while preserving the full equipment and original-core cells. This is an appropriate negative branch; falsely claiming that its cartesian restrictions fail would contradict the inherited theorem.

For actual faithful certificate retention, the all-tree endpoint-thin criterion and the β repair are already available from ACT. For scalar equations, the theorem must identify whether scalar 1, all units, or another chosen class is marked: identifying 1 with a nonidentity unit breaks reflection of the normalized mark even if intrinsic scalar invertibility survives. Declare the allowed equation family before asserting stability.

### 6.3 Keep the exact all-tree domain and positive family

Quantify over incidence-compatible named diagrams, actual child/composite spans, and every allowed nullary insertion. Necessity of local tests is justified for a **retained diagram containing those components**, not automatically for its flattened root map. Closure under arbitrary finite substitutions must include newly realizable runs; a fixed-profile check does not certify all future graphs/spans. Supported-subset inclusions plus identity spans are a nontrivial already-proved positive family; upward graph activation is an independently checkable already-proved quotient family. A final E5 result may combine these with a faithful marking condition, but must state and prove the interaction, rather than count each inherited theorem again.
