Learned local certificates under shared priority arbitration
Research report 31 October 2026

RESULT

There is a conditional local-to-shared transport construction for an
explicit anonymous callback interface. Separately learned intrinsic work and
FIFO facts remain usable when modules share one primitive arbiter, provided
the wiring preserves their work law. Isolated wall-clock completion promises
do not survive contention. The shared completion model must instead be
derived from the arbiter's actual event rule and the retained joint
evidence.

For one all-ready productive job per module, a sound lower work bound
greater than the callback-lag bound makes callback order equal service
order. Learned priority comparisons then define a partial order. The main
result is an exact O(n^3) test for whether every linear extension of that
partial order assigns the observed bits to the same physical role-parity
vector. It can certify the mission without identifying the full priority
order or every report's origin. A four-job physical example is accepted by
this test but not by the earlier all-perfect-matchings test, even when the
matching graph uses exact marginal possible ranks. The gain comes from
retaining joint precedence constraints, not from merely narrowing
independent arrival windows.

The result also includes a limitation and a repair. There are n!
shared-priority worlds with identical complete local, drained observations
and valid identical grant-conditional local certificates. Under the
one-commit-per-tick protocol and its tight deadline, local information alone
gives optimal worst-world missed-or-unsafe risk 1-(n+1)/2^n. With an empty start and exactly the
required useful jobs in the production manifest, a hard-safe controller
completes with probability at most 2^(1-n). Safe joint fast/slow timing
probes distinguish the missing priority comparisons and restore the burst
mission, with acquisition and resource costs charged. This is not an
impossibility result for all safe probes: informative joint probing is the
constructive repair.

These are mathematical and executable-model results, not hardware safety
certification. The fast/slow operation choice and atomic
admission-before-dispatch primitive are explicit interface capabilities. The
negative family further restricts the protocol to one atomic batch commit
per legal tick; allowing repeated separately dispatched commits at the same
tick defeats that sharp bound, including the two-module obstruction. A
separate, weaker nonoptimal bound still gives a growing obstruction for n>=3
under repeated same-tick commits. The positive poset theorem only requires
its actual all-ready batch, not this additional restriction. Fast/slow
choice is richer than the preceding anonymous FIFO interface; failure and
repair use the same newly declared interface, not an unchanged predecessor.
Positive certification permits arbitrary dependent binary payloads and
bounded lags. The fair-bit assumption is used for the numerical
negative-witness risks. No general composition calculus, literature novelty,
generic polynomial joint scheduler solver, or one-mission end-to-end speedup
is claimed.

1  What is retained and what must be supplied

Each addressed module exports a FIFO of jobs. A job j has a public admission
time r_j, a known physical role rho(j), and intrinsic work d_j in a sound
local work set. Intervals [L_j,U_j] are sufficient outer bounds. A unit-rate
exclusive grant consumes one unit of work per unit time; no work is consumed
while the job is ungranted. The FIFO head completes exactly when its demand
is consumed. Every actual completion produces exactly one reliable callback
with delivery lag in [0,J]. Payloads and lags may be dependent across jobs
and modules.

A callback exposes its timestamp, binary payload and stream occurrence, but
no source, request identifier, grant record, or persistent source rank.
Addressed admissions are public actions, not callback tags. The API has no
priority-dependent immediate status or admission side channel. A distinct
supplied physical bridge connects the fresh productive bit of each job to
its actual role load and protected output requirement. For each role a, the
command is

    ell_a = XOR over required jobs j with rho(j)=a of x_j.

Every required fresh job must actually run. Probe bits cannot replace useful
work. A wrong command, refusal, omission or missed physical deadline is
failed completion. Isolation, no-actuation diagnostic gates, retention
through the entire execution and final tail, and command/actuation
capabilities are independent premises. Queue mathematics does not establish
the physical bridge.

A singleton isolated callback at elapsed time a_j gives

    L_j=max(0,a_j-J),  U_j=min(D_j,a_j),

using an independently supplied work cap D_j. Strictly positive work makes a
zero lower endpoint open. The measured interval has width at most J.

The local work/FIFO contract is grant-conditional. Under uninterrupted
isolated unit-rate service, it yields the familiar isolated timing law.
After wiring, it preserves demand and order, not a promise to finish within
the old elapsed time after admission. For example, a demand-Q job still
needs Q granted time, but may wait for another module before receiving that
grant.

Isolated measurements can bound intrinsic work only because this service
primitive is supplied. Finite timing tests cannot establish that contention,
cache effects, memory traffic, or context-dependent code leave work
unchanged. If wiring changes demand itself, the retained contract is invalid
unless a new sound work set covers the change. Work invariance,
calibration-to-production stability, FIFO, the hard lag bound, exactly-once
delivery, clock semantics and a certified empty start or independently
justified drain remain trusted or separately enforced properties.

The concrete failure/repair interface has two existing addressed operations
per module: fast P_i with demand Q and slow D_i with demand 2Q. Choosing the
operation chooses its work demand, not its uncontrollable payload. Both are
safe to exercise under the diagnostic gate. Productive jobs use P_i. This
additional operation structure is necessary to the displayed separating
probe; it is not inferred from anonymous callbacks or supplied secretly
during repair.

2  Deriving the shared model from the wiring

Wire the local FIFO heads to one known unit-rate, work-conserving,
nonpreemptive server. Its unknown strict module priority theta is persistent
and belongs to a declared finite family. Arbitration depends on module
labels and readiness, not hidden payloads. Each declared atomic batch is
queued in full before its idle-resource selection. No external traffic or
unexplained carry-in is allowed. Where several commits share a numerical
timestamp, their public event order still matters; a batch barrier does not
silently combine separate commits.

For a request log whose same-time requests are jointly admitted before
arbitration, let H_m contain the first unserved job of each nonempty module
after m service completions, and let c_m be the preceding completion time,
starting at the certified empty time c_0. The next event is determined by

    s_(m+1) = max(c_m, min over j in H_m of r_j),
    A_(m+1) = {j in H_m : r_j <= s_(m+1)},
    j_(m+1) = head in A_(m+1) whose module has highest theta priority,
    c_(m+1) = s_(m+1) + d_(j_(m+1)).

A ready head prevents idle time; if none is ready, work conservation waits
to the earliest head admission. Nonpreemption and unit service give
completion. This recurrence changes cross-module order and latency while
preserving every module's FIFO and intrinsic service facts. For other
per-commit event orders, eligibility must follow that actual admission order
rather than timestamps alone. One common theta is used throughout, not an
independently favorable priority for each event.

For a fixed feasible service permutation sigma, completion bounds propagate
as

    l_m = max(l_(m-1),r_(sigma(m))) + L_(sigma(m)),
    u_m = max(u_(m-1),r_(sigma(m))) + U_(sigma(m)).

Induction gives l_m <= c_m <= u_m. The readiness/priority guards must also
hold for that same global trace. With uncertain work, readiness may change;
using only a nominal permutation is unsound. An exact model retains every
feasible parameter/work trace. A conservative model may retain all
FIFO-preserving permutations and their windows, including impossible traces
but not omitting truth. Generic trace enumeration can be factorial; the
cubic theorem below uses a narrower batch structure and does not solve
arbitrary shared scheduling.

For a finite request log with r_j >= c_0, a coarse closed callback horizon
is

    max_j r_j + sum_j U_j + J.

After the last release, all remaining work fits its total upper work under
work conservation. Initialization, actual request counts, drain, callback
handling and physical tails still require their own checks.

Let E be the retained diagnostic record. Keep every parameter/work
realization consistent with E under the same shared semantics and
safe-diagnostic transfer law. Sound acquisition only removes inconsistent
candidates, so the actual world remains. Running the recurrence on the
retained candidates derives a joint production trace family containing the
actual trace.

One callback per job and a closed reliable horizon yield a single actual
bijection between jobs and report occurrences. If W_j encloses all arrival
times of j in that trace family, the graph with edge j--v when timestamp t_v
lies in W_j contains every edge of that actual bijection. The imported
all-matchings parity test is therefore sound on the newly derived graph. The
old isolated graph is not transported unchanged. Graph disagreement means
UNRESOLVED, not two physically feasible worlds or an all-policy
impossibility.

3  The batch specialization and its exact parity question

There is exactly one prescribed productive job per module. All n jobs must
be ready before the first arbitration. Atomic batch admission, or an
equivalent admission-before-dispatch barrier, is a supplied and costed
capability. Several sequential calls bearing the same numerical timestamp do
not establish it: the first call might already start a lower-priority job
nonpreemptively.

Suppose L_j>J for every productive job. Consecutive completions differ by
the next job's work, and consecutive callback arrivals differ by at least
L_j-J>0. Thus raw callback order equals service order despite arbitrary
dependent lags. This rank is derived for this batch; no stable source rank
has been assumed. The lag convention is one-sided [0,J], not symmetric
timestamp error; a different error model needs a different separation
condition.

Let P be the strict partial order of acquired priority comparisons, where
x<y means that x has higher priority and is served before y in the all-ready
batch. Reject cycles. Write LE(P) for all linear extensions, namely the
total orders respecting every comparison. The actual persistent priority
belongs to LE(P). If comparisons are the only retained restrictions on an
otherwise unrestricted strict priority, every extension is an allowed
priority too. Other diagnostic records may impose additional joint
restrictions; ignoring those produces only a sound outer model, not an exact
physical converse.

Let b_1,...,b_n be the observed binary payloads in callback rank order. For
sigma in LE(P), define

    V_a(sigma) = XOR over ranks k with rho(sigma_k)=a of b_k.

The question is whether V(sigma) is the same for all extensions. Exact
production timestamp values can remove some extensions, so this is exact
within the poset abstraction, generally only sufficient for the full timed
physical record. It retains one common total order rather than independently
choosing each job's possible rank.

4  The exact cubic criterion and proof

For two incomparable jobs x,y, let Pred and Succ denote strict transitive
predecessors and successors. Define

    D_xy = Pred(x) union Pred(y),
    U_xy = Succ(x) union Succ(y),
    a_xy = |D_xy|+1,
    z_xy = n-|U_xy|-1.

Use one-based ranks. The jobs x,y can occupy adjacent ranks k,k+1, in either
order, in some extension exactly when a_xy <= k <= z_xy.

Necessity follows because every predecessor of either job must precede the
adjacent block, and every successor of either must follow it. For
sufficiency, D_xy and A=jobs outside U_xy union {x,y} are order ideals, with
D_xy contained in A. Topologically order D_xy and then A outside D_xy. Its
prefixes extending D_xy give an ideal I of every size from |D_xy| through
|A|. Choose |I|=k-1, append x,y in either order, and topologically order the
remaining jobs. All predecessors of x,y are already in I, and neither job
precedes the other. This constructs the two required complete extensions,
including both interval endpoints.

THEOREM  The role-parity vector V is constant on LE(P) if and only if every
incomparable cross-role pair x,y satisfies

    b_k = b_(k+1) for every integer k in [a_xy,z_xy].

To prove it, swapping adjacent incomparable x,y at ranks k,k+1 changes V by

    (b_k XOR b_(k+1)) times (e_rho(x) + e_rho(y)) over F_2,

where e_a is the unit vector for role a. A violated condition therefore
gives two explicit valid extensions with different vectors, using the
construction above. Conversely, any two extensions are connected by adjacent
incomparable swaps. Move the next desired job left toward its position in a
target extension; every crossed job is incomparable, since a comparable pair
has the same relative order in both extensions. Repeat after fixing each
prefix. Under the criterion every swap preserves V, proving sufficiency.

An implementation first computes transitive reachability in O(n^3) Boolean
operations and rejects cycles. For each incomparable pair, counting the two
set unions costs O(n), hence O(n^3) in total. An available optimization
precomputes prefix sums of transition indicators t_k=[b_k differs from
b_(k+1)]; each interval's no-transition test is then O(1). For fixed P,
compile the intervals before production and test a new word with O(n^2)
interval checks. The supplied checker instead directly scans feasible
adjacent ranks; this is still O(n^3). These are algorithmic bounds, not
measured runtime guarantees. One topological order gives the unique vector
on acceptance. If a particular target vector was specified, that reference
vector must also equal the target; invariance alone does not prove the right
target. A failure returns the pair, rank and two topological orders with
different directly recomputable vectors. Reachability storage is O(n^2) bits
in the elementary model. Encoding, finite arithmetic and actual
implementation costs must be charged; the graph-operation count is not a
hardware WCET bound.

For every possible payload word, the criterion holds exactly when every
incomparable pair has the same role. Same-role swaps always preserve parity.
A cross-role incomparable pair has a nonempty adjacent-rank interval; a word
with a transition at one of those ranks violates the criterion.
Consequently, uniform mission sufficiency may leave within-role priority
entirely unknown. Fixed-payload sufficiency can be weaker still because
equal neighboring bits can make some cross-role ambiguity harmless.

Accepted vectors are physically correct by actual-priority inclusion and the
batch-rank proof. A rejected poset certificate proves only nonconstancy over
LE(P). Its two extensions become genuine same-record physical witnesses only
if both reproduce the entire retained diagnostic record and production data.
For the common-demand fixture below, every extension does so: all productive
work is the same tau>J, jitter is zero, diagnostics retain exactly the
declared pair comparisons, and rank payloads can be coupled to give the same
word. A common tau merely lying in marginal work intervals is not by itself
enough to establish this compatibility with earlier evidence.

5  A strict improvement over exact marginal ranks

Take jobs 0,1,2,3 with roles (A,B,A,B), priority comparisons 0<3 and 1<2,
and observed rank bits (0,0,1,1). The six linear extensions are

    (0,1,2,3), (0,1,3,2), (0,3,1,2),
    (1,0,2,3), (1,0,3,2), (1,2,0,3).

Every extension gives parity (A=1,B=1), so the poset certificate accepts.
Jobs 0 and 1 each have exact possible ranks {1,2,3}; jobs 2 and 3 each have
exact possible ranks {2,3,4}. The marginal-rank graph therefore admits
(0,2,1,3), whose parity is (0,0). That order violates 1<2 and is not an
extension. An all-perfect-matchings test on the exact marginal-rank graph
correctly returns UNRESOLVED; the joint poset test certifies the same
record.

This is a physical six-world example, not only an abstract graph. Fast/slow
pair probes test only (0,3) and (1,2) and yield those comparisons. Their raw
timing records depend only on the tested comparisons, so all six priorities
reproduce the full diagnostic history. Equal productive work tau and zero
lag give the same timestamps tau,2tau,3tau,4tau under every priority. The
rank word (0,0,1,1) is a permissible positive-probability realization of iid
fair bits; coupled by rank, it is the same observation in all worlds. Each
world has the same required parity (1,1). The impossible marginal matching
is not presented as an additional physical world.

6  Reusing evidence and refining the local contracts

Local groups may already have internal comparison orders P_a, provided these
were learned under the restriction of the same persistent global priority.
Add new cross-group comparison evidence E, take the union and transitive
closure, and reject any cycle. This reuses old chains rather than relearning
every comparison. Comparisons from another arbiter or another priority
regime need a separate transfer justification.

If sound new evidence refines P to P', then LE(P') is contained in LE(P).
Any old universal fixed-payload certificate remains sound for the same job
set, roles and observed bit word; additional evidence may enable new
acceptance. Adding jobs changes ranks and the batch itself, so recompute the
model and certificate. There is no such monotonicity across changed
missions.

There is a corresponding operational refinement law. View a local contract
as its allowed intrinsic work/output histories at the port. If C_i' permits
only histories already allowed by C_i, with the same admission, role, FIFO
and callback interface, then for the same global scheduler theta and
physical bridge,

    Traces(W_theta(C_1',...,C_q'))
        is contained in Traces(W_theta(C_1,...,C_q)).

For any refined execution, its concrete demands, outputs and delays are
allowed by the larger contracts. Replaying its public actions and the same
single theta reproduces each arbitration and completion by the recurrence.
An adaptive controller also replays after coupling its private random seed,
because identical public histories induce identical actions. If the older
model used additional joint uncertainty constraints, the substituted tuple
must preserve or refine those too; marginal local inclusion is insufficient.
For the batch rank theorem, refined lower bounds must still exceed J.

Grouping component or evidence records differently is coherent only while
retaining the same single physical scheduler, global queue labels and shared
parameter. Union and closure are independent of the grouping of evidence;
flattening tuples merely renames records in the same event recurrence.
Installing a scheduler per group, cloning capacity, or replacing global
priority with hierarchical arbitration changes the plant. No reassociation
or substitution statement covers those changes, a changed physical bridge,
or new required jobs/deadlines.

7  The local information obstruction on a growing family

For every n>=2 use the fast/slow plant with Q>0, J=0, one shared arbiter,
and unknown persistent priority pi among all n! permutations. Every
operation produces an uncontrollable independent fair bit. The productive
P_i bit is for physical role i, so all n roles are distinct.

Every complete adaptive experiment that never has pending work in two
modules at once is priority-blind. This includes unlimited singleton
measurements, within-module FIFO tests, drained serial calibration and
observation-dependent stopping. Timing depends only on the chosen demands;
bits are iid fair. Couple bits by public callback occurrence to get
identical full local histories in every priority world. All local
intrinsic/FIFO certificates are correct, yet they contain no information
about shared priority.

The admission grid is R=nQ. The n productive jobs become freshly available
at time zero. At most one atomic batch commit is permitted per legal grid
tick, enforced across all callers, with capacity for all n requests. Its
independently declared deadline is

    H=nQ+gamma, with 0<gamma<Q.

gamma pays a supplied bound for readout, checking, command delivery,
actuation and final tail. It is not free computation. Hardware may be
provisioned with that bound for the selected n, or Q may grow to cover the
implementation cost.

Every useful job must be admitted at zero: the next positive admission tick
is nQ, and even one Q job admitted then finishes after H. The useful batch
already occupies nQ service. Any diagnostic completed before that batch ends
adds at least Q and misses H. The one-commit-per-tick rule forces all
required jobs into the same batch before dispatch. Merely offering an
optional batch call would not force this: separate same-tick singleton
commits could start a controller-chosen job first, defeating even the n=2
bound. Starting from certified empty queues, the burst is therefore forced.
If residual calibration is allowed instead, reachable service endpoints are
Q-grid points and any positive carry-in costs at least Q; it cannot help.
Diagnostics queued after all useful work cannot reveal priority in time.
Calibration still may have been arbitrarily extensive, but only its
local/drained information is granted in this lower bound.

For the productive-only execution, timestamps are Q,2Q,...,nQ in every
priority world. For policies adding extra work, use this as a virtual
extension on all worlds: each actual successful record equals its
productive-only record, while counting additional virtual successes can only
raise an upper bound. The argument never conditions on actual success and
then asserts uniform priority. For a uniformly unknown pi, the observed rank
word y is independent of pi. Given a word with k ones, the required role
vector is uniform among binomial(n,k) vectors with k ones. Hence every
local-information-only policy has

    average_pi success
        <= sum from k=0 to n of [binomial(n,k)/2^n]/binomial(n,k)
         = (n+1)/2^n.

Some fixed world therefore has missed-or-unsafe risk at least 1-(n+1)/2^n. A
uniform random priority guess attains that risk in every world, so it is the
minimax risk for this fixture. The guessing policy may issue a wrong
physical command and is not hard-safe. With an empty start and exactly the
required useful jobs in the production manifest, a policy required to have
zero wrong-command probability in every world can complete only when all
bits agree, with probability 2^(1-n); completing precisely those cases
attains it. Refusal still fails the mission. For n=2 the exact minimax risk
is 1/4 and, under that productive-only manifest, hard-safe completion is at
most 1/2. A controller told pi, but not future payloads, completes the same
jobs by H with zero risk.

The obstruction concerns local information at this coarse admission grid,
one-commit-per-tick protocol and tight deadline. It does not forbid safe
joint calibration, arbitrary multiway timing probes, or serialization under
a longer authorized deadline. It does not cover arbitrarily fine, zero-cost
admission staggering or multiple independently dispatched commits at the
same tick.

7a  Allowing repeated commits at the same grid tick

Keep the coarse grid, work, freshness and deadline, but allow arbitrarily
many sequential atomic commits at zero, each dispatching immediately when
idle. The preceding sharp constants no longer apply. In particular, two
sequential singleton commits solve n=2 by forcing a known first job. The
growing local-information obstruction nevertheless survives for n>=3.

Condition on the priority-blind local history and private random seed. No
fresh callback arrives before Q, so the first committed productive subset S
is independent of priority. Its highest-priority member starts immediately;
all remaining useful jobs must be admitted at zero during that service. On
successful branches with no completed extra work, service order is the
winner of S followed by the latent priority with that winner removed. For an
upper bound, extend this productive-only order to unsuccessful worlds as
well; ignoring extra-work failures can only strengthen the controller.

If |S|=1, the first identity is known and the tail is uniformly permuted.
Its exact optimal average success is n/2^(n-1). If |S|=s>=2, each service
order has at most n-s+1 latent-priority preimages: reinsert its first winner
before the earliest remaining S member. For a rank word with k ones, a
proposed role vector is compatible with k!(n-k)! unrestricted service
orders. Conditional success is therefore at most (n-s+1)/binomial(n,k), with
the exact bound 1 at k=0,n. Averaging and combining the singleton and
larger-subset cases yields

    average_pi success <= max(2n,n^2-2n+3)/2^n.

For n>=3 this is below one and tends to zero exponentially up to its
polynomial factor. Some fixed world has missed-or-unsafe risk at least one
minus this quantity. The bound is valid but not claimed optimal. It is not
the exact minimax formula of the one-commit protocol.

For an empty-start productive-only production manifest, the exact optimal
hard-safe completion probability in the relaxed API is 2^(2-n). Force a
known first singleton and complete precisely when all tail bits agree. For
any fixed first identity, all tail permutations remain possible, so a
nonconstant tail cannot be commanded hard-safely. If the first subset has
multiple members, varying the winner also forces the first bit to match the
tail, leaving only constant full words. Randomizing subsets cannot improve
the singleton strategy's hard-safe bound for this manifest. Extra queued
diagnostic jobs can filter priority worlds through delay even if no
diagnostic finishes on successful runs. No exact hard-safe optimum is
claimed for those larger production manifests; the combined-success upper
bound still covers extra-work policies through its idealized comparator. All
these results concern local information and a coarse grid, not all safe
joint probes.

8  Safe joint acquisition and mission-aware stopping

From certified drained idle, atomically admit D_i with demand 2Q and P_j
with demand Q, for i!=j. With zero lag, the relative callback times are

    (2Q,3Q) if i has higher priority,
    ( Q,3Q) if j has higher priority.

The first anonymous arrival time alone gives the comparison. No callback
tag, payload pilot, controlled bit or role-load sensor is used. The total
service horizon is 3Q in both outcomes. The safe gate, probe tail, drained
state and unchanged production arbitration law must cover the whole
experiment.

All binomial(n,2) comparisons suffice to recover pi. Ordinary adaptive
sorting can instead use at most n ceil(log_2 n) such comparisons as a
conservative bound. A binary-comparison decision tree distinguishing all n!
priorities needs at least ceil(log_2(n!)) comparisons in its worst case.
This lower bound is only for binary-comparison experiments: unrestricted
multiway timing batches can yield more outcomes, and no optimum over all
legal probes is claimed. More generally, there are n!/(product_a n_a!)
possible role-by-rank words. A binary pair-probe tree guaranteeing uniform
role sufficiency from no order evidence needs at least
ceil(log_2(n!/(product_a n_a!))) probes on some branch. Existing evidence
changes that count. The exact first-arrival rule displayed here is only for
J=0; nonzero-lag acquisition needs its own certified separation test.

Full priority need not be the acquisition goal. Start with sound
comparisons, find any still-incomparable pair of different roles, perform
the pair probe, and repeat until no such pair remains. Each probe removes at
least its own cross-role incomparability; transitive closure may remove
more. If role a contains n_a jobs, at most

    sum over distinct role pairs a<b of n_a n_b

probes suffice from an empty comparison record. The resulting partial order
passes the uniform all-payload criterion while potentially retaining every
within-role ambiguity. Reserve the worst-case probe allowance before
observing outcomes; earlier stopping still leaves charged waiting to the
independently fixed T0. For a single global parity role, no priority probe
is needed. Existing sound group evidence can reduce the remaining queries.
For a particular production word, the exact fixed-payload test may also
accept before uniform role identification would have been sufficient. This
is mission-relative certification, not an assertion of optimal acquisition.

Ordinary drained serial production is a legal alternative: admit P_i alone
at (i-1)R and bind its uniquely isolated callback at (i-1)R+Q. It completes
by

    H_serial=(n-1)R+Q+gamma.

This repairs the task if that larger deadline is authorized and the same
hardware already supplies the longer isolation/retention lease, storage and
idle/tail resources. For n=2 it is 3Q+gamma rather than the burst's
2Q+gamma. Joint commissioning can cost more than one useful mission, so the
shorter post-release burst is not a one-mission end-to-end speedup. Repeated
batches may amortize the acquisition, but no generic amortized advantage is
asserted.

9  Complete acquisition and production accounting

Let C_init cover justified initial drain/setup, rho_probe the physical probe
tail/drain, C_probe callback processing, exact comparisons, dispatch
preparation and endpoint guards, and C_compile evidence validation and
production readiness. For mission-aware acquisition, C_probe also covers
selecting the next pair and updating closure, rather than deferring these
operations for free. Before observations, supplied caps D_P>=Q and D_D>=2Q
give H_pair=D_P+D_D. Exact already-acquired work facts, or publicly supplied
exact fixture caps, permit H_pair=3Q; unlearned simulator truth does not.
With grid R=nQ and a fixed worst-case comparison allowance m, reserve

    S_init = R ceil(C_init/R),
    S_pair = R ceil((H_pair+rho_probe+C_probe)/R),
    S_compile = R ceil(C_compile/R),
    A_joint = S_init + m S_pair + S_compile.

For unpaid local learning, singleton fast and slow probes add

    A_local = sum_i R ceil((D_P+rho_P+C_P)/R)
            + sum_i R ceil((D_D+rho_D+C_D)/R)
            + separately rounded local validation/setup.

These are pre-observation reservations from public caps, not measured-demand
horizons chosen with access to hidden work.

There are 2n local invocations, 2m joint invocations, and n fresh useful
invocations per production burst. No probe counts as required productive
work. Fix a grid-aligned commissioning deadline T0 before observing probes
and require A_local+A_joint<=T0, or explicitly identify the local learning
cost as already paid. A favorable probe history cannot retroactively move
T0.

Charge all batch construction, buffer/port capacity, pre-tick submission,
atomic-admission setup and dispatch guards. If a strictly positive physical
guard is required, include it rather than hiding it at a half-open endpoint.
Processing, tails, legal-grid waiting, compile time, command energy, storage
and any retained comparison proof also consume their declared resources. The
elementary parity checker uses O(n^2) reachability storage; a learned
priority/inverse uses O(n log n) bits and a fresh payload word O(n) bits,
apart from role encoding and algorithm working storage.

For the zero-lag equal-work burst, callbacks finish by T0+nQ. The physical
mission allowance gamma must cover all later work and final tail, and the
retention lease must cover each role's full hold time. Runtime issuance must
check the actual public current time and remaining allowance, not merely a
planned timestamp. More generally, bounded lag belongs in the callback
horizon and all post-read reservations must still fit the physical deadline.
Declared model allowances are not measured Python worst-case execution
times.

10  Executable evidence and its limits

The standard-library poset checker returns a positive invariant vector and
reference order, a target-mismatch order, or two noninvariance orders. Its
separate verifier imports no producer code. Failure verification checks the
original precedence edges and direct parity sums. Positive verification uses
independently computed reachability and the proved interval criterion; it is
a theorem-based verifier, not a second proof of the theorem.

The exact checker agrees with exhaustive linear-extension enumeration on
376,292 binary-role/binary-payload cases over all 407 naturally labeled
posets of sizes one through five. The poset counts are 1,2,7,40,357. Those
checks validate 278,644 noninvariance witnesses, all 4,127 attainable
adjacent-rank constructions, and 12,130 reverse-label cases with distinct
roles and all payload words. Dedicated cases cover a supplied correct
target, target mismatch and cyclic-input rejection. Naturally labeled means
every relation points forward in one fixed topological labeling; every
finite poset has such a labeling, but the finite tests do not separately
enumerate every possible relabeling. The written proof supplies arbitrary n
and labels.

A separately written small-DAG script checks 16,928 fixed-payload fixtures
through four vertices against full enumeration, as well as the uniform
criterion and the n=2,3,4 success-bound summations. These are overlapping
finite validation routes, not evidence of independent physical trials. An
exact relaxed-admission enumeration covers every first-subset size for
n=2,...,6 and all rank words, checking the preimage multiplicities, success
upper bounds and productive-only hard-safe probabilities against exact
permutation counts.

The shared-arbiter reference pipeline passes 60 end-to-end fixtures at
n=2,4,6,10, using identity, reverse and shuffled priorities with five seeded
payload streams each. Every fixture pays local work calibration, adaptive
comparison sorting, a predeclared commissioning deadline, anonymous
production, exact certification and independent verification, then checks
the command against simulator-only physical truth. Separate controls cover
1,108 exhaustive productive bit words with known full priority, 24
mission-aware acquisition fixtures, and the six actual worlds in the
four-job joint-poset versus marginal-rank example. The all-same-role
mission-aware cases use zero joint probes and block-role cases retain
within-role ambiguity.

An additional fixture has true fast work 75 under public upper cap 100,
learns fast work 75 and slow work 150, and uses those measurements without
changing its previously fixed T0=7200 and absolute deadline 7620. Controller
reservations use public caps, not hidden true work. The decision code has no
live empty-state oracle: initial emptiness is a supplied setup premise, and
subsequent draining follows closed upper horizons and its own request log.
Hidden priority, job identities, grant/service logs and true demands are
used only by the validation oracle. Public callbacks remain time/bit pairs.
Both commissioning and production deadlines are frozen before learning.

The simulator retains persistent queues, running jobs and clock across
probes. It enforces one commit per legal tick, even after an empty commit,
and exercises admission exactly at a service-completion boundary. Controls
also cover identical isolated histories under opposite priorities, a wrong
naive origin command, no-learning refusal, useful same-role and
payload-specific ambiguity, legal longer-deadline serialization, positive
bounded lag, rejection when lag can reorder callbacks, cyclic evidence,
extra/missing request manifests, lateness and insufficient actual remaining
allowance. Productive manifests reject extras before invocation.

For the main Q=100 fixtures, full-sort commissioning allowances T0 are
2400,7200,19200,62000 for n=2,4,6,10, with mission deadlines
2620,7620,19820,63020 respectively. Actual comparison counts are 1, 4--5,
7--11 and 15--23; total actual invocation counts are 8, 20--22, 32--40 and
60--76, including local learning and useful work. These are charged model
costs, not Python benchmarks or proof of minimum acquisition. The runtime
checks actual now+8+4<=deadline for supplied processing/command bound 8 and
physical tail 4. Those constants require an appropriate implementation; full
certify and verify calls are not a measured constant-time Python operation.

Finite tests support the supplied implementation on tested cases; the
mathematical arguments supply the general results. Independently written
checks still share model assumptions and may have correlated errors. They
are not external human peer review, proof-kernel verification, a measured
WCET analysis, or hardware validation. The implementation does not establish
physical noninterference, work invariance, exactly-once delivery, hard
timing bounds, initial emptiness, stationary arbitration,
diagnostic-to-production transfer, safe isolation, retention, or the
payload-to-role-load hazard law. Those remain premises.

Classical service-demand accounting, network calculus, scheduler contracts,
trace refinement, finite-hypothesis learning and comparison sorting are
relevant baselines, not claims of invention here. The bounded source
comparison includes Le Boudec and Thiran's Network Calculus and Regehr's
Composing Scheduling Policies. It does not establish a literature gap in
anonymous assignment or a general new compositional assurance framework.

The concrete result is narrower: preserve the correct grant-conditional
local object, derive one explicit shared arbiter's joint behavior, learn
missing cross-module comparisons with safe anonymous probes, and certify
exactly the mission's parity within a sharply defined poset abstraction.
Outside that scope, a failed certificate must not be promoted to physical
impossibility, and a passing finite trace must not be promoted to an
unconditional guarantee that arbitrary separately learned components
compose.
