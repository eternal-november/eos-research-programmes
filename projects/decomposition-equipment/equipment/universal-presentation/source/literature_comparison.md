# Primary literature comparison for original E2

Prepared 31 October 2026 for the E2 authors and independent auditor. The target is the original E2 in `Original_Completion_Contract_v0.1_VERBATIM.md`, lines 434–448. This is a bounded attribution and scope comparison, not an independent acceptance of the new proof or an exhaustive novelty search.

## Main conclusion

The term-model method, algebraic encoding of chosen universal operations, and strict-versus-coherent interpretation-category pattern are established infrastructure. They should be credited. They do **not**, without an explicit encoding and additional proofs, supply the requested virtual-equipment transformation theorem, C5 local reconstruction, original-profile conservation, or quotient-saturation analysis.

The inherited dossier already supplies much more than a free-object existence assertion. In particular, ENV gives transformation extension and coherent change of choices for its free syntax; EXT gives a reusable local graph–span presentation with a proved normal form and full original-profile recovery. E2 should import those substantive results with their actual hypotheses, correct the inherited restriction-preservation warning, and prove the new generality and saturation claims separately.

### Partial algebra and an actual category level comparison

Erik Palmgren and Steven J. Vickers, *Partial Horn logic and cartesian categories*, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic **145** (2007), 314–353. Inspected [author preprint dated 30 March 2009](https://sjvickers.github.io/partialalgebras.pdf).

* Introduction, p. 3: quotient equalities can make previously undefined partial-operation arguments defined; naive fixed raw-term quotients require care.
* Theorem 22, p. 19: the term partial algebra is initial.
* Theorem 29, pp. 22–23: a quasi-equational theory morphism induces a reduct functor with a left adjoint.
* Theorem 54, pp. 48–49: `T-PMod(D) ≅ StrCart(C_T,D)`.
* Lemma 55, pp. 49–50: unique extension of model homomorphisms to natural transformations.
* Theorem 56, p. 50: `T-PMod(D) ≃ Cart(C_T,D)` without strict choice preservation.
* Definition 63 and following discussion, p. 57: essentially algebraic domain specifications and their relation to quasi-equational theories.

**Use and limit.** This is a precise baseline for term models and strict/coherent classifying comparisons, not merely an object-level slogan. Its functors are cartesian-category functors, not Cruttwell–Shulman equipment functors; the equipment-specific encoding and transformation extension remain to be proved. In particular, Theorem 29 alone is not the required two-dimensional theorem. [Exact classifying-category comparison](https://sjvickers.github.io/partialalgebras.pdf#page=48).

### Generalized algebraic syntax

Marc Bezem, Thierry Coquand, Peter Dybjer, and Martín Escardó, *A Note on Generalized Algebraic Theories and Categories with Families*, inspected [arXiv:2012.08370v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08370v2), dated 16 March 2021. The published paper is titled *On generalized algebraic theories and categories with families*, Mathematical Structures in Computer Science **31** (2021), 1006–1023.

Section 2.1, pp. 3–4, fixes strict cwf morphisms. Section 3, pp. 5–6, defines the finite signature-building regime. Theorem 1 and its construction, Section 4, pp. 6–8, build the initial model by raw syntax, derivable partial equivalence relations, and interpretation. Remark 2, p. 6, explicitly excludes type equations from this paper's chosen presentation notion. Section 5.7, p. 15, discusses a particular extension to infinitely many symbols.

**Use and limit.** This is a verified modern primary source for generalized algebraic term syntax, crediting Cartmell. Do not silently apply its finite-signature theorem to every small dependent equipment presentation, or promote its strict initiality statement to an equivalence of equipment-functor/transformation categories. An explicit E2 syntax can establish the required small infinitary scheme directly. [Initiality construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08370v2#page=6).

### Equipment morphisms and transformations

G. S. H. Cruttwell and Michael A. Shulman, *A unified framework for generalized multicategories*, Theory and Applications of Categories **24** (2010), 580–655. Inspected [arXiv:0907.2460v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2460v3), dated 9 December 2010.

* Definition 3.1 and following paragraph, pp. 15–16: cell-natural transformations and the 2-category `vDbl`.
* Paragraph preceding Example 5.9, p. 31: normal functors preserve universal units; this is not literal preservation of selected unit objects/cells.
* Definition 7.1, p. 37: all-arity cartesian restriction and coherent comparison of choices.
* Definition 7.6, p. 38: virtual equipments and the locally full sub-2-category with normal restriction-preserving functors.
* Theorem 7.24 and following paragraph, p. 42: every full-equipment-to-full-equipment functor preserves restrictions, but not necessarily units.

**Use and limit.** Normality remains substantive; restriction preservation is automatic in the stated full-equipment setting. Neither result preserves an arbitrary additional admissible or exact marking. The E2 term comparison must use these actual transformation components and naturality, including the vertical component of an empty source. [Preservation theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2460v3#page=42).

## Required scope corrections and the remaining new proof obligations

**Restriction warning.** EXT line 283 says that bare local equations need not make either the derived restriction or unit universal among extra target cells. For an arbitrary target VDC this warning is appropriate. Once both source and target are full virtual equipments, the restriction half is superseded by CS Theorem 7.24. The independent normality/unit requirement and any literal-choice or extra-marking requirements remain. This is the scoped correction already recorded in the accepted E1 literature comparison; no inherited file is changed here.

**General syntax.** An E2 algebraic/sketch formulation must declare the actual sorts and domains, the scope of allowed parallel equations, and the factor operations witnessing the universal properties. If equations change typing data or enable partial operations, the simultaneous typing/equality construction needs proof. Calling the package essentially algebraic does not replace that work. A set-sized family of finite-arity rule instances is not a finite presentation.

**Transformations.** The category-level universal property needs its own extension/restriction proof for the declared E1 transformations and markings. Initiality in a category of underlying set-based models proves less. Conversely, the inherited ENV transformation argument should not be described as only a hom-set bijection; it already supplies the stronger free-syntax result in its original scope.

**Quotient uniqueness and its actual boundary.** The elementary cancellation criterion remains: a fixed cartesian postcomposition bijection descends bijectively exactly when equivalent composites have equivalent lifts. But the new direct proof in `reconstruction_theorem.md`, Section 6.2, Theorem 6.1 and equations (6.4)–(6.5), shows that this compatibility is **automatic for a complete virtual equipment with fixed vertical category, horizontal names, full cell sorts and boundaries**, when the parallel equivalence is closed under all ordinary VDC substitution, including nullary whiskering. Restriction inverses and contextual unit inverses are fixed ordinary substitution contexts. The quotient therefore retains restrictions and contextual units without extra `L/U` saturation. A bare inverse-bijection counterexample is not a full-equipment countermodel. This is the new package's explicit argument, independently checked in the audit; it is not attributed to a literature theorem or inferred circularly from CS Theorem 7.24, whose target already must be an equipment. The substantive saturation duty and possible original-profile cost lie in incomplete preequipment or relative marked presentations before those contexts exist, as in Sections 6.3 and 8. The result does not establish preservation of selected nonnullary `Ex_op` factor operations or arbitrary markings, and does not cover quotients changing vertical or horizontal sorts.

**Reconstruction.** A universal presentation theorem and an evaluation into a semantic model are not yet a completeness theorem. The substantive inherited C5/graph–span proof is the local normal form with retained partitions, actual witness maps, global scalars, full boundaries, and occurrence data. Additional retained vertical data and span generators must stay visible. Fullness of the concrete semantic comparison must not become fullness of the smaller free-envelope embedding by rhetoric.
