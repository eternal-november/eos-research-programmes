Partial subsystem discovery with an unobserved exterior
Research results for the approved milestone of 28 October 2026

Lay abstract

A useful mechanism can be hidden inside a messy sensor patch. Some recorded variables belong to the mechanism we need; others merely reflect an unseen surrounding system. Reconstructing everything outside may be impossible or unnecessary. This milestone gives a conditional mathematical procedure for keeping the local distinctions needed for a chosen task, discarding exterior-driven distractions, and discovering the incoming signals and memory needed to run the resulting local model.

The procedure does not assume that the useful local state or its sufficient input boundary is already known. It starts from identifiable noisy readouts, a task anchor, and explicit bounds on the local search. It either finds the coarsest task-preserving local description that passes the stated causal requirements, or produces a finite obstruction within that search universe. The exterior itself can remain unidentified and can have arbitrarily large or infinite memory.

There is an important price: an actual randomized probe must reliably excite every admitted hidden bypass, without directly changing the local mechanism. Passive agreement alone cannot provide this guarantee. The probe and its causal assumptions are additional access, not something inferred for free from observations.

A nonlinear example recovers a two-variable core and a delayed input while rejecting a contaminated variable. Exact probability-table checks support the construction. This is not yet a finite-data experiment, a robot controller, or a physically sealed replacement component. It is a task-preserving open response model with an exact dependent realization inside the original whole, subject to a clearly stated experimental contract.

What was established

The main result is constructive discovery inside a bounded, initially unknown local patch. The patch may be non-Markovian when viewed as a whole because an observed nuisance variable still depends on hidden exterior history. We do not demand closure of that entire patch. Instead, we discover a quotient: a grouping of patch states that forgets irrelevant distinctions while preserving a designated task readout.

Under the declared assumptions, finite partition refinement returns the coarsest quotient whose next-state response ignores the hidden bypass and depends only on its present quotient state and a discovered bounded input history. A second refinement turns that history into a recursively updated boundary register. Alternatively, a failed probe test certifies that no permitted deterministic task-preserving quotient exists in this patch-state universe.

This removes the need for complete sensor coverage of the exterior. It does not remove all structural assumptions, establish an unrestricted hidden-architecture algorithm, or prove that no useful physical subsystem exists anywhere. Enlarging the patch, adding instruments, allowing longer histories, or changing the task changes the universe and can remove an obstruction.

The experimental contract

Let X be the finite latent state of the instrumented patch, with d possible values. Its labels and sensor channels need not be supplied. Three conditionally independent, sufficiently informative noisy views identify its alphabet and observation channel up to relabeling, under the usual identifiable-mixture conditions. Aligned histories require the corresponding conditional independence of sensor noise across sampled times. Population identification is not instantaneous decoding of X on a real run.

Among m accessible input channels and a maximum history length L, the true local effect uses an unknown context A* containing at most k channel-and-lag slots. The exterior may choose those inputs using arbitrary history and feedback. Once the entire pre-probe history H, including current inputs, is fixed, a recorded binary probe R is randomized independently of H. A hidden binary bypass Z then satisfies

  P(Z=1 | H,R=1) - P(Z=1 | H,R=0) >= delta > 0.

The patch response has a fixed kernel F(X' | X,w_A*,Z). The probe has no direct local effect, and this complete Z captures every admitted hidden bypass acting on the patch outside the bounded observed context. The response kernel cannot secretly retain further exterior-history dependence. These are substantive causal promises, not certificates obtained by fitting the same sensor tables.

The exterior can nevertheless have unlimited internal state or memory: only its manner of affecting this patch is restricted. A more general companion theorem replaces binary Z with a finite hidden effect alphabet, or a bounded-dimensional effect space. Experimental mixtures must separate that space after conditioning on the proposed local state and inputs. Unconditional separation is insufficient.

A nonconstant task signature h(X), specified or identified through a designated readout, prevents the useless constant quotient. Every allowed quotient must preserve h. A learned task channel must have its declared conditional-memoryless timing given the latent path; matching isolated emission marginals does not preserve correlated task transcripts. An injective task can conversely prevent compression altogether. The theorem therefore discovers a subsystem for an explicit task, not an intrinsically privileged division of nature.

Why two different discovery tests are necessary

For a proposed quotient, compare its joint next-state law between probe regimes, conditioning on X and every candidate bounded input context. All such comparisons must agree. Testing only the eventual winning context could average away a hidden response difference.

At the unknown true context A*, the regime difference factors into the hidden response contrast times an average excitation of at least delta. Thus a nonzero hidden response contrast cannot disappear. Conversely, if the quotient response ignores Z pointwise, randomization makes every pre-probe-context comparison invariant. This proves bypass independence within the declared complete-effect model.

Probe invariance does not identify ordinary inputs that R leaves unchanged. Boundary discovery is a separate conditional-entropy search. Among contexts of at most k slots, minimize the entropy of the next quotient state given X, the context, and R. Once bypass independence holds, the true context attains the full-history predictive entropy. The conditional Kullback–Leibler identity then shows that a minimizing context reproduces the full response kernel almost surely.

Arbitrary new input policies require more than that almost-sure conclusion. The data must cover every joint assignment of X and the union of any two candidate contexts, containing up to 2k slots. This pair-union coverage upgrades equality on training histories to pointwise agreement with the true sparse response. If ports U and V always coincide during training, U can otherwise falsely substitute for a true V dependence and fail when a new policy separates them. Without the stronger coverage, the conclusion must stay within the covered support.

How the quotient and recursive boundary are found

Start by grouping patch states with the same task signature. Test that partition for hidden-bypass dependence. If it passes, recover its mode-independent response rows using the entropy step. Split any block whose states have different probabilities of reaching a current block for some admitted input. Repeat until there are no further splits or a probe test fails.

There are at most d minus the initial number of blocks strict refinement rounds. The proof of coarseness is an induction: every valid task-preserving quotient refines the starting partition, and states it identifies cannot be separated by any response-row split. A probe failure on an intermediate partition would also contradict every valid refining quotient. Successful termination therefore gives the coarsest robust quotient; failure supplies a finite contrast witness, not merely an unsuccessful heuristic search.

Input histories then need their own implementation test. Histories with identical current response rows are initially grouped. Refine those groups until equivalent histories remain equivalent after every admitted new input symbol shifts the raw history register. This produces the coarsest deterministic causal history quotient preserving those rows. Its boundary state updates from its previous state and the next accessible input, without consulting hidden exterior state or a noncausal history oracle.

The chosen state quotient and boundary need not jointly optimize every conceivable memory or communication tradeoff. Multiple adequate input subsets can remain. Input wires and retained memory are charged. If a port is only latent and noisily measured, the same construction describes a generative interface, not an exactly maintainable controller register. Runtime filtering and its resource costs remain separate.

What survives connection to an unknown exterior

The discovered kernel is a genuine nonnegative stochastic mechanism. It is not merely a signed predictive representation. To preserve the original whole law, draw the new quotient state from this kernel, then draw the remainder from the original conditional law given the complete past and that quotient value. Ordinary conditional disintegration preserves all correlations exactly.

The remainder includes the discarded part of the patch in the appropriate quotient fiber. Those fibers may have unequal sizes and need not form a Cartesian product. Independent resampling of local and exterior pieces would generally be wrong; the conditional construction deliberately retains dependence.

For a default generative communication contract, expose the initial quotient state and send the full new quotient state at every step. The remainder retains the residual fiber and history, hence enough information to reconstruct the old patch state. This full-state outgoing message is charged explicitly. A smaller task-output-only message needs a separate sufficiency proof.

This is inward autonomy, not bidirectional physical encapsulation. The actual exterior may still read discarded private local state through channels not restricted by the inward theorem. Replacing a physical component behind a narrower outward interface requires additional evidence. Similarly, separately closed marginal subsystems need not have a closed joint law: two fair output bits can hide an exterior-dependent correlation. Their union must pass a joint test.

Pointwise response independence survives arbitrary history-dependent exterior input policies preserving the effect and timing contract, including feedback. The probe is required during identification, not deployment: once mode independence is established, it may be removed. The same OPEN response kernel works; the unconditional local trajectory distribution need not be the same when the exterior supplies different inputs. Approximation comparisons must use the same causal input policy on the retained transcript or the same imposed input sequence. Uniform one-step total-variation error epsilon gives an H-step bound min(1, epsilon0 + H epsilon), including initial-law error epsilon0. For the learned quotient this covers retained task observations, not raw full-patch sensor traces; the latter also require remainder and observation-channel control.

The nonlinear stress family and checks

The stress construction has local latent bits a,b,y, an input v, and a one-tick delayed v register. The a and b updates interact nonlinearly and use current or delayed v. The decoy y also receives an unobserved bypass. An N-bit exterior produces v, receives feedback from a, and influences y. Its size is unrestricted; it is not an irrelevant independent decoration.

The probe changes the bypass probability by .35 at every pre-probe history. Consequently y has a .14 regime effect, while the retained joint (a,b) response has none. The task readout anchors a; dependence of its future on b forces retention of both. The discovered boundary uses v and its one-step memory. That bit can be assigned to either side of the generative interface, but must be counted once.

Exact-law fixtures for N=2,3,5 have 128, 256, and 1024 oracle whole states. The learner receives the same-sized noisy observable table, containing 20,000 entries, rather than the oracle state or transition matrix. It calibrates unknown eight-state and four-state patches, aligns their overlap, and tests 576 private binary-coordinate charts. Eight survive, representing the same factors up to swaps and flips; the nearest rejected chart has residual above .089. Two of four port quotients survive, differing only by bit complement. The joint core regime gap is below 9e-16 numerically, against the decoy gap .14. Current core, current port, and delayed port arguments all have essential contrasts above .15.

The focused fixture omits the unrelated third sensor bundle described in the family. Larger-exterior core existence and probe gaps follow analytically, not by extrapolating three runs. Uniform product-chart separation across all exterior sizes remains an explicit identification condition.

The noisy example uses a separate overlap and product-chart discovery route. Its raw patch (b,v) overlaps the main patch, so their joint raw Cartesian support fails; it is not an unqualified instance of the directly accessible-port theorem. The recovered v is a latent generative port, not a decoded runtime message. The tests exercise complementary pieces, not one end-to-end implementation.

A separate known-kernel check exhaustively examines all 225 task-preserving partitions of the eight-state example. Exactly one is valid: the four-state (a,b) quotient. It also checks unequal fibers and a joint-only leakage obstruction. Companion closure checks cover rank failure, conditional versus unconditional rank, and adaptive feedback. Independent AI review accepted the population and conditional finite-data theorems within their stated scope. These are reproducible algebra and population-law numerical checks, not finite-sample sensor experiments, Lean verification, external human proof review, or complete discharge of the broader research contract.

Limits that cannot be removed by more passive data

A hidden fair bypass feeding a bit with success probability .2+.4Z produces exactly the same passive process as an autonomous Bernoulli(.4) source. Changing the bypass distribution to Bernoulli(.1) or Bernoulli(.9) changes the first source to .24 or .56, while the autonomous source stays at .4. Even unlimited passive records with identical sensor noise cannot distinguish their replacement guarantees.

A separate finite-window obstruction uses a hidden shift register. For any tested window length, a strictly positive longer-memory process can agree with independent fair bits on all those windows and disagree later. Neither positivity nor a successful bounded-history fit establishes an unbounded-memory exclusion. The present theorem avoids reconstructing the exterior, but still needs a bounded local response description and a complete separating experiment.

A conditional finite-data stability theorem supplements the population algorithm; it is not an implemented estimator. It reuses the preceding compact three-view calibration lemma, with a finite but unevaluated constant C_cal, and propagates tensor-table error through deconvolution, conditional probabilities, entropy comparisons, and refinement. Required margins separate nonzero probe effects, response-row distinctions, task signatures, and insufficient-context entropy gaps. A confidence band around the entropy minimum must precede smallest-context selection; exact empirical minimization followed by size minimization is invalid.

For Q requested raw tables of at most J cells, N >= J^2 log(2QJ/alpha)/(2 eta^2) independent windows per table suffice for simultaneous one-norm error at most eta with probability at least 1-alpha. Choosing eta below all stated calibration, coverage, and separation thresholds recovers the population quotient and boundary, or its obstruction, up to latent labels. Overlapping trajectory windows require a justified concentration replacement; arbitrary exterior memory supplies no automatic mixing. This is an existence and stability bound, not a practical sample budget or optimizer.

Fixed patch size, sparse-context width, memory depth, and alphabet sizes give polynomial dependence on candidate-channel count, with potentially poor constants and exponential width dependence. They do not give a general polynomial hidden-architecture algorithm.

Relationship to established work and remaining limits

Influence-based abstraction already gives exact local behavior without enumerating exterior state, and influence-augmented planning already learns influence predictors. Those constructions begin with specified local factors or a supplied factored simulator. Local-module identification already handles unmeasured network nodes and correlated noise under supplied topology and excitation assumptions. Invariant causal prediction already links invariance and intervention richness to causal selection. Givan, Dean and Greig give the direct stochastic-bisimulation and partition-refinement baseline for controlled state minimization. Here those response rows must first be recovered and shown independent of unresolved exterior effects; the whole patch need not itself be autonomously Markovian. Causal-state and finite-state transducer minimization are further precedents, not new mechanisms introduced here.

The defensible contribution is their conditional integration around an unsupplied task-preserving quotient and recursive incoming boundary from noisy local readouts, while leaving exterior architecture unresolved. It is not a priority claim that local prediction, abstraction, or invariance is new. The proof does not materially require the broader typed-decomposition or equipment machinery, and it does not complete physical architecture discovery or the original research contract. Remaining gaps include a finite-data implementation, filtering under the permitted observation access, and discovery of restricted outgoing interfaces. A stronger architectural result would have to establish both directions of interaction and joint implementation of the discovered parts. Those claims are not included in this milestone.
