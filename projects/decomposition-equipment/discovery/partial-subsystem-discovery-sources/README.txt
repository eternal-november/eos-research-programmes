Partial subsystem discovery source checkpoint
28 October 2026

Start with Partial subsystem discovery results.txt and its lay abstract.

This checkpoint establishes readout-based discovery of a task-preserving, exterior-robust local quotient and a recursive incoming boundary, conditional on an explicit randomized-probe and response-universe promise. It can leave the exterior's architecture and memory unidentified. It is not a completed physical architecture-discovery theorem, a bidirectionally sealed module, or a claim that the broader virtual-equipment research contract is discharged.

Main files

main/Partial_Subsystem_Discovery.md gives the population algorithm, coarsest-quotient proof, input-history discovery, finite failure witness, and dependent positive whole-law realization.

main/Finite_Data_Stability.md supplies the conditional finite-data theorem. Its calibration constant is proved finite but not numerically evaluated. Independent sampling, conditioning, coverage and separation assumptions remain explicit; no full finite-data estimator is implemented.

exterior/Exterior_Closure_and_Feedback.md explains why conditional regime separation upgrades averaged invariance to pointwise closure, gives failure examples and adaptive-feedback bounds, and states the separate outward-interface obligation.

stress/Unobserved_Exterior_Family.md gives nonlinear feedback, delayed input, a hidden bypass, and a growing unobserved exterior. The noisy latent-port fixture is a complementary identification example, not an unqualified implementation of the generic directly accessible-port theorem. Raw overlapping patch supports and runtime latent-state access need their stated treatment.

Review_Partial_Subsystem.md and audit/ contain the independent AI proof review and primary-source/scope audit. The main mechanisms have established predecessors; no scientific-priority claim is made. The proof does not materially require the full virtual-equipment machinery.

Reproduction

Verified with Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5. From the extracted archive directory:

OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python main/check_refinement.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python exterior/check_exterior_closure.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python stress/check_unobserved_exterior.py

All three scripts passed in the final regression run. Recorded outputs are under reproduction/, and scripts save adjacent QA JSON files. The main check exhausts 225 task-preserving partitions of a small known response kernel. The exterior check tests rank and feedback claims. The noisy stress check uses whole-state models only as exact-law data generators and validation oracles; its discovery calculations receive bounded noisy observable tables, not those matrices.

These checks are exact-law algebra/numerical regressions, not finite sampled-data experiments, physical experiments, external human proof review, or Lean verification. They support implementations and examples; the all-size claims depend on the written arguments.

Important limitations

- A randomized probe must cover the declared hidden effects and have no direct local effect. Passive fit cannot verify this experimental promise.
- Pair-union input coverage prevents a correlated training input from falsely substituting for the true input under a changed policy.
- The local state and incoming boundary are discovered within a supplied patch, task, input bank and bounded history universe.
- Local response invariance is not equality of unconditional trajectories under different exterior input policies.
- Joint closure needs a joint test; separate marginal closures are insufficient.
- Exact dependent gluing charges a full outgoing quotient-state message. A narrower physical outgoing channel needs further evidence.
- Noisy latent-state identification does not grant a controller instantaneous access to that state or a free physical wire.
