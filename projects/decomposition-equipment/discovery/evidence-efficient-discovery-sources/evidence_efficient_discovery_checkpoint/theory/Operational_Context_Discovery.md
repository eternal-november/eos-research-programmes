# Model-free discovery and certification for a declared operational context language

## Plain-language abstract

A predictive decomposition need not reproduce every possible detail of a robot's recorded history to support a declared collection of decisions. We can instead require it to reproduce every observation that an explicitly specified language of operational tests can make. The tests can examine joint signals, temporal dependencies, and the outputs of executable finite-memory monitors. They need not be selected individually or after seeing a favorable fit.

The result below searches for a positive, recursively executable decomposition and checks its predictions against real complete-episode records. It does not assume that the physical environment is generated by any candidate decomposition. If no candidate passes, the procedure must abstain. The guarantee is uniform over the declared tests and controllers, not over all measurable events or all controllers. It therefore loses the earlier full-total-variation guarantee while obtaining a sharply smaller evidence requirement for the same declared practical queries.

The statistical ingredients are standard uniform concentration and empirical approximation. The additional deliverable is an executable predictive representation, with discovered internal scopes and memory, that satisfies all admitted joint and temporal tests; it is not a claim to identify the unique physical architecture. Computational search and representation costs remain separate from the amount of physical evidence required.

## 1. Objects and explicit scope

Fix a horizon H and a finite family Pi={pi_1,...,pi_m} of causal controllers. Each controller can be adaptive within an episode. For each i collect n_i independent complete episodes under pi_i, with the same declared initial/reset distribution. Independence concerns complete episodes, not successive time stamps. Alternatively, assign controller IDs independently at episode start and condition on the resulting sample sizes. There must be no hidden confounding of controller assignment and the environmental episode law.

Write P_i for the actual action/readout transcript law. No Markov, finite-state, independence-of-components, or candidate-model realizability assumption is imposed on P_i. The permitted transcript and intervention regime, horizon, initial law, and data validity remain substantive assumptions.

Let G_i be an explicitly declared finite or countable collection of computable functions g:Omega_i->[0,1]. Each is a test of the observable transcript, possibly implemented as a typed causal monitor with bounded memory. A test cannot access a candidate's hidden state on the physical side. Its whole input must be an admitted observable record. Include the actual operational risk/task monitors among these tests, together with the joint and temporal interaction tests needed to exercise the discovered representation.

A candidate D is a typed, positive recursive program: local state spaces; local state update and output mechanisms; message alphabets, schedule, and ownership; initialization; and any shared source and its distribution. Its controller-i execution yields Q_{D,i}. Every source distribution is nonnegative and normalized, and all update operations are executable in the declared computational model. Such a program may be a simulator rather than an online local filter of physical observations. These are distinct capabilities and must be stated separately.

Define the operational discrepancy

  d_G(P,Q_D) = sup_{i,g in G_i} |E_{P_i}g - E_{Q_{D,i}}g|.

This is a pseudometric on observable laws. Equality in this pseudometric is not physical or full predictive equivalence.

## 2. Uniform certificate without a realizability premise

Assign nonnegative confidence weights a_{i,g} with sum_{i,g} a_{i,g} <=1. Set

  r_{i,g} = sqrt(log(2/(delta a_{i,g}))/(2 n_i)).

Zero weight means no finite certificate. Let hat p_{i,g} be the empirical average of g on the n_i complete episodes for controller i. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound imply, with probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously for every admitted test,

  |hat p_{i,g} - E_{P_i}g| <= r_{i,g}.                       (1)

Consequently, on that same event, for EVERY candidate program D, including one chosen adaptively after inspecting the records,

  |E_{P_i}g - E_{Q_{D,i}}g|
      <= |hat p_{i,g} - E_{Q_{D,i}}g| + r_{i,g}.             (2)

This does not require a union bound over candidates. Concentration is applied to the physical-data averages, which are shared by every candidate. It does require that the test class and its confidence weights are valid independently of their empirical deviations. A fixed prefix-code language permits adaptive selection among its tests; assigning short codes only after inspecting data does not.

Thus accept D at tolerance epsilon iff the right side of (2) is at most epsilon for all demanded tests. A sound acceptance establishes d_G<=epsilon. There is no claim about omitted tests. If a physical hazard is not itself an admitted observable test, it needs a separate sound relation to such a test.

For m controllers, at most L tests per controller, equal n, uniform weights give

  r = sqrt(log(2mL/delta)/(2n)).                            (3)

The test language can include controller-specific functions; no identical domains are needed.

### Numerical candidate expectations

If model expectations are computed with certified errors b_{D,i,g}, replace the right side of (2) by

  |hat p_{i,g} - tilde q_{D,i,g}| + r_{i,g}+b_{D,i,g}.

Deterministic interval arithmetic introduces no extra failure probability. Monte Carlo model simulation requires its own simultaneous confidence event, including adaptive reuse/stopping, and its own failure budget. It is not free precision. It is nevertheless computational/synthetic evidence, not additional physical episodes.

## 3. Discovery, approximate completeness, and savings

Fix a finite resource-bounded program language D_B, containing all permitted choices of local scopes, state spaces, wiring, shared sources, and numerical parameters. For a finite demanded test budget, an exhaustive search evaluates the certificate for each candidate and returns a minimum declared-cost passing candidate, or abstains. An unrestricted countable test language gives a simultaneous statistical statement, not a terminating exhaustive certification algorithm. This is an existence algorithm, not a polynomial-time claim.

On event (1), every returned candidate is sound without assuming that any adequate candidate exists. Conversely, in the equal-radius/exact-expectation case, if a candidate D* satisfies

  d_G(P,Q_D*) <= epsilon-2r,

then D* passes, and the returned candidate has cost at most cost(D*). Proof: empirical discrepancy of D* is at most epsilon-r, and the certificate adds r. With certified expectation errors bounded by b, sufficient slack is 2r+2b under the displayed conservative numerical test.

This is approximate completeness relative to the declared program language, not completeness for arbitrary physical systems. No decomposition is given as input, but a vocabulary and resource bound are unavoidable. Enriching that vocabulary can increase search cost, make the representation less interpretable, or leave no compact passing program. It does not invalidate the soundness theorem.

For a true margin epsilon-d_G(P,Q_D*) >= eta>0, taking

  n >= 2 log(2mL/delta)/eta^2

suffices for acceptance with probability at least 1-delta, with exact expectations. For example, m=4,L=448,delta=.05,n=12,000 gives radius about .0216 and 48,000 physical episodes. A measured maximum residual <=.0284 then certifies error .05. These figures are illustrative sufficient thresholds, not an assertion that a particular program achieves the residual.

The dependence is logarithmic in the number of admitted tests, not linear in the number of possible histories. The total acquisition cost is sum_i n_i; paying for more controllers must be explicit. Full-episode controller randomization avoids per-action inverse-propensity products for this controller family. It does not justify previously untested policies, including a new controller selected by an unconstrained training process. A new controller must belong to a predeclared covered family or have a separate valid off-policy/experimental coverage argument.

If uniformly mixed controller IDs are used, all concentration statements can condition on the realized n_i, because assignment is independent and episodes within each stratum have law P_i. A minimum sample count must be achieved or the certificate abstains. Fixed balanced strata are simpler.

## 4. Compositional semantics: what follows and what does not

The correct closure condition is pullback closure of observable tests.

Let C be an admitted deterministic observation-processing context, taking a transcript to a monitor output or a transformed transcript. Suppose every demanded downstream test h has pullback h o C in G_i. Then (2) gives

  |E_{C_*P_i}h - E_{C_*Q_{D,i}}h| <= epsilon.

For a randomized context with externally specified randomness independent of the system, use the bounded pullback g(omega)=E[h(C(omega,U))|omega]. The same statement follows if that g is admitted. This is an exact semantic implication, not a heuristic based on matching a few local moments.

A typed syntax can generate contexts and monitors by composition; typing ensures observable access and resource legality, while the certificate covers the generated composite tests. A length budget must include the composed program and its coding overhead. Merely checking generators does NOT establish their composites. Repeated composition can exceed the certified resource budget.

A context that changes actions is not just postprocessing. Its composite controller must be among Pi, or separately covered. Wiring two candidate mechanisms together can alter the physical experiment and must not be justified using postprocessing closure alone. Likewise, independent local test agreements do not certify a joint law: the test language must include the needed joint/temporal tests explicitly or by its grammar.

For a sound physical-hazard upper bound g>=1_unsafe pathwise, the predicted expectation plus epsilon bounds true risk. If the hazard relation is probabilistic or approximate, its separate error is added. Nothing here certifies an unobserved thermal or collision event without such a relation.

## 5. Countable prefix-coded context language

For each controller use a prefix-free binary encoding of executable tests, with length ell(g). Kraft's inequality gives sum_g 2^{-ell(g)}<=1. Assign controller weights beta_i>0 summing to1 and a_{i,g}=beta_i 2^{-ell(g)}. Then with probability at least1-delta, all encoded tests, including tests selected after seeing the data, satisfy

  |P_i g - Q_D,i g| <= |hat p_i,g-Q_D,i g|
      + sqrt((ell(g) log2+log(2/(delta beta_i)))/(2n_i)).

Thus longer compositions consume a quantitatively stated evidence budget. For a uniform tolerance and maximum code length B, it is enough to enumerate all admitted codewords of length<=B, although their number and execution time can be exponential in B. This is not a computational tractability theorem. For unbounded lengths the confidence radius may become vacuous; there is no small-error guarantee over all computable tests from finite data.

## 6. Necessary nontrivial demonstration burden

A successful example must:

1. Fix controllers, observation access, horizon, test grammar, and tolerance before inspecting heldout data.
2. Discover program scopes/memory/messages or source structure, rather than supply the desired decomposition as a premise.
3. Return an explicitly positive executable joint program and account for its initialization/shared randomness/messages.
4. Pass tests of joint temporal interactions, not only selected terminal safety scores.
5. Produce observable separating tests against important cheaper alternatives (for example memoryless or independent-source candidates). These are falsifications within the specified candidate access contract, not proofs of unique physical architecture.
6. Report actual heldout sample counts and empirical residuals, with all candidate-expectation approximation errors.
7. Compare the SAME practical queries to the prior stronger TV certificate, while openly stating that omitted queries and new controllers lose its automatic coverage.

Directly estimating every listed task score from records is a baseline and can use the same concentration theorem. The reason to discover D is to obtain a reusable executable representation with local state and explicit joint mechanisms, not to claim that representation is required to answer a single listed score. Whether its memory, simulation, interpretation, or downstream query benefits justify its synthesis cost must be measured separately.

## 7. Optional agnostic all-event result, NOT the assurance certificate

A finite candidate class admits pairwise likelihood-sign tests under distinguishing controllers. Such tests can norm candidate-pair distances and yield classical minimum-distance/Scheffe-type inequalities of the form

  d_Pi(P,Q_hat) <= 3 inf_D d_Pi(P,Q_D) + statistical error.

This can suggest candidates and explain description-length sample bounds under approximability. The unknown infimum prevents converting a small finite-test residual into an absolute full-TV assurance claim for an unrestricted physical process. It is not the main certificate above and must not be silently substituted for it.

## 8. Concrete rich monitor family: every bounded score on any k observations

Suppose the recorded episode has B binary observable coordinates, which may lie at different times and on different readout ports. Let S range over all k-element subsets of those coordinates. Write P_i^S and Q_D,i^S for their 2^k-outcome marginal distributions, and hat P_i^S for the empirical distribution.

For any s-outcome distribution, TV(hat P,P)=max_A(hat P(A)-P(A)). For the 2^s-2 nonempty proper subsets A, one-sided Hoeffding and union bound yield

  Pr[TV(hat P,P)>r] <= (2^s-2) exp(-2nr^2).

No independence between observations within an episode, between projections, or between tests is used. Therefore for m controller strata with n episodes each and J=binom(B,k) projections, with probability at least1-delta,

  max_{i,S} TV(P_i^S,Q_D,i^S)
      <= max_{i,S} TV(hat P_i^S,Q_D,i^S)
           + sqrt(log(m J (2^(2^k)-2)/delta)/(2n)),          (4)

simultaneously for every candidate D chosen from the data. Exact candidate marginals are assumed; certified model marginal-TV computation errors add to the right side.

Thus a single certificate covers EVERY [0,1]-valued score of ANY k selected observable coordinates, not merely one chosen risk event or one set of coordinate moments. It includes same-time joint interactions and cross-time dependence. Scores involving fewer than k coordinates follow by marginalization, provided B>=k. Linear combinations of such local scores with sum of absolute coefficient weights <=1 inherit the same bound. Unnormalized sums inherit the sum-weight times the bound.

For H=8, two binary readout ports observed initially and after each step give B=18. With k=3,J=816,m=4,delta=.05,n=20,000, the confidence radius is approximately .020386. Total heldout physical episodes are80,000, plus all training/acquisition episodes. If the measured maximum marginal-TV residual is <=.0296, the certificate gives .05 error for the entire stated score family.

This family is much richer than several handpicked terminal scores, but still strictly weaker than full-transcript TV. In particular, parity on four or more independent binary coordinates can differ completely while every three-coordinate marginal agrees. Nor does the certificate cover the arbitrary Boolean combination of two admitted three-coordinate monitors: that combination can depend on up to six coordinates. Adding such compositions requires explicitly enlarging k or the context test language and paying the resulting confidence penalty.

A fixed causal controller may base its actions on all recorded history, while the audited score uses three readout coordinates. This is valid because the complete episode is collected under that controller. It does not certify a new controller obtained by composing or switching those controllers within an episode.

## 9. Final protocol: finite-valued executable monitor laws

**This supersedes the three-individual-bit demonstration as the primary example.** The earlier numerical example in section 8 remains a development-stage illustration, not the final protocol or final evidence accounting.

For each controller i and each admitted deterministic observable monitor j, let T_j:Omega_i->Y_j have a finite output alphabet of size d_j. Let hat P_i^j be its empirical output law and Q_D,i^j its law under the candidate. Complete-episode independence implies

  Pr[there exist i,j with TV(hat P_i^j,P_i^j)>r]
      <= m [sum_j (2^{d_j}-2)] exp(-2nr^2).                (5)

A constant monitor has d_j=1 and contributes zero. Formula (5) follows from one-sided Hoeffding for each nonempty proper output subset and a union bound; it assumes neither independent monitors nor Markov physical dynamics. Thus

  n >= {log(m sum_j(2^{d_j}-2)/delta)}/(2r^2)

suffices, when the nonzero sum is used. On the resulting event, simultaneously for any selected candidate D,

  max_{i,j} TV(P_i^j,Q_D,i^j)
      <= max_{i,j} TV(hat P_i^j,Q_D,i^j)+r.                (6)

Every bounded [0,1] score of a single admitted monitor output inherits this error bound. This includes all threshold events of a maximum-run monitor, not only a selected threshold. There is again no candidate-count penalty, because the simultaneous event concerns the true monitor laws versus their empirical laws, not the candidate-selection procedure.

### Exact final family and evidence

There are four declared feedback controllers. Each episode has horizon H=8, nine audited joint binary-pair readouts y_0,...,y_8, and a separate observed warmup readout used in initialization and by the relevant controller.

The family contains:

- All binom(9,3)=84 choices of three readout times. A monitor returns the three complete joint readouts, so d=4^3=64. These tests retain both ports and their cross-time coupling at every chosen triple of times.
- For each of the four joint readout patterns, the maximum length of any consecutive run of that pattern among the nine audited times. Each output lies in {0,...,9}, so d=10. These monitors inspect the entire episode and certify, for example, every threshold for persistent occurrence of a readout pattern.

There are 50,000 fresh validation episodes under each controller, 200,000 total. With r=.0231, the union bound is

  4 [84(2^64-2)+4(2^10-2)] exp(-2*50000*.0231^2)
      < .041484.

The exact saved count/model calculation gives maximum empirical model discrepancy plus r of approximately .04042731731498145, rounded outward to .040428. Hence every bounded score of any one admitted monitor is predicted with error at most .040428, simultaneously over the four controllers, with confidence exceeding95.85%, under the episode-sampling contract.

The prescribed one-pass workflow uses10,000 proposal-training episodes plus200,000 validation episodes, total210,000. The actual development study used500,000 episodes, including the earlier90,000-episode and210,000-episode development protocols. These are different accounting quantities; neither should be substituted for the other. The final validation uses fresh predetermined seeds and the unchanged previously trained candidate. Confidence of earlier and final protocols must not be jointly advertised as95% without an additional joint failure accounting.

### Scaling and executable model computation

For a fixed readout alphabet of size a, all k-time joint-readout projections have binom(H+1,k) monitors of constant output size a^k when k is fixed. Their logarithmic confidence penalty is

  O(log m + k log(H+1) + a^k + log(1/delta)).

A maximum-run monitor has H+2 possible outputs; its contribution to the logarithmic penalty is O(H), not exponential in the number of possible full histories. This is an evidence bound for the stated monitor family, not full trajectory TV. Enlarging k or the observation alphabet can be expensive, and all-controller-family coverage must still be paid for.

If a candidate Q is a finite recursive program and T_j is a finite-state causal monitor, its predicted monitor law can be computed by dynamic programming on the product of the candidate's state, controller state, and monitor state. Positive normalized transitions preserve positivity. Rational inputs permit exact arithmetic. Time is polynomial in H and the explicitly expanded product-state/transition size, not necessarily in a compressed factorized description: product states, source expansion, controller memory, and rational bit lengths must be charged. In this example exact DP uses the candidate's (previous,current) joint-readout state plus the monitor state; it does not enumerate all4^{H+1} readout histories. This is a model-computation advantage, not an inference of finite physical state from the data.

### Precise price of the narrower guarantee

The family does not certify full-transcript TV, arbitrary policies, or arbitrary Boolean combinations of separate monitor outputs. In particular, separate maximum-run laws need not determine the joint law of two maximum-run statistics. To certify their conjunction, admit their joint-output monitor and charge its larger output alphabet, or directly admit the particular conjunction test under the scalar-test theorem. Composition is licensed only when the required pulled-back observable test is included with its confidence allocation.

Collecting on-policy records under four controllers is a substantive experimental capability. It is not counterfactual coverage from arbitrary passive logs, does not establish safe exploration, and does not certify suitability before the first such experiments are performed. The result concerns prediction and decomposition discovery within this observational/experimental contract, not unrestricted physical safety assurance.

### Controller closure under an exogenous episode-start mixture

The same event also certifies every randomized mixture of the covered controllers selected once at the start of an episode, with its controller index held fixed throughout that episode. Let alpha be any probability vector on the four controllers. The selector must be exogenous to the physical episode (in particular, independent of hidden environmental context), and the physical experiment and candidate simulator must use the same alpha and the same declared initial-law contract. For any admitted monitor T,

  TV(sum_i alpha_i P_i^T, sum_i alpha_i Q_D,i^T)
      <= sum_i alpha_i TV(P_i^T,Q_D,i^T)
      <= max_i epsilon_i.

More precisely, the weighted bound sum_i alpha_i epsilon_i is available when per-controller bounds epsilon_i are retained. This follows directly from the triangle inequality for the signed probability vectors. The bound holds for every alpha simultaneously on the existing confidence event, so it adds no statistical penalty and requires no new experiment. Alpha may be selected using the completed calibration dataset, provided the subsequent episode's selector is independent of its environmental randomness and the episode law remains the declared one.

If the selected controller ID is retained as an observable, the joint laws of (ID,T) satisfy the stronger exact identity

  TV((alpha_i P_i^T)_i, (alpha_i Q_D,i^T)_i)
      = sum_i alpha_i TV(P_i^T,Q_D,i^T).

Thus controller-ID-specific bounded monitor scores are covered too. This closure does not cover switching among controllers in response to observations during the episode, selecting a controller based on a warmup signal correlated with that episode's environment, or a selector correlated with unrecorded environmental context. Those produce a different experiment and require separate coverage or assumptions.
