# Persistent hidden memory: support transfer for discovered parity components

## Lay summary

A hidden influence can have memory even when its average effect in a training table appears simple. An average near zero does not rule out hidden effects that cancel. There is one useful exception: an average of numbers between minus one and one can equal plus one only if every contributing number is plus one. Thus an exactly saturated parity response identifies an update that is unaffected by the hidden situations actually reached during training.

The result below transfers that fact to deployment without pretending that the visible system is Markov. It requires deployment to use the same physical mechanisms, to start within the hidden situations covered by training, and to choose actions that training could also choose. Hidden memory can remain unidentified. Exact conclusions transfer; approximate conclusions incur an explicit, potentially exponential, coverage penalty.

## 1. Full-history model and timing

Let X_t in {0,1}^n be the observed current readout and H_t a possibly persistent hidden state. Hidden state may include arbitrary physical memory. Let F_t be the full pre-action history, including X_t,H_t and any past needed to determine future physical laws. A finite-state controlled model is sufficient, but the proof also applies on measurable spaces with regular conditional laws.

At time t:

1. An action U_t is selected.
2. A base output Y_t in {0,1}^n is generated by a fixed physical response kernel conditional on F_t,U_t. This kernel may depend on hidden memory and need not factor through X_t.
3. A fresh vector E_t of independent bit flips is drawn, independent of F_t,U_t,Y_t and any already-generated physical randomness. Set X_(t+1)=Y_t xor E_t.
4. Hidden memory may be updated by the same fixed physical kernel, including dependence on the realized output/noise if the physical model allows it. The only required independence is that E_t is fresh when it generates the current readout. Future hidden memory is allowed to remember E_t.

The bit-flip probabilities epsilon_i are fixed and known or separately calibrated, and lie strictly between zero and one half. These are fresh noise in the recorded update X_(t+1)=Y_t xor E_t. If X is used as a feedback register, the same register timing must be preserved. The model is not automatically obtained by attaching a noisy sensor to an arbitrary latent state process; additional measurement layers need their own identification argument. The strict bounds ensure positive update noise and nonzero parity attenuation. They are structural assumptions, not consequences of exploration. Different coordinates may have different epsilon_i.

All hidden physical kernels and noise laws are unchanged between exploration and deployment. Changing a hidden mechanism itself is not covered merely because the action policy changes.

Write alpha_t(u|F_t) for the exploration propensity and beta_t(u|F_t) for deployment. We assume the finite action set is fully supported:

alpha_t(u|F_t) >= p_min > 0

on exploration-supported histories, for every admitted action. An exploration policy depending only on observed history and satisfying the same bound has this property. Deployment is an arbitrary causal policy on its admitted observations; it need not resemble exploration. The proof also applies to policies using a larger history, but does not grant controllers access to hidden variables.

The deployment initial full-state/history law nu_0 is absolutely continuous with respect to the exploration initial law eta_0, with density at most R. This condition concerns hidden memory and relevant prehistory, not merely the marginal law of X_0. If an initial record is used by either policy or the physical mechanism, include it in the initial state/history.

For approximate statements, assume the exploration process is stationary in the specific sense that the per-update error probability defined below is the same delta at each time. A stationary augmented process with a time-homogeneous exploration policy suffices. Exact transfer needs no stationarity if zero mismatch is established separately for every update time. A single population table establishes the required identities at every time only under stationarity or another explicit time-transfer hypothesis.

## 2. A single observed validation score

For S subset {1,...,n}, write chi_S(x)=(-1)^(sum_(i in S) x_i), and lambda_S=product_(i in S)(1-2 epsilon_i). Let f(X_t,U_t) be any proposed Boolean function taking values in {-1,+1}. It may come from a finite discovery grammar or a generative proposal. Once f is fixed, define the observed population score

 delta_(S,f,t) = [1 - lambda_S^(-1)
   E_alpha[chi_S(X_(t+1)) f(X_t,U_t)]] / 2.

Fresh independent update noise gives

 E_alpha[chi_S(X_(t+1)) f(X_t,U_t)]
   = lambda_S E_alpha[chi_S(Y_t) f(X_t,U_t)].

Since both factors on the right are Boolean, the score is exactly

 delta_(S,f,t) = P_alpha(chi_S(Y_t) != f(X_t,U_t)).

In particular it is nonnegative, despite its expression through a deconvolved moment. An exact zero score certifies the base-update identity almost surely under exploration. This certificate requires neither a polynomial conditional-response assumption nor identification of E[chi_S(Y_t)|X_t,U_t]. It also needs no full visibility of hidden effects. Its burden is the calibrated fresh-noise model, exact score saturation, and coverage transfer below. The observed summand chi_S(X_(t+1))f(X_t,U_t) is bounded in [-1,1]; division by small lambda_S magnifies statistical error.

This is a certificate for a proposed support/function, not yet an efficient discovery algorithm. Finding a useful f and S, bounding the grammar/search size, and checking closure through the retained component remain separate tasks. If f is selected using the same finite data on which its score is assessed, use uniform bounds over the declared candidate class, a valid complexity bound, or independent validation data. A negative empirical score does not establish exact zero; finite noisy records do not certify equality without a gap/model promise or an explicit approximate conclusion.

**Optional conditional-response route.** Fresh noise also gives

 E_alpha[chi_S(X_(t+1)) | X_t=x,U_t=u]
   = lambda_S E_alpha[chi_S(Y_t) | X_t=x,U_t=u].

If the base conditional mean equals a Boolean f_S(x,u) at every exploration-supported context, the score is zero. A bounded-degree regression class can make such an f discoverable from compact moments, but that class is not needed for the score identity or for validating a fixed candidate. Ordinary nonsaturated averages establish neither pointwise hidden independence nor zero score.

The score may be defined for each t. Under stationary exploration, one stationary population score applies at every update. Without stationarity, exact all-time transfer requires delta_(S,f,t)=0 for every t in question, and approximate bounds use the corresponding sequence of scores. A single observed-time equality alone is insufficient.

## 3. Exact finite-path support transfer

For a path through update t, the Radon–Nikodym derivative of deployment with respect to exploration is

L_(t+1) = (d nu_0/d eta_0)(initial history)
           product_(k=0)^t beta_k(U_k|F_k)/alpha_k(U_k|F_k).

The physical transition factors cancel because they are identical in both experiments. Therefore

0 <= L_(t+1) <= R p_min^(-(t+1)).

This is an identity on the full latent path, not just observed readouts. It follows by the chain rule for conditional probabilities; standard induction extends it to general measurable histories. Exploration-null paths remain deployment-null.

Consequently each exact parity identity with zero exploration mismatch at time t transfers to deployment at that time. If zero mismatch holds at every t (for example by a stationary zero population score), a countable union of null events shows that all such identities hold simultaneously for all t>=0 with deployment probability one. This all-time exact conclusion is a null-set statement, not a uniform finite-sample guarantee or a claim of finite total variation error on infinite trajectories.

No deployment visit to a hidden mode outside the initial/support-reachable exploration family is covered. A change in physics breaks the cancellation argument. Positive observed-state support alone does not repair either defect.

## 4. A controlled Markov component even when X is not Markov

Let S_1,...,S_m be disjoint nonempty supports discovered from the readouts, and define the retained component bits

Q_j(x) = xor_(i in S_j) x_i.

Suppose the saturated response functions factor through the retained component and admitted action/interface:

f_(S_j)(x,u) = (-1)^(g_j(Q(x),u)).

This factorization is a required output of the discovery/closure procedure. Saturation alone would only recover an update depending on all of X.

Assume each proposed parity identity has zero exploration mismatch at every time, as supplied by stationary zero scores or a separate per-time argument. By support transfer,

Q_j(X_(t+1)) = g_j(Q(X_t),U_t) xor N_(j,t)

almost surely under deployment, where N_(j,t)=xor_(i in S_j) E_(i,t). Disjoint supports make the N_(j,t) mutually independent within each update. Their probabilities are

P(N_(j,t)=1) = (1-lambda_(S_j))/2.

They are fresh across time and independent of the full pre-update history. Thus Q has the specified controlled Markov transition kernel conditional even on the full history, although X itself may have arbitrary non-Markov dependence through H. A policy adapting to other readouts may make the marginal Q-only process non-Markov after actions are integrated out; the controlled transition law conditional on Q_t,U_t remains valid.

Disjointness is sufficient, not logically necessary. Overlapping parity supports give a known correlated noise vector, and the same proof works if its joint pushforward law is retained rather than replaced by independent parity noise.

This theorem gives an operational component for deployment trajectories in the same support class. It does not assert equality of transition rows at every mathematically imaginable hidden state, especially null or unreachable states. Nor does it permit changes to the hidden physics unless those changes themselves are represented as supported actions in the unchanged kernel family.

## 5. Approximate saturation and the coverage price

The single observed score in section 2 already equals the exact exploration mismatch probability for a proposed parity update. No regression assumption is needed for that conclusion. Alternatively, let the exploration response be m_S(x,u)=E_alpha[chi_S(Y_t)|X_t=x,U_t=u], and choose a Boolean candidate f_S. The same mismatch probability is

P_alpha(chi_S(Y_t) != f_S(X_t,U_t))
 = E_alpha[(1-f_S(X_t,U_t)m_S(X_t,U_t))/2].

For several discovered parities define B_t as the event that at least one base parity violates its proposed update. If the individual mismatch probabilities are delta_j, then P_alpha(B_t)<=sum_j delta_j=:delta; a measured joint bound may be sharper. A uniform regression error |m_S-f_S|<=eta implies mismatch at most eta/2, but only averaged over exploration's hidden conditional law. It does not imply a pointwise bound over H.

Under stationary exploration with P_alpha(B_t)<=delta, full-path domination yields

P_beta(B_t) <= min(1, R p_min^(-(t+1)) delta).

For H updates, union bound gives

P_beta(any B_t for 0<=t<H)
 <= min(1, R delta sum_(t=0)^(H-1) p_min^(-(t+1))).

For nonstationary training replace delta by delta_t inside the sum. This geometric factor may be exponential in horizon. It is a sound transfer bound, not a polynomial-horizon assurance theorem. Better concentrability, occupancy-ratio, mixing, or direct conditional-hidden guarantees would be additional assumptions/results.

Couple the true deployment component to the proposed reduced recursion with the same initial Q, actions and fresh parity noises. They agree until a B_t occurs. Hence the displayed bound controls total variation of their Q-path laws when they use the same Q-history-adapted policy, or more generally when any policy side information has a specified common causal coupling maintained while the Q paths agree. For a policy reading discarded X information, a stand-alone Q model does not by itself specify the action law; retaining/coupling that side information must be explicit. The exact controlled-kernel claim in section 4 still holds under arbitrary causal policies.

Finite empirical confidence in delta and calibration can be combined with this bound on the event that those estimates are valid. It does not transform the finite-data result into pointwise hidden uniformity. Identifying exact zero mismatch from finite noisy records still requires a separation/model promise.

## 6. Two unresolved persistent mechanisms with the same one-step readouts

The companion check constructs a five-bit augmented state (a,b,n,h,k), where X=(a,b,n), H=(h,k), and the desired component is q=a xor b. For action u, draw a fresh fair Z and set

Y_1=Z,
Y_2=Z xor q xor u,
Y_3=h.

Add independent observation flips of probability epsilon to all three bits. Update hidden memory to (h_new,h), where, in spin notation,

E[(-1)^h_new | h,k] = kappa (-1)^k.

For 0<kappa<1 this is a persistent order-two hidden process. Under fair exploratory actions, its stationary law has h,k independent fair bits; (a,b) are independent fair bits; and n is a noisy copy of k. All augmented states have positive stationary mass. Different kappa values yield exactly the same complete stationary one-step readout tables P(X_t,U_t,X_(t+1)), while the discarded coordinate has lag-two correlation kappa(1-2epsilon)^2. Thus X is not Markov and its hidden memory remains unidentified by the supplied one-step evidence.

Nevertheless Y_1 xor Y_2=q xor u exactly, so the discovered parity component has kernel

q_next = q xor u xor E_1 xor E_2

under either persistent mechanism and any supported causal deployment policy. This is not a claim that all full readout histories are indistinguishable: an allowed lag-two observation can distinguish the hidden worlds. The available one-step evidence is sufficient for the retained component without resolving that difference.

The fixture supplies a verification example for support transfer. The main discovery algorithm must still recover the parity support and closure from its admitted readout moments, rather than being given q by this proof example.

## 7. Integration with the growing nonlinear pair-discovery family

There is also a direct persistent-memory extension of the growing family used for discovery. Let X have m raw bit pairs, q_i the XOR of pair i, and let F be the nonlinear bijection

F(q) = (q_1,...,q_(m-1), q_0 xor (q_1 and q_(m-1)))

for m>=3. An action may XOR a known action bit into the last output, preserving bijectivity for each fixed action. Let H_t be a stationary fair binary Markov chain with persistence r in (1/2,1), independent of all observation noise. Set every pre-noise residual bit to the same hidden bit:

Y_(2i)=H_t,
Y_(2i+1)=H_t xor F_i(q(X_t),U_t).

Then add independent Bernoulli(epsilon) noise to every raw coordinate. The hidden bit persists through time, and its transition parameter r need not be identified.

Every pre-noise even parity cancels H_t. In particular, all pair-output response functions used by a pair-discovery protocol are fixed Boolean functions of the retained q and action. The true within-pair response is exactly chi_(S_i)(Y)=(-1)^F_i(q,u). Cross-pair responses are products of zero, one, or two coordinate functions of F. These are bounded-degree Boolean polynomials in raw X: for the displayed F, raw degree at most eight suffices uniformly in m. The latent common residual mechanism is strongly coupled and persistent, but remains invisible to these even response queries.

Under independent exploratory actions, the stationary q law is uniform because F(.,u) is a bijection and XOR noise preserves uniformity. More strongly, q_t is uniform conditional on the hidden trajectory and the realized current observation noise: the preceding uniform q is permuted and translated. The current raw residual bits are noisy copies of H_(t-1). Thus the stationary distribution of X_t depends on the fair marginal of H_(t-1) and the noise rate, but not on r. All current-state Gram moments and all conditional pair-output response moments are consequently identical for different r. Those are the readouts admitted by this discovery protocol.

This is deliberately not equality of all possible observations. Odd output queries, or suitable temporal residual statistics, can identify persistence. The full observed X process generally retains hidden-memory dependence; no X-Markov assumption is needed to certify the discovered parity component. The supplied pair-only query protocol has insufficient information to resolve that hidden memory, yet its saturation information certifies the same controlled q update across the persistent worlds.

Strictly positive observation noise gives positive mass to every raw X and every H under the stationary law. Any deployment initialization on this finite augmented state therefore has a finite domination constant R, although that constant can be large and must be charged. Initializing an additional hidden mode absent from this universe, changing r during deployment when r is fixed in the world contract, or introducing new physical channels is not covered. The theorem holds separately for each fixed r-compatible world under its own same-physics support transfer.

The check compares two persistence values, verifies equality of all stationary current-state distributions and all even pair response tables, confirms disagreement of an odd response, and checks the retained parity noise law. It does not enumerate an exponentially growing system; the small m instance verifies formulas whose proof applies to all m.
