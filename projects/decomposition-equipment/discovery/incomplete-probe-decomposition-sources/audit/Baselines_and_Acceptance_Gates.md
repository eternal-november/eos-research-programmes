# Incomplete-probe discovery: baseline audit and acceptance gates

28 October 2026. Initial audit, before inspecting a finished candidate theorem. This is not novelty clearance.

## Plain-language conclusion

Keeping several possible explanations is not itself new, including in our own work. The substantive next step is to discover a useful component and its boundary despite experiments that leave some influences unresolved, and then determine which separately discovered components genuinely belong together. The theorem should construct this structure from observations, not simply name the set of all models fitting them.

## 2. External primary baselines

### Incomplete inverse problems and identified functionals

Newey–Powell, *Instrumental Variable Estimation of Nonparametric Models* (2003), Proposition 2.1 and discrete Theorem 2.4, relate identification to injectivity/completeness and matrix rank. Author PDF: https://eml.berkeley.edu/~powell/npiv.pdf . The previous audit checked the numbered statements; extraction of this PDF is garbled, so use rendered pages when quoting it.

Babii–Florens, *Is Completeness Necessary? Estimation in Nonidentified Linear Models*, January 2025 revision, studies estimation when Kφ=r is nonidentified, including structural/average structural functions and regularization. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03473 . Thus learning a useful functional while leaving a nullspace unresolved is an established ambition. Our distinct burden is unknown decomposition structure, positive mechanism realization and compatible causal boundaries, not merely a linear estimand.

Santos (2012), *Inference in Nonparametric Instrumental Variables With Partial Identification*, Econometrica, DOI https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA7493 , is a directly relevant inference baseline. Only publisher abstract checked in this pass; do not attribute specific theorem scope without inspecting full text.

### Local laws and global positive realization

Abramsky–Brandenburger (2011), *The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and Contextuality*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.0264 , Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 8.1: existence of a global distribution section is equivalent to a factorizable hidden-variable realization in their empirical-model setting. Their linear feasibility formulation already separates compatible overlaps from globally realizable distributions. Generic cyclic inconsistency and marginal LP infeasibility are therefore baseline mechanisms, not a new obstruction theory.

Their measurement cover and local empirical models are supplied. The target can differ by discovering component coordinates/boundary memory and deriving causal constraints over histories from readouts. This difference must be proved, not asserted by relabeling marginal contexts as typed components.

### Coupled uncertainty across states and time

Wiesemann–Kuhn–Rustem, *Robust Markov Decision Processes*, publisher source https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.1120.0566 , and Li–Kuhn–Sutter, *Policy Gradient Algorithms for Robust MDPs with Non-Rectangular Uncertainty Sets*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19004 , establish a substantial existing literature on coupled (nonrectangular) uncertainty. The latter distinguishes exact but inefficient evaluation from efficient approximation. Holding one uncertain kernel fixed rather than selecting rows independently is essential, but not new. Do not claim general efficient verification from local width alone: temporal/shared-parameter coupling may destroy separability.

The HTML rendering of the latter showed spurious template-author names; use the arXiv record/PDF for bibliographic details, not that rendering's author line. No theorem-number-specific hardness claim is made here.

### Transport across experiments

Bareinboim–Pearl (2013), *Meta-Transportability of Causal Effects*, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v31/bareinboim13a.pdf , supplies complete conditions/algorithm for combining heterogeneous experimental domains to identify a target effect, conditional on supplied selection diagrams. This is a strong baseline for deciding which additional experiments resolve ambiguity. Our target is not equivalent unless it discovers the structural objects and delivers jointly realizable decompositions, rather than assuming the graph and identifying a causal query.

## 3. Acceptance gates for the candidate theorem

1. **Unsupplied structure:** say exactly which states, partitions, component maps, interfaces and source identities are learned, and which are supplied. Calibrated known mixture matrices do not count as discovery of hidden influences.
2. **Useful partial result:** show a nontrivial task-preserving component/interface certified while the full mechanism remains nonidentified. A singleton quotient, identity quotient or arbitrary full-state boundary is inadequate without a substantive resource result.
3. **No disguised complete probe:** local coverage, dictionary richness or positivity assumptions must not force all relevant hidden distinctions to be recovered. Exhibit admitted indistinguishable worlds inside the positive theorem.
4. **Coupled positive realization:** local fit plus overlap consistency is not enough. Preserve joint hidden-source identity, positivity, time evolution, initialization and one fixed world. Distinguish static parameters from dynamic latent states.
5. **Beyond enumeration:** a finite semialgebraic/CSP enumeration can prove existence, but must not be advertised as practical discovery. State complexity in observation width, memory, uncertainty dimension and proposed certificate size.
6. **Genuine nonlinear growth:** family size must increase interacting structure, not merely duplicate a fixed marginal inconsistency gadget. Include nonlinear context-sensitive local response and unknown useful quotient/boundary.
7. **Experiment recommendation:** return a realizable permitted test distinguishing specific residual alternatives, or prove none exists in the allowed bank. Merely stating a hypothetical separating test exists repeats completeness.
8. **Quantifiers:** distinguish one common decomposition for all worlds, world-dependent proof witnesses, and one implementable runtime mechanism. Conditional physical guarantees must remain conditional on model membership and represented hazards.
9. **Typed machinery:** identify an actual reconstruction/refinement/interface operation whose laws prevent a false assembly. If ordinary finite probabilistic automata plus LP give the entire result, credit that and do not claim necessity of virtual-equipment structure.

## 4. Scope of this audit

The cited external baselines do not establish that the full proposed theorem is already known. They prevent weaker sublemmas from carrying an inflated novelty claim. Final judgment requires inspecting the constructed discovery algorithm, its nonlinear family and its explicit assumption ledger.

## Emerging paired-challenge route (23:27)

Main agent proposes two context challenges sharing the same hidden mode Z and independent conditional local innovations. Their joint outcome probabilities identify a Gram matrix of response functions. Squared response differences then test equality almost surely without identifying Z or requiring a complete experiment operator. Applying those equality tests during partition refinement could discover a conditional task quotient and its boundary.

**Preliminary assessment:** this is a different inference route from our prior source-fiber theorem, not merely its incomplete-rank reformulation. However, the Gram identity is elementary and the new work must lie in the discovery/reconstruction theorem and its model family.

Closest directly checked primary multiview baseline: Song et al., *Nonparametric Estimation of Multi-View Latent Variable Models*, ICML2014, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/songa14.pdf , equations (8)–(11) express pair/triple conditional-independent-view moments as latent feature products; Proposition1 gives parameter identification under linearly independent conditional distributions. The new route would intentionally avoid full parameter identification and use only response equality. That is a scope distinction, not by itself proof of novelty.

Specific proof obligations:

* Pair challenges must preserve the same hidden mode while independently refreshing local randomness and setting contexts. Randomization alone does not grant this protocol. Give a concrete admitted implementation and an exclusion ledger.
* Ordinary sequential measurements with a drifting latent source are not such replicas. Nor is equality of marginal challenge laws evidence of shared modes.
* Gram-zero certifies only almost-sure equality under the challenge-mode law. Transfer to deployment requires support domination (or an explicit quantitative bound), not a claim about arbitrary untested hidden modes.
* Finite noisy estimates cannot establish exact zero without a separation margin, an exact symbolic promise, or an approximate conclusion.
* Conditional Z-lumpability does not make the quotient autonomous. A shared latent response vector remains a boundary resource with unresolved causal dynamics; it cannot be treated as a robot-readable signal.
* Marginal Gram consistency does not settle higher-order joint response laws. Keep the common positive response-function distribution/process as a genuine shared witness, not independently chosen per component.

### Shared-response algebra note review

Inspected `gluing/Shared_Response_Algebra.md` at 23:28. Its kernel-ideal obstruction and full-support positivity argument are sound: closure under multiplication gives k² in the nullspace, while a positive reference average forces k=0. This is elementary algebra/variance, not the discovery theorem. Finite algebra closure is correctly conditional on supplied candidate response tables. Positive causal-kernel assembly and the marginal/hidden-Markov examples are correctly marked baseline.

A further paired-challenge assumption deserves separate naming: cross-context comparisons require the **same reference mode law** for both contexts. Ordinary stationary conditional distributions Z|X=x can differ with x. Their independently estimated conditional moments are not a common Gram matrix. Intervening on/resetting local state must preserve the shared hidden mode and avoid conditioning it differently; otherwise an explicit coupling/alignment assumption is required.

Recovering the entire relevant response algebra by flat moments may be valuable, but it changes the claim: remaining exterior ambiguity may then be only irrelevant hidden detail. For the strongest partial-discovery target, exhibit unresolved *relevant* positive higher-order or temporal response laws while the particular useful quotient/boundary remains certified.
