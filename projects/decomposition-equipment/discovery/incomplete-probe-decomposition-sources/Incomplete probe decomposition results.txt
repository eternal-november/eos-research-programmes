Discovering conditional components from incomplete experiments
Research results and limits
28 October 2026

Lay abstract

Incomplete experiments need not reveal every hidden influence before they reveal a useful component of a system. The result here identifies which distinctions a component must retain to preserve a specified task, while leaving some of the mechanism genuinely unresolved. Its central example reduces eight arbitrarily labelled states to four. The two-state task description is insufficient; the full eight-state description is unnecessary. Importantly, the discovered four-state component still responds to a shared hidden influence. The method does not falsely call it autonomous. A numerical extension recovers this reduction through unknown noisy sensors from exact stationary observation probabilities.

The key is to observe pairs of candidate patches simultaneously. Their responses reveal a space of hidden-effect directions even when ordinary averages conceal those effects. Comparing state transitions in that space discovers the useful reduction without choosing one hidden explanation. Separate local explanations must then fit one common matrix frame and one positive stochastic mechanism. Going around a cycle can expose incompatible explanations. An additional pair experiment can remove a shared scale or shear ambiguity, while a four-patch experiment can distinguish worlds that every pair experiment misses.

These are exact results for a structured family, supported by reproducible numerical checks and an independent AI review. Candidate measurement patches, adequate joint experimental coverage, independent local innovations, and a particular affine shared-effect model remain assumptions. The local state and input dynamics can be nonlinear, but arbitrary physical architecture is not recovered. Nor is a sampled-data learning implementation established. The advance is narrower and useful: discover a task-preserving conditional component first, and carry its unresolved shared dependence honestly into the whole-system model.

1  What has been established

The principal result is a gauge-invariant discovery theorem, not merely a catalogue of compatible worlds. From conditional single-patch and simultaneous pair-patch readout laws it constructs the unique coarsest deterministic task quotient valid throughout the declared hidden-effect family. A second test decides whether that quotient is autonomous or necessarily conditional on a retained latent influence. A finite input-history construction identifies its recursively maintainable observed boundary.

A separate matrix-atlas theorem decides algebraic compatibility of the learned local response frames and describes their remaining freedom. Joint positivity then restricts that common family. The resulting assembly uses one source occurrence per update and one fixed mechanism choice per world. Explicit examples distinguish useful conditional discovery, autonomous discovery, rejection of autonomy, and incompatibility of individually plausible local fits.

The exact theorem and bounded examples are the deliverable. They do not discharge a general unknown-architecture discovery contract, certify absent unmodelled hazards, or identify a unique physical implementation.

2  The universe and the information actually used

For patch i, let c denote its old local state and admitted observed input history, and v its next state. The conditional response is

  K_i(v | c,z) = B_i(v | c) + F_i[(c,v),:] z.

Here B_i is stochastic, each context's F_i rows sum to zero, and every displayed K_i row is nonnegative on the admitted source support. Both B_i and F_i are unknown. They may depend nonlinearly on local state and input. Only their dependence on the retained shared vector z is affine.

Conditional on the full pre-update history, source memory, and experimental regime, z has mean zero and covariance I, or a known scalar multiple absorbed into normalization. This is a pointwise conditional promise, not merely a stationary covariance statement. Given z and the contexts, fresh patch innovations are independent. One common z serves every patch in an update.

Singles identify B_i. After subtracting the product of the singles, a flattened simultaneous pair table satisfies

  D_ij = F_i F_j^T.

If patch i and an observed partner j have full column rank r, then range(D_ij) equals range(F_i). Thus the response subspace V_i is observed even though the hidden coefficients, coordinate frame, and source law are not. Means alone can hide the response because z is centered; pair dependence reveals its directions.

This replaces a universal separating probe with strong, different assumptions. Every affected patch needs adequate responding partners. Every required joint context pair must actually be covered under the same conditional moment regime; marginal coverage is insufficient. An effect acting only on an unpaired patch, or outside the affine universe, is not certified absent. Candidate measurement patches and the task target are supplied, not discovered physical ownership boundaries. The task partition may be supplied or recovered from an identifiable task-emission channel.

Raw patch states and sensor calibration need not be supplied. Recovering them requires a valid identification step with its own conditional independence, rank, label alignment, and support assumptions. The new noisy-sensor fixture exercises this step at the exact observation-law level. Identifiability of hidden states does not make their realized values directly available to a controller.

3  Discovering the component without fixing the hidden frame

Start with states grouped by their task signature. For each current next-state block, aggregate the baseline rows and the corresponding rows of any basis U_i of V_i. Split old-state blocks whenever these response signatures differ, considering every admitted input history. Repeat until no split remains.

Equality on U_i is equality on F_i: changing the unknown invertible hidden frame cannot change whether a contrast vanishes. The algorithm therefore does not select a convenient source law, invert the full effect mechanism, or assume that hidden amplitudes have been identified.

The proof of coarseness is short. Any valid task-preserving conditional partition refines the initial task partition. If it refines the current partition, equality of its finer block responses implies equality of the current block responses by summation. The next split never separates states that it legitimately identifies. Induction therefore shows that every valid partition refines the algorithm's final partition. Stability supplies validity. At most |X_i| minus the initial number of blocks strict refinements occur.

Conditional validity means that quotient transitions are well defined for every admitted z. It does not mean independence from z. The separate autonomy flag asks whether every quotient next-block aggregate annihilates V_i. If so, the quotient transition is exterior independent within this universe. If not, no finer deterministic task-preserving autonomous quotient exists: summing its hypothetical zero-response blocks would contradict the observed nonzero aggregate.

For the observed boundary, group finite input histories when both baseline and contrast signatures agree for every quotient state. Refine this relation to a congruence under the specified history-update rule. This is standard finite-state boundary minimization after identifying response equality. It gives a coarsest recursively maintained deterministic summary for those update and initialization rules, not an optimum over arbitrary architectures or unbounded memories. The latent source remains a separately retained interface, not a free sensor wire.

4  A useful component that remains genuinely uncertain

The strongest fixture has eight raw states, written for construction as (a,b,y), with task a. The learner receives randomly permuted state labels and observed B and D tables, not these coordinates or the quotient. Its mechanism factors as

  K(a',b',y' | a,b,y,u,z)
    = [B4(a',b' | a,b,u) + s(a,b,u)(U4 A z)_(a',b')]
      T(y' | a',b',a,b,y,u).

B4 has nonlinear bit and input interactions; s is context dependent. The nuisance transition T has its own memory and responds to old and new core variables. It is independent of z. Summing y' removes the nuisance while retaining a nonzero shared-effect response in the core.

The data force b to be retained because next-task probabilities distinguish its values. They permit y to be discarded because the summed core response does not depend on old y. Exhaustive checking of the 225 task-preserving partitions finds exactly two conditional partitions: the four-state core and the eight-state identity. The four-state partition is uniquely coarsest; no autonomous task partition passes. Every raw transition is positive, with minimum probability approximately 0.0258583.

The discovery order matters. Raw paired residuals against a full-rank reference identify response equalities first. Only after finding the quotient are outcomes aggregated into core tables and local contrast coordinates chosen. The hidden core is not smuggled into the learner's input. Gauge uncertainty consequently acts on the useful discovered component itself, not only on discarded nuisance.

A companion fixture discovers an autonomous four-state core. A small nuisance-to-task feedback perturbation then eliminates every task-preserving autonomous quotient. These provide positive and negative controls. They do not discover the candidate patch cover, and the displayed all-pair-history examples do not include unrestricted cross-patch causal state feedback.

The noisy-readout extension starts further upstream. An oracle constructs a positive 64-state pair chain at fixed input and its nonuniform stationary law, then supplies only observation probabilities: three current sensor views per patch, a 10,000-entry old/next sensor tensor, and current sensor joints with a noisy task bit. Each unknown sensor has ten outputs and eight hidden-state columns. The learner receives neither hidden coordinates, kernels, response matrices, nor the intended quotient.

Three-view calibration recovers the state cardinalities and emission channels. Deconvolution recovers conditional transition tables; the noisy task bit identifies its two emission levels, 0.15 and 0.85. Response ranks are two. Both patches again yield unique four-state conditional quotients and no autonomous task quotient. Five random seeds pass; the largest validation-only kernel error is approximately 8.27e-13. True coordinates are used only afterward for verification. This demonstrates end-to-end numerical recovery from exact stationary observation probabilities, not decoding of individual noisy readings or finite-sample performance. It needs no latent-state preparation or cloned trajectories.

5  When local discoveries belong to one world

Write F_i=U_i A_i in the discovered local basis. Projecting each observed pair gives

  C_ij = A_i A_j^T.

All occurrences of a patch must use the same A_i. On a connected observation graph, choose a root and spanning tree and propagate known matrices L_i. Every solution has A_i=L_i H at even tree distance and A_i=L_i H^(-T) at odd distance.

For a bipartite graph, every remaining edge must satisfy its root-independent predicted identity. If these checks pass, the algebraic freedom is one H in GL(r), not an independent matrix per patch. With an odd cycle, same-parity chords must agree on one symmetric positive definite S=HH^T, or its inverse on the other parity. The remaining family is H=S^(1/2)O with O orthogonal. Inconsistent chords, a nonpositive candidate S, or conflicting S values give explicit obstruction certificates.

A four-cycle with C12=C23=C34=cI but C41=2cI fails, although each edge separately admits positive small-amplitude explanations. Local fitting therefore does not establish one coherent mechanism. Local basis changes are harmless only when transported consistently through every context and incident edge.

Matrix feasibility is not stochastic feasibility. Retain only common H for which every component's conditional row is nonnegative at every admitted context and source value. These constraints couple all components; the same mechanism must persist across time. The admissible set cannot be replaced by independent local ranges.

For each surviving fresh-source world, assemble

  P(x' | x,u) = sum_z pi(z) product_i K_i(x_i' | c_i,z).

Normalization follows by summing conditional rows. Regrouping components preserves this joint law only when the same z occurrence is retained. Multiplying separately averaged factors, independently resampling parameters, or choosing a new compatible frame at every visit creates a different model.

6  What additional experiments can resolve

In a bipartite observation cover, a new opposite-side pair checks a predicted relation but does not restrict H if it passes. A full-rank same-side pair determines HH^T and removes the common scale/shear freedom, leaving orthogonal freedom subject to positivity.

That last freedom is not automatically meaningless. If the source law is fixed and non-Gaussian, a rotation can change higher moments while preserving covariance. In the axis-supported two-dimensional example, replacing every A_i=lambda I by lambda times a 45-degree rotation preserves all pair laws. A four-module centered product changes because the corresponding fourth source moment halves. The displayed fourth-order centered output moments are approximately 1.46484e-7 and 7.32422e-8.

Thus an available quartet experiment separates two named positive worlds that all pair observations miss. This is a specific experimental recommendation, not a promise that any permitted test can settle arbitrary residual ambiguity. The tiny displayed gap also matters: a generic independent bounded-trial estimate requires sample count scaling as the inverse square of the gap, up to confidence factors. Exact distinguishability is not inexpensive distinguishability.

7  Time and source memory

Higher source moments may depend on persistent hidden memory while the conditional mean and covariance remain fixed. Preserve that source process and its initialization as one common witness. Do not choose its law independently at different times or turn shared uncertainty into independent per-row uncertainty.

Equal pair kernels imply equal complete pair histories only when the retained pair's contexts are closed, initial pair laws agree, and both worlds use the same policy based on that pair's history and admitted inputs. Switching observation masks or adapting inputs to unobserved global state introduces additional information and is outside this equivalence. In the positive IID-source fixture, fixed-input stationary pair laws are uniquely determined and agree; uniform initialization is not assumed.

A further qualification is essential. Pointwise quotient closure does not necessarily close the quotient together with source memory. If the source transition reads discarded microstate, retain the residual fiber or impose an appropriate source-transition factorization. Its storage, outgoing fine-state information, and initialization must be charged. The coherent whole-process witness is stronger than a one-step quotient equation, and must not be silently discarded.

8  Stability and verification status

For a true rank-r residual D with smallest nonzero singular value s, operator error epsilon below s/4 gives a leading-subspace projector error at most 2epsilon/s. Aggregate contrast tests inherit this bound multiplied by the functional's norm. Baseline block errors accumulate by summation. Positive rank and separation margins therefore stabilize the finite sequence of splits and autonomy decisions.

The appended finite-observation lemma connects these bounds conditionally to samples. It imports a compact identifiable three-view calibration class with a finite inverse-Lipschitz constant C_cal, aligned labels, bounded channel inverses, positive context floors, and nonzero decision gaps. For Q tables of at most J cells, N independent windows per table suffice for simultaneous raw one-norm error delta with failure probability at most alpha when

  N >= J^2 log(2 Q J / alpha) / (2 delta^2).

Calibration and tensor-product inverse bounds propagate delta to conditional-row error, then to residual operator error. Choosing delta small enough relative to the declared margins recovers the population quotient, autonomy flag, and finite-register boundary. This is a conditional finite-sample statement, not an efficient fitting guarantee: C_cal has no numerical value here, valid-model fitting need not be easy, and the sampled-data learner is not implemented. This lemma certifies local quotient, autonomy, and boundary decisions, not exact graph-cycle identities or global positive-family feasibility from noisy matrices. Joint statistical compatibility needs confidence constraints or a consistent model fit and its own conditioning assumptions. Dependent trajectories require a separate concentration argument. Exact zero is not uniformly learnable without gaps; persistent higher-moment sources need not mix rapidly.

An independent agent mathematical and code review accepted the exact theorem and conditional finite-observation lemma under their stated assumptions. It independently reran all seven companion scripts successfully, including the noisy-calibration default and five-seed runs. The checks cover refinement, positive fixtures, matrix compatibility, stationary pair behavior, and distinguishing moments. They are bounded floating-point or algebraic regression checks, not Lean verification, human peer review, or general statistical validation.

9  Relationship to existing work and the actual advance

The constituent tools are established. Conditional-independent multiview moment factorization and spectral recovery are standard [1]. Controlled lumpability, probabilistic partition refinement, subspace geometry, and matrix completion/synchronization are not claimed as inventions here. Learning useful functionals without identifying an entire inverse problem is an existing objective [2]. Local-to-global distribution obstructions [3] and coupled, nonrectangular uncertainty [4] likewise have substantial prior literatures.

The earlier source-fiber work already retained positive alternative source processes, pair-history equivalence, shared occurrences, and incompatible local marginals. Repeating those facts would not constitute this advance. The present integration identifies an unsupplied task quotient from response subspaces, distinguishes conditional closure from autonomy, and connects that quotient to a jointly positive matrix-frame family and concrete distinguishing experiments. It does so before resolving relevant hidden mechanism uncertainty.

This is a structured conditional discovery result with a consequential eight-to-four example, not a blanket novelty claim. Fixed local dimensions allow a cycle's conditional-table algebra to grow linearly with patch count while the whole state space grows exponentially. This does not establish cheap positivity feasibility, calibration, context acquisition, or weak-signal statistics.
