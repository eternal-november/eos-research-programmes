# Simultaneous persistent interfaces: exact register sharing and a storage/read tradeoff

## Result and limitation first

For finitely many learned linear FIR interface states W_i over a finite field, **minimum global storage and minimum local register-read bandwidth can be attained simultaneously exactly when the generated subspace lattice is distributive**, provided physical input/initialization access is admitted. A common adapted basis gives overlap-local recurrent updates automatically; no invariant complements are required. The compatible-basis ingredient is classical, not a new lattice theorem. Section 2A strengthens necessity beyond linear encodings: with full admissible support and fixed read addresses, saturated storage/read capacities force the same obstruction for arbitrary deterministic zero-error encoders and decoders.

There is a quantitative repair theorem: for d source-history bits and all subset-parity interfaces, permitting b register reads per query reduces the required physical storage from 2^d-1 at b=1 to d at b=d. An overlap-local construction uses at most b(2^ceil(d/b)-1) registers, within factor 2e of a counting lower bound. Thus an obstruction from a nondistributive diamond concerns a **joint storage/read-bandwidth budget**, not unrestricted shared memory.

These results compile exact discovered filters into typed implementations and finite witnesses. They do not themselves improve statistical identification or solve the robust real-valued subspace-incidence problem. The compatible-basis, structured-realization and network-coding baselines in `Linear_Sharing_Realization_Addendum.md` must remain credited.

## 1. Operational class fixed before optimization

All dimensions below are dimensions of functionals on the **admissible source-history states**, quotienting any identically zero directions. For the saturation theorem below, the joint V-demand value must range over all q^d values (as it does under the nondegenerate full-support source models used here).

A scalar register holds one F_q symbol. Its value is a specified linear functional v of the source-history state. Let V be the finite-dimensional span of all demanded interface-state functionals, and let W_i<=V be the state information required by consumer i, of dimension d_i. Demand is to deliver the whole W_i state at each tick, not merely an arbitrary single output of it.

The interface register bank has N scalar registers. Consumer i may read at most b_i register symbols per tick and perform arbitrary linear computation **at that consumer** on the symbols read. It cannot read private component state or other consumers' registers for free. Extra read ports, coded messages, multi-cycle reads and retiming are possible alternatives, but have separately specified bandwidth/latency costs. This is a conventional finite-width register/bus interface, not a prohibition on XOR decoders.

The initial theorem fixes b_i=d_i, the minimum possible reads needed to recover d_i independent symbols. Each register read by i must then lie in W_i: d_i read functionals span the required d_i-dimensional space, hence their span equals W_i. This implication is what makes the algebraic obstruction operational. If b_i>d_i the implication fails, and the theorem's necessity need not apply.

For persistence write the common update of every history functional as

    z_v(t+1)=z_(T v)(t)+beta(v)(u_t),

where T:V->V and beta:V->U* are linear. FIR residual spaces have a nilpotent shift T. Require T W_i<=W_i. Actions or multiple shift modes can be handled by checking every declared T_a and beta_a separately; the mode label must be available at every affected update site.

Physical source regions S_i and source-input access are declared hardware types, not inferred from algebraic similarity alone. A shared register used by consumers I is located at an admitted overlap site with access to the common source inputs and shared old registers. For independent raw source coordinates, the admissible linear input spaces obey U_S* intersect U_R*=U_(S intersect R)*. Require beta(W_i)<=U_(S_i)*. If these conditions are unavailable, return a physical-access blocker rather than interpreting an abstract vector as an observable local input.

For the hidden generative model in `Coupled_Family.md`, source histories are owned latent states of the *synthesized positive mechanism*. This is a generative implementation, not a claim that a real controller can infer those states from noisy sensors at runtime. Deployment requiring observed input access needs a separate estimator/access argument.

## 2. Exact simultaneous minimum theorem

Let d=dim V, V=sum_i W_i, and let L be the subspace lattice generated by {0,V,W_i} under sums and intersections. Work over F_q, so L is finite.

**Theorem 1.** Under the invariant-shift and admitted input/site assumptions above, the following are equivalent:

1. There is a linear interface register bank with N=d and b_i=d_i for every consumer, realizing every W_i.
2. There is a basis B of V for which every W_i is spanned by a subset of B.
3. The lattice L is distributive.

Moreover a basis from (2) supports overlap-local persistent updates, a faithful common initialization and all-time deterministic state identities. The construction retains all permissible bases/support placements if physical labeling is unresolved; uniqueness is not asserted.

**Proof 1=>2.** The d stored functionals must span V, since all demands do; they therefore form a basis. Consumer i reads exactly d_i independent ones, whose span is W_i.

**Proof 2=>3.** Sums and intersections of coordinate subspaces correspond to unions and intersections of basis subsets. These operations are distributive.

**Proof 3=>2 (classical).** For each nonzero join-irreducible J in L let J_- be its unique lower cover, and choose any complement E_J with J=J_- direct-sum E_J. Order join-irreducibles consistently with inclusion. Distributivity gives that J intersects the join of its earlier join-irreducibles precisely in J_-. Hence the E_J sum directly. Every X in L is the direct sum of E_J with J<=X. Choose a basis in each E_J and unite them. This is adapted to all W_i. The complements need not be T-invariant.

**Persistence.** For b in B put I(b)={i:b in W_i} and H_b=intersection_(i in I(b)) W_i. The adapted basis spans H_b by exactly the basis vectors c with I(c) containing I(b). Invariance gives T b in H_b. Consequently

    T b=sum_c a_bc c, with a_bc nonzero only if I(c) contains I(b).

Every old register needed to update b is available to all its consumers. Also beta(b) belongs to every admitted input space of its consumers and hence to their common input space. This proves overlap-local recurrence without independent evolution of ownership atoms. The same argument holds for every T_a.

**Initialization and time.** Draw or supply one admitted source-history state and evaluate every basis functional on it; do not independently initialize overlapping interfaces. If a physical device does not have access to the prescribed initial state, use its declared FIR warm-up or charge initialization communication. Update by the displayed identities with a read-old/commit-new barrier. Induction preserves every demanded state and output for all times. QED.

The theorem minimizes physical scalar register count jointly with the fixed minimum read budgets. It does not optimize update-gate count or wiring: the sparse/dense coefficient lists and their actual per-tick reads must be reported separately.

## 2A. Stronger zero-error necessity: arbitrary deterministic encodings

The minimum-storage/minimum-read obstruction is not merely an artifact of linear register encodings. The following strengthening applies to arbitrary deterministic register encoders and local decoders, with fixed read addresses.

**Theorem 1A (saturated storage and reads).** Suppose the admissible history set H maps onto every joint demand value x in V*, where dim V=d, and consumer i demands x restricted to W_i, with dim W_i=d_i. A device has exactly d q-ary registers, no uncharged persistent side information, and consumer i reads a fixed set J_i of at most d_i registers. Registers may be arbitrary deterministic functions of the entire admitted history; consumer decoders may be arbitrary deterministic functions of their read tuples. Exact recovery of every demand on every admitted history is possible **only if** the generated subspace lattice is distributive. Conversely, under Theorem 1's admitted dynamic/input/initialization hypotheses, distributivity gives its linear persistent construction, and hence is sufficient in this larger class too.

The theorem excludes adaptive read addresses, externally supplied address information, extra private history at the decoder, free public randomness, and uncharged messages. These may define useful alternative architectures but are different resource budgets. It concerns zero-error realization on the whole admitted state set, not average-error compression on a high-probability subset.

**Proof: global saturation.** Let E:H->A^d be the register encoder, |A|=q. Collectively the demanded W_i-values determine x because sum_i W_i=V. Therefore two histories with different x cannot share a register word. There are q^d possible x and only q^d register words. The nonempty sets of register words associated to different x are disjoint; hence each consists of exactly one word and every word is used. Thus E factors through a bijection e:V*->A^d. This argument also excludes storing additional kernel-of-demand history information: there is no spare capacity.

**Proof: local saturation.** Consumer i must distinguish all q^(d_i) values of x|W_i. Its read tuple has at most q^|J_i| values, so |J_i|=d_i. Since the global encoder is onto the entire register product A^d, all q^(d_i) tuples occur. Exact decoding from the tuple to the W_i-value is therefore a bijection. Consequently two x-values have the same W_i-demand if and only if their encoded register words agree on J_i.

**Proof: partition lattice.** For W<=V let P_W be the equivalence relation on V* defined by equality of restriction to W. Its classes are cosets of the annihilator K_W. Intersecting equivalence relations gives

    P_W intersect P_U = P_(W+U).

The join of equivalence relations (transitive closure of their union) gives

    P_W join P_U = P_(W intersect U),

because its difference subgroup is K_W+K_U=K_(W intersect U). Full V* support matters here: the intermediate points realizing these sums are admitted. The map W->P_W is injective and reverses inclusion. It is therefore a lattice anti-isomorphism onto this equality-partition sublattice.

Under the bijection e, each P_W_i is exactly a coordinate-equality relation on the full product A^d. All sums/intersections of their generated relations remain coordinate-equality relations: their join/intersection correspond to intersection/union of coordinate subsets. That lattice is distributive. The anti-isomorphic generated subspace lattice is therefore distributive too. Sufficiency is the linear construction of Theorem 1, including its overlap-local updates. QED.

Thus the defect vector in Section 3 excludes **every deterministic zero-error encoding** under the saturated N=d and fixed b_i=d_i budgets, not just linear changes of basis. The constructive redundant-token checker and quantitative coding bounds below remain expressly linear; this theorem does not extend their optimization claims to arbitrary nonlinear redundant encodings.

## 3. Finite obstruction and why the diamond is not a general impossibility

Failure of distributivity has a finite witness X,Y,Z in L and

    v in X intersect (Y+Z),
    v not in (X intersect Y)+(X intersect Z).

Give bases for X,Y,Z and v together with the sum/intersection derivation expressions from the learned W_i. Gaussian elimination verifies membership/nonmembership. Under Theorem 1A's full-support and fixed-read assumptions, this rules out **every deterministic zero-error** N=d, b_i=d_i implementation in the stated class, not merely a selected linear basis. The witness may be large; no polynomial lattice-generation bound is claimed.

For W_1=<x>, W_2=<y>, W_3=<x+y> over F_2, d=2 and all d_i=1. Three one-read interfaces cannot share only two stored symbols. Storing x,y,x+y uses three physical registers and is exact if their relation is preserved. The joint state still has only four possible values: the third register is redundant physical capacity, not a third independent bit.

Alternatively store x and y only, let the third consumer read **two** symbols, and compute XOR locally. This respects N=2 but changes b_3 from 1 to 2. Or transmit an extra coded message, or use two cycles: charge the message/latency. No theorem here rules out those repairs.

The same distinction separates a minimal filter-state dimension from the number of registers a decoder reads. A one-dimensional computed state view may require two reads from a globally shared coordinate bank.

## 4. General finite repair certificate, with recurrence included

Static spanning alone is insufficient to certify a redundant shared register bank. Use register tokens j=(v_j,I_j): a nonzero projective functional v_j lies in H_(I_j)=intersection_(i in I_j) W_i, and the nonempty visibility set I_j specifies its actual consumers. Two tokens may store the same functional at different sites; both count physically. Scalar-normalize one representative of each projective functional.

A finite certificate consists of:

1. For each i, d_i selected tokens visible to i forming a basis of W_i.
2. For every token j, coefficients witnessing

       T v_j=sum_(l: I_l contains I_j) a_jl v_l,

   and admitted current-input access for beta(v_j).
3. One common initial-history map into the whole register vector, preserving **all linear dependencies** among the v_j.
4. The actual site/wire incidence, update read count, input count and synchronous timing.

These identities are sufficient by induction. They are necessary for linear updates in the specified overlap-local, interface-only register-bank class with those token visibilities. They do not characterize implementations that obtain extra private state, nonlocal observations, nonlinear encodings or multi-cycle messages for free.

Over F_q the candidate projective forms and visibility subsets are finite. A token with identical form and identical visibility need not occur twice when there are no additional per-wire, fanout, or separately constrained site-capacity limits. If those limits are imposed, retain the distinct physical token/site ledger and charge those constraints separately. Thus minimum physical storage in this precise class has a finite exact search with a linear-algebra certificate checker. This is an existence/decision statement, **not** a claimed efficient optimizer or a new generic-feasibility result.

A universal constructive upper bound is sum_i d_i: create private basis registers for each W_i with I_j={i}. Their T-invariance supplies private recurrent updates. It follows that every failure at N=d can be repaired by at most sum_i d_i-d extra scalar registers, subject to admitted private initialization/input access. This bound is sometimes sharp, for example distinct one-dimensional demands under one-read budgets. Shared tokens may substantially improve it, but pairwise intersection dimensions alone do not determine the optimum.

## 5. Quantitative storage/read tradeoff, including a dynamic implementation

The following growing family shows that the resource loss can be arbitrarily large and that allowing local decoders repairs it quantitatively.

There are d independent binary input sources u_1,...,u_d. Define x_j(t)=u_j(t-1): one-tick persistent source records. For every nonempty S subset {1,...,d}, consumer S demands

    q_S(t)=sum_(j in S) x_j(t) over F_2.

Its physical region is precisely S. An interface may only read registers whose forms are supported inside S; it may combine at most b scalar register values per tick. Updates may read the current source inputs in the register's support. Thus no central decoder or forbidden outside-source input is available. Let 1<=b<=d.

**Theorem 2.** Any linear bank serving all these consumers has

    N >= max(d, (b/e) 2^(d/b)).

There is a support-local, persistent construction with

    N <= b(2^ceil(d/b)-1).

The upper bound is within factor 2e of the exponential counting lower bound. At b=1 the exact optimum is 2^d-1; at b=d it is d.

**Lower bound.** Demand includes every nonzero vector in F_2^d, so register forms must span F_2^d and N>=d. Each demand decoded from <=b register values is a sum of a subset of at most b of the N forms. There are at most sum_(j=0)^b binomial(N,j) such vectors, regardless of linear dependencies or physical support restrictions. Since all 2^d vectors must be covered and b<=d<=N,

    2^d <= sum_(j=0)^b binomial(N,j) <= (eN/b)^b.

For b=1, each nonzero demand requires its own stored form, giving exact N=2^d-1. Reading may be assumed nonadaptive for a linear decoder; nonlinear adaptive decision trees are outside this theorem.

**Construction.** Partition the source coordinates into b blocks, each of size at most ceil(d/b). In every block store all its nonzero subset parities. Consumer S reads, from each block intersecting S, the register for S intersect block; there are at most b reads and every register support is contained in S. XOR these reads locally. Update each stored parity by XORing the current inputs in its own support. The register therefore always equals the required one-tick history parity, with T=0 and admitted current inputs. Initialization is obtained from the same previous source vector, or one warm-up tick. Storage is the displayed upper bound; the exact count is sum_blocks (2^blocksize-1). At b=d singleton blocks give N=d.

Register-update input traffic is not free: this construction uses sum_blocks blocksize*2^(blocksize-1) source-to-register scalar incidences per tick, and at most b output reads per consumer. The family has 2^d-1 queries; no polynomial-in-d output-description claim is made. A full one-tick barrier is charged. General longer FIR delay lines can repeat the construction by lag, with their additional storage and timing explicitly charged, but no unproved optimality factor across lag mixing is asserted.

This is a constructive coding/covering bound in a precise physical-support model, not a claim of priority over covering codes, additive bases, index coding or network coding. Its role is to expose the actual frontier that a discovery certificate should report instead of a binary assertion that a crossing decomposition is 'impossible'.

## 6. Using finite-data-discovered filters and preserving crossing witnesses

In the finite-field family of `Coupled_Family.md`, on its stated high-probability structural-recovery event, the learned recurrence matrices and FIR residual maps are exact discrete objects. Derive W_i, T and beta from those inferred maps; then Theorem 1 and the repair checker are exact on the **same event**. No extra continuous-subspace thresholding is necessary. If several structural candidates remain, run the construction/checker for each and retain their different resource ledgers and bases. An obstruction is candidate-conditional until all statistically retained candidates in the declared universe fail the same hardware budget.

This is a conditional theorem composition, not a new sample-complexity improvement. Estimated real-valued subspace intersections can be discontinuous; this note does not solve that harder robust problem.

For the original crossing semantics, use a single source-history record and a single register vector subject to its linear relations. Every regional presentation reads restrictions of that common object. Each shared register and each event/noise token is owned once; all other occurrences borrow its value. Under the recurrence identities, state restriction commutes with the joint update, including old/new timing. A redundant register is a deterministic augmentation of that common record, not fresh stochastic state. Removing such an augmentation is bijective on the constrained graph of the augmentation map, but not on an unconstrained product of independently initialized registers.

The compilation can therefore provide the calculus compilation with explicit state maps, event maps, common-initialization constraints, update identities and incidence ledgers. It does not assert a new planar interchange law, arbitrary nonlaminar gluing theorem, or free equality of different hardware costs.

## 7. Assessment

The nonredundant theorem alone is a classical splitting corollary and does not meet the ambitious discovery target. The substantive integration here is: exact learned finite-field filters plus physically typed persistent sharing, finite all-candidate budget obstructions, and a constructive storage/read-bandwidth repair frontier. Even this must be compared to structured realization and coding baselines, not merely to an unstructured global HMM. No general scientific novelty or full contract completion is claimed.

## 8. Exact bounded regressions

`check_shared_interface_realization.py` exhaustively compared lattice distributivity with existence of a common adapted basis for all 560 three-subspace arrangements in F_2^3. It checked 896 invariant-shift/adapted-basis combinations for the overlap-local update rule, and 36 block-parity construction cases (all 1<=b<=d<=8), including every query in each case. All passed using exact binary arithmetic. `Shared_Interface_QA.json` records the counts. These are regression checks, not substitutes for the all-dimensional proofs.

The nonlinear saturation extension was independently reviewed and approved with its full-support, fixed-read and no-free-side-information restrictions. Additional exact tests enumerated all 65,536 encodings of eight history states (four distinct demand values with two history representatives each) into two binary registers: precisely the 24 demand-value bijections can permit joint exact recovery, and each discards the extra history coordinate. All 24 bijections fail the three-line diamond's simultaneous one-read requirements. The counts are included in `Shared_Interface_QA.json`.
