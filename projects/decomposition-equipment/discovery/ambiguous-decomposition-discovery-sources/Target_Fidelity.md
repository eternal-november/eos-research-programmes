## Quantifiers that must not collapse

Let C(D) be the retained world family after observed data D; let L_B(W) denote implementations of W satisfying an independently declared budget/access type B.

1. Some retained explanation has a lift: there exist W in C(D), I in L_B(W).
2. Every retained explanation is liftable: for every W in C(D), there exists I_W in L_B(W).
3. One observationally deployable implementation works for all worlds: there exists a single I, using only admitted information, with the required world-indexed correctness relation for every W in C(D).

The third implies the second, and the second implies the first when C(D) is nonempty; converses fail. A compilation schema whose constants depend on unobserved W establishes (2), not (3). A common generator of the observable law is weaker than a common physical-state or task realization.

**Exact counterexample.** W_b has fixed hidden physical bit b in {0,1} and identically zero observation history. A physical requirement is u=b. Each world has a zero-memory correct constant policy. No observation-based common randomized policy is surely correct in both: if P(u=1)=q, its success probabilities are q and 1-q, hence worst-world success is at most 1/2. This is a quantifier counterexample, not the desired positive research family. Positive generative realization of the zero observation stream remains trivial and valid.

A claim of common implementation must specify whether its world-dependent simulation relation is merely proof data, whether initialization can depend on inaccessible W, and whether a fixed physical sensor/port is transported or held fixed. Charging world-specific initialization as a free common resource conceals the distinction.
