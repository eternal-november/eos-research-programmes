# Prior lifting boundary: discovery without architecture identification

28 October 2026. Theorem-level audit of the supplied original checkpoints; not a contract amendment, new theorem proof, literature novelty clearance, or empirical certification.

## Finding first

**Removing an identifiable latent-frame assumption is a legitimate new target, but finite ambiguity, partial-chart completion, coherent semantic lifting, and unknown crossing refinement are already covered by earlier results.** The new burden cannot be discharged merely by enumerating candidate worlds, checking their decompositions, and returning all successful candidates. The strongest existing baseline combines:

1. finite partial-chart completion with unknown persistent alignments and nonlaminar component kernels;
2. all-history reconstruction with one fixed physical world;
3. physical-resource-aware semantic discovery over unbounded generated profiles across every admitted world;
4. constructive crossing common-refinement recognition in a declared single-grammar cell-incidence class;
5. separate exact record-lifting and physical-compilation tests.

A genuinely additional constructive result should derive a useful decomposition certificate from **partially informative observation/history laws while several inequivalent physical architectures remain compatible**, without requiring full latent-frame identification or supplying the sought actual chart maps. It must specify which discoveries are possible, robust, or jointly realizable; preserve one common-world witness across crossings and time; and exhibit an advantage over the existing exhaustive completion baseline. Nonuniform history distributions and partially informative readouts must occur inside the positive theorem, not only in a counterexample or a closing limitations paragraph.

## 1. Exact source map and strongest statements already available

### A. Partial-chart completion already retains architecture ambiguity

Source root **S**:
`previously delivered 27 October suitability-reconstruction source checkpoint`

**S/theory/Typed_Persistent_Completion.md, Theorem 1 (§3).** Given finite directed-multigraph charts, finite sets of admissible overlap maps, finite equations/markings, phase/observation/action data, and exact chart-image/domain assertions, the explicit compatible-tuple/subgraph/quotient construction enumerates all generated witnessed realizations up to allowed carrier renaming. No compatible whole or common refinement is assumed. Unknown alignments are permitted. Component quotient kernels may cross; they need not be laminar. Required new exact witnesses are tested on states AND actual event records.

Its substantive scope restrictions are joint separation of whole states and event records by chart projections, no whole records invisible to the chart tuple, and components generated as quotients of the whole. Unbounded invisible multiplicity is not covered. Partial-domain charts require their domains/images to be specified; missing coverage is not invented. Correctness of chart descriptions is assumed, not inferred from sensor data. Exhaustive finite enumeration is expressly not a practical discovery achievement.

**Same source, Theorem 2 (§4).** For every finite and infinite history, reconstructed executions are exactly histories of one physical completion `omega`, held fixed throughout. The result commutes with truncation, same-world concatenation and information-preserving exact occurrence refinement. Edgewise existence of possibly different completions is explicitly insufficient.

**Same source, §§2 and 5.** Physical completion set `Omega` is retained before optional decomposition tests, with mathematical witness fibres `D(omega)`. An empty `D(omega)` does not erase a physically possible world. Suitability uses

`exists one implementation/causal runner; forall physical omega; exists coherent proof d_omega; forall adverse executions of that same world`.

World-specific proof choices are not runtime choices. A demanded single world-independent decomposition is a stronger quantifier order. This already addresses the central common-world warning; restating it is not a new ambiguity theorem.

### B. Unbounded semantic discovery across ambiguous worlds already exists

**S/theory/Operational_Semantic_Profile_Recognition.md, Theorems 1–2 (§4).** For a fixed finite implementation, a finite nonempty completion family, finite candidate maps/physical aliases and independently specified regular joint-support constraints, image/injectivity/surjectivity defect automata recognize exactly all hardware-compatible semantic coordinate families. Context-free/regular emptiness decides existence, universal acceptance, and a paired coarse/fine inclusion test over **all generated finite profiles**, without a tree-depth bound. Proof maps may depend on `omega`; hardware tables and aliases may not. The target support may not be defined as the image of the candidate map, which would make the test vacuous.

The input contains actual finite coupled state/event carriers and permitted map tables. The result is all-outcome support semantics, not stochastic readout-law identification. It has potentially exponential subset automata and no practical complexity claim. Independent arbitrary context-free constraints are not silently intersected.

**Same source, Theorem 3 (§7), crossing common-refinement recognition.** Given two exact coupled descriptions of the same root, with the same outer boundary and one implementation, a single context-free cell-tagged refinement doctrine plus regular row/column tests constructs an unknown common refinement or proves none exists at any permitted finite arity. It returns actual inner maps, named leaf occurrence bijection, both refinement forests, and explicit boundary transport `Phi = H_V inverse(H_U)`. This is stronger than checking a supplied refinement. It is not arbitrary-network recognition, not a general choice functor, and not inference of those parent descriptions from partially informative observations.

**S/theory/Typed_Causal_Presentation_Closure.md, finite-generator realization theorem (§4).** Shared hardware, actual readout timing, resources and declared crossing equations produce coherent realization under generated typed refinements. Merely claiming all-tree causal compatibility or adding an alias registry would repeat this baseline.

## 2. Nonuniform histories and partial readouts: avoid false novelty claims

The old suitability theory already permits nonuniform **sets of visited histories**: `S/theory/Operational_Cut_Capability.md §1` uses W_c and W_a for actual cut/edge visitors, full observable causal prefixes p_c, and newly available readouts o_a. It never manufactures a history at an unvisited cut. Theorem 1 reconstructs one shared-table controller from simultaneous constraints; Theorem 2 handles dynamic finite architectures. `S/theory/Causal_Descent.md Theorems 1–4` already handles partial observation, persistent belief, bounded actual controllers, information-aware invariance and crossing/belief-update failures. Neither result makes arbitrary nonuniform **probability laws** disappear: probability is explicitly additional semantics.

The latest `persistent_interface_discovery_20261028/Coupled_Family.md Theorem A` instead gives polynomial moment discovery in a strict finite-field model whose architecture is identifiable up to latent coordinate permutation. The stationary proof uses uniform lag histories; arbitrary initialization is handled after burn-in for transitions, not exact initial-law recovery. `Constructive_Target.md §9` permits heterogeneous observation kernels but still identifies the relevant pairing/path and joint positive kernel; unidentifiable splitting of an emission noise is not the same as unresolved decomposition architecture.

Therefore merely adding noise, hiding current states, allowing branches of different lengths, or observing a nonuniform finite sample is not enough. The new positive family should have at least two genuinely different compatible architectures remaining after the available observations, while still deriving a nontrivial useful decomposition/interface result. If “nonuniform histories” means a nonuniform actual distribution, say so and prove the result without uniform-orthogonality shortcuts. If it means heterogeneous history lengths/access, retain that as an additional distinct stress case.

## 3. Precise remaining theorem burden

The following is a **proposed burden**, not a claim proved by the old work and not an amendment to the original contract.

Given a declared positive dynamic model class M, observable partial-history data/laws E, and a resource/typed discovery doctrine D, construct a faithful compatibility object C(E) without assuming an identifiable full latent frame or already known state/event chart maps. Derive a useful certificate d on the partial description and characterize or construct its compatible whole lifts. For crossing discoveries d_1,...,d_k, characterize the set

`{ W in C(E) : exists ONE joint realization of all d_i on W, with required anchors, positive kernels, histories and resources }`.

The same W must occur throughout the intersection and all time steps. Distinguish:

- possible discovery: some compatible W has a lift;
- robust structural discovery: every compatible W has a proof-level lift, possibly world-dependent;
- uniform implemented discovery: one permitted implementation/adaptor works across all W;
- joint discovery: all chosen partial discoveries lift together in a single W, not one W per discovery.

A positive result must produce actual coherent maps and an executable local positive realization where claimed, not just a compatible collection of probability marginals. A negative result should name its scope and provide a finite equality/collision, missing tuple, incompatible conditional law, rank/resource, or infeasibility certificate. Failure to identify a frame is not itself an obstruction to useful decomposition.

**Constructive genuinely new residue:** prove a nontrivial family or structural criterion where C(E) contains inequivalent architectures, nonuniform histories and partially informative observations are intrinsic, yet useful lifts/crossing outcomes are derived without global enumeration or full identification. A polynomial/local/symbolic algorithm with a proved compression invariant would exceed the exhaustive partial-chart baseline. Alternatively, a theorem extending record-retaining fullness to hidden-record abstraction with a precise positive, resource-sensitive lift criterion could exceed Theorem B. Simply a finite bounded semialgebraic feasibility reformulation is a correctness baseline, not by itself a demonstrated new discovery mechanism.

The scope cannot quietly shrink to a supplied answer: finite bounds and a model universe are legitimate, but the actual desired partition, latent frame, required common-world anchors or compatible local positive generators must not be inputs masquerading as discovered outputs. Conversely joint generative fitting counts as discovery; it should not be rejected merely because its positive assembly is automatic by parametrization.

## 4. Acceptance and evidence ledger for the new work

A defensible completed result should exhibit (i) actual unresolved observational equivalence; (ii) explicit discovered information shared across that equivalence class or a correctly scoped possible-discovery set; (iii) compatible-whole lifting with exact quantifiers; (iv) genuinely crossing simultaneous realization or obstruction; (v) nonuniform-history and partial-readout positive stress cases; (vi) resource/initialization/causal-access accounting; and (vii) a comparison showing precisely what earlier theorems could not already deliver on the same input.

The earlier audit `related_work_audit_20261028/Discovery_Target_Comparators.md` also rules out broad novelty slogans: partial disentanglement and residual architecture equivalence already appear in mechanism-sparsity theory; alternative stochastic network realizations are established. `Positive_Realization_Audit.md` separates positive realization from signed prediction, and notes that ordinary positive simulations do not preserve prescribed locality/resources. These are comparison constraints, not proof that the proposed theorem is either novel or impossible.

No original contract or historical acceptance status was changed. This note establishes the internal non-retread boundary; the constructive new theorem remains to be supplied and independently checked.
