## Remaining burden and limitations

The finite-data prior-class assumption, independent sensor-triple structure, full-rank calibration, bounded lag/width, full support and temporal product separation are substantial. They make a structured identification theorem possible but do not verify that a real environment lies in this class. Generic separation is not a uniform numerical margin. If a mechanism family ties parameters so all rows lie in an exceptional algebraic set, a genericity argument over unconstrained product laws does not apply.

The theorem discovers elementary conditional-innovation coordinates, not a uniquely privileged physical component ontology. Grouping them into useful subsystems and selecting interface/resource budgets remains a search. The bounded region search and joint summary tests are an actual additional operational step, but only for the stated boundary contract. The expanded gluing note now proves a sound-and-complete patch-compatible unresolved family within the declared product-support/positive-transition universe. Its positive log-potential/KL argument upgrades full patch conditional agreement to global transition agreement. A finite constraint representation and bounded-treewidth solution DAG address compact ambiguous-family representation under explicit bounds on the actual combined constraint graph. Without those width bounds no polynomial global compatibility guarantee is supplied. This supersedes the initial review’s statement that the unresolved-family obligation remained entirely open; it does not extend completeness to invisible states, arbitrary sensor grammars or non-product supports.
