# Finite stock, launch funding and heat on one physical history

Status: constructive resource adapter with universal written storage/heat arguments, a bound PROGRAM/RUNTIME-PORTS inventory and exact checks. The bound inventory supplies one finite resource architecture for the current stored program and port catalogue. Final source application still requires the actual physical price/rate, fault, mechanical-storage and time-conversion laws stated below; finite inventory is not proof that an unspecified device realizes them. A successful repair rate is neither an input nor a conclusion here. Resource sufficiency preserves a requested physical operation's funding; its factual permission, connection, correct content and useful effect remain distinct obligations.

SOURCES records the preceding delegated examination of Moser's finite-storage/interval-energy results and Le Boudec–Thiran's service calculus. PROBES P2 already separates the two charging orders. This adapter uses elementary conservation and interval bounds, not a new storage theorem or a clairvoyant scheduler. The substantive source choices are where energy is physically stored, which launches consume it, how already funded work survives disconnect, and how every hostile use is bounded without assuming nominal traffic.

## Actual stores and launched work

Each host has its own upstream reservoir U_i of finite capacity C_i. No proof spends a neighbor's reservoir or combines disconnected stores into one available balance. Each downstream powered processor/generator branch has its actual cut, current/force limiter and finite local reserve. These are physical apparatus, with defects and adversarial reach assigned by INSTANCE; the claim is conditional on their raw constitutive laws, not on a truthful software meter. A local reservoir is not a clean program donor and does not choose, authorize or validate interventions. A continuing physical defect keeps its real footprint; a lower supply envelope cannot conceal that loss.

For an operation that can complete after CPU reset or power removal, one candidate realization allocates a physically charged finite private one-shot tail store at launch, sufficient for its entire worst-case completion energy. Charging that store is an actual earlier debit from the supplying store. The tail store can power only that latched finite operation, has a bounded completion/dissipation interval, and has no route back into CPU launch authority. Unused energy is dissipated or returned across a separately inventoried route; the sufficient budget credits no refund. Launches cannot share one allocated tail store, and finite hardware slots bound the simultaneously reserved amount. A failed write, rejected packet or cancelled conversion still incurs its actual attempted-work cost. This choice does not guarantee the operation's semantic success, cancel an old effect, or let an unpowered CPU execute another instruction. The conditional post-charge account represents HYBRID's latched, funded tails with explicit storage rather than a negative balance; its physical precharge/rate supplier is described next.

The actual transfer cannot be replaced by an instantaneous accounting debit while a finite physical charging prefix has not yet moved the energy. Incoming energy may overflow the still-full carrier during that prefix. A physical realization must precharge dedicated slots or include the real funding prefix, slot admission/rejection, charging power/time and startup in the service argument. The present post-transfer conservation law and illustrative tail inventory do not establish PROGRAM's nominal instruction/IO rate. RUNTIME-PORTS supplies that finite primitive bridge, including separate ADC/read slots, target/side write slots and command/request pipelines. The bound inventory below consumes its complete77536 port slots,160 instruction capsules and separate in-flight fields. The older illustrative2Bd target-tail allocation is retained only in the comparison inventory and is not used for the main runtime; the4Bd target-side offer bound could not justify it.

An alternative shared-reserve realization is allowed, but then total E and outstanding commitments R must be retained separately and a launch of cost e requires E−R≥e. At reservation E is unchanged and R increases; at completion both decrease by the actually consumed reserved amount; refunds reduce R without creating E. Charging energy to a fictitious free balance with the full physical capacity is unsound: E=10,R=8 leaves free2. An incoming8 overflows a physically full capacity10; the free balance is still2, not min(10,2+8)=10. RESOURCES checks this false certificate. Selected isolated one-shot tail stores avoid this ambiguity; they do not claim that bookkeeping alone creates extra capacity.

Both realizations must agree with PORTS: every refundable compartment able to rearm the retiring bank participates in the strict total-energy drainage law, or is physically prevented from funding new launches. Waiting for each reservation's finite deadline does not suffice if arbitrary code can refund and immediately rebook it. No-new-launch means a real threshold/cut relation, not an untrusted program's halt report. Old writes, packets and forces keep their actual tails after the last possible launch.

Tail funding covers the complete physical operation promised by the launch, including any bounded downstream serialization/write suffix. A finite instruction completion is not automatically the last effect: its emitted packet may arrive later, and a powered destination may retain its command. Those suffixes need their own paid stores/routes and additive causal delay bounds. A remote independently powered actuator that obeys an old command forever is not a finite tail and defeats the present retirement map. The energy head for new-launch clearance measures the common/refundable operating carrier; isolated, non-rearming tail stores remain separately represented until their last effect. An ordinary future receiver response is still receiver work and cannot be charged to a vanished sender or omitted from the shared bill.

Counts do not depend on the horizon or on successful repair. Prices are nonnegative upper bounds with physical energy units; actual nontrivial operations have positive cost. For consumer class j at host i, let n_ij be the installed count, c_ij its maximum energy per event, and sigma_ij+rho_ij L its actual event envelope on every interval. Let p_i be the sum of continuous power ceilings. Then all launch funding and continuous expenditure have the common bound

    D_i([s,t]) <= b_i + d_i (t-s),
    b_i = sum_j n_ij c_ij sigma_ij,
    d_i = p_i + sum_j n_ij c_ij rho_ij.

Endpoint events are included in b_i. All quantities refer to one source history; no nominal-only scheduling assumption appears. At a first alleged funding failure, the same event bound applies to all earlier paid launches and to the attempted next launch, so the argument does not assume its own conclusion. Finite packet stores limit retained data, while the all-symbol envelopes limit repeated rejection work. Neither implies the other.

## Causal finite-reservoir theorem

First take a continuous reservoir with finite power, actual offered supply S and consumption D, reflection/overflow only at capacity C, and no negative storage. Suppose U(0)=C and every interval of length L has D<=b+dL and S>=R max(0,L−T), with R>=d. These are actual potential-supply and offered-work bounds; surplus may be physically spilled. Then

    Delta := sup_(L>=0) [b+dL−R max(0,L−T)] = b+dT,
    U(t) >= C−Delta.

For a direct proof, choose the most recent capacity contact before a putative lower crossing. If there was none after initialization, use time0. From that contact to the crossing there is no overflow, so the drop equals demand minus offered supply and is at most Delta. If capacity contacts have no last member, continuity and the supremum of those times give the same limit; bounded event jumps are included in b. A first failed debit would require a larger drop than C, contradicting C>=Delta. The proof never asks a processor to predict future supply or choose a favorable event order.

A positive reserve margin M gives C=b+dT+M and U>=M. If a consumer needs a branch reserve or an outstanding-commitment margin, it must be included in M and that branch's actual delivery proof. For a shared-reservation implementation, M>=R_max+e_peak gives free energy at least e_peak when the independent finite-slot bound R<=R_max holds. It does not prove a disconnected bank receives power. For the selected isolated-tail implementation, the debit at launch already pays the future tail, and the tail store cannot be spent twice.

Discrete delivery has a different law. Under clip-before-pay, A_k=min(C,U_k+s_k), U_(k+1)=A_k−d_k, the sufficient capacity is b+d(T+1), not b+dT. The exact criterion is demand on [a,b) minus supply on [a+1,b) at most C for every interval. resource_model.py independently enumerates variable-demand/variable-supply histories and compares this interval criterion with actual failures. This source does not obtain continuous direct-use capacity by assigning the same variable names to a slotted charger.

For recurring supply deficits, choose demand d>0, period P>T0>0, and physical supply0 during each length-T0 outage, then r>d during the remainder, with r(P−T0)>dP. The actual deficit dL−S([s,s+L]) is uniformly bounded, so the source has a latency-rate lower envelope with rate d and finite latency even across infinitely many outages.
