# A fixed paired-island input-domain construction

Status: candidate static source construction with independent bounded review and repaired interface checks, not full source admission. The six-token/eight-slot obstruction in [THRESHOLD-INCIDENCE](THRESHOLD-INCIDENCE.md) applies to freely chosen distinct paired domains at the same receiver. This construction instead pins an actual finite installed domain graph before a run. It preserves independent singleton faults on every auxiliary path and primary/execution faults; it adds one shared pair per receiver. It does not reinterpret two distinct installed islands as one cause or derive a favorable raw history from successful control.

## Installed graph and incident interface

Within each even cohort m>=82, retain the forty-neighbor graph and actual fifteen-provider consumer groups. For each receiver r, install one common input/control compartment E_r joining the two auxiliary backend paths whose physical providers are positions1 and2 of the receiver's selected XY group. This choice is a design-time function of physical incidence and head type, not an online choice of honest origins. The two endpoints are distinct actual neighbors and remain the same through repeated acquisition, bank reuse and group changes. Another fixed pair could supply the cardinality proof; the selected pair guarantees an actually two-slot shared-input example in the consumed second-row group.

The compartment contains both source-bank copies of the selected auxiliary paths' raw/control state; each path keeps its physical provider/key binding, location and funding map. The primary frontend is separate. Singleton domains A_(r,o) remain available for every physical auxiliary origin o in N(r), including either member of E_r. T_r retains the direct whole-primary/execution-domain role. Corruptions may recur and several receivers' paired islands may be affected simultaneously. The construction does not impose one paired fault globally or one permanently correct path.

The candidate static input footprint at consumer i and its actual provider list G is:

- T_i reaches primary slot0.
- A_(i,o) reaches o's selected auxiliary slot if present, excluding slot0.
- E_i reaches its two installed providers' selected auxiliary slots if present, excluding slot0.
- T_r, A_(r,o) and E_r for r!=i reach the selected provider-r slot if present, representing any receiver-r inference/output corruption exported by its actual provider role.

These last projections are an interface to be implemented, not a claim that an arbitrary physical compartment necessarily has that reach. In particular, raw source-origin power paths must not turn an island defect into an uncharged failure of several other donors. Each physically reached private selector/predicate, raw snapshot, timer, reset path and output role must be assigned. Input-only variants leave receiver inference intact and retain its factual duty. An actual physical fault creating additional write or power paths needs its complete larger projection.

[fixed_island_model.py](fixed_island_model.py) uses a typed group descriptor (cohort, receiver, plant-row or maintenance-target index) to reconstruct the exact ordered provider group, and checks actual incident-origin membership. Callers cannot reorder a provider list and thereby reassign the primary slot. Context validation runs even for empty histories and zero-budget checks; budgets are exact natural integers. The fixed-pair incident carries only its installed receiver, not an attacker-selected pair field. Unknown incident classes are rejected, not assigned an empty footprint. In particular, a nonlocal calibration source and a physical-fixture/target-status fault do not yet receive a supplier here. The complete source must incorporate them at their actual quantity and role indices; target-common false status at an already charged defective target is not interchangeable with corruption of independent functional load measurements.

## Static support law

Let F be a finite list of incident occurrences admitted at this static interface, k its length, and l the number of E_i occurrences at the consumer i. Repeated occurrences remain counted as occurrences; no execution-host deduplication is used for the raw budget. Let S_i(F) be the union of their actual slot footprints. Then

    l=0: |S_i(F)| <= min(15,k).
    l>=1: |S_i(F)| <= min(15,k-l+2) <= min(15,k+1).

Every occurrence except a local E_i contributes at most one slot. All l local E_i occurrences have the same fixed footprint, with at most two slots. Union subadditivity gives the first inequality directly and the second after replacing their repeated union by that one actual footprint. The argument allows overlaps among singleton and shared domains and different source-bank records; it does not count the two banks twice. For k<=6 it gives at most seven affected slots. One E_i plus five suitable distinct provider incidents attains seven in the selected XY group, so this bound is not merely a loose illustration of a smaller actual interface.

The law is uniform over cohort size and consumer groups supplied by the actual incidence constructor. Its kernel forgets incident times, operation states and costs only for this static union query. The same quotient is not used for energy, raw acquisition multiplicity, old authority, return deadlines or world-output influence. A real causal-window supplier must show that every still-effective event and inherited residue belongs to F before this static result is used. Six currently active acquisitions do not imply six causes over the required history window.

This is a source-design restriction relative to the more permissive paired-domain alphabet, not a proof that every earlier candidate has seven-slot support. Two genuinely different local paired compartments restore the exhibited eight-slot counterexample unless their union is otherwise bounded. A larger common dependency, a moved endpoint, a second shared island at one receiver, or cross-region power/export reach reopens the map. A weighted raw-history alternative remains live if the eventual physical construction needs that broader alphabet.

## Finite checks and physical obligations

The checker maps every T_r, E_r and forty singleton A_(r,o) domains in each tested finite cohort before merging identical static bitmasks. A breadth-first union calculation explores every footprint reachable with at most six causes. Reusing an already used domain cannot increase its static union, so allowing repeated additions does not miss a possible union. Merging equal masks preserves this union/cardinality query only; it grants no common physical identity or reusable energy credit. Tests cover two XY groups, an X group, seam cases and all forty maintenance groups at representatives of82,86 and the enormous fixed cohort. The symbolic union argument supplies the family-wide claim; the finite enumeration is an encoding check.

Independent review checked the union proof and the exact reachable-mask counts9949 (singletons through size6) and11236 (adding the pair plus five other slots). Its empty-input/context and Boolean-budget countercases prompted entry-point validation; typed group reconstruction replaced an unchecked ordering argument. These repairs concern the checker interface, not extra positive-source credit.

Physical admission still requires a finite faultable implementation of the singleton/shared compartments, independent primary boundary, source/receiver funding and private work, reset/expiry/retirement, actual head interpretation and group-key routing. It also requires the raw local acquisition/occupancy envelopes, permanent physical footprints, complete same-history causal projections and recurrent resource/service proof. The new graph does not supply any of those by naming E_r. It is a candidate for the positive family because its installed shared dependency has a provable consumed slot bound while all auxiliary origins remain faultable; scientific acceptance and the full RP0–RP6 completion remain separate.
