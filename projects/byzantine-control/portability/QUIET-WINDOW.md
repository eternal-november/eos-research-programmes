# Finding recovery time among recurrent interruptions

For the amended eighty-body and threshold machines, the elapsed-time certificate can tolerate substantially more inherited activity than a search shorter than one bank's POWER spacing. A quiet interval can exist across many changes of bank. Repeated reappearances of a retained output can also be treated together when their *entire* possible influence lies inside a bounded interval. The result reduces the source obligation to bounding the number of such intervals and the elapsed time they cover. The controller continues to run its installed policy throughout. ## Certificate and causal use

Fix one actual history, consumer, search cut and search duration L. Supply a finite family of closed intervals containing all the disturbances that could interrupt this consumer's local recovery itinerary over that search. Intervals may extend across its boundaries. A single point is an interval of duration zero. The pre-existing paid operations, future delayed deliveries and physical output exposures inside each interval belong to that same history. Grouping several banks or devices requires a common covering interval for their joint effects. Merely observing the same location or retaining a fixed number of buffers proves neither the interval nor its duration.

Clip to the search window, merge overlapping or touching intervals, and let M be the resulting number of occupied components and D their total duration. Even if a particular source has infinitely many harmless crossings inside one such interval, its interval certificate can remain finite. The at most M+1 complementary spans together have duration L−D. If L−D > (M+1)q, one span has width greater than q. An interior closed subinterval of length q then avoids interference at *both* ends, in every admitted order of simultaneous events. Zero width busy points count in M. Centers at 1 and 2 on [0,3] meet every closed unit interval; the non-strict inequality would pass incorrectly. A single occupied component can also consume the whole horizon. Neither total duration nor component count alone suffices.

The Lean function scan returns an actual gap with width greater than q or a certificate that the summed free width is at most the sum of q over the supplied gaps. Its addition/ordering interface applies to exact real durations with the usual order, without synchronized digital clocks or an implicit rounding of cut times. The rational implementation constructs the entire partition, checks its measure identity and returns concrete interior endpoints. The theorem is deterministic at the level of a fixed schedule. It makes no independence assumption about providers, their times or their target's state.

The gap selector is a proof observer. To use the result for return, the particular fixture must have an on-run operation already guaranteed to finish within q of uninterrupted eligible time from **every reachable entering state in this search**. RETURN-ITINERARY provides that local result for the hot post-seat attempt subject to its stated geometry, signed force, genuine input, stage memory and custody hypotheses. Source obligations preceding the first eligible attempt keep their separate costs. If actual controller behavior requires a visible clear signal or poll, include its worst delay in q; if restarting is irreversible or the actor cannot tell that a prior attempt ended, the selection alone supplies no executable strategy. The quiet period does not globally retire the authors of every covered effect. Its result is the particular local terminal identified by the itinerary. This is the same progress distinction as the contextual reservation-service example's separation of retained physical occupancy, genuine service enablement and actual dispatcher steps. ## From physical recurrence to a search budget

Suppose every search placement has M(L) ≤ b + ρL and D(L) ≤ v + ηL. Separate origin, source-bank, incoming and retained-path bounds may be added on their actual joint history. Summing occupied time counts overlaps conservatively; a proven common enclosing interval can improve it. With 1−η−qρ > 0, any L satisfying

    L > [v + q(b+1)] / (1−η−qρ)

suffices. The constructor returns the first whole quantum strictly above this real threshold. This exploits the real elapsed time of the underlying system; one integer deadline does not force discrete event times. For episodes of width at most d with count b+ρL, one valid profile has v=db and η=dρ. An earlier finite pending tail may instead occupy one common clipped prefix of width d₀, regardless of how many child operations are present. Treating it as an arbitrary component is conservative by one gap. Failure of the sufficient test leaves other overlap and source structure available. For point arrivals and a single-horizon count law, equal spacing with all gaps at most q shows why a critical density cannot generally force a strictly longer gap. A sliding-window law or stricter burst bound can exclude that particular schedule.

A delayed first exposure need not coincide with POWER. On one bank, if each physical epoch contributes at most one interfering episode, intervals starting in a search are attributable to its already live epoch or to a birth within the search. Spacing h between genuine births gives at most 1+L/h of the latter, regardless of delay to the first observable effect, provided that effect cannot outlive or independently reactivate outside its assigned epoch or tail. For this installation there are 80 contributing banks (forty hosts times two). Hence the long-run count rate can be 80/h without imposing that the whole search finish inside h. At a partial cut one must separately cover residual old bodies, fixed pending deliveries, and any eligible receiver continuation. Counting all events at the source and omitting later independent remapping at a sink would invalidate this step.
