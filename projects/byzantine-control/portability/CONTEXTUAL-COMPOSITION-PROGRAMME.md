## 6. Choose which physical and quantitative abstractions actually save work

The applications supply mechanism-specific sufficiency and cost results as well as examples of failure.

- **Fixed-plant substitution.** For the linear, memoryless passive part of a network, boundary reduction eliminates internal nodes while preserving the boundary current/effort relation. **Economic assessment.** I would change the *default decomposition* now. For each proposed operation specify the actual input view, the pre-existing commitments it can touch, the shared update, who may choose it and when, and what later consumers can still observe or do. Use the direct physical proof for each primitive and the compositional result for the schedules, substitutions and regroupings for which its hypotheses hold. This is more defensible than adding the entire formal stack to the implementation, and more consequential than adding a checklist. It changes what an implementation must produce before it can become an operand of a larger proof.

The strongest near-term reductions have different economics. The spatial separator costs a new history interface and could remove repeated *global* consistency arguments across population sizes. The local-response criterion costs an explicit action/observation model and can settle impossible prescriptions before mechanism design. Historical retirement and passive updates cost a complete ownership/effect inventory, with the potential to replace repeated arguments about callbacks, stale capabilities and handovers. I would invest in those three before translating the entire plant. Higher coherence, general semantic evaluators, arbitrary feedback traces, stochastic repair laws and infinite coefficient constructions become worthwhile when two actual implementations use the corresponding comparison. An abstract construction with no interpretation of the source effect merely moves its proof into the adapter.

The comparison that would settle net savings is a direct and compositional proof under the same mission, with all setup costs charged. Look for a width-bounded spatial certificate that survives a growth in the number of regions, a response certificate that survives a changed sensor grouping, or a retirement argument that survives a different paid mechanism. Record the actual global argument removed by the second use. A change that needs a new proof over the entire source history, a new trusted gate, or stronger adversary assumptions does not earn that saving. The value already established is narrower and concrete: checked spatial structure, precise policy and causal obstruction tests, and exact replacement laws at several interfaces. The ultimate common recovery proof is still open.
