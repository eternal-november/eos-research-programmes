# One source bank, many independently paced receiving lanes

**Result.** A physical source bank can have one CPU, one operating E, one TX clock serving its eighty fixed branches, and an independently paced receiving endpoint at each branch. Simultaneous receivers spend the same origin U in the actual order of their operations. The construction also composes both operating banks of one origin on that U. Receiver aperture jobs stay private to their lane. A private comparison capture shared by the two source banks remains one physical unit. The finite-history extension follows from these identities and local event rules. The new receiver-local divider is a source design choice whose fault and cost boundaries are stated below. ## Physical allocation and source change

Fix an installed regional host o. Each exposed source bank b has its own actual FramedParent, TimedSource, OperatingBank and already installed common charger. Its ADC and its 80 × 129 DATA slots belong to that charger. Its eighty TX branch destinations are the ordered pairs (t,r) of a fixed neighbor t and its receiver bank r. A receiver is keyed by ℓ=(o,b,t,r). The registered RawLane has a fixed decoder, FIFO, captured head, native parser, records, reset work and local control bytes. For each k actually emitted by that decoder into a record, (ℓ,k) names one physical record and its independent elapsed clock. Every raw payer on these lanes is the same actual U(o). In the two-bank case the operating E and CPU still differ by b. The object roster receives no global timestamp, list of healthy participants or schedule from the analytical matching construction. Comparison capture has a different ownership key (o,t,r,role), where role is primary or auxiliary. Both (o,0,t,r) and (o,1,t,r) use exactly that capture for the same role. Distinct roles and receiver banks have distinct units. A capture freezes its actual lane and comparison key, not a lane chosen again at completion. The product validates object identities including work cells and retains the original captured claim across later operations. It refuses equal-labelled clones, an incomplete fixed destination family requested as closed, or a source with incompatible physical phases. Failing a later bank’s attachment leaves the first bank’s prior ownership and lookup intact. The fixed-layout proof treats a genuinely changed physical route as a new source transition requiring a new support and price account. The decoder’s full fixed tree and each (source key, role, resulting key, lifetime) must identify the installed record. Its lookup and ordered reset enumerations must name the same objects, and queue.control must be that lane’s RX array. Each still-live decrement is bound to its own record and captures a word in that record’s physical counter range. A foreign live claim or wrong fixed reference fails before the affected operation spends or commits. A coherent stale BUSY bit without a live operation can instead return stale-busy without changing the record. The supplied residual-service map describes the scoped clock’s real service attempts; arbitrary loss or replacement of the physical timer/phase independently of mutable valid bits needs its own fault transition. A repeated staged value with a new actual service identity cannot inherit a prior deadline from label equality alone.

In the earlier one-lane paced adapter, a base event flips bit 84 in the **one source reset engine** before testing whether to start RX. The older synchronous LocalSourceBank.native_tick also toggles this bit once and then offers the same selection opportunity to every lane. Calling that toggle separately for each independently paced receiver is wrong. With two equal-period receivers always ordered A then B and initial parity zero, four base periods select A zero times and B four times. The old synchronous toggle once per period gives two opportunities to each. Aggregate event count and available work do not distinguish those outcomes. [Earlier paced source](NATIVE-CLOCK-SOURCE.md)

The independent product uses RX[618] at each receiving endpoint. The FIFO’s 512 occupied bits end at 575. Its selected-head valid bit is 608, and its nine-bit address occupies 609–617. Bit 618 is already inside the 8192-bit RX control allocation. At each physical RX base with an open write gate, the small fixed local operation debits 32 units from the actual origin U, toggles 618 and offers the existing selection on the zero half. At a closed write gate, no toggle occurs. If U cannot pay, the old bit survives, and the separate return aperture can still settle an earlier selection if it is subsequently funded. This is an explicit receiving-side primitive with one fixed bit read, Boolean inversion, write-enable and strobe; 32 is its proposed local work price. Successful toggles dissipate that work in their receiver zone. Its rate and peak have to fit the actual physical rail and clock budget. Ordinary native data cannot select another lane’s phase, invent an extra oscillator edge or debit its operating E to cover a raw-U deficit. The admitted direct input and physical override roots retain their actual declared reach.

The old raw bitmap operation clears RX[618] to zero while ordinary writes are inhibited. A full funded reformation in the cross-bank example takes an old one at that location through actual MASK, charge, dump and literal clear before OPEN. It spends 297681 from that row’s reset cell and leaves the old prepared bitmap valid on completion. The already-installed record/capture and the other source bank retain their own state. An OPEN command alone cannot prove that the sweep took place. A revised source permitting bypass of the priority gate or arbitrarily accelerated local oscillator must price or charge those roots to their real footprint.

The one-lane output comparison holds on executions with matching retained parity, matching edge choices, sufficient additional work, and no differently scoped intervention into the two divider representations. On the synchronized nominal subfamily, a common base schedule with equal parities gives the earlier shared selection pattern. The independently paced source lets physical phases differ and has a real additional work debit. A write or reset affecting only the old source-local bit 84 and a remote lane’s private reformation have different causal paths. The new product does not inherit a universal same-trace claim for all old shared-divider tampering or right after arbitrary cold restoration. To compare direct and reusable proofs at full fidelity, both routes must use this same paid receiver-local source (or exhibit the actual priced common control wiring and its support). The source TX divider is still the original bit pair on its one source bank, with the original lead limiter and work. The optional source dispatcher schedules ADC, TX and flight only when the independent receiver product owns RX. Thus a source-memory diagnostic cannot reset all the receiving calendars by a software count.

A positive value requires that return; at equality, its actual before/after order remains part of the physical history. Consider the source whose elementary operations are the paid bank/flight operations, these receiving base and return operations, true record tick/service, bound capture/finish, shared U and the installed gate transitions. Given a fixed admissible finite source history, project it to the source-bank CPU/E/TX face, each receiver face, each shared capture, and the chronological U/heat account. Retain labels, co-footprints, work, all shared before/after states, and the chosen simultaneous micro-order in these projections. The comparison has four cases.

1. **Positive elapsed time.** On each eligible held chart every source E and its charge/flight coordinates advance once to the same real date. RX phases and record waits are observed at that date. Changing the number of receivers changes which dates delimit the interval without multiplying E transfers or marching the source TX clock once per receiver. The exact rational production here admits the funded and stationary zero-inflow/zero-other-draw charts; variable transit/bleed or scarce positive stocks use their separate actual flow laws. A refused rational chart gives no deadlock theorem. Bank-specific stock and flight prerequisites are checked before advancing either side of the installed origin.
2. **Bank/flight.** One allowed source TX divider completion evaluates the installed source’s current queue branches. Each new child debits the correct DATA reservation and retains its fixed destination, slot and own 128 units. A later arrival is rooted in that same child and reaches that same lane, even after the source cut. The child is consumed exactly once. In a subroster the uninstalled receiving sites are explicit external output faces. Their delivery exposes the actual destination, payload and consumed physical slot. The environment still owes the downstream receipt/load. Closed-roster admission requires all eighty equipped endpoints per exposed source bank.
3. **Receiver/record.** A base, its fixed-delay return, a record tick or paid service changes only its actual row/record or its named consequent, plus the common U/physical cost. Selection and return use exactly the same head/credit/parser primitives as the default single-lane source. If a receiver can still affect its raw output through another held circuit while its clock is stopped, that separate flow or act remains in the source’s global fault/accounting inventory.
4. **Capture and shared choice.** Both source-bank rows of one receiver/role use the same pending descriptor. Selecting another bank can settle the first descriptor *before* trying to fund the next. A failed second call therefore has an actual retained effect. Events competing for U, the same raw capture or one physical FIFO cannot be reordered in general. Independent elapsed phases determine each enabled aperture; simultaneous order is an admitted physical choice, with no nominal central selector supplying favorable outcomes.

The per-owner states and the common physical post-state agree after each such elementary step because each shared transition above is executed once on its actual owner. Unaffected observations rebase on that post-state; an issued child is born with the transferred parcel on that same post-state. The next comparison reads the original mutable state left by its predecessor. ## Getting an upper delay from a live receiving lane

Assume an intact open write/read path, a functioning committed parser, discrete service throughout the interval, nominal independent local speed and ample U for every admitted interfering prefix. An accepted symbol with FIFO rank n∈[1,512] waits through at most n selection opportunities followed by the final σ. There are at most two base periods to the next selection and between selections, so the elapsed bound is 2n/4096+σ. Later arrivals cannot overtake this fixed FIFO rank.

Even a retained unrelated valid head can use one opportunity under these same continuing guards, if its captured address no longer matches the real head. If the first subsequent base is nonselecting, its return settles or discards the old selection before the next selecting base. If the first is selecting, it lies within one base period of the present cut; its return removes the old selector. From then there are n full two-base gaps to the n-th removal. Accounting for the preceding physical return improves this bound. If the first selecting base is due at d>p−σ in its current period p, the preceding nonselecting base has a return still due at d−p+σ, strictly before that first selection. The stale selector is consumed or dropped there under the ongoing guards. If there is no such prior return, d≤p−σ. In the latter case the final return is no later than (p−σ)+2n/4096+σ≤(2n+1)/4096. This also covers the nonselecting-first and quiescent cases since σ<1/4096. At n=512 the bound is 1025/4096, strictly below the old 513/2048q allowance by 1/4096q. The local material witness starts with a saturated real FIFO, a retained mismatched head and its already-passed prior return, d=1/4096−σ. The new completion drops that old selection and the next 512 paid pop/parse operations empty the queue at exactly 1025/4096. This is a permitted dirty-head local cut; its upstream acquisition history is not synthesized by the rank calculation. A permanently invalid/busy parser or an ongoing deficit receives no such deadline. The rank argument is separate from the authenticity and timeliness of producers, existing valid records, the four framing symbols and actual vote formation.

At a single accepted symbol’s local aperture/return, 32 for this new divider, 32 each for old selection and return, 64 for the pop, 11584 for decode and 512 for staging give 12256≤16384 work at the native receiver. For the entire host, the previous single-lane clock inventory already conservatively priced the existing per-base aperture and record work on all 160 lanes. Additional divider burst is 2·160·32=10240 and slope is 2·160·32·4096·1.01=211812352/5 per q, using the same two-ended budget convention. The joined rate including the input and physical gate, previous native clock charge and this new divider is 66873664080613542951532507/8000000. The resulting capacity-minus-burst-minus-two-slopes reserve is 7204713435106881510006369/800000. It exceeds the nominal capture requirement at the bounded intact supply point. These headroom calculations use the earlier source capacities and work/heat conventions. An actual depleted U at a particular cut can still reject simultaneous operations, and additional or relocated active hardware requires its own physical inventory. The elapsed calendars and their bounded phase laws are source timing primitives; raw continuous oscillator, parasitic and thermal realization outside the previously allocated RX clock/port envelopes would have to be supplied and billed separately. ## Reproduced cuts and remaining global work

On the actual one-bank roster at origin 2 the checker creates all 80 fixed destination rows, all 60066 records actually decoded there, and 160 capture-owner/role pairs. It supplies independent initial phases and variable rates. The common E transfers 10240 into its CPU over one δ with full idle input cells. All 80 first receiving opportunities then occur without one additional source TX or ADC edge. Different origins can have different counts of installed records. This run exemplifies the structural finite-roster proof; it is not global simultaneous source formation.

With three receiving lanes and U=160, any two prioritized starts can each pay 32 for the divider and 32 for head capture. The third pays its divider and cannot pay head capture. Reversing the same-time order changes which lanes hold a selection; the following underfunded returns preserve both actual claims. A distinct two-bank fixture has four endpoint rows, two E stores, one U, and six actual shared capture/role objects. Beginning the second bank’s comparison settles snapshot 71 on the first. With sufficient U the subsequent capture returns the second bank’s 84. With only 17 units left, the second begin returns unfunded after settling the old claim. No new claim exists, and the retained snapshot is 71.

Two already issued flights at a common date reach separate fixed receiver banks. Selecting the first RX before its arrival and the second RX after its own arrival gives FIFO occupancies [1,0] following the independent return apertures. Both 128-work flights are paid, and both retained tick and staging pairs finish in their own records. Another genuine single source TX edge emits on two real branches once each; an older child on an uninstalled receiver crosses the explicit external face.
