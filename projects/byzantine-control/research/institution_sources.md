# Institutional, accountability and recovery sources

Checked 2026-10-21 before constructing the institutional/recovery extension. Marks refer to the stated inspection, not bibliographic prestige, proof reconstruction or automatic application to the local model. No novelty claim or current open-frontier claim follows from this bounded assessment.

The comparison changed the construction choice. Established sources already explain institutional power, capability attenuation, protocol accountability and Byzantine reconfiguration. The useful remaining work is their application boundary in one control loop: permission can remove the common safe action; logical suspension need not change physical influence; physical isolation can make service-preserving capacity removal feasible; current and historical evidence need different verifiers; and new configurations cannot mix support or silently reset effective faults. These are proved/constructed locally without claiming that the source mechanisms are novel.

Searches combined institutional power/permission/action logic, formal institutional transitions, capability access/revocation, PeerReview/accountability, Byzantine reconfiguration/forward security, and proactive recovery/key refresh. Author and institutional copies were preferred. An initial author PDF endpoint and an initial PeerReview mirror were unavailable; accessible university/MPI copies supplied the inspected passages. Older paper publication timestamps in search-result snippets were not used as currentness evidence.

Unsearched or unimported areas include current legal recognition systems, empirical actuator-isolation engineering, distributed appeal procedures, all mobile-corruption protocol variants, and a complete present-day frontier survey. Reopen the literature assessment before claiming novelty, replacing the trusted serial registrar with an asynchronous distributed institution, or exporting a stronger cryptographic historical-time or mobile-recovery theorem. None of these claims is made by the current conditional construction.
