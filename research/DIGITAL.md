# Ledger specialization, terminal evidence, and protocol-generated knowledge

This module supplies a precise ledger specialization and its evidence semantics for the program contract P2/P3 and the same-protocol comparison in P14. It develops the earlier D1 instance into an operationally generated execution system with a useful certificate, rather than treating a certificate's desired meaning as an initial fact. [Digital.lean](Digital.lean) proves 19 discrete results; [digital_model.py](digital_model.py) generates the protocol's prefixes and checks their independent event-postcondition presentation. These results are local mathematical verification, not repository acceptance, cryptographic implementation verification, or a claim about HotStuff's different protocol.

The substantive extension is phase-separated terminal evidence. The two-round broadcast protocol decides before producing terminal attestations. Two distinct authentic attestations for a value then imply that every correct participant decided that value. This uses the broadcast agreement theorem and the correct terminal-signing rule together. Threshold geometry alone does not prove it: with three identities and one fault, two arbitrary pairs need not intersect in a correct identity. Earlier proposal and echo signatures can support conflicting values in an admitted execution.

## Execution context and local realization

The context has participants 0, 1, 2, with participant 0 the fixed leader; binary inputs; one static unknown faulty identity or no faulty identity; and ideal signatures binding `(epoch, message-kind, value, ordered signer chain)`. Two additional read-only verifiers A and B, numbered 3 and 4 in the executable model, observe delivered terminal artifacts and the common round clock. They cannot influence the protocol and are not additional fault-tolerant replicas. Adding these observers changes the observation interface but neither the decision algorithm nor its adversary budget.

All correct participants run the same actor-indexed local policy before the faulty identity is selected. The adversary can collude through the one compromised participant, omit its messages, send different allowed messages to different recipients, sign both values with its own key, and choose selective delivery of those signatures. It can inspect the current execution and all inputs. It cannot forge a correct signature, change the static faulty identity, cause a correct actor to sign off-protocol, or see future choices. Since the proof covers every current legal branch, causal adaptation to preceding events is included. Correct messages arrive within their specified synchronous round; the adversary's choices do not erase the next cutoff. This is a stipulated communication and clock interface, not a consequence of signatures.

The fixed command alphabets contain proposal, echo, terminal publication, waiting, retention, and observation. Correct code uses only the actor's own input, validated received packets, own actions, decision register, epoch, and clock. `local_view(prefix, actor)` exposes precisely that history; `local_action(actor, view)` reads no global state or fault identity. The global transition generator uses the fault index solely to determine which policy components are followed and which messages the adversary may produce. The corresponding actor action is identical whenever its permitted local history is identical. The executable audit checks the realized local action classes.

Round 1: a correct leader signs its input and transmits it to both followers. A faulty leader can sign any subset of the two values and transmit any subset of those signatures to either follower. A correct follower retains the set of valid leader-signed values received before the first cutoff. Round 2: each correct follower signs and forwards every such value to the other follower. A follower accepts a second-round echo only when it includes the leader's signature and the other follower's signature on the same value and epoch. Raw leader proposals arriving in this round do not enter the accepted set. At the second cutoff, a correct follower chooses its sole accumulated value if that set is a singleton, otherwise default 0. A correct leader decides its input. Correct participants retain that slot decision permanently.

Round 3: each correct participant publishes one terminal attestation for its committed slot value. A faulty participant may publish either value, both, or neither, independently of its supposed decision. The model includes a selective-delivery point inside round 3 and a final cutoff. At the cutoff, both verifiers have received all correct terminal attestations; faulty attestations may still be withheld or selectively delivered, and the faulty participant may create additional own-key attestations after the selective point. The cutoff event retains previously created tokens and records any newly created ones; signing chronology is not silently moved earlier. A verifier accepts a certificate when it possesses two distinct valid terminal signers for one value in the current epoch. Signatures from proposal/echo phases, duplicate identities, and other epochs do not count.

There are four event transitions per slot: raw cutoff, echo/decision cutoff, selective terminal delivery, and terminal cutoff. `round_clock` maps the last two to the interior and end of round 3. Thus the reported decision bound is two logical rounds and the evidence-availability bound is three logical rounds, not two or three seconds. There are no maximal prefixes before the final cutoff. A single-slot maximal execution ends successfully at that cutoff. Repetition appends a fresh epoch; it does not treat an empty successor set as an infinite-run proof.

The representation kernel removes packet multiplicity, invalid byte encodings, late raw packets rejected before the validated-inbox API, and unbounded Byzantine traffic volume. Observations are validated batches at the stated observation points; individual arrival order and elapsed time within a batch are not visible through this interface. These omissions cannot change accepted message sets under the stipulated policy, but they can change timing knowledge, resource use or denial-of-service behavior in a real implementation; those questions reopen the observation and environment model. The adversary's private input when the leader is faulty has no validity consumer and is fixed to a dummy value in the executable generator. Faulty internal memories and their subjective knowledge are not represented. Correct-participant epistemic claims are explicitly conditioned on that participant remaining correct; public-verifier claims range over every admitted fault identity. The model does not collapse different histories of correct batch receipt, signer identity, message phase, or epoch.

## D2: broadcast agreement, validity, and total termination

Let `r1,r2` be the first-round sets at followers 1 and 2, and let `e1,e2` be the second-round echo sets arriving at them. The formal `Legal` predicate contains only message-generation and delivery rules. If the leader is correct, both raw sets are its singleton input, and an echo signed by a faulty follower can contain only that same leader-authenticated input. If follower 1 is correct, `e2=r1`; if follower 2 is correct, `e1=r2`. When the leader is faulty both followers are correct. There is no agreement, validity, finality, or successful-execution clause in `Legal`.

If the leader is correct, each correct follower's accumulated set is its singleton input: extra valid echoes cannot introduce a different leader signature. If the leader is faulty, the two followers accumulate `r1 ∪ r2` and `r2 ∪ r1`, so their deterministic decisions coincide. These cases prove agreement for all correct identities and correct-leader validity. `correct_leader_validity` and `broadcast_agreement` mechanize these arguments over all bit sets, not only the instances exercised by Python. This is a reconstruction of the signed-broadcast proof pattern, not literal execution equivalence with SM(1): the present first-round interface batches every valid leader value rather than retaining only the first raw order under the published initial-receipt guard. `termination_at_two` and `output_irrevocable` check the prescribed output register and its retention. Progress additionally uses the transition generator's mandatory clock cutoffs; every first- and second-round choice has a legal next phase, independently of Byzantine cooperation.

The operational-to-formal refinement reads `(input,raw1,raw2,echo1,echo2)` from the first two events of a prefix. The generator's branch rules imply `Legal` directly. Conversely, for a faulty leader, every legal raw pair is generated by signing their union and making those deliveries; extra hidden signatures are also represented. For a correct leader, every allowed faulty echo subset is enumerated and correct echoes are the prescribed forwards. An independently transcribed finite `Legal` table is checked against the generator's complete 26-case projection, in both directions. Thus the message-set model is complete for the stated binary, one-fault, fixed-round interface, modulo the declared invalid/duplicate traffic and unused corrupted input. The Lean output for a faulty identity is a convenience value, not an assertion about its behavior; the refinement consumes only correct participants' decisions, while Python marks a faulty decision absent.

Repeated epochs use the same unknown fault identity and fresh signature tags. At every completed slot all correct participants append the same value. `ledger_agreement` proves agreement at every completed-slot/slot index for an arbitrary sequence of legal rounds. `ledger_append_preserves_history` proves that a later append preserves earlier entries. Sequential repetition yields one new log entry per three-round epoch, already decided at that epoch's second cutoff and publicly certified by its third cutoff. No overlapping-slot throughput improvement is claimed. The executable checker independently composes every pair of legal completed-slot cases with the same fault identity: 661 pairs. This finite check supplements, rather than proves, the unbounded ledger theorem.

The preserved baseline queries are agreement of correct committed entries, correct-leader validity per slot, immutable historical entries, and bounded decision/evidence service in logical rounds. Raw and echo sets are tentative local protocol state; they may differ and contain both values without violating ledger agreement. This specialization does not claim transaction semantics, arbitrary external validity, partially synchronous progress, membership change, or state-machine application correctness.

## D3: certificate soundness and operational availability

A terminal token from identity `j` denotes its historical signing of `(epoch, terminal, v)`. For a correct `j`, the sending policy generates it only after `decision_j=v`. A Byzantine signer can generate tokens with arbitrary contents; the token proves attribution, not that the signer followed its code. This is the sole per-signature provenance obligation consumed by `ReceiptWorld.provenance`. The executable terminal-send transition constructs it: correct tokens are taken from `local_action` on the decision history; faulty token sets range over all four subsets. This operational witness is the connection between the symbolic signature abstraction and the theorem's premise.

Two distinct terminal signers include at least one correct identity. That identity's historical decision equals the certificate value, and D2 agreement transfers the value to every correct participant. `certificate_has_correct`, `certificate_sound`, and `certificate_exclusion` prove these steps. In particular, two accepted certificates in one execution cannot disagree even though their two-share supports may intersect only in a faulty identity: D2 constrains the distinct correct signers' decisions. Removing D2 would invalidate this conclusion.

There are at least two correct identities. Both decide, publish their value, and their correct messages reach both verifiers by the third cutoff. `certificate_available` constructs the two honest signers and common value; the round-3 transition supplies actual tokens and delivered bundles without faulty cooperation. The verifier therefore neither accepts everything nor rejects everything. Safety and availability use the same fixed protocol and fault identity. Every generated terminal bundle is checked for provenance and every generated cutoff bundle contains a certificate for the actual correct value.

An earlier signature does not have this meaning. In the admitted faulty-leader trace, follower 1 receives only 0 and follower 2 only 1 in round 1. In round 2 they authentically echo those different values and both accumulate `{0,1}`, deciding default 0. Thus a leader-signed 1 or a valid echo of 1 does not establish finality of 1. Nor does one terminal signature suffice: the faulty leader can attest terminal 1 while the correct followers decide 0. The checker supplies this trace and an indistinguishable public-verifier alternative where terminal 1 is a correct leader's genuine decision. Phase separation and two distinct signers both matter.

## D4: value-specific knowledge and branching finality

At a fixed observation point define `K_A φ` by truth of `φ` at every generated prefix with verifier A's same full local record. This ranges over all input, fault, signing, echo, and delivery alternatives admitted by the rules; runs are not preselected for agreement or successful certificate checks. For a participant `i`, use `K_i^correct` on the sub-system with `i` correct. That restriction is explicit: code determinism does not describe arbitrary compromised internal histories. No such restriction is needed for the read-only public verifiers.

For arbitrarily nested queries, use the total correctness-conditioned assurance operator
\[
E_i\varphi(w)=\operatorname{Correct}_i(w)\land
\forall w'\,[\operatorname{Correct}_i(w')\land\operatorname{view}_i(w')=\operatorname{view}_i(w)\Rightarrow\varphi(w')].
\]
At a faulty-identity world this qualified assurance is false; that does not assert that the faulty actor lacks subjective knowledge. The query language uses E_i for participants and ordinary K_A,K_B for the read-only verifiers, with correctness atoms retained in both representations. The earlier K_i^correct notation denotes the restricted-system component when its correctness guard holds. In particular, the higher-order existential claim is K_A of the existence of distinct i,j satisfying E_i Final(v) and E_j Final(v), so no undefined partial operator is evaluated at a faulty world. The function correct_assurance checks this total interpretation in the generated model.

Define `Final_e(v)` at or after the second cutoff as: in every admissible continuation of the prefix, every correct participant's completed entry for slot `e` is `v`, and every later epoch preserves that entry. This quantifies over future executions, not over the suffix of one chosen run. D2 fixes the slot decisions and append retention makes the same value hold in every later legal continuation. The policy still admits different future inputs and Byzantine choices; finality constrains the historical slot, not the future log.

Possession of a valid terminal certificate is constant on a verifier's local-history class. At every prefix in that class, D3 implies the same correct decision, and the history-retention theorem implies `Final_e(v)`. Therefore `HasCertificate_A(e,v) ⇒ K_A Final_e(v)`. `receipt_warrants_decision` mechanizes the universal current-slot warrant over all provenance-correct `ReceiptWorld`s, and `certificate_historical_finality` supplies the arbitrary-later-ledger part. The executable model checks the observation-class implication for every selective delivery prefix. This is a sufficient implemented knowledge condition, not a claim that certificates are necessary for knowledge in every protocol.

Certificate existence is weaker than possession. At the selective-delivery point the two correct participants have already generated a certificate, but A and B may have received nothing. Their empty-bundle observation classes contain both final values. They can know that some final certificate exists without knowing which value it certifies. If A receives a two-share certificate for 0 while B receives nothing, A knows `Final_e(0)`, but B's possibilities still include 0 and 1. That actual world refutes `K_A K_B Final_e(0)`, since `K_B Final_e(0)` is false there. Treating A's received certificate as a public announcement would incorrectly disclose it to B.

Every correct participant knows its own decision and, under D2 in its correctness-conditioned system, knows the final value. A certificate holder consequently knows that at least two correct participants know `Final_e(v)`. The holder need not identify them: the checker finds one received certificate whose indistinguishable alternatives include faulty identity 0, 1, and 2. Every fixed pair contains a potentially faulty identity. This distinguishes knowledge of an existential sufficiently large correct set from a known set of correct actors. At selective delivery, even first-order knowledge is absent for B, so common knowledge among A and B is also absent. At the final common-clock cutoff this particular model does provide common knowledge of the final value among A and B: every endpoint possesses a sound certificate, so each A- or B-accessibility edge preserves the final value, and induction over the length of any finite accessibility chain preserves it. The checker verifies the requisite value-homogeneous classes. This stronger conclusion depends on the common cutoff and guaranteed correct-message delivery; it is not inferred from arbitrary selective receipt, and no universal phase-count lower bound on knowledge is claimed.

Historical facts are not current-pointer facts. A slot-0 certificate for 0 survives a later slot-1 decision of 1: the log is `[0,1]`, the old entry remains 0, and the latest entry is 1. This is the relevant historical/present distinction for this baseline. There are no HotStuff locks here, so no old certificate is said to reveal anyone's current lock.

## D5: what complete negative evidence establishes

At the end of round 2, suppose a correct follower's accumulated set lacks value 1. Then no correct follower received a leader-signed 1 before the first cutoff. If the leader was correct, valid signatures and first-round delivery give the same input everywhere; absence therefore excludes input 1. If the leader was faulty, both followers were correct and complete second-round forwarding makes their accumulated sets the union of both first-round sets. Absence from that union excludes the historical correct receipt. `absent_after_echo_excludes_honest_receipt` proves the model-level implication, and equality of the follower's local record lifts it to `K_i^correct` of that historical absence. The checker evaluates the corresponding complete observation classes.

The reporting window, fixed epoch, complete retention of first-round sets, and timely correct forwarding are necessary to that inference. Removing the same complete-forwarding premise can also destroy terminal certificate exclusion: a faulty leader can leave the two correct followers deciding 0 and 1, then sign both terminal values and complete a two-share certificate for each. The executable countermodel verifies this dependency; it is outside Legal rather than an attack hidden inside D3. Mere silence before the second cutoff does not suffice: follower 1 may have an empty first-round set while follower 2 received 1. A dropped honest echo would leave that ambiguity at the second cutoff and can split decisions. Absence also does not prove that the faulty leader never signed 1: it may have signed and withheld the value, and the generator includes that execution. The inferred absence concerns a specified historical receipt by a correct actor, not all private signing, all actors' memories, or all future proposals. This is a checked fixed-round report diagnostic; a HotStuff view-change inference requires its own reporting and lock-retention premises.

## D6: direct histories and enriched event products

The source is the pointed system of all operationally generated prefixes at a fixed event stage. Its atomic queries include current clock/epoch, actual historical signing events, correct decision registers, accepted raw/echo sets, and verifier possession of phase-tagged terminal tokens. Its epistemic relations are equality of the permitted local records, with the stated correctness restriction for participant knowledge. The target is an epistemic event product with factual postconditions and the same initial alternatives.

For every enabled source transition `p → p·e`, the enriched event label is `(kind, payload, o_0,…,o_4)`, where `o_i` is the increment to actor `i`'s local record. Its precondition is that the protocol rules enable this event with exactly those increments; its postconditions set the changed message, signature, decision, and possession atoms. Actor `i` distinguishes events precisely by `o_i`. Including the increments is important: a decision emitted after an echo depends on the old inbox as well as the echo payload. A bare echo-payload event with no world-dependent refinement would not generally provide the standard product relation. `event_label` exposes the expanded labels. All enabled alternative events remain in the product; there is no restriction to the analyst's actual event shared by everyone.

Flattening the nested product history into the same initial context and event sequence is a bijection. The transition guards are identical operational guards, and the independently accumulated postconditions in `product_projection` reproduce the direct state. Full records satisfy

\[
\operatorname{view}_i(p e)=\operatorname{view}_i(p)\mathbin{+}\!+o_i(e),\qquad
p e\sim_i q f\iff p\sim_i q\ \land\ o_i(e)=o_i(f).
\]

The reverse implication uses the retained phase markers to recover the previous record and final increment uniquely. The executable checker compares both directions of these observation partitions at every stage, using canonical class keys rather than a quadratic loop over all pairs. It separately compares decisions, signed tokens, receipts, and full local records at all 117,017 points.

The query family is Boolean combinations and arbitrarily nested public-verifier knowledge and guarded participant assurance over the tracked atoms. Atom preservation is the postcondition equality; negation and conjunction follow truth-functionally; a knowledge step follows the bijection of accessibility classes and the induction hypothesis. This gives structural induction over every formula in that family. The history/update identity also iterates over every finite sequence admitted by the same event construction. The executable coverage is all four transitions of one binary epoch, not an arbitrary protocol or unbounded model checker. Repeated fresh epochs retain the old record and append new events, so the same algebraic record factorization continues to apply; the ledger proof separately carries the historical state.

Because the complete guarded transition tree and clock are retained, the presentation also preserves fixed-protocol temporal trace statements, including this protocol's cutoffs and immutable-slot finality. If one keeps only the epistemic snapshots and forgets enabled transitions, no such temporal claim follows. The construction does not quantify over replacement controller strategies: `successors` is the fixed-policy closed loop. Thus an epistemic isomorphism alone is not being offered as a distributed strategy-refinement theorem. The program's separate local-strategy transport interface must retain controller choices before a strategic claim is exported.

Two failing translations have direct witnesses in this same protocol. Pure factual-valuation-preserving DEL cannot change a false possession atom to true when a terminal certificate arrives; factual postconditions or an equivalent history enrichment are required. A truthful announcement of a received terminal payload fails in the one-Byzantine-signature trace above: the payload says terminal 1 while the actual correct value is 0, so such an announcement deletes an allowed actual world. Signature receipt is an attributed event, not an announcement of its truth. The selected enriched event mechanism is established prior art; this application supplies the operational source and query comparison, not a novelty claim.

## Reproduction, adapter, and boundaries

Run `python -B digital_model.py` from this directory. The public `run_checks() -> dict` returns the same audit without writing files. Expected stage counts are `7, 31, 35, 13328, 103616`, with 117,017 event-product points, 586 correct local action classes, and 661 compatible two-slot pairs. These are all generated finite choices under the stated quotient, not Monte Carlo samples. The functions `initials`, `successors`, `traces`, `local_view`, `local_action`, `round_clock`, and `event_label` expose the raw objects needed by the common execution adapter. A `Prefix` retains its original faulty identity, input, epoch, and full event sequence. No observer is given the fault index. The adapter must preserve the successful single-slot terminal verdict or connect phase 4 to the next fresh epoch; adding an uncontrolled dead end would change the service claim.

Compile `Digital.lean` with the packet's installed Lean 4.33.0 binary. Its 19 exported reports use only `propext` and `Quot.sound` where needed; no result contains `sorryAx`, a task-specific axiom, native computation, or an external solver bridge. The message-set proof and event model share the declared operational specification; their agreement is useful cross-checking, not independent empirical evidence for cryptography or networking. The term `ReceiptWorld` is a sound overapproximation of generated receipt bundles, and its provenance hypothesis has the operational construction stated above. Source checking is recorded in [digital_sources.md](digital_sources.md).

This module supplies a recovered protocol, value-specific certificate warrant with universal-continuation finality, higher-order and negative-evidence diagnostics, and a protocol-generated event correspondence. It leaves no Basic HotStuff theorem implicitly imported. Full Basic HotStuff would require actual `safeNode`, per-phase/per-view voting, highest-QC selection, historical lock updates and evolution, commit/decide rules, and post-GST common-view progress premises. Chained HotStuff would require its separate ancestry and commit rules. Those are conditional protocol alternatives, not claims discharged by this specialization. The current baseline's exact guarantees are sufficient for its ledger role, with the actual A-D common-core adapter in [ASSEMBLY](ASSEMBLY.md) and independent program-wide verification recorded in [PROGRAM-AUDIT](PROGRAM-AUDIT.md).
