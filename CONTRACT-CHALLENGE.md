# Adversarial review of the downstream theory contract

Current verdict: needs scope revision. The user's subsequent clarification exposed a missed challenge: the required subject is adversarial fault tolerance supporting decentralized civilizational computational infrastructure, not theory for designing that infrastructure generally. The prior reviews below challenged false completion within the proposed scope but did not adequately reject its expansion of the research subject. Their bounded findings remain useful; their readiness judgment does not establish fidelity to the clarified objective. The governing correction is in [DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT](DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT.md).

This review evaluates [DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT](DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT.md) as a proposed stopping rule for theory supporting decentralized infrastructure. It does not verify the future theory, reopen every old proof, or accept a new research program. The governing comparison is whether fulfilling the written contract would resolve its promised downstream design questions without moving their central burdens into unexplained premises.

## Review boundary and initial verdict

The review used the user's downstream-development clarification, the current motivating formulation, and the proposed contract. An independent reviewer first derived downstream consumers without seeing the draft. Two further read-only reviewers challenged the draft's admissibility and refinement semantics. The coordinator independently examined the returned countercandidates and revised the proposal. Separate model instances and assignments provide useful independence of review, not proof against shared framing errors. No new mathematical result or empirical architecture was validated in this exercise.

The first draft was rejected as a stopping contract. Although it prohibited obvious shortcuts, it left the constructive support envelope and required answer modes to later selection. That would allow a subsequent agent to choose a thin combined family and weak comparison results after learning what was easiest to prove. It also needed a role-based boundary for primitives, meaningful restoration in lifecycle results, and a stronger test of reusable composition.

The revised proposal fixes a finite effective support floor and query modes, source primitive responsibilities, a generative architecture interface, the required combined positive features, and independent composition/refinement obligations. Finite completeness is one required result, not a replacement for those other obligations. The challenge cases below state the strongest disposition justified by their witnesses.

## Challenges to the stronger contract

Stronger requirements can themselves make a program defective. The review therefore also tested the following opposite failures.

**Infinite assurance regress.** Requiring every measurement law, environmental bound, or normative input to be justified by a further fallible mechanism prevents any theoretical stopping point. The repaired boundary terminates at declared primitive schemas and applicability premises, while keeping promised internal capabilities on the theory's side. Empirical justification of a deployed sensor is different from constructing a distributed inference service from nominal sensor relations.

**A universal synthesis demand.** Requiring a terminating complete method for every arbitrarily described distributed, continuous, institutional, or randomized system would exceed the proposed support floor. The exact base query has finite data, deterministic compliant-program memory bounds, explicit causal fault transitions, and fixed safety/bounded-service monitors. Broader semantics and source refinement remain, with their own result scopes. Finite automata describing admissible faults do not restrict Byzantine strategy memory. Randomness and richer multi-execution objectives need separate declarations.

**Enumeration as the new proxy.** Exact finite synthesis is a legitimate theory baseline, but a generic brute-force constructor can leave useful modeling, modularity, and realization untouched. The contract requires component-level input elaboration, sufficient interface summaries with replacement, a combined positive family, actual lifecycle restoration, and source transport separately. A proof of finite completeness cannot close these rows.

**Complexity wishful thinking.** Practical infrastructure does not justify silently promising a polynomial method or production readiness. The contract requires operational constructions and size/cost laws, including boundary information and where modularity fails. It permits difficult computation while exposing the cost. A later deployment target may impose a stricter budget; this theoretical contract does not invent one.

**Unbounded civilizational scope.** Every newly imagined social application could create a new mandatory field and prevent completion forever. The revised contract closes relative to the fixed task, primitive, constructor, query, and responsibility basis. An in-scope omitted effect reopens adequacy. A new task or stronger requirement requires an explicit extension. Legitimacy, arbitrary incentives, universal model learning, and unrestricted mobile faults cannot be smuggled in at closing review, nor can already-required authority implementation or recurring change be removed as if they were those stronger extensions.

**Over-prescribing a research method.** Selecting an exact finite support floor is a proposed usability requirement, not a claim that the phrase logically mandates a particular solver. The theory can use imports, constructive proofs, search, games, contracts, relational methods, or an atlas of accounts. Successful source families may have informative structural restrictions. Their restrictions cannot narrow the exact floor, deactivate required interactions, or be defined merely by possession of a solution.

## Remaining judgment boundaries

No finite document proves that it captures every intended meaning of decentralized civilizational infrastructure. The interpretation of infrastructure as programmable, composable, federated, and maintainable is supported by the downstream use but remains a proposed scope commitment. The exact finite floor is a concrete proposed answer to the previous weak-obligation problem; it is not inferred as the only possible adequate contract.

Source adequacy remains an evidence-bearing judgment. A reviewer must inspect whether a primitive supplies a promised internal capability, whether an abstraction retains the needed source effects, and whether interface summaries remove reusable work rather than hide it. The contract makes those judgments concrete through named responsibilities and challenge witnesses; it cannot mechanically guarantee that future reviewers exercise them correctly.

The independent transfer exercise challenges reuse but is not statistical or exhaustive coverage of future applications. General coverage must come from proofs over the declared generators and result classes. A positive family can satisfy many parameter ranges yet fail source adequacy; conversely, a narrowly represented but faithful fragment can supply a valuable component without closing the whole contract.

The contract does not establish that all its constructive obligations can be satisfied together. Establishing them is the proposed research. A genuine incompatibility must be reported and repaired visibly; it cannot be converted into success by weakening a requirement during the final audit. The current task is complete when the proposal and its challenge record are delivered, not when this future research is complete.

## Prior disposition and subsequent scope failure

The first candidate was not ready: the decisive support envelope and answer strengths were still deferred. After the substantive revisions, both final read-only reviews judged the proposal auditable at its declared scope and found no surviving concrete false-pass among their selected attacks. Their final qualifications required explicit nonempty/satisfiable positive regions, joint-scenario quantification, and permission for a logically reliable service that is constructed rather than assumed. Those three clarifications were integrated and checked by the coordinator against the exact review recommendations. The reviewers' verdicts apply to DOWNSTREAM-CONTRACT; this consolidated challenge record was assembled by the coordinator and is not itself an independently certified artifact.

The resulting prior verdict was an auditable proposed completion contract within its declared support and responsibility boundary. That verdict is now insufficient for Goal readiness because the boundary overexpanded the intended subject. The concrete witness is that a valid theory answering the intended adversarial-fault-tolerance questions could fail the contract for lacking a general service-programming account or total bounded application synthesizer, even though those were not established requirements of the intended support relation. The inverse problem also remains: supplying those extra facilities could consume effort without resolving the central tolerance questions.

Repair requires deriving obligations for adversarial fault tolerance and judging broader infrastructure functions only through their effect on those obligations. Preserve source fidelity, joint faults, actual effects, nonvacuity, assumption responsibility, and substantive construction. Reclassify general programming, comprehensive federation/lifecycle design, and the mandatory finite exact synthesis floor rather than treating every downstream need as an obligation. No new Goal or replacement research program has been created, and no previous mathematical artifact's proof status has been changed.
