# Two-bank execution continuity and local maintenance response

Status: conditional derived construction and checked interface lemmas. This contribution establishes an interval-cover/response law, geometry-to-overlap arithmetic, and conditions under which asynchronous held outputs advance a physical stage. It does not establish the actual image/reset/startup supplier, permission inference, whole-source nonemptiness, sustainable renewal, or program completion. The [TIMING](TIMING.md) comparison remains unchanged; its chart admission is not weakened here. Independent review of this component and its eventual source realization remains required.

The intended alternative is a host with two separately powered execution banks. The standby is actually prepared, inspected, reset, powered and initialized while the old bank continues executing. Both can supply local maintenance outputs during overlap. Physical world actuation, recognized reporting authority and removal of old signal/write reach have separate transitions. The representation retains bank identity, actual ready-execution intervals, local work bounds, powered overlap, stage coordinates and tails. It drops whole-program state transfer and global phase labels only for routines shown to restart from finite initialized state and complete locally; a job requiring an uninterrupted longer transaction reopens that choice.

## 2. Ready means a supplied execution property

Fix a finite local routine class R: bounded copies, scans, observations and responses whose semantics do not require transferring an unfinished whole renewal transaction. A bank is ready for R over an interval only when its actual installed instructions/configuration, initialized control/data state, power and nominal invocation behavior make the specified routines executable throughout that interval. Any needed startup prefix must have completed, including fresh acquisition/drain requirements used by the routine. This is an analyst predicate with source witnesses, not a software READY flag accessible to policy.

Code/configuration equality alone is insufficient. The program counter can be wrong, a stale completion flag can bypass required work, old buffers can produce stale observations, or a necessary startup history can be absent. The source must establish an actual reset and a bounded startup-prefix/immutability theorem. If only code/configuration is inspected, that theorem must explain why the uninspected state is overwritten or rendered irrelevant before R consumes it. The primitive reset may set stated registers; it cannot mean that pristine code or correct world evidence appears. Both banks remain eligible for consequential compromise, including during preparation and startup.

Readiness for execution does not certify every input's truth. A prescribed program can faithfully process a forged primary frame. Sound observation alternatives, current permission and correct image readback still require their own guarantees. Every intact executor retains its prescribed-response duty even when one of those input guarantees fails.

Let I_k=[a_k,b_k] be consecutive actual intervals during which some bank is ready for R; the physical bank identity may alternate between A and B indefinitely. A disrupting fault ends an interval if the routine guarantee no longer holds. Nominal power-off is another possible end. Ending intrusion does not begin a ready interval without actual reconstruction and initialization. The parent must derive these intervals from the common source history; the interval theorem is not permission to assume successful continuity.

## 3. The local work scale and interval-cover theorem

Let Twork bound a nontransferable local routine or one bounded polling round containing it. It must not include the entire renewal sequence, the later power handover, or the overlap interval whose sufficiency is being proved. For a bank with d finite neighbor entries, each visit has a source-derived maximum duration V: it reads bounded input, performs at most the fixed copy/scan/response work, updates its held output and advances a pointer modulo d. It does not wait indefinitely for a peer reply, a whole physical job, or a READY artifact. Missing input yields a bounded safe response and pointer advancement. Different banks may use different initial pointers and arbitrary local clock offsets.

For a job becoming persistently eligible at t, a partly completed visit can have sampled before t. At most V remains in it, at most d-1 other visits precede the next target visit, and that visit takes at most V. Thus a fresh target response is emitted within (d+1)V. The bound is conservative when t occurs at a visit boundary. A local relative wait w contributes at most w/a+J when the clock rate is at least a>0 and invocation lag is bounded by J. Finite instruction/chunk counts, processing and source transport bounds determine V; it is not a successful-renewal deadline.

For the maintenance-force consumer, Twork must cover the applied response, not merely emission. Even if a packet survives sender power-off, the outgoing bank's held force may disappear before the incoming bank's command is applied. With nominal application delay delta, use at least (d+1)V+delta, and retain any additional physical switching tail. If old commands can arrive out of order, a fresh positive command may be overwritten by an old zero. Under the explicit discipline that a persistent stage causes repeated compatible commands with gap at most J, all earlier commands arrive within delta, a fresh repeat follows within J, and its effect arrives within another delta. Thus Ulocal+J+2delta plus the switching tail is a sufficient stable-application bound when Ulocal bounds initial emission. These are separate transport/output obligations, not an inference from ready CPU execution. The alternative is a proved physical held-output bridge that remains effective across retirement; it is not assumed here.

**Interval-cover theorem.** Suppose a_0=0, the interval endpoints are unbounded above, and a_(k+1)+Twork<=b_k for every k. Then for every t>=0, some I_k contains the entire [t,t+Twork]. The endpoints need not be obtained from synchronized clocks. To prove the claim, choose the first k with b_k>=t+Twork. If k=0, initial coverage suffices. Otherwise b_(k-1)<t+Twork, and the overlap condition gives a_k<=b_(k-1)-Twork<t. The chosen interval therefore covers the complete job. `ready_interval_cover` checks the discrete/scaled version in Lean, via the stronger finite-prefix induction `interval_cover_through`; the written real-time argument uses the same order reasoning.

Consequently the local response bound transfers across bank replacements without copying a common scheduler phase: choose the bank whose actual ready interval contains the required response window. The bank does not need to know that it is the witness. Both run the same finite local polling discipline while ready. The exact duplicate-response and shared-output effects must be compatible, bounded and accounted for; this theorem does not make conflicting duplicate writes safe.

The overlap condition is sufficient, not necessary when work safely persists in external physical state. Without either sufficient overlap or such transferable progress, continuous power does not suffice: intervals [0,4],[3,7],[6,10],... cover all time, yet no bank can finish a nontransferable five-unit job. Restarting that job at every replacement can prevent completion forever. This is the bounded-work interruption counterexample, not a claim that every handover must preserve a five-unit interval.

## 4. Derive the ready overlap from contact geometry

For a particular replacement, let p be actual new-bank power-contact closure, r the time its actual ready prefix completes, and e the old bank's actual loss of ready execution. Suppose every admitted nominal removal path must traverse a remaining physical contact distance D before old power opens, its speed is at most vmax, old execution remains ready until that opening, and the actual prepared image/reset/prefix guarantees r<=p+Bready. Here Bready=Bboot+Bstartup includes only the bounded initialization/startup needed for R. It contains no subsequent handover or full-renewal obligation.

Distance integration gives e-p>=D/vmax. Hence

\[
e-r\ge D/v_{max}-B_{ready}.
\]

Therefore D/vmax>=Bready+Twork establishes the overlap consumed above. `geometry_ready_overlap` checks the final interval algebra, taking the source's transit lower bound explicitly. It is not an axiom that the bank is ready at power-on. The invocation/rate envelope may vary persistently; no clock offset or simultaneous invocation is needed. The example trace uses varying stroke speeds1,3/2,2, startup durations1,3/2,2 and D=12, giving ready overlap at least4 for vmax=2 and Bready=2. These are illustrative interface units, not hardware calibration or a complete source instance.

The geometry implication fails if intrusion can open another power path instantaneously, if a common supply outage removes both banks, or if a bounded force model is silently substituted for an admitted teleportation. Those events retain their actual footprint. A false image or failed reset also invalidates the ready-prefix bound even when the powered overlap is physically real. A stopped carriage preserves the overlap lower bound but can destroy eventual replacement progress; an upper-time progress law is separate.

Repeating a two-bank exchange additionally needs actual offline preparation opportunity. If consecutive new-power times are separated by at least C and the intervening contact traversal takes at most D/vmin, a reused bank has at least C-D/vmin time between its power-off and its next power-on. The next preparation can fit there only after its actual work/cost bound is derived. In the finite example C=30 and traversal is at most12, leaving at least18 units; no clean spare is supplied by that subtraction. Local invocation/rate limits and mechanical travel also exclude infinitely many exchanges in finite time. Neither the overlap theorem nor this spacing arithmetic proves the replenishment/repair mechanism.

## 5. Phase-persistent outputs and stiction

The local loop must continue admissible maintenance during standby startup and while world/report authority remains on the other bank. In particular it must not require a neighbor READY token before providing the very maintenance that would enable that neighbor to become ready. Power overlap alone does not prohibit such an all-silent execution. The no-global-scheduler candidate instead gives each bounded-degree neighbor an actual held command port, or another physically demonstrated persistence mechanism. A visit updates the port; the bank can attend another neighbor without withdrawing the first contribution. At retirement, the incoming bank's compatible field must already be physically effective before the outgoing field disappears, or a separately proved persistent port must bridge the interval. Ready overlap through the full stable-application bound supplies the former condition; an emission-only overlap does not. Port realization and shared force/energy limits are source facts, not consequences of finite polling.

Consider one source-defined stage with coordinate x in [0,L]. At zero and L the physical law clips outward motion. For an interior position, an illustrative dry-friction law with coefficient c>0 and threshold s>=0 is zero velocity when |F|<=s, velocity (F-s)/c when F>s, and velocity (F+s)/c when F<-s, subject to a stated speed cap. Require all still-admitted stale nominal actions to be progress-compatible in this stage. If total opposing force and disturbance are at most s, no regression occurs while correct ports are being refreshed. Once g usable contributors hold at least u each and opposition/resistance is at most B, require gu-B-s>0. Then a lower speed is vmin=min(vmax,(gu-B-s)/c)>0.

If fresh valid observations and local loops update those ports within Rpoll and effects arrive within delta, settling takes at most Rpoll+delta. The stage then completes by Rpoll+delta+L/vmin. The proof first excludes backward motion, then integrates the positive lower velocity until the endpoint. The selected Lean `stiction_prevents_reverse`, `positive_force_progress` and `persistent_increment` lemmas check the corresponding elementary arithmetic/discrete sum. They do not derive gu, B, observation soundness or the friction law. The executable tests64 adversarial update-delay combinations for a single stage. No full multi-stage physical mechanism is claimed by that test.

The source must establish alignment of the correct actions from physical-stage observations: after a persistent stage is entered, all relevant nominal responses either choose its common progress action or a proved compatible adjacent-stage action within the polling bound. Synchronizing clock phases is unnecessary for that property, but calling a command stage-compatible is not its proof. A fully forged primary frame can defeat it unless the actual observation construction or a robust common response preserves the needed fact. Source dependence and affected-interface accounting must not be replaced by counting apparent ports.

At a boundary, if position error is at most epsilon, the maximum stale-command duration is Lambda, and speed is at most vmax, a guard distance greater than 2epsilon+vmax Lambda covers the detection/transport lag. The old action must be safe and non-regressive throughout that guard region and saturate harmlessly at its endpoint; the new action must be compatible with the old one there. The source must justify this geometry and its collateral-load effects. A guard distance alone does not make opposing commands compatible. If the old command can continue into a hazardous next stage, or a wrong fresh command can reverse progress beyond the stiction budget, this realization fails and some additional coordination is still required.

This physical progress may persist across many bank intervals. Twork remains the largest nontransferable observation/copy/update routine, not the sum of all stroke durations plus its own overlap. The long stage bound is obtained by continued periodic updates and monotonicity. If some stage instead requires one bank to retain nontransferable hidden state until the whole stroke ends, that longer routine must be admitted explicitly, and its duration can legitimately increase Twork. The distinction prevents a circular definition of the overlap requirement.

## 6. Force, authority, permission and failure cases

Two independently bounded bank outputs of magnitude at most U give a host envelope of2U during overlap. They do not inherit a single-bank envelope U. An actual shared limiter can supply a different aggregate bound, but must remain a primitive force/resource constraint, not a component that knows the correct command. One possible nominal allocation is at most U/(2d) per bank per neighbor with d ports, yielding total U while both banks operate; the progress margin must then use that smaller per-port contribution. A corrupted sibling remains an adversarial effect even when the other bank executes correctly. Bank duplication does not supply independent evidence origins or independent host votes.

Planned startup also needs an output invariant. A not-yet-initialized powered bank can retain a stale opposing field while its old sibling remains correctly executing. Consequently one ready bank per host does not by itself establish the intended nominal host force. Before taking that contribution, prove actual zero/compatible output during the startup prefix, or keep the new driver physically disengaged until that prefix has established its output state. Direct corruption can still violate it and stays in the fault budget. The counterexample has34 old banks supplying2 each,34 uninitialized new banks opposing by2.2 each, six impaired hosts opposing by4.4 each and disturbance−1: net force is−34.2, enough to reverse a threshold28 stroke. No old ready executor vanished. The failed implication is from CPU continuity alone to sustained useful field.

World actuation/report authority switches separately. Old power removal does not consume a queued command or disable an independently powered latch, radio or old credential path. The source must physically cut or bound that reach and retain the whole old-effect tail. The test sends a command at0, removes sender power at1 and delivers its effect at2; treating power-off as immediate effect revocation is false. Conversely maintaining both banks' maintenance ports need not authorize both as world controllers. Duplicate world outputs, thermal cost and source bandwidth require their own compatibility conditions.

The low-load rule governs actual detachment. A fresh observation upper bound plus a growth/tail margin can justify it; a bank's warm state cannot. For example, if intact observations have error epsilon, load growth is bounded by Vload, and the sample-to-operation tail is A, true load at most Lpermit-2epsilon-Vload A is a sufficient robust eligibility margin for the test observed_load+epsilon+Vload A<=Lpermit. This is a conditional inference law. It does not claim that the needed observations are already obtained. An intact executor with forged inputs still owes the rule, and all physically possible malicious detachments remain in the source relation.

The retained counterexamples are materially different: continuous power with no long-enough bank interval; all donors waiting for READY and issuing zero; fair but opposite-phase donor bursts that never jointly cross stiction; a command arriving after sender power-off; equal code and power without initialized control state/fresh buffers; doubled overlap force crossing a protected limit; opposition large enough to reverse completed progress; retirement before an incoming response is applied; and stale startup fields cancelling ready old-bank support. In the retirement case the new bank is ready at3, emits at4, the old bank loses power/held force at4, and the new effect arrives at6. The one-unit ready overlap covers response emission but leaves a two-unit force gap. The opposite-burst case has two nominal donors alternately supplying1 against threshold3/2, so progress is always0; persistent held outputs can supply2 and make progress. These identify actual persistence/application consumers instead of confusing fair attention with a force guarantee.

For a recurrent host-level fault bound, planned bank transitions need not automatically count as host execution outages when the geometry, ready prefix and interval-cover conditions are derived. Faults that invalidate those conditions do count through the same physical/source history. The retained enactment-style implication may be used: a missing required host contribution witnesses an affected old bank, replacement, preparation, power or relevant input path. Its disjointness/footprint proof must identify actual shared dependencies; one compromised installer may affect many hosts. This contribution does not derive that footprint bound, renew those suppliers or turn one successful interval trace into an infinite source theorem.
