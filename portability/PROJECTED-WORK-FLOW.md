## Local scalar estimate

Fix an actual closed interval I with constant endpoints. For an integrable scalar desired velocity f and initial z₀ in I, the scalar stop is the absolutely continuous solution of z′+n=f, n∈N_I(z) almost everywhere. Write v=z′ for its actual velocity. Fixed half-lines, an unrestricted coordinate and a fixed singleton have the same construction. In a singleton the reaction absorbs f and v=0. No finite subdivision at the times when the constraint binds is required.

Take two solutions on [0,t] with differences denoted by Δ. Where Δz≠0, monotonicity of the **scalar** normal cone gives sgn(Δz)Δn=|Δn|. Thus (|Δz|)′+|Δn|≤|Δf| there. On the coincidence set Δz=0, the derivative Δz′ is zero almost everywhere. Indeed at any accumulation point of the zero set where Δz is differentiable there is a sequence of other zeros giving a zero difference quotient; the isolated zeros form a countable set. At almost every such coincidence the equations give Δn=Δf and (|Δz|)′=0. Integration yields

```text
|Δz(t)| + ∫₀ᵗ |Δn| ≤ |Δz₀| + ∫₀ᵗ |Δf|,
            ∫₀ᵗ |Δv| ≤ |Δz₀| + 2∫₀ᵗ |Δf|.
```

The second estimate follows from Δv=Δf−Δn and is valid for arbitrary changes of sign and for independently timed arrivals at the same stop. Its factor two is sharp. Start both paths at 1−ε in [0,1]. During a first ε interval use f=1 and g=0. During a second ε interval use f=g=1. The first trajectory moves only during the first interval and the other only during the second. Input distance is ε and actual velocity distance is 2ε. Coordinate projection or uniform convergence of position alone would not give this strong integral estimate. The proof uses scalar order and a fixed physical stop on each independent coordinate; it does not extend this estimate to arbitrary coupled normal cones. A moving stop needs an actual input/relative-coordinate law and its work.

## Held-mode closure

Fix one finite installed roster, one held topology/attachment mode, initial compatible stocks and positions, and one admitted causal choice of measurable external signals on the interval. Retain y=(z,x). The coordinates z are independent fixed interval stops after reducing genuine rigid identifications. Their desired velocities are u(t,y). Other coordinates x obey the earlier product-stop and diagonal passive-brake system, with coefficients F(t,y,v), bⱼ(t,y,v)≥0, now also permitted to depend on the **actual** v=z′. A circular coordinate can be lifted to a real local coordinate, carried continuously, and reduced modulo its circumference for observation. At a wrap no actuator work disappears. Include in F only an actual regular elimination of the remaining ports. This formulation permits a winding whose derivative depends on v and a drive whose desired velocity depends on the retained current. An algebraic loop specifying the very same u as a function of an as-yet-undetermined v needs a separately solved simultaneous port relation.

Suppose the coefficients are measurable in t, uniformly bounded on a compact local state cylinder for the bounded physical velocities, and continuous in the state/velocity arguments for almost every t. The projected directions stay bounded. The old frozen stop/brake problem is well posed for integrable prescribed drift/coefficients. For a prescribed input curve ŷ, first solve the scalar stops under u(t,ŷ). Then solve the remaining diagonal system under F(t,ŷ,v), bⱼ(t,ŷ,v). All solves start from the specified initial coordinate values. If x and x̃ solve that second frozen problem, monotonicity of its fixed normal cone and of each scalar Sign relation gives

```text
supₛ≤ₜ |x(s)−x̃(s)| ≤ |x₀−x̃₀| + ∫₀ᵗ ( |F−F̃| + Σⱼ |bⱼ−b̃ⱼ| ).
```

For this inequality subtract the equations, test against Δx, and split bⱼξⱼ−b̃ⱼξ̃ⱼ into a monotone term and a remainder bounded by |bⱼ−b̃ⱼ|. One can divide by (|Δx|²+ε²)¹ᐟ² before integrating and let ε↓0. As in the earlier theorem the braked coordinates are unrestricted by the other stops. Finite dimensionality allows a fixed norm convention and fixed finite factors for the coordinatewise estimates.

**Existence.** For h>0, evaluate the state arguments at y_h(max(0,t−h)). Construct y_h on successive h-intervals by the two frozen solves, including the newly computed velocity in the second. On a sufficiently short time cylinder all these curves stay in the cylinder by their common speed bound. The normal projection has bounded speed when its forcing is bounded, and the passive brake adds a bounded drift. By compactness of bounded equicontinuous curves, take a uniformly convergent subsequence y_h→y. Its delayed arguments have the same limit. By continuity almost everywhere and dominated convergence the desired directions converge in L¹ to u(t,y). The scalar estimate now forces **strong L¹ convergence of the actual velocities** to the stop velocities for that input, including intervals spent on the stops. Take an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence of the velocities. Boundedness and continuity of the other coefficients give L¹ convergence of F and every bⱼ. The frozen stability inequality identifies x with the solution for those limiting coefficients. The position components likewise equal their limiting stop solutions. Together these identities solve the original coupled equations. This argument preserves every fixed admitted exogenous choice and supplies local continuations from every compatible state. It does not assert that separate solutions at unrelated inputs are identical.

**Uniqueness under a stronger regularity assumption.** If u is uniformly locally Lipschitz in y, and F and b are uniformly locally Lipschitz in y and v on the cylinder, the operation on two prescribed input curves with the same initial data has output sup-distance at most C h times their input sup-distance on a horizon of length h. This follows by summing the two displayed estimates and bounding the coefficient differences by the state sup-distance and the L¹ actual-velocity difference. On a small enough interval it is a contraction. Restart on successive bounded intervals for uniqueness. This branch applies to fixed positive compliance in A′’s explicit held drive, even when the chosen numerical compliance differs between scenarios. The existence argument still applies under measurable positive compliance approaching zero when the desired velocity remains clipped to its fixed speed cap and the right side is continuous in y at almost every t. Such variation alone does not satisfy the uniform Lipschitz hypothesis for uniqueness. Allowing an arbitrary instantaneous, discontinuous state-dependent adversarial feedback would be a different input class.

A finite-horizon physical bound and the compatible limit of a finite-time trajectory permit restarting the same local construction at its endpoint. The bound must cover actual world, currents, heat and stocks. Existing held-mode charger saturation changes can be split at monotone cap hits as in [OPERATING-FLOW-JOIN](OPERATING-FLOW-JOIN.md). Genuine discrete intervention and topology changes instead require the established finite root/descendant inventory, every permitted finite tie order, and nonempty actual outcomes at forced boundaries. Limiting passage at infinitely many contacts of the **same continuous stop** in a held mode has introduced no new discrete root. A source that uses such a contact to issue a command still owes the separate rate/hysteresis certificate for that command.
