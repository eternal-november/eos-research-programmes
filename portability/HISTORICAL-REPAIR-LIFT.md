There is a decision-relevant difference even between summaries retaining both per-owner tables. Consider the diagonal pair of possible histories and the antidiagonal pair. As sets of separate labels, both summaries admit each cut order and each ADC order. On qualifying accepted jobs, the antidiagonal ensures completion by the 62nd subsequent strobe. The diagonal admits the 63rd. Under the continued oscillator and held cut of these fixtures that is exactly one quantum divided by 4096 of additional elapsed time; the next oscillator edge itself has the same time in all cases. Recording the two actual labels *together* on a single run is sufficient to recover this distinction. It is the replacement of their joint history by separate possibility sets or law marginals that fails. No randomized adversary is required. These are source-interface histories, with the whole-mission reach of their starting PC/register state a separate matter. ## Common-history construction at the actual cut

Take the chronological observer j_b=id_K, the owner observer j_c=m and the directed comparison a=m. The primitive observer square a j_b=j_c holds by the actual labelled cut and ADC projections. Introduce the *administrative* repair relation H={(x,e) | e=d(x)} with augmentation inherited from x, resource R=Q with its unit augmentation, and update u(x)=(x,d(x)) under K⊗Q≅K. The operation records the obligation still owed to an already admitted ADC child. The physical owner, stock, elapsed time and raw child state were supplied by the original source; u creates no ADC request, CPU return, power or discharge of that obligation.

The common-history lift of `21e5b6f` gives the enlarged observers (j_b,u) and (j_c,u) on the same retained history and the comparison a×_Q id_H. Both presentations now answer the downstream residual query exactly. Reverse prefix restriction forgets only the administrative insertion. The physical clock, cut and accepted offer remain in their historical places. Here K has dimension four, J has dimension three and H is the four-dimensional graph of d. The two enlarged joints have dimensions seven and six while the actual enriched observers have rank four. They do not cover arbitrary new target arrows.
